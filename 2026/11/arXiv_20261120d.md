## Key Findings

- Cold intergalactic sheets can fragment below the scale resolved in large simulations. The predicted sky area covered by neutral hydrogen depends on which column-density threshold and sheet temperature are used. [2611.25220](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25220) Charles Willard.
- In cosmic-dawn models, increasing the fraction of massive stars raises both ultraviolet production and the feedback that suppresses the light reaching an observer. [2611.25027](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25027) Andrea Ferrara.
- A weak infrared signal from a little red dot is compatible with dust intercepting a small fraction of the disc luminosity. The optical continuum predicts the measured infrared light more reliably than broad H-alpha. [2611.26483](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26483) C. Ramos Almeida.
- In Shapley, a sizable atomic gas reservoir can persist in a filament galaxy even as its star formation slows. [2611.25871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25871) Lwandile Gwebushe.

## Gas and galaxy shapes across the cosmic web

- [2611.25220](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25220) Charles Willard `Multiphase Gas in High-z Cosmic Sheets`
  - **Question:** How does a sheet of intergalactic gas break up, and how does that change its absorption cross section?
  - **Data:**
    - Two AREPO simulations follow sheets with different collapse temperatures. In their cold cores the cell sizes span `0.4-3.2 physical kpc` at `z=4`.
  - **Method:**
    - Vary resolution and the initial perturbations, track bending of the cold layer, and measure the area above fixed neutral hydrogen column densities.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Improving spatial resolution fourfold increases the area fraction above the partial Lyman-limit threshold by a factor of `1.5` in the cooler sheet. The area above the full Lyman-limit threshold grows by a factor of `3.5` in the hotter sheet.
  - **Position:** Bending of the shocked layer offers a route to absorbers outside halos. Their cosmic incidence is uncertain because the calculation samples two imposed collapse histories.

- [2611.25871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25871) Lwandile Gwebushe `Neutral hydrogen in the Shapley Supercluster core II. Gas content and star formation in the filaments connecting the core`
  - **Question:** Does atomic gas disappear as galaxies approach the dense Shapley core?
  - **Data:**
    - Five new MeerKAT filament pointings and archival core observations yield `157` filament and `127` cluster neutral hydrogen detections in the adopted redshift window.
  - **Method:**
    - Match radio sources to optical members, estimate upper limits for nondetections, and compare gas fractions and star formation rates across environments.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - About `45%` of filament members and `30%` of core members are detected at comparable sensitivity. Some gas-rich filament members would take longer than a Hubble time to consume their atomic gas at their current star formation rate.
  - **Position:** Gas retention and efficient star formation can come apart before cluster infall. Depletion times are ratios at the observed epoch and depend on sample selection.

- [2611.26593](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26593) Calum Murray `Star-forming disc galaxies align tangentially with the cosmic tidal field`
  - **Question:** How do projected disc shapes respond to the surrounding tidal field?
  - **Data:**
    - Legacy Surveys galaxy shapes and SPHEREx redshifts define a clustering sample of `39.2 million` galaxies after masking, with separate color and morphology cuts for the shape samples.
  - **Method:**
    - Correlate projected ellipticity with galaxy positions and repeat around regions at either end of the smoothed density distribution. Test the result with independent Dark Energy Survey shapes.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Blue discs align tangentially around overdensities and radially around underdensities. The sign change favors a linear contribution to the tidal response.
  - **Position:** The sign matters for intrinsic-alignment models in weak lensing. An uncertain multiplicative shape calibration limits direct use of the fitted amplitude as a survey prior.

## Starlight, magnetic fields and black-hole seeds at cosmic dawn

- [2611.25027](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25027) Andrea Ferrara `Top-heavy Initial Mass Functions are not enough at cosmic dawn`
  - **Question:** How much brighter would early galaxies become if they formed more massive stars?
  - **Data:**
    - A metallicity-dependent stellar initial mass function and a Salpeter reference both span `0.1-300 solar masses`. Published ultraviolet counts provide the observational comparison.
  - **Method:**
    - Calculate emergent light at fixed halo mass, propagating the stellar mass distribution into supernova rates, metal production and dust. Repeat the comparison in the DELPHI galaxy formation model.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Changing only light per unit star formation brightens the model by about `1.5 mag`. In the self-consistent DELPHI runs, the predicted luminosity functions differ by less than `0.5 dex` at `z~11-17`.
  - **Position:** The size of the compensation depends on the assumed dust yield, geometry and feedback. It limits this proposed explanation for the bright counts.

- [2611.25937](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25937) Evangelia Ntormousi `Magnolia: a highly magnetized galaxy at redshift z~9`
  - **Question:** Can rapid assembly magnetize a galaxy before it develops an ordered disc?
  - **Data:**
    - The Magnolia cosmological zoom follows a galaxy at approximately `10 pc` resolution with radiation and magnetized gas.
  - **Method:**
    - Track the mass-weighted field during clump assembly and decompose the transfer of kinetic energy into magnetic energy by compression and tension.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The simulated field reaches a mean of about `6 microgauss` at `z=8.6`. Compression supplies the largest kinetic-to-magnetic transfer at the onset of amplification.
  - **Position:** This calculation identifies a mechanism in one simulated galaxy. Its resolution leaves open the contribution of a small-scale turbulent dynamo in real systems.

- [2611.25355](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25355) Emir Tataroglu `The Cosmic Infrared Background from Population III.1 Supermassive Stars and Black Holes`
  - **Question:** Could an infrared background measurement reveal the stars that seeded early massive black holes?
  - **Data:**
    - The fiducial calculation includes `100,000 solar-mass` primordial stars, a lower-mass stellar population and accreting remnants. Optical and infrared background measurements bound the integrated light.
  - **Method:**
    - Integrate the stellar, nebular and accretion spectra over redshift and vary the duration and rate of remnant growth.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The fiducial stellar signal lies `2-4 orders of magnitude` below present infrared background measurements. Accreting remnants can dominate the modeled signal, with a strong dependence on their assumed growth history.
  - **Position:** This is a conditional prediction for a particular seeding scenario. Other high-redshift galaxy populations and source-clustering fluctuations require separate modeling.

## Dust around accreting black holes

- [2611.26483](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26483) C. Ramos Almeida `The Galaxy Activity, Torus, and Outflow Survey: N. Little Red Dots are not dust deficient: constraints from Seyfert 1 JWST spectra`
  - **Question:** How much infrared emission should accompany the optical light of compact little red dots?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST spectra cover eight nearby type 1 Seyferts. Comparison data include a stack of `249` little red dots and a local analog.
  - **Method:**
    - Normalize the Seyfert spectra separately by broad H-alpha and the continuum at 5100 angstroms. Compare both predictions with the little-red-dot spectra and fit disc and dust emission together.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The optical continuum predicts infrared emission within a factor of about `1.5-2`. A joint fit favors a reddened disc and dust that intercepts only about `10-20%` of its light.
  - **Position:** Weak infrared emission can coexist with nuclear dust. The high-redshift stack reaches only about `4 microns` in the rest frame, and host light can enter the aperture.
