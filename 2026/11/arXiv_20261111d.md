## Key Findings

- Early assembly inferred from photometry is model-dependent: simulated recovery can be biased even when average mass histories agree. Treat the reconstructed `z>15` population as a conditional estimate ([2611.13835](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13835) L. Ciesla).
- Extreme emission-line analogues and UV-selected luminosity functions constrain different populations; neither alone measures escaping ionizing radiation ([2611.14332](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14332) M. Delgado Mancheno; [2611.13518](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13518) Lei Sun).
- Apparent environmental trends need physical checks: cluster-radius normalization explains much of the radio-size trend, while gas-tail chronology depends on distance and stripping thresholds ([2611.13185](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13185) Souvik Manik; [2611.13951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13951) E. Sarpa).
- Accurate redshift peaks do not guarantee calibrated errors: seven of eight lens 68% intervals miss the reference redshift ([2611.14229](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14229) Shuaibo Geng).

## Early galaxies and the UV population

- [2611.13835](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13835) L. Ciesla et al. `Tracing early galaxies back in time: implications for the dearth of galaxies beyond z~15`
  - **Question:** Can observed early galaxies constrain their progenitors beyond redshift 15?
  - **Data:** JWST/HST: 400 photometrically selected GOODS-South galaxies at `9<z<15`; dust-free SPHINX20 mock photometry calibrates the reconstruction.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit spectral energy distributions with CIGALE using correlated stochastic fluctuations around a smooth star formation history; reconstruct cumulative stellar mass and earlier UV emission.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Under this model, 28 galaxies had assembled 20% of their observed stellar mass by `z=15`; 11 meet that threshold at `z=17`. These are reconstructed progenitors, not detections at those epochs.
    - The model is tuned to the same simulations used for validation. Individual formation redshifts remain uncertain by about one redshift unit; recovery at z=9 is biased early for 68% of mocks. Dust-free assumptions, photometric selection and star formation priors limit extrapolation.
  - **Position:** Extends stochastic star formation modeling to archaeological estimates of the population beyond the current detection frontier.

- [2611.14332](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14332) M. Delgado Mancheno et al. `Tracing the Epoch of Reionization through extreme emission line galaxies around Cosmic Noon`
  - **Question:** How closely do accessible extreme emission-line galaxies resemble reionization-era emitters?
  - **Data:** HST CANDELS/EGS: 102 Hbeta+[O III] candidates at `1.40<z<1.90` and 127 Halpha candidates at `0.67<z<1.14`; 27 sources have spectroscopic redshifts.
  - **Method:**
    - Select J-band excesses; compare photometric and spectroscopic line properties, redshift probabilities, matched controls and high-redshift samples.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Typical stellar masses near `10^8 solar masses` and specific star formation rates near `10^-8 yr^-1` identify vigorous dwarf starbursts. Their equivalent widths and masses resemble selected high-redshift extreme emitters.
    - The full sample is not spectroscopically confirmed. Section 3.2 and the abstract give different Hbeta+[O III] confirmation rates, and the stated denominators need care; no single rate is adopted here. Selection and star formation timescales differ across comparison samples; analogy does not measure ionizing escape.
  - **Position:** Provides lower-redshift laboratories for extreme emitters, rather than a representative census of all reionizing galaxies.

- [2611.13518](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13518) Lei Sun et al. `The UV luminosity function at 1.6 < z < 2.2 from UVCANDELS`
  - **Question:** What is the faint-end UV luminosity distribution near cosmic noon?
  - **Data:** HST UVCANDELS F435W: 5,212 galaxies at `1.6<z<2.2`, reaching `M_UV=-16.8` above a 30% completeness threshold.
  - **Method:**
    - Inject mock galaxies, recover them through the imaging pipeline, and fit a Schechter luminosity function by maximum likelihood.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The faint-end slope is `alpha=-1.586 (+0.040/-0.042)`. Its change between the two redshift bins is only `1.4 sigma`; stronger evolutionary evidence uses the wider baseline including earlier F275W measurements.
    - Completeness depends on assumed galaxy profiles. UV luminosity densities integrated to M_UV=-15 extrapolate below the measured limit and exclude dust-obscured light; bright bins have small counts.
  - **Position:** Adds a deep blank-field reference for the faint galaxy contribution to unobscured cosmic star formation.


## Gas excitation and environmental history

- [2611.13951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13951) E. Sarpa et al. `A Virgo Environmental Survey Tracing Ionised Gas Emission (VESTIGE). XXIII. NGC 4569 past orbit reconstruction with ORFEO`
  - **Question:** Can a stripped gas tail recover a galaxy orbit and stripping chronology?
  - **Data:** NGC 4569: sky positions and line-of-sight velocities of 94 Halpha regions, gas surface-density constraints, and Virgo cluster mass and X-ray gas profiles.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit transverse velocities using an orbit plus a one-dimensional gas-tail drag model; repeat the reconstruction at fixed trial distances.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At `d=16.5 Mpc`, the fitted tail has a dynamical age of `250 (+37/-19) Myr` and peak ram pressure occurs at `t=-163 (+11/-15) Myr`, with the present defined as zero.
    - Tail fit quality alone permits both pre-peak and post-peak solutions at different distances. An independent stripping threshold favors the latter. The age describes the modeled phase-space sequence, not gas recombination or visibility; the model omits resolved hydrodynamics.
  - **Position:** Offers a fast inverse constraint to guide tailored ram-pressure simulations.

- [2611.13116](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13116) Olivia R Pereira et al. `Revealing Hidden Excitation in the Core of NGC 4696: New Infrared Diagnostic Diagrams with JWST`
  - **Question:** Can infrared lines separate excitation conditions hidden in optical diagnostics?
  - **Data:** JWST/NIRSpec: the central `620 x 620 pc` of NGC 4696 at `1.7-3.1 microns`; matched-aperture VLT/MUSE optical line ratios.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit continuum and emission lines in core, swirl and filament apertures; compare molecular-to-recombination ratios and hydrogen excitation diagrams with photoionization reference models.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Filament H2 1-0 S(1)/Paalpha ratios are roughly `4-8 times` the compact-core values, despite much weaker regional separation in optical diagnostics.
    - Overlapping photoionization and non-radiative heating signatures prevent a unique energy-source decomposition. Missing low-energy rotational H2 lines make warm-gas masses strongly temperature- and extrapolation-dependent.
  - **Position:** Adds spatially resolved molecular excitation information to optical classifications of cluster-core cooling and feedback.

- [2611.13185](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13185) Souvik Manik et al. `Giant Radio Galaxies from the Third Data Release of the LOFAR Two-metre Sky Survey (LoTSS-DR3): Physical Characteristics and Environments`
  - **Question:** Does cluster mass suppress the physical extent of giant radio galaxies?
  - **Data:** LOFAR LoTSS-DR3: 2,333 newly identified giant radio galaxies, including 382 hosted by brightest cluster galaxies with environmental measurements.
  - **Method:**
    - Visually identify extended radio structures and optical hosts; derive projected sizes from redshifts; regress physical and cluster-radius-normalized size against cluster mass and redshift.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The physical-size mass coefficient is `-0.025 +/- 0.025` (`p=0.314`). The strong decline of normalized size with mass largely reflects the mass dependence of the denominator, the cluster radius.
    - The sample excludes previously cataloged giants and has angular-size, surface-brightness and host-identification selection. Few objects populate the highest-mass bins; absence of a detected trend does not establish environmental independence.
  - **Position:** Clarifies how radius normalization can create an apparent environmental trend in radio-source growth.


## AGN dust and blended-source inference

- [2611.13181](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13181) Chi-Zhuo Wang et al. `Dust Reverberation Mapping of Low-Mass Active Galactic Nuclei`
  - **Question:** Do low-mass active nuclei show radially stratified dust reverberation?
  - **Data:** ZTF optical and WISE infrared light curves: 65 quality-selected active nuclei from 1,033 sources with sufficiently sampled paired light curves.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare cross-correlation and two light-curve modeling methods; test unrelated variability and subtract an assumed accretion-disk contribution.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The corrected W2/W1 lag ratio has median `1.24`, with sample 16th-84th percentiles `1.03-1.57`, consistent with cooler dust responding farther out.
    - This range is population dispersion, not uncertainty on the median. Sparse infrared cadence, lag-peak selection and the assumed disk spectrum affect inference; the core sample is strongly quality-selected.
  - **Position:** Extends dust reverberation measurements toward lower-mass active nuclei.

- [2611.14229](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14229) Shuaibo Geng et al. `The PAU Survey: Dissecting strong-lensing systems with narrow-band photometric redshifts`
  - **Question:** Can narrow-band photometry recover separate redshifts in blended strong lenses?
  - **Data:** PAU narrow bands plus HSC/CFHT broad bands for eight known strong lenses selected with spectroscopic source redshifts below 2.
  - **Method:**
    - Define component regions using broad-band lens models; subtract point-reflected foreground light; fit independent redshift distributions and compare with unsubtracted and broad-band controls.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - All eight lens peaks and four source peaks meet `|delta_z|<0.05`, where `delta_z=(z_phot-z_spec)/(1+z_spec)`. Subtraction reduces median source absolute residual from `0.336` to `0.071`.
    - Seven of eight lens central 68% intervals miss spectroscopy despite accurate peaks. Spectroscopic sample selection, an upper redshift grid boundary of 2, unpropagated geometry errors and foreground asymmetry prevent a calibrated blind-survey claim.
  - **Position:** A component-photometry proof of concept for lens follow-up; uncertainty calibration remains necessary.
