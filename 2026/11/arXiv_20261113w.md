## Key Findings

- This week's gas probes constrain different observables: Mg II absorption changes near a characteristic stellar mass, chemical dilution does not correlate significantly with single-sightline Lyalpha, and faint protocluster radio stacks remain compatible with star formation. These are not interchangeable measures of gas supply or feedback ([2611.12734](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12734) Zeyu Chen; [2611.14701](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14701) Alejandro J. Olvera; [2611.11107](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11107) Yuheng Zhang).
- Blue UV colors and abundant early sources do not uniquely determine escaping ionizing radiation: stellar age, nebular continuum and feedback timing must be modeled together ([2611.17208](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17208) C. Bondestam; [2611.15931](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15931) Nicholas Choustikov).
- Pristine stars can form near enriched systems in simulations, while a supermassive primordial-star population remains a separate hypothetical scenario with conditional 21 cm forecasts ([2611.15915](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15915) Elizabeth Bryce; [2611.16006](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16006) Nicholas Kern).
- Ionized outflow loading and slitless spatial maps depend on what the observation resolves and which gas phase it traces ([2611.15884](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15884) Michael Romano; [2611.16504](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16504) L. Gabarra).
- Photometric ancestry and line-based dust corrections require validation beyond an average fit: reconstructed early assembly retains prior dependence, and better median line consistency coexists with worse outlier tails ([2611.13835](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13835) L. Ciesla; [2611.08594](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08594) Owen Zafuta).

## Gas mapping and feedback

- [2611.16286](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16286) Xihan Deng et al. `Machine-Learning Reconstruction of Intergalactic Medium Column-Density Maps from Observed Lyalpha Emission Cubes`
  - **Question:** Can noisy Lyalpha emission recover projected intergalactic gas structure?
  - **Data:** Keck/KCWI protocluster cubes at `z~2.3`; 6,000 simulated inputs from 25 IllustrisTNG100 pointing centers.
  - **Method:**
    - Train an ensemble of convolutional networks on simulated emission and projected gas maps; compare with held-out views, FABLE simulations and empty observational controls.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Observed predictions contain coherent structures extending over roughly `1 comoving Mpc`. The test recovers broad simulated filaments, but the output is a model-conditioned column-density map, not a direct three-dimensional gas measurement.
    - Training and test sets share pointing-center environments; radiative transfer uses a local empirical correction that cannot reproduce nonlocal scattering. Dust is disabled and Gaussian noise tests omit physical-model uncertainty.
  - **Position:** A feasibility study linking emission mapping to gas simulations; independent-environment validation remains necessary.

- [2611.14701](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14701) Alejandro J. Olvera et al. `DIISC-VIII: Chemical Signatures of Gas Accretion in Star-Forming Galaxies`
  - **Question:** Do metal-poor disk regions reveal recent gas supply?
  - **Data:** Binospec optical spectra, VATT imaging and VLA HI maps of 11 nearby star-forming galaxies; HST/COS quasar sightlines probe circumgalactic gas.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare N2 oxygen abundances against radial gradients and the resolved mass-metallicity relation; estimate dilution masses and test Lyalpha absorption residuals.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The two definitions identify 75 anomalously metal-poor regions, with 29 selected by both. No significant relation links Lyalpha excess to disk metallicity gradients or the radial-gradient-selected anomalous fraction.
    - One circumgalactic sightline per galaxy, small samples and censored absorption limit this null result. Dilution-based supply rates assume gas metallicity, mixing lifetime and representative slit sampling; they are not measured inflow velocities.
  - **Position:** Connects resolved disk chemistry with absorption-based circumgalactic tests.

- [2611.15884](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15884) Michael Romano et al. `A statistical study of ionized outflows in low-mass galaxies from DESI DR1`
  - **Question:** How efficiently do low-mass galaxies expel ionized gas?
  - **Data:** DESI DR1 dwarf spectra: 5,781 robust outflow candidates at `z<0.45`, predominantly star-forming systems.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit narrow and broad [O III]/Halpha components; compare inferred outflow rates with star formation rates and stacked spectra.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The ionized mass-loading factor, outflow rate divided by star formation rate, spans about `0.001-1`, with median near `0.01`. Individual detections emphasize starbursts; stacked ordinary galaxies have lower average values.
    - The adopted `1 kpc` radius, density, oxygen abundance and ionization correction set the normalization. Atomic and molecular phases are absent; low ionized loading cannot establish weak total feedback.
  - **Position:** Extends dwarf outflow statistics while preserving the distinction between detected bursts and average galaxies.

- [2611.11107](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11107) Yuheng Zhang et al. `Probing faint radio activity from cores to filaments in two z=2.24 protoclusters with uGMRT`
  - **Question:** Does faint radio activity vary between protocluster cores and filaments?
  - **Data:**
    - uGMRT 1.26 GHz maps of BOSS1244 and BOSS1542 at `z=2.24`; 187 and 105 Halpha-selected members fall inside the usable radio areas.
  - **Method:**
    - Cross-match radio sources, compare radio luminosity with dust-corrected Halpha star formation rates, and median-stack undetected members and projected environmental bins.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - After removing robust and potential counterparts, 173 and 102 objects yield `2.8 sigma` and `3.2 sigma` stacks consistent with star formation. Two Halpha emitters and the quiescent galaxy QG1 show strong radio excess. Environmental bins exclude robust counterparts only: their higher central/near-filament signals are not statistically significant and do not establish enhanced AGN activity.
  - **Position:** Extends individual protocluster radio detections to the faint population while retaining the star-formation/AGN ambiguity.

- [2611.12734](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12734) Zeyu Chen et al. `The Cool Circumgalactic Echo of the Stellar-to-Halo Mass Relation`
  - **Question:** How do cool circumgalactic gas structure and kinematics vary with stellar mass?
  - **Data:**
    - DESI Year 1 foreground galaxies and background quasars; millions of usable pairs, with Mg II coverage requiring `z_gal>0.3` and the principal analysis using `20<D<180 kpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Normalize quasar spectra, median-stack in galaxy rest frames, fit absorption and subtract instrumental/redshift broadening; test radius, redshift and star-formation dependence.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Absorption peaks near `M*=3e10 Msun`; radial profiles flatten with increasing stellar mass. The corrected ensemble line width crosses the adopted virial reference near `log(M*/Msun)=10.5`. A simple stellar-to-halo mass scaling underpredicts massive red galaxies by about `0.24 dex`. Equivalent width is not a gas-mass measurement, and the virial comparison depends on an assumed halo-mass relation.
  - **Position:** Connects statistical cool-gas absorption with galaxy-formation efficiency; it does not uniquely identify feedback or inflow.

## First galaxies and reionization

- [2611.15931](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15931) Nicholas Choustikov et al. `Anatomy of LyC Escape in MEGATRON: Connecting Feedback Physics to Demographics, Leakage Timescales, and Explorations of ISM Density as a Tracer of Escape Fraction`
  - **Question:** Which feedback conditions allow ionizing photons to escape?
  - **Data:** Four MEGATRON radiation-hydrodynamic re-simulations of the same Milky Way progenitor initial conditions, with different star formation and feedback prescriptions.
  - **Method:**
    - Follow starburst-to-escape delays and synthetic line diagnostics; compare angle-averaged escape with gas density and recent star formation histories.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Post-starburst systems dominate faint leaking populations in these models. A UV-slope calibration can overpredict escaping ionizing luminosity by about `100 times` during the first `5 Myr` of a burst.
    - The [O II] density trend is statistical and not strongly predictive; it weakens for multiple emitting regions and does not recover sightline-specific escape. Four prescriptions in one zoom do not establish universal calibration.
  - **Position:** Makes feedback delay explicit in translating UV populations into reionization emissivity.

- [2611.17208](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17208) C. Bondestam et al. `The ultraviolet continuum slopes of galaxies at z >= 12 selected from 0.6 deg^2 of JWST imaging`
  - **Question:** How blue are galaxies near the present redshift frontier?
  - **Data:** JWST/NIRCam: 55 candidates at `11.5<=z<=15.2` from more than `0.6 deg^2`, supplemented by a lower-redshift comparison and available spectroscopy.
  - **Method:**
    - Jointly fit UV power-law slope and redshift; test selection and measurement bias with simulated photometry.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The inverse-variance weighted UV slope is `beta=-2.57+/-0.04`, consistent with a typical dust-attenuation-free stellar plus nebular continuum. No individual source robustly requires `beta<-3` within its uncertainty.
    - This is a UV-selected population average, not proof that every early galaxy lacks dust. Individual slopes are noisy; red sources remain compatible with the sample and slope-fitting choices affect comparisons.
  - **Position:** Extends the high-redshift UV-color baseline without requiring exotic stellar populations.

- [2611.15915](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15915) Elizabeth Bryce et al. `Where Do Population III Stars Form? Unveiling Late PopIII-Forming Environments with CampFIRE-6k`
  - **Question:** Must late Population III formation occur in isolated environments?
  - **Data:** CampFIRE-6k: a `5 comoving Mpc` simulation volume with gas mass resolution about `6,000 solar masses`, evolved to `z~6`.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify star formation below critical metallicity `~1e-5 Z_sun`; examine halo masses, stellar neighbors and gas metallicity.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At `z~6`, seven halos contain Population III stars younger than `10 Myr`, and three still form them. Active sites include an isolated system and pristine clumps near enriched stars.
    - The small volume limits rare-object statistics and the resolution misses detailed minihalo formation. Halo-averaged enrichment does not give the metallicity of the star-forming clump.
  - **Position:** Guides spatially resolved searches for late primordial stars; it does not establish their observed incidence.

- [2611.16006](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16006) Nicholas Kern et al. `A flash before dawn: 21 cm signatures of transient reionization from supermassive Pop III stars`
  - **Question:** Could transient early ionization be distinguished with 21 cm measurements?
  - **Data:** Modified 21cmFAST simulations of hypothetical supermassive Population III.1 sources, plus SKA Low and global-signal noise forecasts.
  - **Method:**
    - Evolve ionization, recombination and spin temperature; perform Fisher forecasts with foreground scenarios and nuisance parameters.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Relic hot bubbles can boost the `z>20` 21 cm power spectrum. A representative optical-depth forecast reaches `sigma_tau~0.012` under moderately optimistic foreground handling.
    - This is a conditional forecast, not a detection. Spin-temperature fluctuations complicate ionization inference; source formation and particle-physics assumptions matter, and global measurements need low-frequency coverage with very small residual foregrounds.
  - **Position:** Explores an observational test of an exotic early-source model rather than evidence that it occurs.

- [2611.13835](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13835) L. Ciesla et al. `Tracing early galaxies back in time: implications for the dearth of galaxies beyond z~15`
  - **Question:** Can observed early galaxies constrain their progenitors beyond redshift 15?
  - **Data:** JWST/HST: 400 photometrically selected GOODS-South galaxies at `9<z<15`; dust-free SPHINX20 mock photometry calibrates the reconstruction.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit spectral energy distributions with CIGALE using correlated stochastic fluctuations around a smooth star formation history; reconstruct cumulative stellar mass and earlier UV emission.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Under this model, 28 galaxies had assembled 20% of their observed stellar mass by `z=15`; 11 meet that threshold at `z=17`. These are reconstructed progenitors, not detections at those epochs.
    - The model is tuned to the same simulations used for validation. Individual formation redshifts remain uncertain by about one redshift unit; recovery at z=9 is biased early for 68% of mocks. Dust-free assumptions, photometric selection and star formation priors limit extrapolation.
  - **Position:** Extends stochastic star formation modeling to archaeological estimates of the population beyond the current detection frontier.

## Survey methods

- [2611.16504](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16504) L. Gabarra et al. `Euclid: Data Release 1 (DR1) - First NISP emission-line maps: Methodology, validation, and applications to galaxy evolution`
  - **Question:** Can Euclid slitless spectra measure resolved line emission?
  - **Data:** COSMOS performance-verification blue-grism data over `0.5 deg^2`: 116 exposures; Halpha analyses at `0.4<z<0.9`.
  - **Method:**
    - Resample at fixed wavelength, subtract continuum, inject simulated sources and compare two-dimensional line fits with one-dimensional spectra.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - A stack of 2,634 galaxies gives Halpha-to-continuum half-light-radius ratios of `1.27, 1.12, 1.11, 1.10` across four size bins after point-spread-function matching. The maps also recover a consistent mass-metallicity relation for a stricter line-selected subsample.
    - One dispersion orientation leaves morphology and velocity shifts degenerate. Neighboring emission-line contamination is not explicitly modeled; oversampling does not improve intrinsic resolution.
  - **Position:** Demonstrates resolved emission recovery from survey slitless data, with wider applications awaiting validation.

- [2611.08594](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08594) Owen Zafuta et al. `Constraining Dust Attenuation Curves in Star-Forming Galaxies at Cosmic Noon Using Multiple Emission Line Ratios`
  - **Question:** Can three hydrogen lines constrain a galaxy-specific attenuation curve?
  - **Data:**
    - JADES NIRSpec spectra from the DAWN archive; 386 accepted solutions from 848 attempted fits at `0.5<=z<=7`.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit a power-law attenuation curve to three hydrogen lines assuming Case B recombination; test unused transitions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Paschen lines provide more leverage than Balmer-only combinations. Median line-to-line star-formation-rate scatter is `0.07 dex`, versus `0.09` for SMC-like and `0.11` for Calzetti-like corrections. However, Section V also gives a larger factor-of-two discrepancy fraction for the fitted curves (`7.5%` versus `6.2%` and `5.5%`); improvement is not uniform. Selection, Case B assumptions and slope-normalization covariance prevent a universal dust-law inference.
  - **Position:** Tests three-line attenuation fitting and its high-redshift limitations.
