## Key Findings

- Clustering adds information beyond ultraviolet number counts, but the tighter stellar-to-halo scatter constraint depends on the selected mass threshold. ([2611.18850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18850) Ivan Nikolic).
- Present-day unbound dark matter explains much of halo secondary bias statistically; the result does not yet measure gas accretion or establish a causal history. ([2611.19491](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19491) Qinglin Ma).
- Cluster-core luminosity inequality shows no significant evolution since redshift one; stable relative dominance is compatible with continued absolute stellar-mass growth. ([2611.18929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18929) Katiya Fosdick).
- The X-ray-emitting phase may dominate the ionized outflow mass in NGC 4151; neither the total molecular reservoir nor integrated line luminosity alone establishes feedback efficiency. ([2611.20236](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20236) Lea M. Feuillet).

## High-redshift galaxies and primordial remnants

- [2611.18850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18850) Ivan Nikolic `Interpreting JWST UV luminosity functions and angular correlation functions via a "universal language" for high-redshift galaxies`
  - **Question:** What breaks the degeneracy between star formation and the galaxy-halo connection at high redshift?
  - **Data:**
    - CANUCS ultraviolet luminosity functions at `z=8-12`, restricted to `M_UV > -20`; COSMOS-Web mass-selected clustering at `z=6-10.5`, with stellar masses above `10^9 Msun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit conditional distributions linking halo mass, stellar mass, star formation rate, and ultraviolet magnitude; use priors spanning five simulations and a halo occupation model on angular scales above `0.005 deg`.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Adding clustering narrows the 68% interval for stellar-to-halo mass scatter from `0.20-0.86 dex` to `0.16-0.60 dex`. Star-forming-main-sequence scatter supplies a median `61%` of ultraviolet variance at `M_UV=-20`, with a broad `39-81%` interval.
  - **Position:** Builds on the CANUCS luminosity functions and COSMOS-Web clustering analyses. The scatter improvement disappears with the other tested mass thresholds. Missing off-diagonal covariance can make posteriors overconfident; short-timescale burstiness remains prior-dependent. Predictions beyond `z=14` are extrapolations, not candidate redshift measurements.

- [2611.21580](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21580) Maulik Bhatt `Gravitational Waves from Population III Black Hole Mergers in Primordial Gas Disks: Constraining Astrophysical Scenarios with the Laser Interferometer Space Antenna and the Einstein Telescope`
  - **Question:** Could Population III stellar remnants produce detectable gravitational waves in primordial gas disks?
  - **Data:**
    - A semi-analytic `10^6 Msun` halo at `z=20`; two favorable parameter configurations, each sampled with 1,000 stochastic realizations.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine disk fragmentation, a top-heavy stellar initial mass function, simplified remnant prescriptions, gas drag, accretion, and inspiral strain; apply an orientation-averaged signal-to-noise threshold of `8`.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The illustrated best LISA realization gives 11 detectable mergers; the Einstein Telescope example is one of only two realizations yielding a detectable event. The detectors probe different primary-mass ranges, approximately `10^3-10^4 Msun` versus a few tens of solar masses.
  - **Position:** A proof of detectability, not a cosmological rate forecast. The model assumes prompt merger at the primary Bondi radius, neglects binary hardening and satellite binary evolution, and treats gas as static for drag. Favorable configurations and best realizations cannot represent the halo population.

## Environment and galaxy assembly

- [2611.19491](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19491) Qinglin Ma `Unbound matter in the circum-halo medium: a common mediator of halo secondary bias`
  - **Question:** Which environmental property accounts for the dependence of halo clustering on assembly and structure?
  - **Data:**
    - TNG300-1-Dark distinct haloes at `z=0`, spanning `10^10.5-10^14.5 h^-1 Msun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure the unbound matter fraction in the `2-3 Rvir` shell; compare clustering at fixed halo mass using partial rank correlations and reweighted property quartiles.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The unbound fraction correlates with halo bias at `0.35-0.60`. Conditioning on it reduces correlations of bias with formation time, concentration, spin, shape, and velocity anisotropy to magnitudes around `0.04` or less.
  - **Position:** Extends environmental explanations of halo assembly bias. Small residual correlations do not prove conditional independence or causality. The finite-potential binding definition is not eventual escape; gas supply and galaxy properties require hydrodynamic tests and calibrated observational proxies.

- [2611.18929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18929) Katiya Fosdick `Quantifying the Dominance and Emergence of Brightest Cluster Galaxies in SPT and Planck Clusters: A Novel Application of the Gini Index`
  - **Question:** Have brightest cluster galaxies increased their luminosity dominance relative to cluster members since `z=1`?
  - **Data:**
    - 92 Sunyaev-Zeldovich-selected clusters: 76 HST-SPT systems at `0.2<z<1.0` plus a low-redshift SDSS-Planck anchor, selected along a common mass-evolution track.
  - **Method:**
    - Compute a Gini index of red-sequence member luminosities within `0.15-0.20 R500`, with matched limiting magnitudes; fit linear redshift evolution with intrinsic scatter and vary centering and membership choices.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The combined BCG-centered fit gives Gini slope `0.00 +/- 0.03`, mean index `0.72 +/- 0.01`, and intrinsic scatter `0.07 +/- 0.01`. Relative luminosity dominance is consistent with no evolution; this does not imply no absolute stellar-mass growth.
  - **Position:** Offers an alternative to the two-galaxy magnitude gap. The index depends strongly on the limiting magnitude. Estimated HST-SPT contamination is `47%` overall and rises with redshift; robustness tests are reassuring within the adopted field-luminosity model, not proof of uncontaminated membership.

- [2611.20884](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20884) Prem Kumar `Orbital decomposition and evolution of LEGA-C-like galaxies in IllustrisTNG from z = 1 to z = 0`
  - **Question:** How do stellar orbital components change between redshift one and today?
  - **Data:**
    - IllustrisTNG100 analogs of the LEGA-C survey: 3,058 galaxies at `z=1`, with 2,281 surviving distinct descendants at `z=0`; a separate TNG50 sample tests resolution.
  - **Method:**
    - Average an approximate stellar circularity estimator over 11 snapshots; classify cold, warm, hot, and counter-rotating orbits within twice the stellar half-mass radius; compare merger and accreted-stellar-mass histories.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Hot orbits become more prominent toward higher mass and later times. Major and gas-poor mergers accompany stronger orbital heating; star-forming systems retain more ordered motion. Accreted stellar mass correlates with orbital fractions after controlling for stellar mass.
  - **Position:** Connects LEGA-C-like initial conditions to local orbit-based studies. These are simulated predictions, not observed LEGA-C decompositions. Surviving descendants define the evolutionary sample, and TNG50 retains more cold/warm orbits at `z=0`; aperture and circularity definitions also affect comparisons.

## Multiphase active-galactic-nucleus feedback

- [2611.20236](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20236) Lea M. Feuillet `Unveiling the Multi-Phase Outflow in NGC 4151 with JWST Observations - I. Integrated Properties of the Outflow`
  - **Question:** Which gas phase carries the largest ionized outflow mass in NGC 4151?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST MIRI and NIRSpec spectroscopy, Chandra imaging, and archival X-ray spectroscopy of the nearby Seyfert nucleus.
  - **Method:**
    - Align and fit infrared emission-line cubes; use Cloudy photoionization models to distinguish nebular, coronal, and X-ray-tracing ions and fit the integrated spectrum with five components.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Ionized profiles share a component near `-250 km/s`. The X-ray-emitting phase has inferred mass `(1.4 +/- 0.2) x 10^5 Msun`, exceeding the other modeled ionized phases. Molecular gas is a larger reservoir but shows no compelling outflow signature in these data.
  - **Position:** Extends infrared tracing of X-ray gas to a multiphase comparison. Integrated masses assume an average radius of `72 pc` and differ from earlier resolved estimates. The warm-to-cold molecular extrapolation is model-dependent; feedback efficiency and the proposed post-blowout interpretation await spatially resolved tests.
