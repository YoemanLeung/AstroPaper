## Key Findings

- A resolved surface-brightness break separates disk and halo emission in a dwarf, but the metal-poor clump remains an accretion candidate, not a confirmed inflow trajectory ([2611.17480](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17480) Chloe Neufeld).
- Ionizing-photon accounting and gas-phase selection affect inferred feedback strengths: a gas-inclusive escape ratio is different from stellar-photon survival, and ionized winds do not measure the full mass budget ([2611.17582](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17582) B. Melekh; [2611.17592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17592) Anna F. McLeod).
- Stellar age explains much of the low-mass environment-shape association in FIREbox; this is a conditional correlation, with resolution and observational-transfer limits ([2611.17427](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17427) Francisco J. Mercado).
- Large active-galactic-nucleus samples remain calibration-dependent: luminosity corrections shift inferred obscured fractions, while variability selection needs explicit null tests and spectroscopic follow-up ([2611.18286](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18286) Ryan J. Turney; [2611.17996](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17996) Suvendu Rakshit).

## Gas cycling and ionizing radiation

- [2611.17480](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17480) Chloe Neufeld `The ISM-CGM Transition of a Star-forming Dwarf Galaxy Resolved in Ionized Gas Emission`
  - **Question:** Where does the interstellar medium give way to circumgalactic gas in a low-mass galaxy?
  - **Data:**
    - Keck KCWI/KCRM maps of UGC 6578, with stellar mass `10^7.8 Msun`, supplemented by HI, ultraviolet, and optical imaging; emission reaches about `6 kpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit the Halpha radial profile; combine emission-line ratios, a photon budget, and resolved velocities to test the gas origin.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The profile changes from exponential to shallow power law at `2.5 kpc = 0.033 Rvir`. Stellar photoionization can explain the inner halo emission. The external clump has inferred oxygen abundance about one dex below the galaxy, favoring accretion over enriched ejecta.
  - **Position:** This is a single-system extension of resolved disk-halo emission studies. Strong-line branch choices, upper limits, and line-of-sight velocities limit interpretation; an optically faint satellite remains possible.

- [2611.17582](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17582) B. Melekh `Hydrodynamic and photoionization analysis of a Cosmic Dawn dwarf galaxy based on a JWST-inferred star formation history`
  - **Question:** How does an evolving wind bubble modify the escaping spectrum of a Cosmic Dawn dwarf?
  - **Data:**
    - A previously JWST-inferred star formation history for galaxy 2779 supplies a model sequence from `z~29.5` to `9.5`; this is not a time-resolved observational history.
  - **Method:**
    - Couple BPASS stellar populations to spherical wind-bubble components and nebular radiative transfer; compare the emergent spectral energy distribution with JWST photometry.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Ionizing escape varies non-monotonically as the shell and ambient gas change opacity. The defined ratio `Q_escape/Q_stellar` can exceed one because its numerator includes diffuse gas emission; it is not a stellar-photon escape probability.
  - **Position:** This extends the earlier photometric reconstruction with evolving gas. The final spectrum overpredicts the observed flux; the lower-mass comparison is illustrative, not a refitted mass. Spherical geometry and omitted stages between bubbles prevent population-level reionization conclusions.

- [2611.17592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17592) Anna F. McLeod `Observations of stellar feedback: tracers, diagnostics, measurements, and physical interpretation`
  - **Question:** Which parts of stellar feedback are actually constrained by observed gas and stellar emission?
  - **Data:**
    - A literature review of ionized gas, bubbles, molecular-cloud dispersal, multiphase outflows, and escaping ionizing radiation, including nearby resolved systems and JWST-era galaxies.
  - **Method:**
    - Trace each conversion from observables to physical quantities, then examine stellar-population assumptions and environmental dependence.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Ionized outflow rates scale inversely with adopted electron density and flow radius at fixed line luminosity and velocity. A single gas phase cannot determine the total outflow budget; stellar-population degeneracies also propagate into inferred feedback strengths.
  - **Position:** A synthesis rather than a new homogeneous measurement. Sections on matched tracers and spatial scales provide a practical framework for comparing observations with simulations; local calibrations need testing before high-redshift transfer.

## Environment and stellar structure

- [2611.17427](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17427) Francisco J. Mercado `Effects of Environment on the Star Formation Histories and Shapes of Simulated Galaxies`
  - **Question:** Does environment retain a connection to galaxy shape after controlling for stellar age and mass?
  - **Data:**
    - FIREbox at `z=0`: 1,720 galaxies in a `22.1 comoving Mpc` box. Shape measurements impose a stricter 300-star-particle aperture cut, excluding masses below roughly `10^7.2 Msun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure cumulative stellar formation times and intrinsic axis ratios; quantify neighbors through a tidal-strength index; use partial correlations controlling stellar mass and formation time.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Below `10^8 Msun`, the environment-shape correlation becomes consistent with zero after those controls. Between `10^8` and `10^10 Msun`, a modest residual remains (`rho~0.2`). Stronger tidal environments host earlier-forming, rounder low-mass systems.
  - **Position:** The statistical controls do not establish a causal sequence. Missing active-galactic-nucleus feedback, possible numerical heating, the adopted reionization history, and intrinsic rather than observed shapes bound the result.

## Active galactic nuclei: obscuration and variability

- [2611.18286](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18286) Ryan J. Turney `Extending an X-ray & Mid-Infrared diagnostic for absorption in Active Galactic Nuclei to higher redshifts using NuSTAR and XMM-Newton`
  - **Question:** Can X-ray to mid-infrared luminosity ratios identify heavily obscured active galactic nuclei when spectra are faint?
  - **Data:**
    - NuSTAR and XMM-Newton spectral fits for 47 bright nuclei, median `z=0.35`, with WISE photometry; a broader selected sample contains 350 nuclei, including 103 X-ray luminosity upper limits.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit absorption models; bootstrap the luminosity-ratio relation accounting for measurement errors; propagate the diagnostic to the broader sample and apply a statistical luminosity correction.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The corrected observed Compton-thick fraction is `4.9 (+2.0/-2.3)%` at one sigma, versus `2.6 (+2.3/-0.6)%` without that correction. The calibration offset is associated with X-ray luminosity, so a redshift-only interpretation is insufficient.
  - **Position:** This extends local Swift/BAT-based diagnostics. The fraction describes the selected observed sample, not an intrinsic cosmic fraction; upper limits, host contamination, and approximate luminosity corrections matter.

- [2611.17996](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17996) Suvendu Rakshit `WISE-VAULT: Decade-long Mid-Infrared Variability of WISE AGN`
  - **Question:** Which infrared-selected nuclei vary over a decade, and which warrant transient follow-up?
  - **Data:**
    - WISE color-selected parent catalog of 4,543,530 candidates; AllWISE plus NEOWISE-R 2023-release measurements through December 2022 in the W1 and W2 bands.
  - **Method:**
    - Quality-filter and average measurements into six-month epochs; combine a constant-flux test with positive excess variance and ensemble structure functions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The selection yields 632,939 variable candidates; 54,897 have peak-to-trough W1 changes above `0.5 mag`, while 624 exceed `0.5 mag` in noise-corrected intrinsic amplitude. These are distinct statistics. Constant-source simulations pass the variability cuts in `7.5%` of trials, a null-test rate rather than the catalog contamination fraction.
  - **Position:** This expands infrared variability searches using an existing color-selected parent sample. Host dilution and blending are uncorrected, most candidates lack spectroscopy, and infrared flares alone cannot establish changing-look nuclei or tidal disruptions.
