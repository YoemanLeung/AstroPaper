## Key Findings

- Dense peaks drive the X-ray AGN excess in Hyperion; neither the whole-structure average nor the peak comparison establishes a causal quenching mechanism ([2611.07047](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07047) Ashlen Coburn).
- Early disks show offsets from local mass-velocity relations, while the baryonic offset from cosmic noon is less decisive ([2611.07064](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07064) Diego A. Gomez-Espinoza).
- Dust survival is sensitive to both history and modeling: observed reservoirs do not uniquely identify quenching physics, and simulated destruction remains resolution-dependent ([2611.07237](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07237) Sarah Bodansky; [2611.07190](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07190) E. Huscher).
- Seed formation remains a conditional inference: stellar collapse models require specific initial conditions, and black-hole demographics need consistent selection and accretion diagnostics ([2611.07546](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07546) Dominik R.G. Schleicher; [2611.07006](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07006) Fabio Pacucci).

## AGN activity and environment

- [2611.07047](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07047) Ashlen Coburn et al. `The HST-Hyperion Survey: X-ray AGN Enhancement in the Hyperion Proto-supercluster`
  - **Question:** Where within Hyperion is nuclear activity enhanced?
  - **Data:**
    - Chandra COSMOS-Legacy, COSMOS2020 and spectroscopy/grism redshifts at `z~2.5`; galaxies above `log(M*/Msun)=10`.
  - **Method:**
    - Assign environments from Voronoi density maps; propagate redshift and mass selection through 100 Monte Carlo realizations, combining sampling and binomial errors.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - AGN fractions are `7.4(+3.2/-1.8)%` across Hyperion and `3.2(+0.6/-0.5)%` in the field. Peaks reach `12.3(+5.5/-3.4)%`; outskirts are consistent with the field. Small counts limit peak-mass trends; X-ray selection misses obscured nuclei, and enhancement does not prove feedback causes quenching.
  - **Position:** Extends protostructure AGN comparisons to resolved environmental regions within one structure.

- [2611.07028](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07028) Quinn O. Casey et al. `AGN Archaeology: A Systematic Search for Fading AGN in MaNGA`
  - **Question:** Can spatially resolved line ratios reveal nuclei that have recently faded?
  - **Data:**
    - MaNGA DR17 integral-field spectra; 34 selected fading-AGN candidates.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare inner and outer empirical AGN line fractions; reject old-star ionization with equivalent widths, test shocks, and model ionization-cone orientation.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The mean projected light-travel time is `1.1e4 yr`; the census implies an AGN-phase lower limit of `1.3e5 yr` under its counting assumptions. Nuclear dilution can mimic fading in nearly edge-on systems, while face-on echoes are missed. These are conditional lifetime constraints, not measured light curves.
  - **Position:** Expands ionization-echo samples using spatial diagnostics beyond single-fiber classifications.


## Galaxy dynamics and dust after quenching

- [2611.07064](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07064) Diego A. Gomez-Espinoza et al. `The ALPINE-CRISTAL-JWST Survey: The Tully-Fisher Relation at z = 4-6`
  - **Question:** How does the Tully-Fisher relation evolve at early times?
  - **Data:**
    - ALPINE-CRISTAL-JWST: NIRSpec IFU observations of 18 galaxies at `z=4-6`, NIRCam morphology and ALMA [C II] information.
  - **Method:**
    - Apply morpho-kinematic disk criteria; correct rotation for inclination and pressure support; infer gas masses from [C II] and fit fixed local slopes.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Five systems qualify as disks. Stellar and baryonic zero-points lie `0.96+/-0.18` and `0.57+/-0.20 dex` below local relations. The baryonic difference from cosmic noon is only `0.30+/-0.20 dex`. Small samples, pressure-support assumptions and gas conversion scatter limit a universal evolutionary interpretation.
  - **Position:** Extends resolved mass-velocity comparisons beyond cosmic noon; a borderline disk may be an unresolved interaction.

- [2611.07237](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07237) Sarah Bodansky et al. `Dust Survival in Lensed Quiescent Galaxies at z>1 Depends on How They Quench`
  - **Question:** Does dust survival depend on the route to quenching?
  - **Data:**
    - ALMA Band 6 continuum for eight lensed, spectroscopically selected galaxies at `z~1-2.5`, combined with five archival REQUIEM measurements.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit stellar histories, derive quenching times, and compare modified-blackbody dust masses with destruction and regrowth models.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Dust-to-stellar mass ratios span more than two orders of magnitude. Rapidly quenched systems favor stronger destruction in the model comparison; two dust-rich systems permit regrowth or continued production. Dust-temperature assumptions contribute `~0.3 dex` uncertainty, differential lensing may matter, and single-band continuum cannot exclude obscured star formation.
  - **Position:** Connects individual dust measurements to spectroscopic histories rather than treating all quiescent galaxies as gas-free.

- [2611.07190](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07190) E. Huscher et al. `CosmicGrain: An On-the-fly Dust Physics Module`
  - **Question:** Which dust processes control retention and survival in a simulated galaxy?
  - **Data:**
    - Gadget-4 cosmological zooms of one Milky Way-like halo, with a sequence adding individual dust processes and resolution comparisons.
  - **Method:**
    - Track dust superparticles with production, growth, drag, destruction and radiation pressure; compare a cumulative process sequence.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Drag raises interstellar dust-to-gas ratio by more than two orders of magnitude relative to the preceding setup. Subgrid density boosts strongly affect growth. Shock destruction is numerically under-resolved, and total dust mass remains resolution-sensitive; the sequence is a diagnostic experiment, not calibrated population prediction.
  - **Position:** Adds explicit particle histories to dust modeling, with useful limitations for interpreting galaxy and circumgalactic dust.


## Black hole seeds and future spectroscopy

- [2611.07546](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07546) Dominik R.G. Schleicher et al. `Violent relaxation in hierarchical stellar systems: pathways toward massive black hole formation in the early Universe`
  - **Question:** Can hierarchical stellar dynamics rapidly assemble a compact black-hole precursor?
  - **Data:**
    - Analytic stellar-cluster models motivated by compact high-redshift systems; no new observational sample or direct seed census.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare cold collapse and cluster mergers, with complete versus incomplete mixing of massive and lighter stellar components.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Incomplete mixing can preserve an initially colder, concentrated massive component and permit central decoupling. Repeated mergers often expand the system instead. Collisionless evolution requires an initial phase-space difference; gas is omitted, and runaway collisions and seed production are subsequent possibilities rather than demonstrated outcomes.
  - **Position:** Clarifies when stellar dynamical assembly can evade a long two-body relaxation time.

- [2611.07006](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07006) Fabio Pacucci et al. `Black Hole Seeds and Little Red Dots: Connecting Formation and Growth with a JWST Multi-Cycle Treasury Program`
  - **Question:** Which observations can separate black-hole seeding from later accretion?
  - **Data:**
    - Literature synthesis of JWST broad-line sources and little red dots (LRDs), compared with seeding and growth models.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine uniformly selected low-mass host samples, grating spectra, deep MIRI imaging and repeated observations of the same fields.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Low-mass demographics and accretion distributions offer complementary tests. Broad lines alone need not provide reliable virial masses, and an overmassive black hole does not uniquely imply a heavy seed. The proposed coordinated observations are future tests, not a newly completed census.
  - **Position:** An observing-strategy synthesis linking LRD identity, selection and black-hole growth histories.

- [2611.07070](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07070) Adam C. Carnall et al. `On the importance of medium-resolution continuum spectroscopy for JWST's extragalactic legacy`
  - **Question:** What depth and resolution are needed for representative stellar and neutral-gas diagnostics?
  - **Data:**
    - Existing EXCELS NIRSpec spectra and an illustrative JWST survey design.
  - **Method:**
    - Calibrate continuum signal-to-noise against magnitude and exposure; estimate mass completeness and multiplexed survey yields.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The empirical relation suggests about `20 hr` per medium-resolution grating for optical magnitude `24.5` and `S/N~20` per pixel at `R~1000`. The roughly `1000 hr` survey is illustrative. Master-background subtraction could improve efficiency but introduces systematic errors requiring tests at comparable depth.
  - **Position:** Connects absorption-line stellar histories and neutral-gas science to quantitative survey design.
