## Key Findings

- The more massive central galaxy, CRISTAL-21, contains a larger share of its local stellar mass. Around CRISTAL-19, most of that mass is still distributed among companions. The contrast traces where stars have accumulated within each overdensity. [2611.33624](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33624) Shaoze Geng.
- Silicon and carbon relative to oxygen leave the stellar origin of some metals ambiguous during reionization. Requiring the same simulated gas to match iron abundances as well makes the first-star interpretation more discriminating. [2611.34789](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34789) Giulia Pruto.
- Continuum fits permit small black hole masses if the sources radiate at three times their Eddington luminosity. The scarcity of detectable ionized winds in a separate sample supplies a kinematic test for models that predict strong outflows at those accretion rates. [2611.33798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33798) Haitong Liu and [2611.34743](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34743) Mainak Singha.
- At cosmic noon the largest infrared extrapolation errors occur on dusty sightlines. The discrepancy is directly measurable in the mid-infrared even though the total power reradiated by dust still depends on a model. [2611.34802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34802) Benjamin P. Collins.

## Where early galaxies assemble

- [2611.33624](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33624) Shaoze Geng et al. `JWST Reveals Contrast Overdensities around Two ALMA-CRISTAL Galaxies at z ~ 5.2`
  - **Question:** Where has stellar mass accumulated around two galaxies in the same large-scale structure?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST slitless spectra identify H-alpha emitters around two ALMA targets. Imaging measures the light of the targets and their companions.
  - **Method:**
    - Link emitters by projected separation and line-of-sight velocity. Separately compare stellar masses in the target-centered companion samples using a common catalog.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The two linked groups have `8` and `11` members and lie about `25 comoving Mpc` apart in redshift space. In the companion samples, the targets hold `4.5%` and `39%` of the measured stellar mass, respectively.
    - Descendant masses depend on which members eventually collapse together. Both groups could contribute to the same cluster.
  - **Position:** The companion mass budget distinguishes two stages of assembly that a comparison of the target galaxies alone would miss.

- [2611.34789](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34789) Giulia Pruto et al. `The THESAN-ZOOM project: interpreting candidate Population III-enriched absorption line systems during reionization`
  - **Question:** Which element ratios distinguish enrichment by metal-free first stars in quasar absorption systems?
  - **Data:**
    - THESAN-ZOOM follows metal enrichment in 14 high-resolution regions. Observed absorbers selected through neutral oxygen provide comparison abundances.
  - **Method:**
    - Select largely neutral simulated gas and compare its gas-phase silicon, carbon, oxygen and iron ratios with the measurements. Vary stellar yields and rerun one region with first-star enrichment.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At `z = 6`, some gas in the baseline model matches the candidates in silicon/oxygen and carbon/oxygen simultaneously. Adding the iron ratios exposes discrepancies for the same gas.
    - The run including first stars improves agreement, but it also changes the yields of later stellar populations and samples only one region.
  - **Position:** Measurements of iron alongside carbon, oxygen and silicon can test the origin of the metals in an individual absorber.

## Accretion and outflows in compact sources

- [2611.34743](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34743) Mainak Singha et al. `Black holes in little red and blue dots drive no superwinds in the first 1.5 billion years`
  - **Question:** How often do compact broad-line sources show the ionized winds seen around nearby active nuclei?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRSpec grating spectra provide usable doubly ionized oxygen ([O III]) lines for 67 little red and blue dots. Nearby type 1 active nuclei supply reference wind profiles.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit the oxygen doublet with and without a second component. Inject local wind profiles into the data to measure detection sensitivity.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The primary search yields `0/67` wind candidates. For the local sub-Eddington wind distribution restricted to widths below `1,500 km/s`, the incidence is below `13%` at 95% confidence.
    - Two higher-resolution refits show small blueshifts below the inferred host dispersions. The incidence bound applies to the specified ionized-gas profiles.
  - **Position:** Line kinematics constrain how strongly accretion couples to gas on host-galaxy scales.

- [2611.33798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33798) Haitong Liu et al. `Less massive black holes in faint hosts: SED fitting of Little Red Dots within the Black Hole Star scenario`
  - **Question:** What black hole and stellar masses follow if a gaseous envelope produces the red optical continuum?
  - **Data:**
    - Low-resolution JWST/NIRSpec spectra of 22 little red dots span roughly `z = 4-9.5`.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit the continuum with starlight and an accreting black hole enveloped by gas. Convert the inferred nuclear luminosity to mass assuming three times the Eddington luminosity.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Within this model the median black hole mass is `10^6.4 solar masses` and the median host stellar mass is `10^8.7 solar masses`.
    - The adopted Eddington ratio sets the black hole mass scale. Unmodeled nuclear ultraviolet light can bias the inferred host mass upward.
  - **Position:** The continuum fit quantifies how a choice of accretion model propagates into the inferred black hole to stellar mass ratio.

## Radio emission from compact galaxies and quasars

- [2611.34144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34144) Ailing Wang et al. `Radio Emission From Low-redshift Little Red Dots`
  - **Question:** How widely do the radio properties of nearby little red dots vary?
  - **Data:**
    - Archival radio maps cover published optical positions for 44 sources at `z = 0.101-0.882`, with observations spanning 144 MHz to 6 GHz.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure radio flux at the optical positions, validate associations and compare low-frequency detections with upper limits.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Eight targets have radio detections in at least one band. The five detections at `144 MHz` span a factor of `20` in luminosity.
    - Sensitivity varies with survey and redshift. Transferring these spectra to distant sources requires assumptions about spectral evolution and which sources enter the sample.
  - **Position:** Nearby analogues supply measured radio spectra for designing high-redshift stacking observations.

- [2611.34149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34149) A. Caccianiga et al. `Evolutionary Map of the Universe (EMU): the fraction of radio-detected quasi-stellar objects at high redshift`
  - **Question:** How do the radio detection rate and the radio-loud fraction compare at high redshift?
  - **Data:**
    - The Evolutionary Map of the Universe covers 808 known type 1 quasars at redshifts four to nine at an observing frequency of 943 MHz.
  - **Method:**
    - Match optical positions to radio sources. Bin quasars by optical magnitude and compare detection fractions with the fraction above a radio-to-optical luminosity ratio of ten.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The survey detects `124/808` quasars. The radio-loud fraction is `8 +/- 1%` over redshifts four through six, while the radio detection fraction reaches about `35%` for the optically brightest bin.
    - Jet orientation can explain some radio detections below the loudness threshold. The detection fraction alone cannot measure the incidence of jets.
  - **Position:** The optical magnitude distribution matters when comparing radio surveys of distant quasars.

## Dust emission at cosmic noon

- [2611.34802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34802) Benjamin P. Collins et al. `Tipping the scales in Blue Jay: Dust energy balance overpredicts IR luminosity in galaxies at z ~ 2`
  - **Question:** How accurately does dust attenuation inferred from starlight predict mid-infrared emission?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/MIRI photometry covers 111 Blue Jay galaxies at cosmic noon. The energy-balance test uses 89 star-forming galaxies.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit Hubble and JWST/NIRCam photometry and predict MIRI fluxes from the absorbed starlight. Repeat with NIRSpec spectra or MIRI photometry as additional constraints.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The overprediction grows from about `10%` at `7.7 microns` to a factor of `3` at `21 microns`. Including MIRI lowers the inferred total infrared luminosity by up to `0.6 dex` in dusty systems.
    - MIRI misses the far-infrared dust peak. Total infrared luminosity remains a model estimate, and individual dust grain parameters are weakly constrained.
  - **Position:** A comparison to measured mid-infrared flux tests the assumption that attenuation along our sightline represents absorption across the galaxy.
