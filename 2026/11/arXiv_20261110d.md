## Key Findings

- Faint protocluster radio emission does not yet establish an environmental AGN enhancement: the stacks remain compatible with star formation. ([2611.11107](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11107) Yuheng Zhang).
- Cool-gas absorption and radio-burst dispersion probe different circumgalactic components. Their present constraints do not uniquely determine a common gas reservoir or feedback mechanism. ([2611.12734](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12734) Zeyu Chen; [2611.12860](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12860) Affan Khadir).
- Cosmic-noon emission-line counts improve with multiple sightlines, but inferred star-formation densities retain dust, line-blending and integration-limit dependence. ([2611.12284](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12284) Qianqiao Zhou).
- Environmental signatures depend on the observable: stellar disk relations can remain similar while gas-related offsets and cluster velocity decompositions change. ([2611.10910](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10910) C. Bacchini; [2611.12845](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12845) Congyao Zhang).

## Protocluster activity and circumgalactic gas

- [2611.11107](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11107) Yuheng Zhang et al. `Probing faint radio activity from cores to filaments in two z=2.24 protoclusters with uGMRT`
  - **Question:** Does faint radio activity vary between protocluster cores and filaments?
  - **Data:**
    - uGMRT 1.26 GHz maps of BOSS1244 and BOSS1542 at `z=2.24`; 187 and 105 Halpha-selected members fall inside the usable radio areas.
  - **Method:**
    - Cross-match radio sources, compare radio luminosity with dust-corrected Halpha star formation rates, and median-stack undetected members and projected environmental bins.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - After removing robust and potential counterparts, 173 and 102 objects yield `2.8 sigma` and `3.2 sigma` stacks consistent with star formation. Two Halpha emitters and the quiescent galaxy QG1 show strong radio excess. Environmental bins exclude robust counterparts only: their higher central/near-filament signals are not statistically significant and do not establish enhanced AGN activity.
  - **Position:** Extends individual protocluster radio detections to the faint population while retaining the star-formation/AGN ambiguity.

- [2611.12734](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12734) Zeyu Chen et al. `The Cool Circumgalactic Echo of the Stellar-to-Halo Mass Relatio`
  - **Question:** How do cool circumgalactic gas structure and kinematics vary with stellar mass?
  - **Data:**
    - DESI Year 1 foreground galaxies and background quasars; millions of usable pairs, with Mg II coverage requiring `z_gal>0.3` and the principal analysis using `20<D<180 kpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Normalize quasar spectra, median-stack in galaxy rest frames, fit absorption and subtract instrumental/redshift broadening; test radius, redshift and star-formation dependence.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Absorption peaks near `M*=3e10 Msun`; radial profiles flatten with increasing stellar mass. The corrected ensemble line width crosses the adopted virial reference near `log(M*/Msun)=10.5`. A simple stellar-to-halo mass scaling underpredicts massive red galaxies by about `0.24 dex`. Equivalent width is not a gas-mass measurement, and the virial comparison depends on an assumed halo-mass relation.
  - **Position:** Connects statistical cool-gas absorption with galaxy-formation efficiency; it does not uniquely identify feedback or inflow.

- [2611.12860](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12860) Affan Khadir et al. `Search for a Dispersion Measure Excess Toward Nearby Galaxy Halos Using Fast Radio Bursts from the Second CHIME/FRB Baseband Catalog`
  - **Question:** Can fast radio bursts map ionized gas in nearby galaxy halos?
  - **Data:**
    - Second CHIME/FRB Baseband Catalog: 1,748 unique sources before exclusions; 66 halo-piercing sightlines for galaxies within 50 Mpc and 628 within two halo radii for the 150 Mpc sample.
  - **Method:**
    - Subtract a Milky Way dispersion model, compare uniformly weighted piercing/control means using permutations, and fit radial electron-density models outside the innermost contaminated bin.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The nearby excess is `119+/-44 pc cm^-3` (`p=0.0053`); the larger-sample radial decline has `p=0.0046`. Both are about `2.6 sigma`, below a secure detection. The fitted density slope is `beta=0.68(+0.15,-0.16)` under the adopted beta model. Small-separation deficits are consistent with some bursts residing in the matched galaxies; unknown burst redshifts and multiple foreground halos limit inference.
  - **Position:** Provides a complementary electron-column probe to absorption and X-ray stacking, with tentative current significance.


## Emission-line populations and star formation

- [2611.12284](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12284) Qianqiao Zhou et al. `A Comprehensive Analysis of the Halpha Luminosity Functions at z~1.3 and z~2.0 in the CANUCS Lensing Fields with JWST NIRISS`
  - **Question:** What is the faint-end shape of the Halpha luminosity function at cosmic noon?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRISS CANUCS spectroscopy in five cluster and five parallel fields: 433 emitters at `1.1<z<1.5` and 329 at `1.8<z<2.2`.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify lines and inspect spectra; calculate lensing-dependent volumes and source-specific recovery/flux biases, then fit Schechter functions including cosmic variance and external characteristic-luminosity priors.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The measured luminosity functions use blended Halpha+[N II]. Faint-end slopes are `-1.51(+0.06,-0.04)` and `-1.42(+0.09,-0.06)`; the difference is modest. With `A_Halpha=1 mag`, [N II] correction and integration above `log L=40.5`, star-formation densities are `0.103(+0.008,-0.007)` and `0.149(+0.016,-0.013) Msun yr^-1 Mpc^-3`. Bright-end constraints are prior dominated; changing dust or integration limits changes these densities.
  - **Position:** A multi-sightline benchmark for slitless line surveys, with explicit separation of measured counts and model-dependent star-formation conversion.

- [2611.12147](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12147) J.-H. Kim et al. `Intermediate-redshift ultra-luminous infrared galaxies as transitioning objects between main sequence and starburst galaxies`
  - **Question:** Are intermediate-redshift ultraluminous infrared galaxies between normal and starburst modes?
  - **Data:**
    - ALMA CO(2-1) observations of 22 Herschel-selected galaxies at `0.46<z<0.81`; 14 detections, with infrared photometry and Suprime-Cam optical morphology.
  - **Method:**
    - Infer molecular masses using a sample-wide metallicity-based CO conversion, compare efficiency and main-sequence offsets, and inspect disturbed morphologies.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Detected galaxies occupy efficiencies between comparison normal and starburst samples, with depletion times of order hundreds of Myr. Roughly half show optical disturbances, consistent with a possible merger contribution. Infrared selection and CO sensitivity restrict the efficiency distribution; uncertain metallicity/conversion factors and incomplete kinematics prevent identification of minor mergers as the unique cause.
  - **Position:** Bridges local infrared populations and higher-redshift star-forming galaxies using molecular-gas measurements.


## Environmental memory and cluster feedback

- [2611.10910](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10910) C. Bacchini et al. `Impact of the large scale environment on the baryonic scaling relations of disc galaxies: Insights from the GASP survey`
  - **Question:** Does ram-pressure stripping alter disk mass, rotation and angular-momentum relations?
  - **Data:**
    - GASP MUSE disks near `z~0.05`: 81 objects for rotation-mass relations, with 73 stellar, 49 gas and 71 baryonic angular-momentum measurements.
  - **Method:**
    - Model stellar/ionized-gas kinematics, correct pressure support, infer gas from the inverse star-formation law, and compare cluster, field and stripping subsamples.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Stellar and baryonic relations show no compelling environmental displacement beyond their scatter. The gas specific-angular-momentum relation is lower by `0.16+/-0.06 dex` in cluster versus field and stripped versus control comparisons. Enhanced star formation and truncated Halpha disks contribute; the offset is not a direct measurement of lost cold-gas angular momentum. The targeted disk sample and limited radial coverage bound the result.
  - **Position:** Tests environmental perturbations of disk scaling relations without requiring all components to respond identically.

- [2611.12470](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12470) Shuo Xu et al. `The Connection between Halo Assembly History and the Stellar Distribution II: A Radially Stratified Fossil Record of Past Halo Mass`
  - **Question:** Which epochs of halo assembly are encoded at different stellar radii?
  - **Data:**
    - IllustrisTNG300-1 at `z=0.4`: 3,388 halos above `1e13 Msun`; the main correlation analysis uses 2,035 above `10^13.2 Msun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure projected stellar aperture/annular masses, trace progenitors, calculate correlations and top-ranked halo retention, and compare three-dimensional and in-situ/accreted components.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Peak correlations with past halo mass shift from `z~1.0` inside 10 kpc to `z~0.7` at 100-150 kpc. Accreted stars carry most radial stratification; inner in-situ stars retain early assembly information. Annular masses beyond 10 kpc select massive progenitors better than current satellite richness at intermediate redshift. These are population correlations in one simulation, not individual formation-time measurements.
  - **Position:** Gives a simulation-based interpretation of deep stellar-halo profiles as assembly tracers.

- [2611.12845](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12845) Congyao Zhang et al. `AGN Feedback Turbulence in Cool-core Clusters: Evidence from XRISM`
  - **Question:** Do AGN and mergers drive distinct turbulence components across cool-core clusters?
  - **Data:**
    - XRISM velocity measurements of A1795 and A2029, compared with Perseus and X-ray/radio morphology; tailored idealized merger calculations.
  - **Method:**
    - Account for X-ray-weighted line-of-sight scales, combine merger motions with a phenomenological feedback velocity field, and estimate turbulent heating under a cascade model.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Feedback dominates the modeled A1795 core within about `100 kpc`; inferred heating can be comparable to radiative cooling. In A2029, mergers dominate and the feedback contribution is only bounded above. A matching merger snapshot is about `1.6 Gyr` after pericenter. Geometry, unresolved injection scales and turbulent filling factors limit the decomposition and heating estimate; an evolutionary sequence remains an interpretation.
  - **Position:** Extends the two-driver interpretation of Perseus to clusters with different dynamical states.
