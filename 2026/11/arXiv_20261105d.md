## Key Findings

- Extremely weak oxygen emission is not a unique Population III signature: abundance calibration and ionization structure remain decisive in [2611.05487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05487) M. Messa. Simulations likewise distinguish early total-metal enrichment from delayed iron production ([2611.05589](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05589) Pratik J. Gandhi).
- Survey depth helps, but it does not erase line-selection bias. The two Euclid forecasts share a mock framework and should be read together, not as independent performance confirmations ([2611.05482](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05482) C. Mancini; [2611.05484](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05484) P. Cassata).
- Candidate identification and galaxy evolution require different evidence: LRD internal validation is not population completeness ([2611.06026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06026) Chenxuan Zhang), and extended quenched AGN hosts do not prove feedback causation ([2611.06443](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06443) Xiao Han).
- Radiation-background uncertainty is concentrated near diffuse-gas thermal equilibrium; the reported sign-change fraction describes sampled parameter space, not cosmic gas mass ([2611.06360](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06360) Laura Di Federico).

## Nearly pristine gas and early enrichment

- [2611.05487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05487) M. Messa et al. `A pocket of oxygen in a near-pristine galaxy at z=4.2: chemical inhomogeneity at 10-parsec scales in LAP2`
  - **Question:** How chemically uniform is the extremely faint lensed galaxy LAP2?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRSpec IFU PRISM and NIRCam imaging of LAP2 at `z=4.199+/-0.001` behind Abell 2744; nominal combined exposure `13.62 hr`, with reduced effective depth near a pointing edge.
  - **Method:**
    - Extract integrated and core spectra, estimate noise from spectral scatter, and combine lens-corrected morphology with extrapolated strong-line oxygen calibration.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Integrated [O III]/H-beta is below `0.61` at `3 sigma`, implying `12+log(O/H)<=6.4` under the adopted calibration; the core gives `6.6+/-0.1`. A higher core ionization parameter can mimic enrichment. The calibration is unconstrained by direct-temperature measurements below abundance `~7`, so these are conditional abundances. He II non-detection does not establish Population III stars.
  - **Position:** Extends the earlier slit-spectrum study of LAP2 by Vanzella+2026 with spatial information; unresolved abundance calibration remains the decisive limitation.

- [2611.05589](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05589) Pratik J. Gandhi et al. `The impact of Population III stars on the elemental enrichment of ultra-faint galaxies in the FIRE-3 zoom-in simulations`
  - **Question:** Does adding Population III feedback resolve the ultra-faint mass-metallicity discrepancy?
  - **Data:**
    - FIRE-3 cosmological zoom simulations of isolated ultra-faints and an LMC-mass environment; finest baryonic resolutions `~30` and `~110 Msun`, respectively.
  - **Method:**
    - Vary the low-metallicity, top-heavy stellar-population prescription and white-dwarf supernova enrichment; compare star-formation histories and multiple elemental abundances.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Raising the Population III threshold increases the metallicity floor but does not consistently recover the observed iron-abundance plateau near `[Fe/H]~-2.5`. Extended subsequent star formation permits delayed white-dwarf supernovae to raise iron abundances. Total metal enrichment and iron enrichment must be distinguished.
  - **Position:** Builds on FIRE ultra-faint calculations and places early massive-star feedback alongside delayed enrichment as separate tests of the Local Group abundance data.


## Emission-line surveys and selection effects

- [2611.05482](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05482) C. Mancini et al. `Euclid. Predicting scaling relations for emission-line galaxies using simulated data`
  - **Question:** Which galaxy scaling relations can Euclid spectroscopy recover without strong selection bias?
  - **Data:**
    - MAMBO mock star-forming galaxies over `0<z<4`, passed through FastSpec and the official Euclid spectral pipeline for Wide and Deep survey depths.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare recovered star-formation, dust and metallicity relations to both the full magnitude-limited parent and the line-selected truth; apply redshift reliability and line-quality cuts.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At `log(M*/Msun)>=10.3`, deep H-alpha selection nearly recovers the input main sequence at `0.42<z<0.84`, while retaining `~0.2 dex` bias at `0.84<z<1.88`. The multi-line sample at `1.49<z<1.88` favors high star formation and low metallicity at fixed mass. Typical metallicity uncertainty is `~0.1 dex`, with low-metallicity biases up to `0.15 dex`.
  - **Position:** Euclid Collaboration forecast grounded in imposed empirical scaling relations; recovery of the mock does not validate those relations in nature.

- [2611.05484](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05484) P. Cassata et al. `Euclid: Forecasting the number counts of bright line emitters in the Euclid Wide Survey and Euclid Deep fields`
  - **Question:** How many emission-line galaxies might Euclid recover, and how model-dependent are those forecasts?
  - **Data:**
    - MAMBO, SPRITZ, GAEA and Flagship mock catalogues, with MAMBO alone propagated through FastSpec and the official spectroscopic recovery pipeline.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare intrinsic line counts across mocks; simulate instrumental effects, fit lines and redshifts, and separate recovered candidates from those with correct redshifts.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The MAMBO-based calculation predicts roughly `42 million` recovered H-alpha emitters in the Wide survey and `1.7 million` in the Deep survey. Wide H-alpha contamination is `~14%`; these totals are not counts of independently confirmed redshifts. Oxygen-line predictions differ substantially among mocks, and slitless spectral overlaps are omitted.
  - **Position:** Companion to the Euclid scaling-relation forecast; the shared MAMBO/FastSpec basis means the two papers are not independent validations.


## AGN candidates and host evolution

- [2611.06026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06026) Chenxuan Zhang et al. `Spectroscopy-informed Deep Learning Searches for Photometric Little Red Dots in JWST Surveys`
  - **Question:** Can spectroscopy improve searches for little red dots using imaging alone?
  - **Data:**
    - Paired JWST/NIRCam images and NIRSpec PRISM spectra; training/validation split includes `102/25` labeled little red dots (LRDs), with galaxies and brown dwarfs as negatives.
  - **Method:**
    - Align image and spectral representations through contrastive learning, freeze encoders, train an image classifier, and inspect available grating H-alpha spectra of selected candidates.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At classifier score `>=0.8`, internal validation recovers `23/25` positives with `6` nominal false positives: recall `0.92`, precision `0.79`. Among `13` candidates with usable grating coverage, `10` favor broad H-alpha. The DJA imaging search yields `357` candidates. These scores are not calibrated membership probabilities or an independent completeness measurement.
  - **Position:** Extends cross-modal representation learning to rare JWST populations while retaining spectroscopy as the test of candidate identity.

- [2611.06443](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06443) Xiao Han et al. `AGN Host Galaxies in COSMOS-Web: Linking Structural Evolution and Star Formation Quenching up to z=5`
  - **Question:** Must star-formation suppression coincide with compact structure in AGN hosts?
  - **Data:**
    - COSMOS-Web imaging and multiwavelength photometry for `3955` active galactic nuclei (AGN) at `0<z<5`, selected through five overlapping diagnostics.
  - **Method:**
    - Decompose images into point source plus Sersic host, fit galaxy-plus-AGN spectral energy distributions, and compare optical size-mass and star-formation versus compactness distributions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - In the massive-host comparison at `2.2<z<3`, only `28.4%` of SFR-selected quiescent hosts meet the compactness criterion, versus `95.7%` at `0.5<z<1`. This supports diversity in the relative timing of structural change and star-formation suppression; population snapshots do not demonstrate an evolutionary track or AGN causation.
  - **Position:** Tests the compaction-quenching picture against resolved host light; selection differences and model-dependent masses/SFRs remain essential context.


## Gas thermodynamics and the cosmic web

- [2611.06360](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06360) Laura Di Federico et al. `Net Cooling Tables for Cosmological Simulations under Multiple UV/X-ray Background Models`
  - **Question:** How sensitive are simulation cooling tables to the adopted ultraviolet/X-ray background?
  - **Data:**
    - CLOUDY 23.01 calculations under HM12, KS19 and FG20 radiation backgrounds, with H/He plus element-by-element contributions for `13` heavy elements.
  - **Method:**
    - Calculate separate heating and cooling in optically thin ionization equilibrium; compare net rates, thermal boundaries and positive cooling times with the earlier Wiersma framework.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Near thermal equilibrium in diffuse gas, cooling times can be `~10-11` times shorter than the older tables by `z~4.6`. At `z~8.1`, net-rate signs differ over `8.8-9.9%` of the sampled diffuse density-temperature plane. This is a grid-area statistic, not a fraction of cosmic gas mass or volume.
  - **Position:** Provides reusable sensitivity tests for the Wiersma+2009 element-by-element approach; equilibrium tables cannot directly describe patchy reionization.

- [2611.06632](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06632) Elisa A. Tau et al. `Living near filaments: How environment shapes age profiles in low-mass galaxies`
  - **Question:** Which environments favor U-shaped radial stellar-age profiles in low-mass galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - `1301` TNG50 galaxies with `10^9<=M*/Msun<=10^10`, after removing potentially contaminating nearby companions.
  - **Method:**
    - Smooth spherical radial age profiles, require a `>=70%` U-shape score and `>=0.4 Gyr` contrast on each side, and compare DisPerSE filament and group distances.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - `145/1301` galaxies (`11.1%`) qualify; their median filament distance is `265 kpc`, versus `351 kpc` for other profiles. About `72%` are centrals. The trend persists across two mass bins, but gas supply and stripping are proposed mechanisms rather than demonstrated causes.
  - **Position:** Extends earlier isolated-dwarf age-profile work to large-scale environments; morphology and gas-reservoir tests remain future work.
