## Key Findings

- Galaxy density alone misses distinct gas environments around quenched galaxies; the absorption tracer cannot uniquely distinguish gas removal from ionization ([2611.00190](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00190) Amir H. Khoram).
- Redshift, compactness and red colors do not establish a black-hole mechanism: broad-line confirmation remains absent for the two little-red-dot systems ([2611.00195](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00195) Takumi S. Tanaka; [2611.00202](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00202) Jon Judez).
- Seed-growth histories remain conditional: the small MARTA host does not date its black-hole seed, and Aurora demonstrates a prescription-dependent environmental signal ([2611.00199](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00199) Mirko Curti; [2611.01315](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01315) Harry George Chittenden).
- Cosmic-dawn masses shift substantially when population assumptions change; the larger literature offsets include differences beyond the initial mass function ([2611.01595](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01595) Gibson B. Bowling).

## Galaxy environments and quenching

- [2611.00190](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00190) Amir H. Khoram et al. `Developing Environments: Formation and Quenching at Cosmic Noon Traced by the Large-scale Distribution of Gas and Galaxies`
  - **Question:** How does quenching depend on galaxy density and intergalactic gas?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST: 139 Blue Jay galaxies at `1.7<z<3.5`, plus 57 massive DAWN prism targets; COSMOS-Web counts and LATIS tomography overlap at `2.2<z<2.8`.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit spectra and photometry with nonparametric Prospector star-formation histories. Compare normalized 50%-to-90% mass-assembly intervals with galaxy density and normalized Ly-alpha absorption.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Massive quiescent galaxies have longer assembly intervals in denser galaxy environments: `rho=0.6`, permutation `p=0.01`.
    - At low galaxy density, seven of eight massive quiescent Blue Jay galaxies host AGN and five show neutral outflows; one higher-density AGN is an explicit exception. Weak absorption can mean ionized gas or a genuine gas deficit, not a direct total-gas measurement.
    - The gas comparison samples one Hyperion core; small AGN counts, line-of-sight smoothing and uncertain old/accreted stellar populations limit evolutionary interpretation.
  - **Position:** Connects stellar assembly to complementary galaxy and gas maps, extending earlier LATIS environmental comparisons.

- [2611.01315](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01315) Harry George Chittenden et al. `Introducing Aurora: A New Black Hole Seeding Model With Explicit Environmental Coupling`
  - **Question:** Can black-hole seeding retain an environmental imprint after subsequent growth?
  - **Data:**
    - Millennium halo merger trees evolved with the Mimic/Sage semi-analytic framework and the Aurora seeding module.
  - **Method:**
    - Assign effective seeds using halo mass, overdensity and radial skewness; calibrate against the stock model and scramble environments while retaining the seed spectrum.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At `z~3.1`, the calibrated seeded-galaxy quiescent fraction in halos above `10^12 M_sun/h` rises from `0.40` to `0.48`, approximately a `21%` relative increase.
    - The advertised `70%` increase belongs to a stronger-coupling trial, not the calibrated case. Scrambling removes the environmental gradient; seeds supply `29%` of all black-hole mass ever formed in the calibrated calculation.
    - These are model-dependent effective seeds at the resolution threshold, not direct measurements of primordial seed masses or observational confirmation of a formation channel.
  - **Position:** Provides a controlled extension of Sage for testing environmental seeding signatures.


## Compact red sources and early black holes

- [2611.00195](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00195) Takumi S. Tanaka et al. `EMBER: A Little Red Dot Candidate at z_spec=10.36 in the Seed Black Hole Era`
  - **Question:** Is the compact red source CW-LRD-z10 a little red dot at cosmic dawn?
  - **Data:**
    - EMBER JWST/NIRSpec PRISM spectrum, supported by COSMOS-Web NIRCam and MIRI photometry.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit a broken-power-law continuum, shared-redshift emission lines and alternative intergalactic/circumgalactic absorption models; test stacked weak lines.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The break and tentative lines give `z=10.361(+0.003/-0.002)`; the V-shaped continuum turns over at `0.356(+0.010/-0.013) microns`.
    - Broad Balmer lines are outside the current coverage. Blending uncertainties permit stellar ionization; neither the AGN interpretation nor the little-red-dot classification is definitive.
    - Ly-alpha changes from `S/N=0.5` to `4.2` when circumgalactic absorption is added, with only `Delta BIC=2.6` improvement. It does not establish an ionized bubble.
  - **Position:** Extends spectroscopic redshift confirmation of little-red-dot candidates beyond ten while separating redshift from physical classification.

- [2611.00202](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00202) Jon Judez et al. `A Supermagnified Little Red Dot with a Resolved Host`
  - **Question:** What does resolving a strongly lensed little red dot reveal about its host?
  - **Data:**
    - CANUCS NIRCam and NIRSpec PRISM observations of Best Friend at `z=8.297+/-0.002`; lens magnification `36(+8/-12)`.
  - **Method:**
    - Forward-model a diffuse host and three clumps through the lens and point-spread function; extract host and nucleus separately and fit their spectra and photometry.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The central half-light radius is `<6.8 pc` at `2 sigma`; host stellar mass is `log(M_star/M_sun)=7.3(+0.2/-0.8)`, including lensing and star-formation-history uncertainty.
    - The integrated optical continuum is blue despite a red central component, illustrating host-dilution selection bias. Broad lines are not detected; a dense stellar interpretation is disfavored but not excluded.
  - **Position:** Uses lensing to expose host-contamination effects in compact-source selection.

- [2611.00199](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00199) Mirko Curti et al. `MARTA: A "little" black hole hidden in a clumpy, metal-poor galaxy at z~3.8`
  - **Question:** Can a small metal-poor galaxy already host a comparatively massive black hole?
  - **Data:**
    - MARTA NIRSpec spectra plus HST/JWST imaging and MUSE Ly-alpha observations of MARTA 3887 at `z=3.7694`.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit narrow and broad line components with instrumental convolution and robustness tests; infer host populations with BAGPIPES and conditional virial black-hole masses.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Broad H-alpha has `FWHM=1270(+260/-250) km/s`; under a broad-line-region interpretation, `log(M_BH/M_sun)=6.52(+0.18/-0.23)` plus a separate `0.30 dex` calibration systematic.
    - The fiducial host has `log(M_star/M_sun)=8.16(+0.09/-0.10)`. Its `M_BH/M_star~2.3%` does not identify a seed channel; host age does not date black-hole formation.
    - Single-epoch data leave transient or dense-outflow alternatives. Ly-alpha and Mg II suggest porous gas, but there is no direct Lyman-continuum detection or measured intrinsic ionizing escape fraction.
  - **Position:** Probes the low-mass broad-line population with resolved host light and explicit seed-growth degeneracies.


## Stellar populations and large-scale structure

- [2611.01595](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01595) Gibson B. Bowling et al. `Physically Motivated Stellar Populations for Galaxies at Cosmic Dawn with a Temperature-Dependent IMF`
  - **Question:** How sensitive are cosmic-dawn stellar masses to a temperature-dependent initial mass function?
  - **Data:**
    - JADES DR5 photometry and DR4 secure redshifts for 58 galaxies at `z>8`, including five above twelve; separate CEERS candidate comparison.
  - **Method:**
    - Implement temperature-dependent populations in CIGALE; adopt `45 K` below redshift twelve and `60 K` above, young age grids and fixed metallicity, then compare conventional fitting configurations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Inferred masses are `0.32-0.55 dex` lower than conventional CIGALE fits. The nearly `2 dex` extremes concern a separate comparison with published candidate masses; its median decrease is `1.26 dex`.
    - Age, metallicity, attenuation and nebular assumptions also differ between configurations. The comparison demonstrates model sensitivity rather than isolating the IMF or proving a unique resolution of cosmological tension.
  - **Position:** Adds a public stellar-population library for testing high-redshift mass-to-light systematics.

- [2611.00132](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00132) J. van Marrewijk et al. `Galaxy cluster thermodynamics at z=1.7: Resolved tSZ and X-ray analysis of CL J0459.6-4946`
  - **Question:** Can combined millimeter and X-ray data resolve thermodynamics in a young cluster?
  - **Data:**
    - ALMA Bands 1/3 and ACT thermal Sunyaev-Zeldovich data for CL J0459 at `z~1.7`, with Chandra `122 ks` and XMM-Newton `699 ks` clean exposures.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit pressure profiles and X-ray density, then infer temperature and entropy through the ideal-gas relation; compare maps and hydrostatic masses with weak lensing.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The central entropy is approximately `200 keV cm^2`, with a hot core and declining temperature. Outer temperatures tending to zero are a profile-extrapolation artifact.
    - The hydrostatic bias is `0.27(+0.32/-0.67)`, so a nonzero bias is not established. The southern entropy dip is consistent with sloshing but remains speculative at its low significance.
    - Clumping notation differs between abstract and body: Section 5.1 defines the squared factor as the density-moment ratio. Avoid treating their numerical conventions as interchangeable.
  - **Position:** Extends joint pressure-density thermodynamics to a distant cluster while exposing geometry and profile uncertainties.

- [2611.00212](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00212) Stefania Barsanti et al. `The MAGPI Survey: the evolution of galaxy spin-filament alignments since z~0.3`
  - **Question:** Does the relation between galaxy spin and filament direction evolve?
  - **Data:**
    - MAGPI at `0.20<z<0.42`: 342 stellar and 523 gas kinematic measurements; comparison with low-redshift SAMI and GAMA/MAGPI/DESI filament tracers.
  - **Method:**
    - Reconstruct filaments with DisPerSe, infer spins using kinematic angles and disk inclinations, and fit the mass dependence with matched comparisons and bootstrap tests.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The stellar transition masses are `log(M_star/M_sun)=10.81+/-0.28` in MAGPI and `10.22+/-0.09` in SAMI: a difference near `0.6 dex`, approximately `2 sigma` with propagated errors.
    - A positive shift occurs in `99.5%` of valid bootstrap fits, conditional on discarding fits without a crossing. Weak alignment preferences and selected resolved samples limit mechanistic claims.
  - **Position:** Tests cosmic-web angular-momentum evolution through an observed stellar-mass comparison rather than directly measured inflow rates.
