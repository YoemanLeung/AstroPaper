## Key Findings

- Stronger optical lines toward reionization support increasingly variable star formation, but line strength alone cannot separate changing histories from gas conditions ([2611.08563](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08563) Isaac J. B. Holst).
- The QSO1 near-infrared turnover constrains continuum models without resolving dust-versus-gas processing; its stellar interpretation conflicts with the adopted dynamical mass limit ([2611.09397](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09397) E. Iani).
- Population III mass functions depend on when and how masses are counted. A long accumulated population and a still-accreting disk population are not interchangeable IMF measurements ([2611.08598](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08598) Yasmine J. Meziani; [2611.08603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08603) Vasilii Pustovoit).
- Environmental chemical offsets require a stated comparison variable: clusters have lower N/O at fixed O/H but higher N/O and O/H at fixed stellar mass ([2611.09330](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09330) Yllari K. Gonzalez-Koda).

## High-redshift galaxies and nebular diagnostics

- [2611.08563](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08563) Isaac J. B. Holst et al. `A combined PRIMER+MINERVA study of the evolution of [O III]+Hbeta equivalent width from z=3 to z=8.5`
  - **Question:** How do optical emission-line strengths change from cosmic noon toward reionization?
  - **Data:**
    - PRIMER+MINERVA NIRCam and HST imaging in UDS: 19 filters; 1,964 galaxies with `M_UV<=-19.25` in five bins across `z=3-8.5`.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine five redshift estimates; fit BPASS populations with non-parametric histories in Bagpipes and benchmark equivalent widths against 115 NIRSpec spectra.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Median rest-frame [O III]+Hbeta equivalent width rises from about `350` to `1100 Angstrom`. For the narrower `-21.6<M_UV<=-19.75` sample, the fraction above `1000 Angstrom` rises from `0.14+/-0.02` to `0.50+/-0.10`. Bursty histories are consistent with the distributions, but gas density can also suppress [O III], and stellar masses remain sensitive to outshining.
  - **Position:** Extends medium-band equivalent-width work across redshift with a common analysis.

- [2611.09397](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09397) E. Iani et al. `Where the red rise stops: JWST/MIRI reveals a near-infrared turnover in the Little Red Dot Abell2744-QSO1`
  - **Question:** What does the near-infrared turnover of Abell2744-QSO1 reveal about its red continuum?
  - **Data:**
    - Three lensed images at `z=7.04`: new MIRI F770W/F1000W, HST/NIRCam imaging, NIRSpec spectra and infrared/millimetre upper limits.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine decontaminated, magnification-corrected photometry; compare continuum models with dynamical and dust-energy constraints.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The `0.95-1.24 micron` continuum is nearly flat in flux density; a `4450+/-60 K` blackbody or a hotter reddened continuum fits. Favored stellar SED solutions exceed the inferred host-mass limit by roughly `140-200` times; a mass-limited cocoon model misses F1000W by `5 sigma`. Accretion is favored if the dynamical limit holds, but neither dust nor gas processing is uniquely established.
  - **Position:** Combines near-infrared and dynamical tests of little red dots (LRDs).

- [2611.08594](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08594) Owen Zafuta et al. `Constraining Dust Attenuation Curves in Star-Forming Galaxies at Cosmic Noon Using Multiple Emission Line Ratios`
  - **Question:** Can three hydrogen lines constrain a galaxy-specific attenuation curve?
  - **Data:**
    - JADES NIRSpec spectra from the DAWN archive; 386 accepted solutions from 848 attempted fits at `0.5<=z<=7`.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit a power-law attenuation curve to three hydrogen lines assuming Case B recombination; test unused transitions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Paschen lines provide more leverage than Balmer-only combinations. Median line-to-line star-formation-rate scatter is `0.07 dex`, versus `0.09` for SMC-like and `0.11` for Calzetti-like corrections. However, Section V also gives a larger factor-of-two discrepancy fraction for the fitted curves (`7.5%` versus `6.2%` and `5.5%`); improvement is not uniform. Selection, Case B assumptions and slope-normalization covariance prevent a universal dust-law inference.
  - **Position:** Tests three-line attenuation fitting and its high-redshift limitations.


## First-generation stars: distinguish population history from early collapse

- [2611.08598](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08598) Yasmine J. Meziani et al. `FORGE'd In The Early Universe II: Constraining The Pop III Initial Mass Function`
  - **Question:** How does prolonged formation across several minihalos change the inferred Population III mass distribution?
  - **Data:**
    - One FORGE'd in FIRE dwarf-progenitor zoom: 56 unique stars/protostars across five minihalos, followed for about `500 Myr` from `z~16` to `z~7`.
  - **Method:**
    - Evolve radiation-magnetohydrodynamics, chemistry and feedback; count zero-age main-sequence masses or maxima in the highest protostellar stage.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The accumulated distribution has `dN/dM proportional to M^(-1.9)` above `2.2 Msun`. Early snapshot slopes are about `1.4-1.5`, but snapshot and accumulated distributions are different statistics. The low-mass turnover is unresolved, close binaries are merged numerically, and the masses need not be final post-accretion values.
  - **Position:** Extends the evolutionary baseline without establishing a universal initial mass function (IMF).

- [2611.08603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08603) Vasilii Pustovoit et al. `FORGE'd in Fragments: Pop III star formation in a Milky Way-like progenitor halo`
  - **Question:** How does primordial disk fragmentation proceed in a massive early halo?
  - **Data:**
    - A Milky Way-progenitor atomic-cooling halo at `z~21`, followed for about `15 kyr` after the first protostar; finest gas mass resolution about `1e-3 Msun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Refine a cosmological gas cutout with radiation-magnetohydrodynamics and sink particles; measure disk stability, fragmentation, inflow and sink growth.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - About 60 sink-formation events occur in the first `8 kyr`; total protostellar mass exceeds `150 Msun`, with a high-mass slope `1.01+/-0.14`. All sinks remain pre-main-sequence and accreting. Missing chemical heating, weak jet coupling and unresolved progenitor minihalos bias fragment counts, masses and collapse history; this is not a final stellar IMF.
  - **Position:** Provides an early disk-scale view; differing environments, physics and mass definitions prevent direct IMF comparison.


## Environment and active-galaxy feedback

- [2611.09330](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09330) Yllari K. Gonzalez-Koda et al. `The N/O-O/H relation across large-scale environments`
  - **Question:** Does large-scale environment shift the relation between nitrogen-to-oxygen ratio and oxygen abundance?
  - **Data:**
    - Central 3-arcsec SDSS spectra at `0.025<=z<=0.050`, classified into clusters, voids and neither-cluster-nor-void environments within the CAVITY framework.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit spectra, select star-forming systems and compare strong-line abundance residuals; test calibrations and matched samples.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At `12+log(O/H)>8.63`, cluster galaxies lie up to about `0.025 dex` lower in N/O at fixed O/H. At fixed stellar mass, both N/O and O/H are higher in clusters. Void and intermediate-density galaxies show no significant difference. Central apertures and abundance calibrations limit interpretation; strangulation and enriched-gas recycling remain possible mechanisms, not identified causes.
  - **Position:** Connects environmental abundance shifts to stellar-mass scaling relations.

- [2611.08828](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08828) Alison L. Coil et al. `KCWI Observations of Type 2 Quasar-driven Ionized Outflows at z~0.1`
  - **Question:** How often do known quasar outflows contain distinct large-scale kinematic components?
  - **Data:**
    - Keck/KCWI integral-field spectra of seven known-outflow quasars at `z=0.04-0.12`, selected heterogeneously from earlier studies.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit multiple Gaussian components; map velocities and extents; derive Hbeta-based masses assuming an electron density.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Five of seven objects have multiple outflowing components (`71+/-17%`), and typical extents reach `4-10 kpc`. Faster components have comparable or smaller extents. Distinct episodes are one interpretation; opposing sides of one flow can also appear separately. Unknown density introduces at least order-of-magnitude uncertainty in mass and energy, and only the ionized phase is measured.
  - **Position:** Separates host and outflow emission; the incidence is not a population-wide quasar rate.

- [2611.08599](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08599) Antonio J. Porras-Valverde et al. `Magnetically Arrested Accretion and Black Hole Spin in a Semi-Analytic Model of Galaxy Formation`
  - **Question:** How does coupling black-hole spin to accretion alter galaxy quenching?
  - **Data:**
    - Mad Sage semi-analytic models on TNG merger trees, calibrated with TNG50-4 and rerun on TNG100-1 against the local stellar mass function.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare fixed-efficiency, coherent and mixed accretion using relativistic jet/spin prescriptions, with and without additional mechanical feedback.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Incoherent hot-halo accretion drives low spins and weak jet efficiency, overproducing galaxies above `M*~1e11 Msun`. A mechanical-efficiency floor of `0.02` improves the fit. This is a calibrated remedy within the model: variants are independently retuned, and assuming magnetically arrested accretion at all rates is uncertain.
  - **Position:** Tests accretion-state prescriptions for feedback; no universal efficiency is measured.
