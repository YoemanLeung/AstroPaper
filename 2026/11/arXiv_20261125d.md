## Quasars beyond redshift seven

- [2611.31385](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31385) J. Wolf et al. `Euclid: Discovery of a quasar at z = 8.13 hosting a 360-million-solar-mass black hole`
  - **Question:** How massive is the black hole in J1504+6252, and how rapidly is it accreting?
  - **Data:**
    - Euclid imaging identified the candidate. Follow-up included a JWST/NIRSpec spectrum covering both narrow and broad H-beta emission.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit narrow H-beta to obtain the systemic redshift. Combine broad-line width with continuum luminosity to estimate a single-epoch virial mass.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The narrow line gives `z = 8.129 +/- 0.005`. The benchmark broad H-beta calibration gives `log10(M/M_sun) = 8.56 +/- 0.43`.
    - Across the calibrations, the inferred luminosity is `12-44%` of the Eddington limit. This measures the current accretion state and leaves the earlier growth history open.
  - **Position:** The spectrum anchors early black hole growth models with a redshift and a mass estimate from different components of the same line.

- [2611.31352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31352) Mainak Singha et al. `Spectroscopic identification of z>7 quasars with Roman Space Telescope`
  - **Question:** How faint can a simulated quasar be before Roman grism spectroscopy misses its continuum break?
  - **Data:**
    - Synthetic quasar spectra span `z = 7.5-10` and three prescriptions for Lyman-alpha emission strength.
  - **Method:**
    - Simulate dispersed images with detector noise, extract one-dimensional spectra and test for a break at the input redshift. Compare spectral shapes with cool dwarfs and passive galaxies.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At `J = 24.5 AB`, break recovery ranges from `32% to 59%` across the redshifts and line-strength prescriptions tested.
    - The forecast omits overlapping traces and a blind redshift search. Classification against the limited template libraries leaves survey purity undetermined.
  - **Position:** Break recovery and rejection of contaminants are separate selection effects when forecasting faint quasar counts.

## Early star formation and its tracers

- [2611.31355](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31355) Mirko Curti et al. `JWST/NIRSpec observations of AMORE6 suggest active Pop III star formation in a self-polluted galaxy`
  - **Question:** What powers strong helium emission in a galaxy with detectable but weak metal lines?
  - **Data:**
    - Deep JWST/NIRSpec spectra of the lensed image AMORE6-B at `z = 5.725` cover helium, hydrogen, carbon and oxygen lines.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit the emission lines and compare their ratios and the spectral energy distribution against models of stellar populations and accreting compact objects.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The optical He II 4686/H-beta flux ratio is `0.080 +/- 0.015`. Weak [O III] 5007 is detected at `10 sigma` and C IV at `8 sigma`, establishing metals in the emitting gas.
    - The model comparisons favor ionization by metal-free stars in enriched gas. An obscured accreting black hole and other hard sources remain model-dependent alternatives.
  - **Position:** The metal lines constrain the gas composition directly. Inferring the composition of the stars requires modeling the ionizing spectrum.

- [2611.32342](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32342) Hiddo Algera et al. `The ALMA REBELS Survey: multi-band dust continuum observations reveal heavily obscured starbursts among UV-bright galaxies at z ~ 7`
  - **Question:** How much star formation can ultraviolet selection miss within galaxies already bright in the ultraviolet?
  - **Data:**
    - ALMA Bands 6 and 8 cover ten primary REBELS targets and three companions at `z = 6.5-7.7`. The stack uses the ten primary targets.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit the continuum measurements with modified blackbodies. Combine the infrared and ultraviolet star formation rates to estimate the obscured fraction.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The stack gives an obscured fraction of `0.75 +/- 0.11`. For a fixed dust emissivity index of `2`, the typical temperature is `49 K` with an interval of `41-60 K`.
    - The follow-up preferentially targets galaxies previously detected in dust emission. The average obscured fraction therefore applies to this selected population.
  - **Position:** For dust-detected galaxies near redshift seven, ultraviolet brightness alone can substantially underestimate ongoing star formation.

## Gas near galaxies and quasars

- [2611.31382](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31382) C. Yang et al. `Low-excitation molecular gas in a Lyman-alpha-associated companion of the z=4.7 quasar in BR1202-0725`
  - **Question:** Can a companion retain a cold molecular reservoir close to a luminous quasar?
  - **Data:**
    - ALMA measures CO(5-4) and neutral carbon in LAE-2 in the BR1202-0725 group at `z = 4.7`. Archival CO(2-1) provides the lower-excitation comparison.
  - **Method:**
    - Separate the sources in the interferometric data. Compare CO brightness-temperature luminosities and infer molecular masses independently from neutral and ionized carbon.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The CO(5-4) to CO(2-1) ratio is `0.22 +/- 0.04` in LAE-2 and `0.88 +/- 0.16` in the host. Neutral carbon implies roughly `90 billion solar masses` of molecular gas.
    - The gas mass depends on the carbon conversion factor, and the separation is projected. Local stars can power the inferred radiation field, although quasar heating remains possible.
  - **Position:** Adopting the excitation of the quasar host would underestimate the gas reservoir of its companion.

- [2611.31387](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31387) Omry Ginzburg et al. `The hot circumgalactic medium: implications of mixing and resolution at the disk-halo interface`
  - **Question:** Can unresolved mixing at the disk surface alter the inferred structure of a hot halo?
  - **Data:**
    - Idealized inflow calculations and FIRE-2 simulations of Milky Way analogues are compared with Galactic halo constraints, including X-ray emission across the eROSITA bubbles.
  - **Method:**
    - Vary the disk boundary condition and spatial resolution. Compare the predicted near-axis entropy profiles with the simulations and observational constraints.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - In the idealized model, inflow on the cooling timescale produces roughly `10 times` the density of the nearly constant-entropy solution, implying up to a `100-fold` difference in emission measure.
    - FIRE-2 favors nearly constant entropy near the axis, while the Galactic constraints favor entropy decreasing inward. The simulations cannot distinguish physical turbulent mixing from mixing caused by finite resolution.
  - **Position:** The halo above the poles provides a more sensitive test of disk boundary conditions than the flow near the disk edge.
