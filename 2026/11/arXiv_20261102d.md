## Bursts, quiet phases, and observed structure

- [2610.41489](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41489) Tobias J. Looser et al. `OASIS I: Spectroscopy of Mini-Quenched and Lulling Galaxies Constrains the Quiet Side of Bursty Star Formation at z > 4`
  - **Question:** How common are quiet phases in low-mass galaxies at high redshift?
  - **Data:**
    - OASIS NIRSpec/PRISM exposures of 9.3-28 hours; 54 analysis galaxies at `z~4-8.8`, from 65 secure high-redshift sources.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit stellar continua and emission lines with ppxf; compare Balmer-line star-formation rates with the main sequence and distinguish selection tiers.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Three mini-quenched and five lulling galaxies occupy the suppressed population. Seven of ten deliberately selected photometric candidates instead lie near or above the main sequence.
    - In tiers without star-formation-state selection, the relevant `z=4-5.5`, `log(M*/Msun)=8-9.5` subset contains `5/17` suppressed objects: `29%`, with a `17-44%` 68% interval. This is a small, flux-limited sample, not a completeness-corrected population fraction.
  - **Position:** A targeted spectroscopic census of quiet phases.

- [2610.41490](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41490) Tobias J. Looser et al. `OASIS II: The Ionized Gas Traces Feedback-Driven Declines into the Quiet Side of Bursty Star Formation at z > 4`
  - **Question:** Do suppressed galaxies reflect brief fluctuations or prolonged declines?
  - **Data:**
    - The same 54 OASIS galaxies; Balmer lines and continuum-derived stellar histories, with line non-detections retained as upper limits.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare nebular star-formation rates with 100-Myr averages; use cumulative formed mass and oxygen-line ratios to examine timescales and gas ionization.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Lulling and mini-quenched classes formed only about `13%` and `2%` of their stellar mass in the last `100 Myr`, respectively. The mini-quenched histories support suppression lasting `>=100 Myr`.
    - Low ionization and rapid declines favor feedback over passive gas exhaustion, but gas removal is inferred. Low-resolution spectra cannot distinguish stellar ionization, obscured active nuclei, or shocks decisively.
  - **Position:** Adds timescale and gas diagnostics to OASIS I.

- [2610.41491](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41491) William M. Baker et al. `OASIS III: Bursty star formation drives observed galaxy sizes at z >= 4`
  - **Question:** Does the burst phase affect measured galaxy sizes?
  - **Data:**
    - NIRCam imaging and OASIS spectra for 51 galaxies at `z~4-9`; poorly fit morphologies, weak continua, a broad-line active nucleus and the highest-redshift targets are excluded.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit joint single-Sersic light profiles with ForcePho; compare short-to-long-timescale star-formation ratios using partial correlations and stacked profiles.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The size-burstiness anticorrelation persists when controlling for mass and redshift. Controlling for mass alone gives `rho=-0.57`, `p<0.001`; this coefficient should not be assigned to the two-control test.
    - Bursting galaxies have more concentrated stacked light. The measurement is a continuum half-light radius, not a stellar-mass radius or direct evidence of dynamical contraction.
  - **Position:** Connects burst selection with size-dependent completeness.

- [2610.40796](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40796) Claire Bolda et al. `NGDEEP: Anchoring the 0.5 < z < 3.5 Star-Forming Main Sequence at Low Stellar Masses with Ultradeep JWST/NIRISS WFSS`
  - **Question:** How variable is star formation in low-mass galaxies on short timescales?
  - **Data:**
    - NGDEEP NIRISS spectroscopy at `0.5<z<3.5`: 414 galaxies after quality and empirical mass-completeness cuts, including the 284 Balmer-detected galaxies highlighted in the study.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare dust-corrected Balmer rates with 100-Myr cigale spectral-energy-distribution rates; fit each main sequence with Bayesian linear regression and examine mass-dependent scatter.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Balmer-based intrinsic scatter exceeds the longer-timescale estimate by about `0.2-0.4 dex` below `z=2.5`, consistent with stronger short-timescale variability. The difference is not significant in the highest-redshift bin.
    - The comparison labeled UV-based uses spectral-energy-distribution-derived rates, not independent direct UV measurements. Above `z=2.5`, Hbeta, model-based dust correction and increasing line-selection bias complicate interpretation.
  - **Position:** Extends spectroscopic main-sequence measurements to low masses.


## Circumgalactic gas and chemical structure

- [2610.41681](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41681) Zoe Harvey et al. `The Evolution of the Circumgalactic Medium after a starburst in DESI Post-Starburst Galaxies`
  - **Question:** How does cool circumgalactic absorption evolve after a starburst?
  - **Data:**
    - DESI DR2: about 1.98 million background quasars and 143 million galaxy-quasar pairs; foreground luminous red galaxies at `0.4<z<0.76`.
  - **Method:**
    - Classify galaxy spectra with independent components; stack continuum-normalized Mg II with inverse-variance weights and pair-bootstrap errors; check age ordering with Bagpipes and null stacks with randomized redshifts.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Within `71 kpc`, post-starburst Mg II equivalent width is `1.58 +/- 0.32 Angstrom`, versus `0.69 +/- 0.15` for star-forming and `0.22 +/- 0.05` for quiescent galaxies. Ongoing starbursts reach `2.32 +/- 0.62`, so post-starbursts are not the strongest innermost class.
    - Older post-starburst subsets have weaker inner and stronger `~300-1000 kpc` absorption. This is consistent with outward redistribution; saturated equivalent width does not directly measure gas mass, and absorption beyond `~1 Mpc` includes neighboring structure.
  - **Position:** Extends SDSS stacking to age-resolved circumgalactic profiles.

- [2610.41557](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41557) Sijia Li et al. `The JWST/PASSAGE Survey: Metallicity Radial Gradients in Emission-Line Galaxy Pairs at Cosmic Noon with NIRISS`
  - **Question:** Do interacting systems at cosmic noon have distinct metallicity gradients?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/PASSAGE NIRISS line maps and NIRCam imaging; 17 galaxies with usable gradients at `z~1.7-3.2`.
  - **Method:**
    - Select close, morphologically disturbed, spectroscopic pairs; fit strong-line metallicity and attenuation in Voronoi bins, then radial slopes; test calibration and signal-to-noise choices.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Gradients span roughly `-0.2 to +0.45 dex/kpc`; redshift-bin means are consistent with zero. Mass-ratio correlations are not significant.
    - Changing the strong-line calibration shifts galaxy metallicities by a median `-0.15 dex`, and median gradients by `-0.012 dex/kpc`; gradient detections at `>=2 sigma` change from five to four. Beam smearing is not corrected, and no matched control establishes a merger effect.
  - **Position:** Spatial chemical measurements for candidate interacting pairs.


## Dust assembly and reliable inference

- [2610.40790](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40790) Alessia D'Angelo et al. `The assembly of dusty galaxies at z >= 4: grain growth and the evolutionary origin of the dust-to-stellar-mass scatter`
  - **Question:** What drives dust diversity at similar stellar mass in early galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Five selected dustyGadget galaxies at `z~4.5`, including three systems spanning the dust-transition region; stellar-only chemical modeling and SKIRT post-processing.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare total and stellar-source dust budgets; relate enrichment and gas structure to assembly histories and synthetic continuum observables.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Within this model, stellar production explains the low-dust branch, while grain growth is needed for the transition and scatter. Its onset occurs around `Z~0.04 Zsun`, below the cited local reference near `0.2 Zsun`.
    - The three transition systems occupy similar cosmic-web nodes; internal assembly and gas density distinguish their dust content. This does not establish environment independence for the wider population.
  - **Position:** Interprets the dust scaling relations of Di Cesare+2023.

- [2610.40770](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40770) Daniela Breitman et al. `When precise inference is wrong: Diagnosing model misspecification with applications to cosmic dawn and reionisation`
  - **Question:** Can model misspecification be detected without knowing the correct alternative?
  - **Data:**
    - HERA upper limits, HST/JWST UV luminosity functions, and a simulated `1080 h` SKA-Low power spectrum.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit data partitions and their combination with 21cmEMU; calculate conditional suspiciousness and calibrate non-Gaussian behavior with noisy mock realizations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - A model omitting molecular-cooling galaxies fits the mock power spectrum while biasing inferred astrophysics. Its redshift-split statistic lies beyond all `150` well-specified mocks: empirical significance `>=2.72 sigma`; the quoted `~3.4 sigma` requires tail extrapolation.
    - Under the adopted model and likelihood, the UV-luminosity-function tension is `5.0 sigma` in the Gaussian limit, falling to `2.1 sigma` when high-redshift errors double. A null consistency test does not establish model adequacy.
  - **Position:** Extends Handley and Lemos+2019 to cosmic-dawn inference.
