## Key Findings

- Two tests constrain why early galaxies look bright. Feeding the extra supernovae from a top-heavy stellar population back into a galaxy model reduces its luminosity gain. Independently, clustering limits how widely stellar mass can vary between halos of equal mass. [2611.25027](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25027) Andrea Ferrara, [2611.18850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18850) Ivan Nikolic.
- The gas tracer sets what can be inferred about a quasar environment. Carbon-line kinematics resolve diverse motions in cold host gas at high redshift. A thermal distortion at cosmic noon measures energy in hot gas, with its source still uncertain. [2611.21755](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21755) Marcel Neeleman, [2611.23487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23487) Kirsten R. Hall.
- For little red dots, the optical continuum predicts the measured infrared emission, while broad H-alpha width adds little independent spread to a virial mass estimate at fixed line luminosity. Together these observations constrain dust geometry and mass estimators through different observables. [2611.26483](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26483) C. Ramos Almeida, [2611.24217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24217) Haoyu Wang.

## What sets the light output of early galaxies?

- [2611.25027](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25027) Andrea Ferrara `Top-heavy Initial Mass Functions are not enough at cosmic dawn`
  - **Question:** How much brighter would early galaxies become if they formed more massive stars?
  - **Data:**
    - A metallicity-dependent stellar initial mass function and a Salpeter reference both span `0.1-300 solar masses`. Published ultraviolet counts provide the observational comparison.
  - **Method:**
    - Calculate emergent light at fixed halo mass, propagating the stellar mass distribution into supernova rates, metal production and dust. Repeat the comparison in the DELPHI galaxy formation model.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Changing only light per unit star formation brightens the model by about `1.5 mag`. In the self-consistent DELPHI runs, the predicted luminosity functions differ by less than `0.5 dex` at `z~11-17`.
  - **Position:** The size of the compensation depends on the assumed dust yield, geometry and feedback. It limits this proposed explanation for the bright counts.

- [2611.18850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18850) Ivan Nikolic `Interpreting JWST UV luminosity functions and angular correlation functions via a "universal language" for high-redshift galaxies`
  - **Question:** What does the spatial distribution of early galaxies add to ultraviolet number counts?
  - **Data:**
    - CANUCS ultraviolet luminosity functions at `z=8-12` and COSMOS-Web mass-selected clustering at `z=6-10.5` probe overlapping populations.
  - **Method:**
    - Jointly fit the distribution of galaxy properties at fixed halo mass to counts and angular correlations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Including clustering narrows the `68%` interval for stellar-to-halo mass scatter from `0.20-0.86 dex` to `0.16-0.60 dex` for the adopted stellar mass threshold.
  - **Position:** The gain depends on the mass cut. Missing off-diagonal covariance can make the interval too narrow.

- [2611.17582](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17582) B. Melekh `Hydrodynamic and photoionization analysis of a Cosmic Dawn dwarf galaxy based on a JWST-inferred star formation history`
  - **Question:** Can the surrounding nebula change the ionizing output inferred from a stellar population?
  - **Data:**
    - Galaxy 2779 has a star formation history reconstructed from JWST photometry.
  - **Method:**
    - Evolve stellar populations, spherical wind bubbles and radiation transport through the surrounding gas.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The total escaping ionizing photon rate can exceed the stellar production rate because gas emits additional photons. The final model overpredicts the measured flux.
  - **Position:** The numerator includes nebular radiation, so this ratio cannot be interpreted as a probability for an individual stellar photon to escape.

- [2611.25937](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25937) Evangelia Ntormousi `Magnolia: a highly magnetized galaxy at redshift z~9`
  - **Question:** Can rapid assembly magnetize a galaxy before it develops an ordered disc?
  - **Data:**
    - The Magnolia cosmological zoom follows a galaxy at approximately `10 pc` resolution with radiation and magnetized gas.
  - **Method:**
    - Track the mass-weighted field during clump assembly and decompose the transfer of kinetic energy into magnetic energy by compression and tension.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The simulated field reaches a mean of about `6 microgauss` at `z=8.6`. Compression supplies the largest kinetic-to-magnetic transfer at the onset of amplification.
  - **Position:** This calculation identifies a mechanism in one simulated galaxy. Its resolution leaves open the contribution of a small-scale turbulent dynamo in real systems.

## Cold and hot gas around galaxies and quasars

- [2611.25220](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25220) Charles Willard `Multiphase Gas in High-z Cosmic Sheets`
  - **Question:** How does a sheet of intergalactic gas break up, and how does that change its absorption cross section?
  - **Data:**
    - Two AREPO simulations follow sheets with different collapse temperatures. In their cold cores the cell sizes span `0.4-3.2 physical kpc` at `z=4`.
  - **Method:**
    - Vary resolution and the initial perturbations, track bending of the cold layer, and measure the area above fixed neutral hydrogen column densities.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Improving spatial resolution fourfold increases the area fraction above the partial Lyman-limit threshold by a factor of `1.5` in the cooler sheet. The area above the full Lyman-limit threshold grows by a factor of `3.5` in the hotter sheet.
  - **Position:** Bending of the shocked layer offers a route to absorbers outside halos. Their cosmic incidence is uncertain because the calculation samples two imposed collapse histories.

- [2611.21755](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21755) Marcel Neeleman `XQR30: ALMA Observations Reveal the Diverse Structure of Cold Gas within z>6 Quasar Host Galaxies`
  - **Question:** How widespread is rotation in the cold gas of luminous early quasar hosts?
  - **Data:**
    - ALMA imaging provides a carbon fine-structure line, [C II], and far-infrared continuum for a sample of `41` quasar fields.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit spatially resolved line kinematics and compare the orientations of the velocity field, line emission and dust continuum.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Of `39` line detections, `18` are rotation-dominated and `9` dispersion-dominated. The remaining `12` lack the signal needed to classify them.
  - **Position:** The unclassified hosts limit a population-wide disk fraction, and inclination and disturbed motions complicate dynamical mass estimates.

- [2611.23487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23487) Kirsten R. Hall `Excess intergalactic heating around a dual quasar system 2.8 Gyr after the Big Bang`
  - **Question:** How much thermal energy is stored in gas around the two active nuclei?
  - **Data:**
    - Radio through submillimeter measurements of J1549+1245 at `z~2.37` include ALMA interferometry.
  - **Method:**
    - Separate synchrotron and dust emission from the thermal Sunyaev-Zeldovich distortion, then check the residual signal in the interferometric measurements.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The localized distortion implies `(9.7 +/- 1.6) x 10^60 erg` in thermal energy. A decrement on roughly `600 kpc` scales has only `2.9 sigma` significance.
  - **Position:** The energy measurement constrains the hot phase. Heating by the two nuclei and by halo assembly remains difficult to apportion.

## What can spectra tell us about little red dots?

- [2611.26483](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26483) C. Ramos Almeida `The Galaxy Activity, Torus, and Outflow Survey: N. Little Red Dots are not dust deficient: constraints from Seyfert 1 JWST spectra`
  - **Question:** How much infrared emission should accompany the optical light of compact little red dots?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST spectra cover eight nearby type 1 Seyferts. Comparison data include a stack of `249` little red dots and a local analog.
  - **Method:**
    - Normalize the Seyfert spectra separately by broad H-alpha and the continuum at 5100 angstroms. Compare both predictions with the little-red-dot spectra and fit disc and dust emission together.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The optical continuum predicts infrared emission within a factor of about `1.5-2`. A joint fit favors a reddened disc and dust that intercepts only about `10-20%` of its light.
  - **Position:** Weak infrared emission can coexist with nuclear dust. The high-redshift stack reaches only about `4 microns` in the rest frame, and host light can enter the aperture.

- [2611.24217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24217) Haoyu Wang `A Tight Broad-line Width-Luminosity Relation in Little Red Dots and Its Consequence for Virial Black Hole Masses`
  - **Question:** How much spread in inferred black-hole mass can broad H-alpha width contribute at fixed luminosity?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST spectra of `29` little red dots with uniform line fits provide the main sample.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit width versus luminosity with correlated errors and repeat the fits with different line decompositions and spectral windows.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The baseline intrinsic width scatter is `0.054 dex`. Its one-sided `95%` bound implies less than `0.16 dex` of virial-estimator scatter at fixed luminosity.
  - **Position:** The bound depends on the fitting window and describes the estimator. The accuracy of the true black-hole masses remains unknown.
