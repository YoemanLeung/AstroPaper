## Key Findings

- BOSS1244 shows a weak but significant tendency for galaxies in denser regions to have redder colors. In the filamentary BOSS1542 field, the color trend is consistent with zero and the mass trend depends on the statistical test. [2611.30054](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30054) Dongdong Shi.
- In AMORE6, dense gas can suppress optical oxygen emission. The upper limit on ultraviolet C IV relative to H-beta independently restricts how much metal that gas can contain. The numerical bound depends on the assumed ionization parameter. [2611.30384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30384) Takahiro Morishita.
- The apparent uniformity of broad H-alpha widths in little red dots depends on how far into the wings the measurement extends. Synthetic lines in the observed noise can reproduce the increase in scatter even with single-Gaussian profiles. [2611.29615](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29615) Haoyu Wang.

## Galaxy environments at cosmic noon

- [2611.30054](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30054) Dongdong Shi et al. `Environmental dependence of the properties of H-alpha emitters in the extremely massive galaxy protoclusters BOSS1244 and BOSS1542 at z = 2.24`
  - **Question:** Does local density predict the colors and stellar masses of galaxies in two protoclusters at the same redshift?
  - **Data:**
    - Narrowband excess and broadband colors identify 207 H-alpha emitters in BOSS1244 and 151 in BOSS1542 at redshift 2.24. Spectroscopy preferentially covers the dense regions.
  - **Method:**
    - Estimate projected density from the fifth-nearest emitter. Compare color and photometrically inferred stellar mass against density within each field.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - For color versus density, the Spearman rank coefficient is `0.28` in BOSS1244 (`p = 0.000060`) and `0.10` in BOSS1542 (`p = 0.21`). Stellar mass also increases weakly with density in BOSS1244.
    - In BOSS1542 the mass trend passes the Pearson test but falls short in the rank test. Sample size, scatter and the density range affect these coefficients.
  - **Position:** The two fields show why using one protocluster to infer a universal environmental trend at cosmic noon is risky.

## Gas and star formation near redshift six

- [2611.30384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30384) Takahiro Morishita et al. `Deep UV Spectroscopy of an Extremely Metal-Poor Galaxy at z = 5.725`
  - **Question:** Could high gas density hide substantial chemical enrichment in AMORE6?
  - **Data:**
    - VLT/X-shooter spectra of two lensed images at redshift 5.725 cover Lyman-alpha and several rest-frame ultraviolet metal lines.
  - **Method:**
    - Use the measured Lyman-alpha width to set metal-line flux limits. Compare C IV and [O III] relative to H-beta with photoionization grids that vary gas density and metallicity.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At the fiducial ionization parameter, the C IV upper limit excludes gas with both an electron density above `100,000 per cubic centimeter` and a metallicity above `0.0032 solar`.
    - The ionization parameter is unconstrained by detected metal lines. Lowering it relaxes the metallicity bound to about `0.01 solar` in the models tested.
  - **Position:** The ultraviolet spectrum tests a specific alternative to an extremely metal-poor nebula. The stellar population powering it remains uncertain.

- [2611.29560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29560) Shuowen Jin et al. `Discovery of Two Hyperluminous Infrared Galaxies at z ~ 6`
  - **Question:** How concentrated are the dust and line emission in two infrared-luminous galaxies near redshift six?
  - **Data:**
    - AzTEC and SCUBA-2 selected the COSMOS sources. ALMA and NOEMA spectroscopy confirms AzTECC63 at redshift 6.092 and S2COS-84 at 5.90.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit elliptical profiles directly to the interferometric measurements of S2COS-84. Fit the infrared luminosities of both galaxies using their broadband emission.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - In S2COS-84 the effective radii are `0.31 +/- 0.01 kpc` for dust and `0.82 +/- 0.07 kpc` for singly ionized carbon. The infrared luminosities imply star formation rates of about `1000` and `2300 solar masses per year` in AzTECC63 and S2COS-84, respectively.
    - AzTECC63 is spatially unresolved. An obscured active nucleus could supply some infrared luminosity and lower the inferred star formation rate.
  - **Position:** Resolved dust and carbon emission offer distinct size scales for interpreting the compact starburst.

## Continua and line wings in little red dots

- [2611.28209](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28209) Callum Witten et al. `Quasi-hostless LRDs with intrinsically red, faint UV continua`
  - **Question:** What does the ultraviolet spectrum look like when host-galaxy light contributes little to a compact red source?
  - **Data:**
    - Archival JWST spectra cover 132 little red dots. Five extreme objects, including two previously known examples, enter the comparison stack.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit the ultraviolet continuum and select the weakest ratios of 1500-angstrom to 5100-angstrom luminosity. Stack spectra and compare their continuum and emission lines.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Three newly classified sources lie at `z = 3.10, 6.73 and 7.25`. As ultraviolet light weakens relative to optical light, the ultraviolet slope reddens and [O III] weakens relative to H-alpha.
    - A screened central source plus a faint host can reproduce the red continuum. The same model attenuates the Balmer emission lines much more strongly than observed.
  - **Position:** The continuum model predicts a growing host contribution toward the far ultraviolet, which can be tested with wavelength-dependent sizes.

- [2611.29615](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29615) Haoyu Wang et al. `Velocity-Window Dependence of the Broad H-alpha Width-Luminosity Relation in Little Red Dots`
  - **Question:** How does the velocity range used to measure H-alpha affect the apparent uniformity of little red dots?
  - **Data:**
    - The paired comparison uses 20 high signal-to-noise JWST/NIRSpec little red dots and 167 Sloan broad-line active nuclei.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure the flux-weighted velocity dispersion inside successively wider symmetric windows. Refit the width-luminosity relation at each window and bootstrap the change in residual scatter.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - For the little red dots, extending each side from `2500` to `4500 km/s` raises the observed scatter from `0.036` to `0.084 dex`. The paired change is `0.048 dex` (95% interval `0.026-0.064 dex`).
    - Tests that inject synthetic lines into the observed noise accommodate both a single Gaussian and a core with wings. The measured increase alone cannot establish intrinsic diversity in the wings.
  - **Position:** Matching velocity windows matters when using broad-line widths to compare the kinematics of compact red sources.

## Stellar populations and dust at cosmic dawn

- [2611.31148](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31148) Keita Fukushima et al. `On the effect of a redshift- and metallicity-dependent top-heavy IMF on high-redshift UV luminosity functions`
  - **Question:** Can a stellar population richer in massive stars account for the abundance of ultraviolet-bright galaxies at early times?
  - **Data:**
    - A semi-analytic galaxy model follows halo merger trees. Published ultraviolet luminosity functions supply comparisons from redshift seven into the cosmic-dawn regime.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare a standard stellar initial mass function with one varying with redshift and metallicity. Track its effects on both starlight and enrichment, calibrate dust attenuation at redshift seven, then vary the effective optical depth.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - For the selected bright-galaxy samples, the best fits use `51%` of the fiducial optical depth at `z = 9.7` and `21%` at `z = 13.04`.
    - Changing the magnitude cut at redshift 9.7 moves the preferred fraction from `0.51` to `0.35`. Too few populated bright bins and a shortfall even near the dust-free limit prevent a robust constraint at redshift 13.5.
  - **Position:** The inferred attenuation depends on both the stellar model and the galaxy sample. Galaxy counts alone give no direct measurement of the dust mass.
