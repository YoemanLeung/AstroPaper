## Key Findings

- Physical conditions matter for early-galaxy classifications: lower C/O and higher density blur standard UV AGN boundaries, while H-alpha-selected stacks provide a complementary chemical baseline. [2611.23391](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23391) Jiayang Yang; [2611.24630](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24630) Claudia Di Cesare.
- Hot-gas calorimetry supports substantial quasar energy deposition, but the largest spatial-scale signal and the division between feedback and halo heating remain uncertain. [2611.23487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23487) Kirsten R. Hall.
- LRD line shape alone is insufficient to establish a mass or scattering mechanism: tight widths reduce independent virial-mass information, and a local radio detection tests cocoon geometry. [2611.24217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24217) Haoyu Wang; [2611.24387](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24387) Giovanni Mazzolari.
- Galaxy and satellite alignments can trace shared assembly geometry. They do not by themselves demonstrate jet-triggered star formation or establish that observed satellite asymmetries challenge cosmology. [2611.24490](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24490) Peter Breiding; [2611.23849](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23849) Janvi P. Madhani.

## Early galaxies and chemical diagnostics

- [2611.23391](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23391) Jiayang Yang et al. `Are AGN Required? Updated Photoionization Models for Rest-UV Line Diagnostics in Reionization-era Galaxies`
  - **Question:** Can rest-UV line ratios alone establish an active galactic nucleus (AGN) in reionization-era galaxies?
  - **Data:** JWST/NIRSpec G140M and G395M spectra of eight C3PO galaxies at `z~7-9`; UV and optical lines constrain nebular conditions.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit emission lines, derive abundances and densities, and compare Cloudy grids illuminated by BPASS and BC03 stellar populations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Lower C/O and higher density shift stellar grids into nominal composite regions. The strongest He II remains unexplained: C3PO-13264 has `EW=3.53+/-0.55 Angstrom`, exceeding the explored stellar-grid maximum of about `1.2 Angstrom`.
  - **Position:** Extends established UV diagnostics to observed early-galaxy conditions. Agreement with stellar models does not exclude AGN; C III]-based electron density is not directly the uniform model hydrogen density.

- [2611.24630](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24630) Claudia Di Cesare et al. `The Gas-Phase Metallicity and Nitrogen abundances in typical galaxies at z~5`
  - **Question:** How do typical H-alpha-selected galaxies enrich their gas at early times?
  - **Data:** JWST/NIRCam grism spectra of `1748` galaxies at `z=3.8-6.6`, combining CONGRESS, COLA1, EIGER, FRESCO and ALT; broad H-alpha sources are removed.
  - **Method:**
    - Median-stack H-alpha-normalized two-dimensional spectra in stellar-mass bins; fit Voigt profiles and apply high-redshift [N II]/H-alpha, [S II]/H-alpha and [N II]/[S II] calibrations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Metallicity rises with stellar mass. Sulfur-based values sit about `0.3 dex` above nitrogen-based estimates. The stacks show no evidence for enhanced N/O in the dominant H II-region emission; this does not exclude rare nitrogen-rich components.
  - **Position:** Adds a complementary grism-selected baseline to auroral-line and UV-selected samples. Strong-line calibration, variable S/O, low-mass incompleteness and averaging limit interpretation; agreement with a simulation does not uniquely identify its feedback physics.

## Quasar heating and little red dots

- [2611.23487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23487) Kirsten R. Hall et al. `Excess intergalactic heating around a dual quasar system 2.8 Gyr after the Big Bang`
  - **Question:** Can the hottest gas around a dual quasar reveal feedback energy missed by cooler tracers?
  - **Data:** Radio-to-submillimeter measurements of J1549+1245 at `z~2.37`, including ALMA and ACA; the companion lies at a projected separation of `19 kpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Decompose synchrotron, dust and thermal Sunyaev-Zeldovich (tSZ) emission; subtract positive sources in visibility space and compare with FABLE halos with and without AGN feedback.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The localized spectral distortion implies thermal energy `(9.7+/-1.6) x 10^60 erg`. A much larger, roughly `600 kpc` decrement is only `2.9 sigma` and remains tentative. A coupling fraction of at least `0.6%` assumes the primary quasar supplies the energy over `100 Myr`.
  - **Position:** Provides an individual-system calorimetric test of quasar feedback. The energy source can be either quasar; uncertain halo mass, SED decomposition and large-scale structure heating affect the attribution.

- [2611.24217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24217) Haoyu Wang et al. `A Tight Broad-line Width-Luminosity Relation in Little Red Dots and Its Consequence for Virial Black Hole Masses`
  - **Question:** How much independent mass information do little red dot (LRD) broad-line widths contain?
  - **Data:** Uniform public JWST/NIRSpec fits for `29` LRDs at `z=3.36-7.15`, `15` other broad-line sources, and luminosity-matched SDSS quasars.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit broad H-alpha profiles and width-luminosity relations with measurement covariance; test repeat spectra, alternative narrow-line treatments and injected profiles.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The baseline intrinsic width scatter is `0.054 dex`, with a one-sided `95%` upper limit of `0.075 dex`. Most tested choices allow a conservative ceiling near `0.10 dex`; the widest fitting window reaches `0.119 dex`. Baseline virial-mass scatter at fixed line luminosity is below `0.16 dex`, not a measurement of true mass accuracy.
  - **Position:** Challenges independent interpretation of LRD virial mass functions. The slope depends on three bright sources and line decomposition; orbital and scattering/envelope interpretations remain physically distinct.

- [2611.24387](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24387) Giovanni Mazzolari et al. `Radio emission from a local Little Red Dot: a challenge to the ionized cocoon scenario`
  - **Question:** Can radio and optical measurements distinguish broad-line mechanisms in a nearby LRD?
  - **Data:** Local source J1047 at `z=0.1682`: VLA detections, a LOFAR limit, SDSS/Keck/Gemini spectroscopy and broadband photometry.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare Gaussian and scattering line profiles, infer scattering-column parameters, and predict free-free radio absorption and star-formation contributions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The preferred exponential-wing fit implies an electron column near `2.4 x 10^24 cm^-2`. A uniform `0.01 pc` layer would give a `5 GHz` free-free optical depth near `10^6`, incompatible with the detected radio emission under that geometry.
  - **Position:** Adds a radio test to LRD line-profile interpretations. Partial covering or radio emission outside the cocoon remains possible; the unresolved source and one local analog do not settle high-redshift LRD physics.

## Galaxy assembly and environment

- [2611.24490](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24490) Peter Breiding et al. `Powerful Radio Jets and Galaxy Assembly Align at Cosmic Noon`
  - **Question:** Do powerful radio jets share an axis with galaxy assembly and young stellar structures?
  - **Data:** HST F140W/F606W and radio imaging of `35` type-2 3CR galaxies at `z>1`; `33` have usable HST data.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit optical host structure, segment UV regions, compare projected orientations, and test stellar-continuum ages against assumed jet propagation times.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - After shape cuts, `26` hosts and `38` UV regions in `27` galaxies yield median jet misalignments of `16.9` and `16.7 degrees`, respectively. Seven regions satisfy the strict timing condition at an assumed advance speed of `0.01c`; timing compatibility does not establish triggering.
  - **Position:** Links the alignment effect to a possible common assembly axis. Scattered quasar light and nebular emission can contaminate UV/optical morphology; two-band stellar models and projected companions cannot determine the net star-formation response.

- [2611.24803](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24803) Alice Chen et al. `Cross-Dataset Inference of Central-Satellite Classification and Host Halo Masses from Galaxy neighbours with Machine Learning`
  - **Question:** Can local galaxy neighbors identify centrals and estimate their host halo masses across simulations?
  - **Data:** TNG100 training and held-out tests, with independent EAGLE RefL0100N1504 tests; stellar masses and ten nearest-neighbor distances in real and projected redshift space.
  - **Method:**
    - Train XGBoost classifiers and regressors, align stellar-mass distributions, and augment training with several stellar-mass thresholds.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Central classification retains high purity and completeness across simulations. Halo-mass inference worsens for inferred rather than true centrals: misclassified satellites can strongly underestimate their host masses. Distribution alignment improves transfer but does not remove this failure mode.
  - **Position:** A practical environment estimator awaiting lightcone and survey tests. Simulation stellar masses are cleaner than observed estimates; the finite boxes poorly sample massive clusters and do not validate extrapolation to dwarf halos.

- [2611.23849](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23849) Janvi P. Madhani et al. `Zippers and Twisters II: The lopsidedness of satellite systems around local massive galaxies emerges from a shifting cosmic web landscape`
  - **Question:** Can evolving cosmic filaments generate lopsided satellite populations without recent major galaxy mergers?
  - **Data:** Twelve Milky-Way-to-Centaurus-A-mass systems in NewHorizon, with resolved luminous satellites and reconstructed surrounding gas filaments.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure hemispheric and cone asymmetries; compare isotropic and halo-tracing null models, filament orientations and time-dependent filament mergers.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Five of twelve systems have broad angular overdensities above `2 sigma`. Maximum-asymmetry planes align with nearby filaments at a mean angle of `13.9 degrees`. M31 lies only about `1-1.7 sigma` from the simulated distribution under the adopted comparisons.
  - **Position:** Connects satellite geometry to intermediate-scale gas accretion. Small host counts and satellite completeness prevent a definitive population-level verdict on M31; dark-matter-only null models need care.
