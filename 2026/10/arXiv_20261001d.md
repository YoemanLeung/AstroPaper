## Key Findings

- Lyalpha fractions constrain reionization jointly with galaxy emission physics. [2609.40363](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40363) Aritra Kundu reproduces the observed evolution after calibration at `z~6`; changes in dust or ISM/CGM hydrogen could shift the inferred neutral fractions.
- Resolved star-formation relations depend on both scale and sample. [2609.40233](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40233) C. Cornil-Baiotto measures CO-dust decorrelation in one lensed galaxy, while [2609.39940](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39940) Chryssi Koukouraki finds a weak linewidth term in a selected nearby subset. Neither establishes a universal new star-formation law.
- Population trends require a matched selection function. [2609.38430](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38430) Gregory Troiani finds morphology differences in a mid-IR-selected sample; [2609.39036](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39036) A. Nigoche-Netro maps DESI targeting distortions. Apparent changes cannot be assigned wholly to intrinsic evolution.
- Environmental null results and environmental associations have different limits. [2609.39689](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39689) Anuroop Dasgupta has too few cluster members to exclude dense-environment effects; [2609.38330](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38330) Karri I. I. Koljonen can recover a group redshift while selecting the wrong group for the source.
- Good fits do not uniquely establish physical identities. [2609.38310](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38310) Sangjun Cha demonstrates lens mass-decomposition freedom; [2609.39977](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39977) Emma Schwartzman retains six radio AGN-pair candidates; [2609.38373](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38373) Ming-Yang Zhuang favors a supernova origin without a secure transient subtype.

## Reionization and early structure

- [2609.40363](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40363) Aritra Kundu et al. `Probing the Reionization History and Bubble Sizes with JWST Lyman-alpha Fraction Measurements`
  - **Question:** How strongly do JWST Lyalpha fractions constrain neutral hydrogen and ionized regions?
  - **Data:**
    - Published UV-selected Lyalpha fractions at `z~6-14`, the empirical `z~6` EW distribution, and the `95.5 cMpc` THESAN-1 simulation.
  - **Method:**
    - Calibrate intrinsic EWs at `z~6`; calculate damping-wing transmission along simulated sightlines and with a semi-analytic bubble model.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - For `EW>25 Angstrom`, inferred mass-weighted neutral fractions are `0.38 (+0.14/-0.38)`, `0.57 (+0.23/-0.57)`, and `0.93 (+0.05/-0.17)` at `z~7, 8, 11`; the EW threshold changes these estimates.
    - THESAN reproduces the higher-redshift evolution after calibration. The inference fixes bubble sizes at matched neutral fraction and assumes redshift-invariant intrinsic EWs; these are conditional constraints, not independent bubble-radius measurements.
  - **Position:** Connects UV-selected Lyalpha statistics to reionization while exposing the dependence on ISM/CGM calibration.

- [2609.39399](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39399) Yao-Yu Li et al. `The impact of primordial magnetic fields on the formation of galaxies`
  - **Question:** Can primordial magnetic-field perturbations accelerate galaxy assembly after baryonic feedback is included?
  - **Data:**
    - Six hydrodynamical simulations: one LCDM control and five PMF initial-condition models, each with `384^3` gas and dark-matter particles in a `17 h^-1 Mpc` box.
  - **Method:**
    - Modify baryon and dark-matter initial power spectra separately; evolve cooling, star formation, stellar feedback and black-hole feedback to `z=0`.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - PMF-enhanced initial power increases early halo and galaxy abundance, star formation and black-hole growth. Subsequent gas consumption and feedback weaken, but do not completely erase, the differences.
    - The peak scale of the initial power enhancement strongly controls the response. These are comparative simulations, not a detection of primordial magnetism or a calibrated explanation of observed high-redshift abundances.
  - **Position:** A test of how initial small-scale power and baryonic regulation jointly shape early galaxy populations.


## Resolved gas, star formation, and chemical structure

- [2609.40233](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40233) C. Cornil-Baiotto et al. `Sub-kiloparsec Test of the Kennicutt-Schmidt Relation in a Strongly Lensed Dusty Star-Forming Galaxy at z~2.78`
  - **Question:** At what scale does the gas-star formation relation cease to describe individual regions at cosmic noon?
  - **Data:**
    - ALMA CO(3-2) and rest-frame `803 micron` continuum at `0.2` and `0.6 arcsec` in SMMJ0658 at `z=2.7768 +/- 0.0002`, plus a JWST-based Bullet Cluster lens model.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify resolved CO and continuum clumps, ray-trace the maps, and compare CO-to-dust ratios in apertures centered on each tracer.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The tracer-centered ratios diverge below `0.8 +/- 0.1 kpc`, consistent with different stages of the star-formation cycle. Clump depletion times span `0.82-2.56 Gyr` under the adopted conversions.
    - The best source-plane scale is about `200 pc`, but the average resolution is about `450 pc`. Inclination, clump blending, dust as an SFR tracer and CO conversion systematics limit the interpretation; cloud lifetimes remain assumption-dependent.
  - **Position:** Extends resolved gas-star formation decorrelation tests to a strongly magnified galaxy at cosmic noon.

- [2609.39940](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39940) Chryssi Koukouraki et al. `The role of velocity dispersion in the Kennicutt-Schmidt relation`
  - **Question:** Does molecular velocity dispersion explain part of the resolved Kennicutt-Schmidt scatter?
  - **Data:**
    - Spatially matched gas, SFR and linewidth measurements for 45 ALMaQUEST, 105 EDGE-CALIFA and 53 PHANGS-ALMA galaxies, plus M51.
  - **Method:**
    - Classify galaxies by the orientation of linewidth gradients relative to the KS relation; fit residual trends and a three-variable relation for group B.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - For group B, equal-weight averaging of ALMaQUEST and EDGE-CALIFA coefficients gives `Sigma_SFR proportional to Sigma_mol^1.15 Delta_v^-0.59`. The negative linewidth dependence explains only a modest fraction of the variance.
    - This is a subset-dependent relation, not a universal correction. PHANGS supplies no group B galaxies in this classification; sparse linewidth coverage can contribute to that absence.
  - **Position:** Provides a nearby comparison for interpreting kinematics alongside resolved gas-depletion measurements.

- [2609.39689](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39689) Anuroop Dasgupta et al. `Internal Structure, Not Environment, Shapes Metallicity Gradients in Low-Mass Galaxies from MaNGA`
  - **Question:** Do internal structure or environment better track metallicity gradients in low-mass galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - MaNGA DR17 spectra for 382 star-forming galaxies spanning `log(M_star/M_sun)=7.2-9.1`, mostly at `0.01<z<0.05`, with three retained lower-redshift exceptions.
  - **Method:**
    - Stack elliptical radial bins, measure N2 abundances, subtract the local stellar-surface-density relation, and test residual gradients against structural and environmental indicators.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The median raw gradient is `-0.007 dex/R_e`. Concentration has the strongest residual-gradient correlation, `r=0.304`, explaining only about `9%` of the variance.
    - No environmental correlation is detected in this mainly low-density sample; cluster processes are insufficiently sampled. A stricter resolution cut shifts the median to `-0.026 dex/R_e`, so near-flat averages are resolution-sensitive.
  - **Position:** A useful field-dwarf baseline for chemical-gradient studies, with no general exclusion of environmental processing.


## Morphology, selection, and environmental inference

- [2609.38430](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38430) Gregory Troiani et al. `Cosmic Collisions I: Optical Morphologies for MIRI-selected galaxies from the PRIMER survey`
  - **Question:** How does mid-infrared selection change the apparent evolution of galaxy morphology?
  - **Data:**
    - PRIMER NIRCam and MIRI imaging in COSMOS and UDS; 14,657 visually classified F770W-selected galaxies, concentrated around cosmic noon.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine citizen-science morphology and interaction votes with reliability weights; use clean subsets for trends and compare overlapping HST classifications.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The selected population favors disks at lower redshift and compact classifications at higher redshift. Selection against dust-poor galaxies and confusion between faint point sources and spheroids affect the apparent evolution.
    - A search identifies `63` systems with optical disks and bright mid-IR cores as possible obscured AGN. They require confirmation; imaging alone does not establish AGN activity or secular growth.
  - **Position:** A morphology resource for dusty populations whose selection and image quality must accompany any evolutionary comparison.

- [2609.39036](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39036) A. Nigoche-Netro et al. `The "Fork" Geometry in the DESI Main Survey DR1: Implications of Target Selection for Galaxy Evolution Studies`
  - **Question:** How much of the DESI redshift-luminosity fork reflects target selection?
  - **Data:**
    - DESI DR1 spectroscopy and Legacy Surveys DR10 photometry, with BGS, LRG, ELG and QSO target classes.
  - **Method:**
    - Inspect target boundaries and surface-brightness effects; use redshift-binned 1/Vmax luminosity functions and Schechter fits as diagnostics of selection distortions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The apparent gap and branch convergence combine intrinsic population differences with targeting, flux limits and structural incompleteness. They cannot be read directly as a physical green valley or evolutionary sequence.
    - The luminosity functions deliberately omit completeness and survey-area corrections. Their normalization is not an intrinsic space-density measurement; the proposed mitigation framework is not a completed selection-function reconstruction.
  - **Position:** A practical warning for interpreting apparent galaxy population structure in target-selected spectroscopic surveys.

- [2609.38330](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38330) Karri I. I. Koljonen et al. `Cosmic-neighbor-associated distances to VHE and candidate VHE BL Lacs via SDSS photometric redshifts`
  - **Question:** Can galaxy neighborhoods constrain redshifts of featureless BL Lac sources?
  - **Data:**
    - SDSS DR19 galaxy photometric redshifts within `2 arcmin` of targets; controls include 16 spectroscopic BL Lacs and three environments with multi-object spectroscopy.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit an error-convolved Gaussian mixture to nearby galaxy redshifts; rank candidate associations by centroid distance and combine independent source constraints.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Associated group redshifts are recovered to roughly `Delta z=0.02-0.04`, but the nearest centroid selects the correct association in only `7/16` spectroscopic controls and `2/3` environment controls.
    - Group-redshift precision is therefore distinct from confidence in assigning a blazar to that group. Proposed distances remain probabilistic, especially with projected structures or incomplete high-redshift membership.
  - **Position:** A transferable environmental-association method with an explicit test of projection ambiguity.


## Lensing systematics for high-redshift sources

- [2609.38310](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38310) Sangjun Cha et al. `Testing Offsets Between Cluster-Scale Halos and BCGs in Strong Lensing Models Using the Jackknife Method`
  - **Question:** Do cluster strong-lensing data require offsets between analytic dark-matter halos and brightest cluster galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Spectroscopic multiple-image catalogs for Abell 370, RX J1347.5-1145 and MACS J0416.1-2403: respectively 35, 38 and 111 source systems.
  - **Method:**
    - Leave one source system out at a time; compare fixed and free halo centers across regularization strengths in the hybrid MrMARTIAN grid-plus-profile reconstruction.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Free halo centers do not significantly improve predictions for excluded images in any of the three clusters. Different mass decompositions can reproduce comparable lensing constraints.
    - Median maximum differences across the tested model set are about `10%` for magnification and time delay; highly magnified images and short delays can be more sensitive. This spread is not a universal error budget.
  - **Position:** Uses predictive validation to qualify physical interpretations of fitted halo offsets and intrinsic lensed-source properties.


## AGN identification and high-redshift transients

- [2609.38350](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38350) Carys J. E. Gilbert et al. `A New Hope for AGN SED Fitting: X-Ray Spectral Analysis of 12MGS AGN with the C2PO-Torus Model`
  - **Question:** Can X-ray torus fits supply physically compatible inputs for infrared AGN modeling?
  - **Data:**
    - NuSTAR, XMM-Newton and Chandra spectra for 43 local 12-micron-selected star-forming AGN, including the first pointed X-ray observation of IRASF03450+0055.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit phenomenological, established torus and new C2PO-Torus models; connect the two-phase clumpy geometry to SKIRTOR parameters and compare multiwavelength luminosity relations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - C2PO-Torus fits diverse spectra while linking torus geometry to the infrared model. Soft excess and relativistic reflection still require extra components; its line-of-sight columns are indicative derived estimates.
    - Low-X-ray-luminosity sources have excess mid-IR and radio emission consistent with star-formation contamination. Full-spectrum decomposition is planned follow-up, not a demonstrated result of this analysis.
  - **Position:** A physically aligned X-ray/IR modeling tool for separating obscured accretion from host emission.

- [2609.39977](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39977) Emma Schwartzman et al. `A New Sample of Closely Separated Dual and Binary AGN Candidates Revealed with the Radio Fundamental Catalog`
  - **Question:** Which compact radio multiples are plausible close AGN pairs rather than jets?
  - **Data:**
    - A BigMAC-RFC cross-match containing 241 systems with archival VLBI images; detailed analysis of ten selected compact multiples.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare radio morphology, compactness, brightness temperatures and multi-frequency spectra with available multiwavelength information.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Six of the ten selected systems favor dual/binary AGN interpretations; four favor compact jets. Several candidate separations are a few to tens of parsecs.
    - The six systems remain unconfirmed, and this preselected subset does not measure the multi-AGN incidence of all 241 matches. Blended low-frequency components make some spectral classifications uncertain.
  - **Position:** An archival route to close-pair follow-up that also identifies structured sources relevant to reference-frame astrometry.

- [2609.38373](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38373) Ming-Yang Zhuang et al. `NEXUS: Discovery of a Luminous Young Transient at z=5`
  - **Question:** What powers the luminous young transient AT 2026abck in a z=5 galaxy?
  - **Data:**
    - NEXUS multi-epoch NIRCam imaging and host NIRCam grism spectroscopy securing `z=5.017 +/- 0.001`.
  - **Method:**
    - Use difference imaging and host decomposition, fit a blackbody to the discovery photometry and upper limit, and compare the location and early-time properties with supernova scenarios.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The source brightened by more than `1.4 mag` over `11.8` rest-frame days. The blackbody fit gives `T~1.7e4 K` and `L_bol~3.1e43 erg/s`; its off-nuclear location favors a core-collapse origin.
    - The host has `log(M_star/M_sun)=9.8 +/- 0.1`. A superluminous event on the rise and interaction-powered alternatives remain possible; the secure host redshift does not provide a spectroscopic transient classification.
  - **Position:** Demonstrates the reach of repeated blank-field JWST imaging for early UV-bright transients in the young Universe.
