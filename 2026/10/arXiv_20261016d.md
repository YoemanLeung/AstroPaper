## Key Findings

- **Lyalpha strength does not uniquely select primitive gas.** [2610.21232](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21232) Lena Pless finds large metallicity scatter at fixed equivalent width; combining stellar mass with line strength is more informative, within a small and strongly selected joint sample.
- **Early dust structure is clearer than its chemical identity.** [2610.20722](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20722) Giulia Rodighiero detects a UV bump at `z=9.3335`; its locally fitted wavelength offset is modest, and graphite versus aromatic carriers remain unresolved.
- **Cold gas origins remain degenerate even with detailed observations.** [2610.20704](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20704) Damir Gasymov supports external gas supply for a counter-rotating disk without directly detecting a filament stream; [2610.21532](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21532) J. A. Gray permits both molecular uplift and condensation after revising the gas-mass assumptions.
- **Environment measurements need calibrated tracers and membership.** [2610.21628](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21628) Sanskriti Das finds richness-dependent thermal pressure, while [2610.20919](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20919) I. Williams shows why statistically useful group catalogs can retain uncertain individual redshifts. Neither eliminates halo-mass and selection uncertainties.
- **Future cold-gas surveys require sensitivity across spatial scales.** [2610.21776](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21776) Kirsty M. Butler specifies halo-to-clump imaging requirements; [2610.20885](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20885) Manuel Aravena quantifies wide versus ultra-deep surveys. Both describe proposed capabilities, not achieved observations.

## Early-galaxy analogues, dust, and black-hole envelopes

- [2610.21232](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21232) Lena Pless et al. `Are High-EW LAEs good analogs for the earliest galaxies? A study on the ionizing conditions and gas properties of LAEs at 2<=z<=7`
  - **Question:** Does strong Lyman-alpha emission reliably identify the most metal-poor galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - MUSE-selected, HST-faint Lyman-alpha emitters (LAEs): 88 targets at 2<=z<=7, with NIRSpec spectroscopy and NIRCam imaging; 16 retain both reliable masses and metallicities.
  - **Method:**
    - Remeasure rest-frame equivalent widths (EWs) and fit stellar populations jointly with photometry and spectra.
    - Fit optical emission-line fluxes with BPASS+Cloudy grids; test parameter recovery and compare metallicity at fixed stellar mass.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - EW alone has large metallicity scatter: the linear test gives `r=-0.12, p=0.51`, while the rank test suggests a moderate trend (`rho=-0.56, p=0.015`). This is not evidence for a robust one-parameter metallicity selector.
    - High-EW systems tend to be under-enriched at fixed mass. Optical-only fits leave density and ionization degenerate; slit offsets and faintness strongly reduce the usable sample. Intermediate sample counts differ between Table 1 and the text; use the consistent 16-object joint sample for mass-metallicity conclusions.
  - **Position:** Tests the proposed reionization-analogue selection directly; stellar mass adds information beyond Lyalpha EW.

- [2610.20722](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20722) Giulia Rodighiero et al. `An Attenuation Bump at z=9.3: Probing Dust Diversity at Cosmic Dawn`
  - **Question:** What does a UV attenuation feature reveal about dust at cosmic dawn?
  - **Data:**
    - ZENITH/EMBER NIRSpec spectroscopy plus HST/NIRCam photometry of the Black Pearl at z=9.3335; PRISM integration 1.5 hr.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit a local power-law continuum plus Drude attenuation profile; test line contamination and wavelength windows.
    - Fit the full spectral energy distribution with a flexible attenuation law and two star-formation histories.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The local fit gives a bump at `2132+/-17 Angstrom`, width `413+/-54 Angstrom`, and peak attenuation `0.21+/-0.02 mag`.
    - The offset from 2175 Angstrom is only `~2.5 sigma` in that fit. Full-spectrum fits recover the bump with broader wavelength uncertainties that include the Galactic value. Small graphite grains and aromatic molecules remain viable; some attenuation and ionization parameters are weakly constrained.
  - **Position:** Extends direct UV-bump spectroscopy to z>9 without uniquely identifying the grain carrier or dust-production channel.

- [2610.20709](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20709) Zihao Wang et al. `How Little Red Dots Turn Blue: Evolution of Kelvin-Helmholtz Contracting Black Hole Envelopes and Observational Diagnostics`
  - **Question:** Can contracting black-hole envelopes connect little red dots to their blue counterparts?
  - **Data:**
    - Semi-analytic envelope models: 2,500 feedback/retention combinations compared with published broad-line spectra.
  - **Method:**
    - Evolve envelope mass and energy under declining gas supply, black-hole accretion, and feedback.
    - Post-process photospheric and stellar light; track UV and optical slopes.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The models predict a red UV excursion before the blue phase; transitional durations span roughly `5` to `>50 Myr`, depending on feedback and gas retention.
    - This is a testable evolutionary hypothesis, not a measured population sequence. Strong feedback can violate the quasi-hydrostatic photosphere assumption; fixed stellar histories and a single accretion episode limit abundance predictions.
  - **Position:** Provides color-space and lifetime predictions for black-hole-envelope interpretations of little red dots.


## Gas supply, halo environments, and feedback

- [2610.20704](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20704) Damir Gasymov et al. `CoRGI in MaNGA - II. NGC 5717: cold-gas accretion and disk growth in a BGG`
  - **Question:** Can an old group-central galaxy grow a young disk from externally supplied gas?
  - **Data:**
    - MaNGA spectroscopy, CFHT imaging, H I constraints, and SDSS/DESI group spectroscopy of NGC 5717.
  - **Method:**
    - Separate two stellar components in full-spectrum fits, excluding the degenerate central region.
    - Compare stellar populations and gas kinematics with a chemical-evolution model and the surrounding filament geometry.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The counter-rotating disk has age `1.35+/-0.16 Gyr`, `[Fe/H]=-0.67+/-0.09`, and `log(M_star/M_sun)=9.9+/-0.1`, favoring in-situ formation from external gas.
    - Filament accretion is a preferred interpretation, not a directly detected stream. The overparameterized chemical model admits multiple infall histories, and unusual gas-rich donors cannot be excluded.
  - **Position:** Shows that substantial disk regrowth can occur in a group-central system, while separating the acquired gas from its inferred origin.

- [2610.21628](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21628) Sanskriti Das et al. `Thermal Sunyaev-Zel'dovich Effect in the circumgalactic medium - III: does richness matter?`
  - **Question:** Does halo thermal pressure depend on the number of member galaxies as well as halo mass?
  - **Data:**
    - ACT DR4 plus Planck thermal Sunyaev-Zeldovich maps and about 7 million DESI Legacy Imaging Survey halos at z<=1, divided by mass and richness.
  - **Method:**
    - Stack with halo-mass/redshift uncertainty weights and bootstrap covariance.
    - Fit one-halo, two-halo, and background terms; compare pressure slopes and offsets before integrating thermal energy.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - For isolated galaxies the fitted thermal-energy relation is `7.3 sigma` shallower than self-similarity; group slopes remain consistent with self-similarity.
    - Some bins require minimum temperatures of `~2-7` times the virial temperature if the cosmic baryon fraction bounds the gas mass. These are conditional floors, not direct temperatures. Halo masses, unresolved profiles, richness selection, and dust correction limit feedback interpretation; thermal SZ alone does not determine gas density.
  - **Position:** Makes richness an explicit variable in circumgalactic thermodynamics and motivates matched samples across gas probes.

- [2610.20919](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20919) I. Williams et al. `Self Calibrated Galaxy Groups from the DESI DR1 Bright Galaxy Survey`
  - **Question:** Can a galaxy-group catalog be calibrated from observed clustering while handling missing spectra?
  - **Data:**
    - DESI DR1: 2,229,741 bright galaxies over 2680 square degrees; three-pass footprint, 0.001<z<0.5.
  - **Method:**
    - Calibrate group-finder parameters using projected clustering and satellite luminosities.
    - Assign missing redshifts using photometric estimates plus neighboring spectra; validate group statistics against highly complete SV3 fields.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Above `log(M_star/[M_sun h^-1])~10.3` and `log(M_halo/[M_sun h^-1])~12.2`, the central quenched fraction tracks halo mass more strongly than stellar mass.
    - About `20.7%` of the pre-final sample lacks reliable spectra; assigned redshifts are wrong roughly half the time. Statistical recovery does not make individual memberships secure. Edge groups, faint galaxies, high-redshift singletons, and unrelaxed clusters require additional caution.
  - **Position:** A large environment resource with explicit membership and halo-mass limitations for downstream analyses.

- [2610.21532](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21532) J. A. Gray et al. `A 13CO Detection in a Massive Molecular Gas Flow in Abell 1795's Central Galaxy`
  - **Question:** Does a lower molecular-gas mass make radio-bubble uplift energetically plausible in Abell 1795?
  - **Data:**
    - ALMA 13CO(2-1), 90 min on source, and re-imaged 12CO(2-1) in the northern molecular filament of the cluster-central galaxy.
  - **Method:**
    - Match beams and compare line emission in a common aperture.
    - Infer column density under local thermodynamic equilibrium while varying excitation temperature and filling factor; compare gas mass with bubble energy, buoyancy, and cooling rates.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - For the adopted assumptions, the filament mass spans roughly `(0.1-2)e9 M_sun` and the CO conversion factor `0.4-5 M_sun/(K km s^-1 pc^2)`. The Galactic factor remains allowed.
    - Direct uplift and in-situ condensation both become plausible at the low-mass end. Higher masses require multiple bubbles or additional cooling. Isotopic abundances, excitation, filling factors, and cavity geometry prevent a unique origin.
  - **Position:** Uses a rarer isotopologue to test the gas-mass assumptions behind feedback energetics.


## AGN demographics

- [2610.22115](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22115) Mugdha S. Polimera et al. `A Comprehensive AGN Inventory for the Dwarf-Dominated RESOLVE and ECO Surveys: Mass-Dependent AGN Types and Occupation Fractions`
  - **Question:** How do AGN detection channels and inferred incidence change across galaxy mass?
  - **Data:**
    - RESOLVE/ECO: 11,314 unique parent galaxies, including 8,352 in the baryonic-mass-limited analysis; SDSS lines and custom WISE photometry.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine complementary optical line-ratio categories and mid-infrared colors with consistent sample denominators.
    - Compare multiple emission-line catalogs and add a separately identified, incomplete archival AGN inventory.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Optical plus WISE AGN fractions are approximately `2-5%`, `16-27%`, and `20-48%` for dwarfs, transitional galaxies, and giants. Ranges include catalog systematics, not just counting errors.
    - The full-population denominator includes galaxies without usable AGN-search data. These are detected-activity fractions and lower bounds; they do not measure total black-hole occupation. Nonuniform X-ray coverage and line-selection biases remain substantial.
  - **Position:** Provides complementary AGN categories and a census framework for mass-dependent demographic comparisons.


## ALMA2040: forecasts and technical requirements

- [2610.21776](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21776) Kirsty M. Butler et al. `ALMA2040: The High-Redshift Circumgalactic Medium. Imaging the fueling and feedback of galaxies in the early universe`
  - **Question:** What observations could separate cold circumgalactic inflow, outflow, and recycled gas at high redshift?
  - **Data:**
    - ALMA2040 white paper using published lensed galaxies, extended [C II] halos, and CRISTAL stacks as benchmarks; no new population census.
  - **Method:**
    - Translate current surface-brightness and spatial-resolution limits into requirements for individual z~4-8 halos and clump-scale winds.
    - Combine emission-line cubes with absorption and multiple species to distinguish gas phases and flow directions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The case requires roughly `5-10x` better line sensitivity, diffuse-gas recovery beyond `10 kpc`, and compact-structure resolution near `100 pc`.
    - Longer baselines alone are insufficient: short spacings and total-power data are essential. Emission kinematics alone leave radial-flow direction ambiguous; baseline and surface-brightness trade studies remain necessary.
  - **Position:** A facility requirements case linking cold-gas imaging to the unresolved physical origin of halo emission.

- [2610.20885](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20885) Manuel Aravena et al. `Unveiling the Baryon Cycle Through Cosmic Time`
  - **Question:** How could wide-band molecular surveys measure the baryon cycle across galaxy populations?
  - **Data:**
    - ALMA2040 forecasts anchored to ASPECS line luminosity functions and an Arp 220-based spectral template.
  - **Method:**
    - Simulate multi-line spectra and forecast three survey tiers with assumed future sensitivity and spectral coverage.
    - Propagate line-excitation and luminosity-function assumptions to detection thresholds and sample yields.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - A proposed `~0.24 deg^2` mosaic reaches ASPECS-like line depth in `~90 hr` on source (`~180 hr` with assumed overheads); an ultra-deep tier covers `4-5 arcmin^2`.
    - These are forecasts. Yield uncertainties are estimated at `~30%` and larger for the ultra-deep tier, which extrapolates luminosity functions about one decade below current constraints; single-template spectra do not represent all galaxy populations.
  - **Position:** Connects a proposed sensitivity/bandwidth gain to explicit survey area, depth, and cosmic-variance trade-offs.
