## Key Findings

- Environmental SFR trends evolve with population mix. COSMOS2020 shows a mild `~0.1 dex` field-to-cluster increase at `2<z<4`, while low-z suppression weakens for star-forming-only samples. Photometric environments and SED uncertainties limit a causal interpretation. [2610.07710](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07710) Sina Taamoli.
- Cold-gas and metal-line selection can diverge. A `z=0.5305` 21-cm absorber lacks detectable Mg II, but the extreme-metal-poor interpretation requires the radio and optical sightlines to probe the same gas; the associated filament remains unconfirmed. [2610.07273](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07273) Abhisek Mohapatra.
- Baryon redistribution extends beyond halo boundaries. TNG, EAGLE, and SIMBA give different Mpc-scale recovery envelopes, while CIB-shear correlations constrain some low-SFR feedback variants. These complementary predictions depend on definitions and model calibration. [2610.07140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07140) Mohammadreza Ayromlou; [2610.08436](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08436) Sara Maleubre.
- Nebular diagnostics retain environmental and population dependence. [O III] equivalent-width calibrations require metallicity and pressure choices; PHANGS-MUSE suggests leaked, hardened radiation may also modify faint HII-region line ratios. The latter coupling remains tentative. [2610.08427](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08427) Dian P. Triani; [2610.07163](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07163) Dalya Baron.
- Rapid growth of individual early black holes does not establish a viable population. Chaotic accretion requires sustained feeding, SEEDZ merger histories change strongly with feedback, and halo-supply tests add occupation and duty-cycle constraints. [2610.07947](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07947) Kastytis Zubovas; [2610.07183](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07183) Daxal H. Mehta; [2610.08731](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08731) Elan Yogeswaren.

## Cosmic-web environments and gas beyond galaxies

- [2610.07710](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07710) Sina Taamoli et al. `The Role of the Cosmic Web in Galaxy Evolution Across 0.4<z<4`
  - **Question:** How does the galaxy-environment relation change across cosmic time?
  - **Data:**
    - COSMOS2020 Farmer/LePHARE photometry and density maps; mass-complete subsamples in four bins spanning `0.4<z<4`.
  - **Method:**
    - Apply the Multiscale Morphology Filter to density maps; use photometric Friends-of-Friends groups and classify the most massive member as central.
    - Compare stellar mass, SFR, and sSFR separately for all galaxies and star-forming subsets.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At `z<1`, central masses rise by up to `~0.35 dex` toward clusters; SFR suppression largely reflects the quiescent population.
    - At `1<z<2` trends weaken; at `2<z<4` median SFR rises by `~0.1 dex` toward clusters. Photo-z projection, classifier settings, SED errors, and changing mass completeness limit interpretation.
  - **Position:** Extends COSMOS cosmic-web classifications to higher redshift; topology-based trends do not independently isolate an environmental cause.

- [2610.07273](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07273) Abhisek Mohapatra et al. `Multiple H I 21-cm absorbers towards blazar PKS 1158+007`
  - **Question:** Can radio absorption reveal cold gas missed by metal-line selection?
  - **Data:**
    - MeerKAT L-band spectroscopy of PKS 1158+007 (`z=1.369`), with SDSS/DESI optical spectra and HSC/Legacy Survey photometric environments.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit HI absorption components; check polarizations, RFI, and off-source extractions.
    - Compare optical metal limits and stacked galaxy photo-z distributions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Two absorbers lie at `z=0.5305` and `0.5653`; the former has `W2796<0.12 Angstrom` at `3 sigma`.
    - The inferred `Z<~1.5e-3 Zsun` requires shared sightlines, a linear curve of growth, and the adopted HI conversion. A possible `~6 kpc` optical-radio offset offers an alternative. Broad photo-z excesses do not confirm a filament or either host.
  - **Position:** Exposes complementary radio/optical selection of neutral gas, with geometry as a central ambiguity.

- [2610.07068](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07068) S. Zarattini et al. `EMIR confirmation of the high-z Lyalpha blob SHARDS20018464`
  - **Question:** Does near-infrared spectroscopy support the high-redshift interpretation of a compact Lyalpha blob?
  - **Data:**
    - Four hours of GTC/EMIR K-band spectroscopy of SHARDS20018464; earlier imaging gives a `5-8 kpc` extent.
  - **Method:**
    - Reduce nodded spectra, extract and smooth the faint signal, and fit two Gaussian emission lines.
    - Check the proposed [O III] doublet flux ratio and calibration uncertainties.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The flux ratio is `2.92`, consistent with [O III]; the reported mean redshift is `3.59 +/- 0.01`, with no detected continuum.
    - Individual lines give `z=3.61` and `3.57`; guiding dominates the stated wavelength budget and flux calibration is uncertain by at least `~20%`. Treat the mean cautiously. Stellar photoionization is a supported scenario, not a unique mechanism; the larger environment remains unmeasured.
  - **Position:** Follows earlier SHARDS imaging with tentative faint-line spectroscopy; establishing a separate compact-blob population requires more objects and firmer calibration.

- [2610.07140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07140) Mohammadreza Ayromlou et al. `And Then There WEre Baryons (ATWEB): The Baryon Budget of the Universe in Halos and the IGM over 13 Billion Years of Cosmic Time`
  - **Question:** On what scales do baryons and total matter recover their reference distributions?
  - **Data:**
    - TNG100, EAGLE, and SIMBA hydrodynamic runs with dark-matter-only counterparts.
  - **Method:**
    - Count the global budget in FoF groups selected at `M200c>=1e10 Msun`; match halos and measure spherical mass profiles.
    - Define closure by recovery of cosmic baryon fraction and compensation by recovery of total mass; derive envelopes from smoothed, sufficiently populated mass bins.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At `z=0`, `72-83%` of baryons lie outside the selected FoF groups; the definition includes matter in smaller halos as IGM.
    - Closure envelopes are `2.4, 1.8, 6.4 Mpc` in TNG, EAGLE, SIMBA; compensation envelopes are `1.9, 1.5, 5.4 Mpc`. They are population statistics, not maximum ejection distances. Resolution, halo definitions, and feedback choices matter.
  - **Position:** Extends the closure-radius framework to total-matter compensation and power-spectrum recovery.

- [2610.08436](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08436) Sara Maleubre et al. `The impact of feedback and cosmology on Cosmic Infrared Background cross-correlations`
  - **Question:** Can CIB cross-correlations discriminate feedback prescriptions?
  - **Data:**
    - FLAMINGO `1 Gpc` boxes with feedback/cosmology variants; existing CIB tomography and Planck NPIPE x DES-Y3 shear measurements.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure bias-weighted SFR density and matter-SFR power; project into angular cross-spectra and fit an amplitude for each model.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Strong-stellar-feedback variants and the strong-jet `Jet fgas-4sigma` run underpredict small-scale CIB-shear power by `~20-30%`; the ordinary Jet run remains compatible.
    - Most calibrated models are similar, and current data weakly distinguish cosmology. Calibration adjusts several parameters; jet runs also change BH repositioning. IR-to-SFR conversion and neglected AGN dust heating add limitations.
  - **Position:** Adds clustering tests beyond low-redshift stellar-mass calibration without isolating a universal feedback law.

## Ionizing radiation and emission-line interpretation

- [2610.08427](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08427) Dian P. Triani et al. `The [O III] 5007 Equivalent Width as an Ionization-Parameter Diagnostic Across Cosmic Time`
  - **Question:** When can [O III] equivalent width replace O32 as an ionization diagnostic?
  - **Data:**
    - BPASS v2.2 plus MAPPINGS V grids; SDSS, EMPRESS, MOSDEF, and higher-redshift emission-line samples.
  - **Method:**
    - Vary age, metallicity, pressure, and ionization parameter for single-star and binary instantaneous bursts.
    - Fit pressure-specific calibration surfaces and compare with O32-derived ionization parameters; select low-pressure branches using [O III]/Hbeta.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Preferred `log(P/k)=6` local and `7` higher-z calibrations give median offsets `-0.09` and `+0.04 dex`, with `sigma68=0.28` and `0.18 dex`.
    - Calibration uses an age near `3.2 Myr`; age/SFH, metallicity, continuum, pressure, and double-valued low-pressure branches remain relevant. The O32 comparison is model based; blended high-z EWs provide only a qualitative check.
  - **Position:** Provides a useful restricted-band diagnostic, conditional on nebular and stellar-population assumptions.

- [2610.07163](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07163) Dalya Baron et al. `DIGging into HII regions: the role of ionizing photon leakage and spectral hardening in coupling HII regions and diffuse ionized gas`
  - **Question:** Can escaping stellar photons couple HII regions and diffuse ionized gas?
  - **Data:**
    - PHANGS-MUSE observations of 19 nearby galaxies at `50-100 pc`; HII catalogs and binned DIG maps, with line-S/N cuts.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare surface-brightness and line-ratio sequences; test projected mixing and coupled radiation propagation with CLOUDY spectral hardening.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Coupled solutions use HII-region escape fractions `0.4-0.6` and mean free paths `~0.5-1 kpc`, improving DIG brightness fits in 13 galaxies. These are local leakage fractions, not escape into the IGM.
    - Radiative coupling remains tentative: unresolved sizes, segmentation, possible DIG-clump misclassification, escape-fraction uncertainty, and simplified attenuation affect the inferred contribution.
  - **Position:** Extends DIG contamination work to possible nonlocal illumination of nebulae and its impact on line diagnostics.

## Black-hole growth, mergers, and feedback

- [2610.07624](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07624) Braden Seefeldt-Gail et al. `Radiation pressure powers quasar broad absorption line winds but fails to drive galaxy feedback`
  - **Question:** How much feedback power is carried by radiatively driven UV-absorbing winds?
  - **Data:**
    - SDSS-IV DR16: 81,388 quality-selected CIV troughs for line-locking analysis; an energetics sample of 116,428 troughs in 73,442 quasars.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare stacked troughs with KDE-based mocks and fit locking fractions.
    - Convert absorbed UV luminosity to momentum and kinetic power with global covering fraction `0.38` and single scattering.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Inferred locking fractions are `60-70%`; the median modeled `Lkin/Lbol~0.01%` lies below commonly adopted feedback efficiencies.
    - This constrains the sampled UV phase. Multiple scattering, absorption outside the measured window, or a faster hot phase could raise total power; stacking masks object diversity. The result does not exclude every AGN feedback channel.
  - **Position:** Connects line-locking diagnostics to a conditional wind-energy estimate; the broad title requires the supplementary caveats.

- [2610.07947](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07947) Kastytis Zubovas et al. `Chaotic accretion can (still) explain early supermassive black hole growth`
  - **Question:** Can stellar-mass seeds still explain extreme early black-hole masses?
  - **Data:**
    - Compilation of high-z BH mass estimates, separating virial measurements from UV- and X-ray-based estimates.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare exponential growth tracks with the observed upper mass envelope; connect chaotic accretion, low spin, radiative efficiency, and duty cycle.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - A `~32 Myr` e-folding time allows virial-mass objects to grow from seeds `<=100 Msun` formed near `z=30`; this requires nearly continuous feeding with low radiative efficiency.
    - The upper envelope is not observationally complete. Later seed formation, nonvirial mass estimates, obscuration, and insufficient gas supply can change the conclusion; the comparison does not measure an individual growth history.
  - **Position:** Preserves chaotic accretion as a conditional alternative to heavy seeds and super-Eddington growth.

- [2610.07183](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07183) Daxal H. Mehta et al. `Hierarchical Black Hole Mergers at High Redshift: Predictions for LISA and LGWA from SEEDZ`
  - **Question:** How does feedback alter hierarchical early black-hole assembly?
  - **Data:**
    - Six SEEDZ runs: Rarepeak and Normal1 regions, each with no, weak, or fiducial thermal BH feedback.
  - **Method:**
    - Follow sink growth, vorticity-modified accretion, dynamical friction, and mergers within `~5 pc`; compare assembly histories and synthetic GW detectability.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - No-feedback BHs can experience tens of mergers, whereas fiducial-feedback BHs have at most three in these runs. Local gas disruption prevents the early accretion growth that enables repeated merging.
    - No-feedback growth is an upper-limit experiment. Two regions, different end redshifts, unresolved final coalescence, and seeding/accretion prescriptions limit population and GW-rate forecasts.
  - **Position:** Shows that merger-driven growth and gas accretion are coupled through feedback, not independent seed diagnostics.

- [2610.08709](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08709) Pranav Satheesh et al. `Galaxy merger-driven signatures in massive black hole pair hosts across cosmic time`
  - **Question:** Do galaxy mergers reliably enhance accretion in black-hole pair hosts?
  - **Data:**
    - TNG50 and two BRAHMA boxes spanning overlapping and higher-redshift intervals; different BH seed and dynamics prescriptions.
  - **Method:**
    - Select post-mergers whose progenitors both host BHs; match non-merger controls in redshift and stellar mass, with additional BH-mass matching for BH properties.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Star formation is enhanced in all three runs; TNG50 gives `~0.21 dex` sBHAR enhancement, while BRAHMA has no significant enhancement despite elevated gas fractions.
    - The selected hosts need not contain a resolved or bound BH pair. Different seed masses, orbital treatments, host distributions, volumes, and resolution cuts prevent attribution to seeding alone.
  - **Position:** Qualifies the use of merger-triggered AGN activity as a proxy for close black-hole pairs.

- [2610.08731](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08731) Elan Yogeswaren et al. `Halo Mass Function Constraints on Early Galaxy and AGN Populations in the JWST Era`
  - **Question:** Are there enough halos to host inferred early galaxies and AGN?
  - **Data:**
    - Analytic halo populations in a Planck-like cosmology, compared with representative JWST mass, AGN, and UV-luminosity measurements.
  - **Method:**
    - Propagate the power spectrum into Sheth-Tormen abundances; compare an illustrative Klypin-corrected branch.
    - Map stellar conversion, BH occupation/duty cycle, and halo-to-light scatter into observable counts.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - A feasible individual mass does not guarantee sufficient host abundance; the rare-halo tail amplifies modest mapping and calibration changes.
    - The corrected branch is restricted to `z<=10`; beyond that it is uncalibrated extrapolation. This is a sensitivity framework, not a demonstration that JWST requires nonstandard cosmology.
  - **Position:** Complements seed-growth calculations with population-level host-supply consistency checks.
