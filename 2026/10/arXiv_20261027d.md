## Key Findings

- Detected early outflows do not by themselves explain distant metal absorbers: inferred propagation distances remain small under the adopted age and halo model. ([2610.33154](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33154) David DePalma.)
- Recent feedback can persist after quenching. The inner wind clock is much shorter than the time since the last starburst, so it cannot identify the cause of the original shutdown. ([2610.33176](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33176) Letizia Bugiani.)
- Dust-law shape can dominate statistical uncertainty in some strong-line metallicity estimates; the size of the bias depends on the diagnostic and galaxy. ([2610.33166](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33166) Gabriel Maheson.)
- A larger photometric LAE catalog need not be a cleaner one: source-level continuum estimates and selection stages require explicit comparison. ([2610.34646](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34646) Bailey Thompson.)
- Clumpy gas can reconcile Balmer diagnostics in little red dots, but this does not identify their central engines or uniquely establish the continuum origin. ([2610.34552](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34552) Seok-Jun Chang.)

## Early enrichment and gas physics

- [2610.33154](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33154) David DePalma et al. `A Composite Rest-Frame UV Spectrum of Galaxies at 5.7 < z < 6.9: Ionization, Outflows, and Implications for Intergalactic Metal Enrichment`
  - **Question:** Can the galaxies detected near early metal absorbers enrich the surrounding intergalactic medium?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRSpec G140M: 104 [O III]-selected galaxies at `5.7<z<6.9` in two EIGER quasar fields; stack-weighted `z=6.50` and `M_UV=-20.7`.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare independent MSAExp and PypeIt reductions; normalize and inverse-variance stack spectra in the [O III] systemic frame. Fit absorption profiles and propagate wind trajectories through evolving halo potentials using inferred stellar ages.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Low-ionization absorption has a mean velocity offset of `-166 +/- 49 km/s` in MSAExp. Launching winds at `200 km/s` gives typical radii of `3-9 proper kpc`, too small to explain many observed galaxy-absorber separations. Fainter clustered galaxies are a plausible enrichment source, not a directly detected population.
    - Limits: Bright emission-line selection and stacking weights limit representativeness. Wind distances depend on uncertain ages, halo masses and a gravity-only toy model; hydrodynamic interactions and recurrent launching are omitted. C III] can suffer second-order contamination.
  - **Position:** The Shapley+2003 composite comparison extends UV outflow diagnostics into reionization; absorber association alone does not establish which galaxy supplied the metals.

- [2610.33166](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33166) Gabriel Maheson et al. `How Steep is your Dust? Direct Measurements of Nebular Attenuation Curves at Cosmic Noon with JWST`
  - **Question:** How much does an assumed dust attenuation curve bias emission-line metallicities at cosmic noon?
  - **Data:**
    - Blue Jay JWST/NIRSpec: 24 star-forming galaxies at `1.8<z<3.3`, each with at least five hydrogen lines detected above `5 sigma`; matched-aperture imaging constrains stellar populations.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit stellar continua and line fluxes; compare hydrogen ratios with Case B predictions. Fit relative attenuation curves and propagate line and curve posteriors through strong-line metallicity calibrations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Most curves resemble familiar optical attenuation laws, yet S32, S3, S23 and S3O3 metallicities can differ by approximately `0.3 dex`, exceeding `5 sigma` in some cases. These are diagnostic-dependent shifts, not a universal metallicity correction.
    - Limits: Strong-line selection favors active galaxies; seven objects violating the adopted Case B line-ratio requirement were excluded. Curves measure relative attenuation, not absolute single-line corrections. Calibration extrapolation and sample differences limit generalization.
  - **Position:** Extends hydrogen-line attenuation measurements to individual cosmic-noon galaxies and exposes systematics missed by a precise Balmer decrement alone.

## Accretion and feedback

- [2610.33176](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33176) Letizia Bugiani et al. `Recently launched AGN-driven outflow in a lensed quiescent galaxy at Cosmic Noon`
  - **Question:** Can a low-luminosity active galactic nucleus sustain a wind after a massive galaxy has quenched?
  - **Data:**
    - INQUEST-JWST NIRSpec IFU and imaging of lensed MRG-M0150 at `z=2.64`; central magnification approximately `5` gives a source-plane PSF sigma of `235 pc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Reconstruct source-plane line maps, test ionization and gas-star kinematics, and fit a biconical wind with MOKA3D. Estimate ionized gas mass using an adopted electron density of `600 cm^-3`.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The wind reaches approximately `1000-1400 km/s`; H-alpha implies `0.6 +/- 0.2 solar masses/yr`. The innermost resolved gas launched within `0.45 Myr`, while the full extent requires activity over at least `2.6 Myr`. The current wind is much younger than the principal quenching episode.
    - Limits: Geometry, lens reconstruction and uniform density affect derived energetics; a 50% density systematic is assigned. Ionized gas traces only part of the wind. One object cannot determine the population duty cycle or exclude every fossil-outflow scenario.
  - **Position:** Provides spatially resolved evidence for ongoing feedback in a cosmic-noon quiescent galaxy; long-term maintenance remains an interpretation.

- [2610.33155](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33155) Aidan P. Cloonan et al. `El Ascua: An ultra-luminous, radio-loud, Compton-thick AGN at z~3.8 hiding in plain sight revealed by MINERVA multi-band MIRI observations`
  - **Question:** Can mid-infrared imaging reveal powerful black-hole growth missed by X-ray selection?
  - **Data:**
    - MINERVA/PRIMER NIRCam and MIRI imaging of el Ascua in UDS, with Chandra nondetection, ALMA 870-micron and VLA 1.4-GHz measurements; photometric `z=3.78`.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit galaxy and torus components with CIGALE. Compare X-ray limits with 6-micron luminosity and test the IR-to-radio relation.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The fitted accretion luminosity is `log(L_acc/[erg/s])=47.12 +/- 0.58`. Strong mid-IR emission with `log(L_2-10keV/[erg/s])<44.03` supports Compton-thick obscuration. Radio excess is consistent with an AGN contribution and possible radio loudness.
    - Limits: Photometric redshift, empirical X-ray/mid-IR relations, SED decomposition and missing far-IR coverage limit interpretation. Neither column density nor black-hole mass is directly measured.
  - **Position:** Extends the obscured accretion census; a pre-blowout stage remains hypothetical.

- [2610.34552](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34552) Seok-Jun Chang et al. `Multiphase Gas Structure in JWST Little Red Dots`
  - **Question:** Can one homogeneous gas component explain Balmer absorption and the Balmer break in little red dots?
  - **Data:**
    - Eleven little red dots with high-quality JWST/NIRSpec H-alpha and H-beta spectroscopy; nine show a Balmer break.
  - **Method:**
    - Use rt-scat Monte Carlo transfer for homogeneous and clumpy media. Fit H-alpha first, transfer its excited-hydrogen column posterior to H-beta, and test consistency with independently fitted continuum breaks.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Under the shared-gas assumption, the clumpy model passes the line-break consistency test for all `9` break-detected objects, versus `3` for the homogeneous model. Several H-beta profiles also favor clumpiness independently of the continuum-break interpretation.
    - Limits: The line-break test assumes a common gas origin. Host subtraction and unresolved cores affect fits. Collisions are neglected; density, clump sizes and origin remain unconstrained.
  - **Position:** Tests gas structure without selecting a central-engine model.

## Selection, catalogs and statistical methods

- [2610.34646](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34646) Bailey Thompson et al. `ODIN: Comparison of Source Extractor and Tractor Source Detection for Lyman Alpha Emitting Galaxy Selection at z=3.1`
  - **Question:** How does source detection and photometry change a narrow-band-selected Lyman-alpha emitter sample?
  - **Data:**
    - ODIN N501 imaging over `9 deg^2` in COSMOS, with HSC broad bands and CLAUDS u-band data, targeting `z=3.1` Lyman-alpha emitters (LAEs).
  - **Method:**
    - Compare Source Extractor aperture photometry with Tractor forward modeling on the same footprint. Track shared and unique detections, selection failures, equivalent widths and broadband color consistency.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The initial selections contain `5,354` Source Extractor and `12,521` Tractor candidates. A noise-aware Lyman-break consistency cut removes `501` and `2,616`, respectively. Continuum estimation and narrow-band excess drive much of the difference; spectroscopy is needed to establish purity and completeness.
    - Limits: Fluxes share the same images and are correlated, so normalized flux differences are not formal independent significances. Equivalent-width models and colors provide indirect contamination estimates; high-equivalent-width tails are not automatically confirmed extreme stellar populations.
  - **Position:** A useful selection-function audit for narrow-band surveys; the preference for Source Extractor applies to this ODIN implementation.

- [2610.35570](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35570) Rebecca L. Larson et al. `SPAM: Survey Overview and JWST/NIRCam Multi-Band Photometry Catalog`
  - **Question:** How much can medium-band imaging improve galaxy selection and redshifts in the CEERS field?
  - **Data:**
    - A `62.9 hr` JWST/NIRCam program adding nine medium bands and one broad band; the combined catalog contains `174,465` sources, with `4,557` confident spectroscopic redshifts for validation.
  - **Method:**
    - Uniformly reduce imaging, correct PSF and aperture effects, and combine hot/cold detection catalogs. Fit photometric redshifts with LAZY and compare fits with and without medium bands against vetted spectroscopy.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Medium bands reduce redshift degeneracies. The former apparent `z~16` CEERS candidate is recovered at photometric `z=4.86`, consistent with its established low-redshift spectroscopic identification, by separating emission lines from continuum.
    - Limits: Filter coverage and depth vary across the footprint; spectroscopic validation is selected and high-redshift statistics are sparse. PSF corrections assume limited morphology changes. Manuscript filter totals contain inconsistent bookkeeping, so no single total-filter count is adopted here.
  - **Position:** A public imaging and photometric-redshift resource for high-redshift galaxies and overdensity searches; the illustrated science cases are not all completed measurements.

- [2610.33603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33603) Zhaoting Chen et al. `Emission line stacking on cosmological scales: equivalence to two-point statistics and modelling of the observational effects`
  - **Question:** How should large-scale emission-line stacks be modeled when clustering and observational filtering matter?
  - **Data:**
    - Analytic estimator derivation and `100` lognormal HI/galaxy mock realizations representing `0.6<z<0.8`, with beam, thermal-noise and foreground-cleaning tests.
  - **Method:**
    - Express stacking as a quadratic two-point estimator and connect it to the cross-power spectrum. Propagate linear operations and aperture windows; compare direct stacks, transformed correlations and their covariances.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The mock estimators agree with the modeled signal within `1 sigma`; in the pure-signal test their variances agree to approximately `1%`. Foreground signal loss must be modeled. Stacking and the equivalent correlation measurement do not supply independent information.
    - Limits: Numerical tests use rectangular lognormal boxes and simplified beams and foregrounds; map-making and gridding compensation are omitted. This validates the formalism under tested assumptions, not a new observational HI measurement or end-to-end instrument calibration.
  - **Position:** Connects intuitive stacked spectra with clustering statistics and joint covariance modeling for line-intensity surveys.
