## Key Findings

- Absorber environments complicate the galaxy-gas connection: inferred HI reservoirs can overlap, and molecular-fraction trends remain limited by CO nondetections. [2610.10117](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10117) T. O'Beirne.
- Hot-corona onset need not wait for classical shock heating; comparisons at fixed physical radius reveal how virial boundaries change the apparent chronology. [2610.08931](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08931) Victor Rufo-Pastor.
- Quenching pathways separate in morphology and environment, but colour-selected transitions do not uniquely identify the quenching mechanism. [2610.08999](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08999) Euclid Collaboration.
- Early-quasar X-ray spectra remain steep across growth proxies. At the mean slope, soft-band penalties are modest; order-of-magnitude losses are not a generic survey forecast. [2610.10522](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10522) A. Tortosa.
- Narrow-line variability supplies evidence of changing ionisation, whereas correlations built from shared luminosities can mimic accretion-state signatures. [2610.10373](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10373) Wei-Jian Guo; [2610.10331](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10331) Wei-Jian Guo.

## Multiphase gas and the circumgalactic medium

- [2610.10112](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10112) Celine Peroux et al. `MUSE-ALMA Haloes XIV: The ALMA Large Programme Data Release`
  - **Question:** How can absorption-selected galaxies be connected to their molecular gas?
  - **Data:**
    - ALMA Cycle 10 targets 39 galaxies within a larger 79-galaxy MUSE/HST parent sample near HI absorbers.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine optical emission and absorption measurements with CO cubes.
    - Self-calibrate bright-quasar fields; deliver natural/Briggs cubes at `15` and `60 km/s`.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The release supplies resolved molecular-gas products for comparison with stars, ionised gas and neutral absorption.
    - The 39 targeted galaxies are not 79 CO detections; sensitivity depends on excitation and CO conversion assumptions.
  - **Position:** A reusable multiphase dataset; the companion kinematics and census studies supply distinct analyses.

- [2610.10117](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10117) T. O'Beirne et al. `MUSE-ALMA Haloes XVI: a census of the condensed baryons in z~0.5 HI absorption-selected galaxies`
  - **Question:** How do atomic and molecular reservoirs contribute to absorber-selected galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - MUSE, HST and ALMA measurements for 79 galaxies at `0.2<z<1.2`; 32 absorbers along 19 quasar sightlines.
  - **Method:**
    - Infer HI mass from optical colour and stellar mass; compare with absorber-based estimates.
    - Fit gas fractions including CO limits; estimate enclosed dynamical mass within `2 R_e`.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Molecular-to-stellar mass declines more steeply with stellar mass than atomic-to-stellar mass; the trend remains tentative.
    - HI masses are indirect, their estimator already contains stellar mass, and overlapping group gas complicates absorber assignment. Inner baryon fractions depend on aperture.
  - **Position:** Connects absorber environments to the condensed baryon budget without treating HI absorption as a direct total-mass measurement.

- [2610.10113](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10113) C. Barfety et al. `MUSE-ALMA Haloes XV: Cold molecular, ionised, and stellar kinematics of HI-rich galaxies at z ~ 0.5`
  - **Question:** What sustains disk turbulence at intermediate redshift?
  - **Data:**
    - ALMA CO, MUSE Hbeta and stellar absorption, and HST morphology for six HI-associated galaxies at `0.43<z<0.83`.
  - **Method:**
    - Classify morpho-kinematics; jointly forward-model CO and Hbeta with DysmalPy for four disks.
    - Compare with feedback/transport models and pPXF stellar kinematics.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The four disks have mean dark-matter fraction `0.42 +/- 0.14` within the effective radius.
    - Ionised gas is tentatively hotter dynamically than molecular gas. Feedback plus transport fits better under the adopted turbulence assumptions; resolution and model choices limit this inference.
  - **Position:** Extends matched multi-tracer disk studies into the transition between local disks and cosmic-noon systems.

- [2610.08931](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08931) Victor Rufo-Pastor et al. `Early onset of the hot circumgalactic medium around Milky Way galaxies`
  - **Question:** Can stellar feedback establish a hot CGM before accretion shocks alone?
  - **Data:**
    - Six VINTERGATAN zoom simulations, including three Milky-Way halo assembly histories and feedback-free comparisons.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare temperatures and cooling/free-fall ratios in feedback and no-feedback runs.
    - Contrast virial-scaled shells with fixed physical apertures.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Feedback establishes hot gas near the virial radius by `z~3`, earlier than the feedback-free comparison.
    - Apparent late evolution partly reflects changing virial boundaries. Results depend on feedback treatment and omit AGN, cosmic rays and magnetic fields.
  - **Position:** Separates hot-corona formation, thermodynamic virialisation and aperture-driven pseudo-evolution.

## Quenching and galaxy-formation models

- [2610.08999](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08999) Euclid Collaboration et al. `Euclid Quick Data Release (Q1). Exploring the complexity of quenching processes across time, environment, and mass through recently quenched galaxies`
  - **Question:** How do fast and slow quenching pathways vary with mass and environment?
  - **Data:**
    - Euclid Q1 photometry at `0.2<z<3`, morphology, and external-redshift spectral stacks; environment analysis extends only to `z~1.5`.
  - **Method:**
    - Adapt UVJ boundaries to Euclid filters using model star-formation histories.
    - Compare population fractions, structure, fifth-neighbour density and stacked spectra.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Fast-quenching populations retain disk-like structure; slow-quenching populations are more concentrated. Dense-environment tails become evident above `z~0.5`.
    - Rejuvenation, photometric-redshift errors and model-dependent UVJ residence times complicate pathway inference; specific feedback or stripping mechanisms are not directly identified.
  - **Position:** Expands colour-based transition-population analysis to Euclid scale, with spectroscopic population checks.

- [2610.09564](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09564) Michael Bradley et al. `SAGE26 Paper I: Modelling the baryon cycle from cosmic dawn to the present day`
  - **Question:** Can revised baryon-cycle prescriptions reproduce galaxy populations across cosmic time?
  - **Data:**
    - SAGE26 on Millennium and miniUchuu merger trees, compared with stellar/molecular mass functions and other observations.
  - **Method:**
    - Separate CGM and hot-halo inflow; add molecular star formation and evolving stellar feedback.
    - Calibrate parameters and implement a halo-mass/redshift threshold for feedback-free bursts.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Burst prescriptions increase massive-galaxy abundances by `0.21-0.65 dex` at `z>6` relative to the model without those bursts.
    - Agreement does not uniquely establish that mechanism; calibration degeneracy, imposed thresholds and sparse high-redshift outputs remain important.
  - **Position:** A transparent extension of SAGE16 for testing baryon-cycle assumptions against multiple observables.

## AGN growth, spectra and time-dependent ionisation

- [2610.10522](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10522) A. Tortosa et al. `Widespread steep X-ray spectra of luminous z~5.85-7.5 quasars and their implications for future X-ray surveys`
  - **Question:** Are steep reionization-era quasar X-ray spectra confined to the fastest-grown black holes?
  - **Data:**
    - XMM-Newton/Chandra data for 28 luminous quasars at `z~5.85-7.56`; 26 enter correlations, with homogenised mass estimates including JWST.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit power-law and cutoff spectra; propagate measurement errors in correlation tests.
    - Simulate spectral-shape effects at fixed intrinsic 2-10 keV luminosity.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Typical photon index is `Gamma~2.4`; no significant dependence on the integrated growth proxy is recovered.
    - For that mean slope, soft-band count reductions are only about `10-20%`; severe losses concern steeper spectra or harder bands. Faint-AGN extrapolation and survey yield remain untested.
  - **Position:** Broadens HYPERION-based tests while separating count-rate effects from full survey forecasts.

- [2610.10373](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10373) Wei-Jian Guo et al. `A Decade-Scale Ionization Echo of Black-Hole Accretion in Galactic Nuclei`
  - **Question:** Can compact narrow-line gas respond to nuclear brightening over decades?
  - **Data:**
    - Repeat SDSS/DESI spectra of 473 variability-selected nuclei, plus WISE/NEOWISE light curves.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit stellar continua and emission lines uniformly; select factor-of-two [OIII] increases.
    - Compare differential line changes with photoionisation grids and infrared excess energy.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Nineteen objects qualify; stacked [OIII] increases by `229 +/- 22%`, while lower-ionisation forbidden lines respond much less.
    - For 13 bounded infrared events, excess energy correlates with [OIII] luminosity change (`r=0.87`). Two spectra constrain timing but do not measure a reverberation radius or prove a unique trigger.
  - **Position:** Adds a compact, variable narrow-line component to time-domain studies of nuclear activity.

- [2610.10331](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10331) Wei-Jian Guo et al. `Changing-look Active Galactic Nuclei from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument. VII. Testing Disk-Corona Diagnostics with eROSITA`
  - **Question:** Does an alpha_OX-Eddington-ratio V-shape independently diagnose accretion states?
  - **Data:**
    - DESI changing-look AGN and eRASS-matched controls; the principal control analysis uses `0.35<z<0.45`.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare optical- and X-ray-based Eddington ratios and stratify by black-hole mass.
    - Test multivariate relations and catalogue-band spectral slopes.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The same 1,200 broad-Halpha controls switch correlation sign when the luminosity estimator changes. Shared observables can generate apparent branches.
    - Non-simultaneity, detection selection and absorption/soft-excess effects prevent the weak hardness trend from establishing a full accretion-state sequence.
  - **Position:** A methodological control for interpreting disk-corona correlations; it does not disprove every physical turnover.

- [2610.10244](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10244) Marko Micic et al. `A Gravitationally Lensed Low-Luminosity AGN From The Cosmic Noon`
  - **Question:** Is a triply lensed faint X-ray source a low-luminosity AGN?
  - **Data:**
    - HST imaging, 121 ks Chandra exposure and published cluster lens maps for SDSS J2243-0935.
  - **Method:**
    - Infer geometric redshift by back-projecting the three images.
    - Jointly fit the sparse X-ray spectrum and correct luminosity for lensing.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At lens-model redshift `z=2.14(+0.12/-0.17)`, intrinsic luminosity is `L_X=1.2(+0.3/-0.2) x 10^42 erg/s`; AGN emission is favoured.
    - Only one robust HST band constrains the host, leaving dust, stellar mass and SFR highly uncertain; redshift lacks spectroscopic confirmation.
  - **Position:** A lens-assisted probe of faint cosmic-noon accretion whose host interpretation needs better photometry and spectroscopy.
