## Key Findings

- Quasar emission requires an unresolved dense gas contribution. [2610.01454](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01454) Andrea Travascio constrains it with HeII/Halpha, while [2610.01400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01400) Titouan Lazeyras finds simulation PDFs too narrow at fixed median density. The inferred clumping is conditional on the PDF and emission weighting.
- Environmental tracers remain selective. [2610.01136](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01136) Canze Deng finds threshold-dependent SMG associations, and [2610.01816](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01816) Jun Toshikawa finds distinct protocluster Lyalpha distributions despite a field-like pooled sample. A mean relation can hide substantial diversity.
- Absolute metallicity and metallicity at fixed mass answer different questions. [2610.01811](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01811) C. Moya-Sierralta finds an elevated LAE stack but a lower mass-metallicity locus; neither comparison directly measures pristine inflow.
- Gas structure changes feedback coupling on very different scales. [2610.00477](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00477) K. Hervella-Seoane finds efficient cluster shock heating during a modeled active phase; [2610.01983](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01983) Ivan Almeida finds enhanced entrainment in pc-scale clumpy gas. Their efficiencies and timescales are not interchangeable.
- Observational systematics can exceed nominal precision. [2610.00463](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00463) Ouldouz Kaboud loses depth when using coarse FIR cirrus templates, while [2610.01541](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01541) Divya Rana finds stellar-mass forecasts sensitive to the assumed inner dark-matter profile.

## Quasar CGM density and line emission

- [2610.01454](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01454) Andrea Travascio et al. `Constraining the density distribution of the cold Circumgalactic Medium of quasars at z>2 through Lya, HeII and Ha emission`
  - **Question:** What density structure produces weak HeII relative to Halpha around luminous quasars?
  - **Data:**
    - KCWI observations of 13 quasars at `z~2.2` with targeted MOSFIRE follow-up, plus 26 deep MUSE quasar fields at `3.1<z<4.5`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure lines in matched apertures and randomized spectral stacks; compare with CLOUDY single-density and truncated lognormal cloud models.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Within selected slit regions out to `35 pkpc`, stacked `HeII/Halpha=0.54 (+0.32/-0.21)` and `Lyalpha/Halpha=9.05 (+5.30/-3.24)` favor recombination from an unresolved dense contribution.
    - For the fiducial EUV slope and median densities `10^-3-10^-1 cm^-3`, models matching the median ratio have finite-grid `C~700-4000`. This is not a unique halo-wide clumping measurement: PDF cutoff, illumination, and bright-region selection matter.
  - **Position:** Extends non-resonant CGM diagnostics from individual nebulae to a sample with matched hydrogen and helium measurements.

- [2610.01400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01400) Titouan Lazeyras et al. `Resolving the physics of Quasar Lyalpha Nebulae (RePhyNe): II. The dense and clumpy CGM of Quasars at z ~ 3.5`
  - **Question:** Do simulated quasar halos reproduce the distribution of bright circumgalactic emission?
  - **Data:**
    - DaLya zoom simulations at two resolutions, COLIBRE m5/m6 and HELLO, compared with MUSE quasar nebulae at `z~3.8`.
  - **Method:**
    - Construct maximal-fluorescence mock maps and compare pixel brightness distributions, cold-gas density PDFs and predicted HeII/Lyalpha ratios.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Most runs underproduce the covering fraction of bright emission; COLIBRE m5 is an exception. The brightness comparison requires gas reaching at least `10 cm^-3` within `50-200 ckpc` under the adopted emission model.
    - At fixed median density, the line-ratio constraint requires clumping factors at least `8` times those of the simulated PDFs. Resolution, self-shielding and cooling are coupled; no unique missing mechanism is established.
  - **Position:** Shows why a mean radial brightness profile can conceal failures in the spatial distribution of emitting gas.

## Protoclusters, dusty galaxies, and chemical enrichment

- [2610.01136](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01136) Canze Deng et al. `Do Submillimeter Galaxies Trace Megaparsec Large-scale Structures? -- An Overdensity Analysis of 449 Submillimeter Galaxies in COSMOS`
  - **Question:** How reliably do submillimeter galaxies identify megaparsec-scale overdensities?
  - **Data:**
    - 449 A3COSMOS SMGs, including 168 spectroscopic redshifts and 52 CO/[CII] redshifts, with COSMOS2025 galaxies and matched main-sequence controls.
  - **Method:**
    - Apply the Poisson Probability Method in five association categories; compare mass/redshift-matched controls and inspect morphology and redshift-selection effects.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Across increasingly permissive criteria, association fractions span `11.5-71.2%` for the CO/[CII] subset and about `1.3-61%` for all SMGs. An SMG is therefore a probabilistic signpost, not a guaranteed protocluster member.
    - The main catalog discussion reports `26` new robust candidates, while the final summary paragraph says `31`; this internal inconsistency needs clarification. Reported excess significances use the scatter of five control resamplings and should not be read as fully calibrated Gaussian discovery significances.
  - **Position:** Provides a broad environmental catalog while making redshift quality and tracer selection central to interpretation.

- [2610.01816](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01816) Jun Toshikawa et al. `Diverse Protoclusters at z~3 Disclosed by Systematic Spectroscopy`
  - **Question:** Can comparable selection reveal diversity among protoclusters at cosmic noon?
  - **Data:**
    - HSC-SSP/CLAUDS U-dropout overdensities, Subaru/FOCAS spectroscopy of 341 targets and multiband SEDs in three fields.
  - **Method:**
    - Test redshift peaks against randomized selection functions; compare Lyalpha EW at fixed UV luminosity and fit stellar populations with CIGALE.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Structures at `z~3.05, 3.07, 3.09` contain at least `6, 9, 10` confirmed members. ID5 has enhanced Lyalpha EW, whereas the pooled protocluster sample is field-consistent.
    - Different assembly stages or HI transmission could explain the diversity, but HI is not directly measured. Sparse Lyalpha-selected spectroscopy and incomplete spatial coverage limit quenching and descendant-mass claims.
  - **Position:** Connects systematic overdensity selection to the field-to-field variation hidden by mean environmental trends.

- [2610.01811](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01811) C. Moya-Sierralta et al. `Galaxy Protoclusters as Drivers of Cosmic Reionization: II. Te-Based Metallicities of Lyman-alpha Emitters`
  - **Question:** Are LAEs in a reionization-era protocluster chemically distinct from field galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Six LAEs with usable JWST/NIRSpec G395H coverage in LAGER-z7OD1 at `z~6.93`; NIRCam and UltraVISTA photometry.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure [OIII]4363 temperatures and O+/O++ abundances at adopted `n_e=300 cm^-3`; propagate Balmer-decrement uncertainty and fit BEAGLE stellar masses.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Five auroral detections span `12+log(O/H)~7.2-8.0`; a five-object stack gives `7.76 (+0.06/-0.05)`, about `0.26 dex` above one comparison LAE stack.
    - The sample lies below several mass-metallicity baselines, but a small LAE-selected sample, uncertain masses and different diagnostics prevent a unique environmental interpretation. Pristine inflow is a proposed explanation, not a measured gas flow.
  - **Position:** Adds direct-method chemical constraints in a known early overdensity without establishing a universal enrichment offset.

## AGN feedback across scales

- [2610.00477](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00477) K. Hervella-Seoane et al. `The impact of relativistic AGN jets on realistic galaxy cluster environments`
  - **Question:** How efficiently do intermediate-power relativistic jets heat cluster gas?
  - **Data:**
    - Six three-dimensional relativistic-hydrodynamic runs through four GADGET-3 cluster environments, with `10^44-10^45 erg/s` jets active for `20 Myr`.
  - **Method:**
    - Track shocked ambient and jet components separately, including bremsstrahlung cooling, and close the injected-energy budget.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - About `80%` of injected energy becomes ambient internal energy during the simulated phase; cooling losses approach `10%` by its end.
    - This is conditional on the injection setup and a hydrostatic, non-turbulent ICM without magnetic fields. It does not establish long-term cooling-flow regulation across the full jet population.
  - **Position:** Extends active-phase relativistic shock-heating tests to intermediate-power FRI-like jets.

- [2610.01983](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01983) Ivan Almeida et al. `The Impact of a Clumpy Ambient Medium on the Dynamics and Synchrotron Emission of AGN Jets`
  - **Question:** Does small-scale gas structure change jet entrainment beyond a simple increase in ambient mass?
  - **Data:**
    - PLUTO relativistic-MHD models of a `Lorentz factor=10` jet in homogeneous and clumpy pc-scale media, with cloud filling factors `0.1%` and `1%`.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare equal jet lengths and matched ambient masses; classify entrainment by velocity/magnetization and compute synchrotron emission from accelerated electrons.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At comparable `11-12 pc` lengths, entrained masses are `0.32, 4.2, 8.8 M_sun` for the homogeneous and two clumpy models. Equal-total-mass homogeneous controls remain much less entrained.
    - Morphology and synchrotron brightening are not unique signatures of clumps; precession can resemble them. Missing radiative cooling and unresolved interfaces limit quantitative entrainment predictions.
  - **Position:** Separates a controlled gas-geometry effect from total ambient mass in very young AGN jets.

## Measurement limits and numerical convergence

- [2610.00463](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00463) Ouldouz Kaboud et al. `LIGHTS. Limitations of Far-Infrared Tracers for Galactic Cirrus Mitigation in the Era of Ultra-Deep Imaging`
  - **Question:** Can existing far-infrared maps clean cirrus from ultra-deep optical images?
  - **Data:**
    - LBT/LIGHTS g/r imaging of NGC 3953 and Herschel/SPIRE `250 micron` imaging with an `18.1 arcsec` beam.
  - **Method:**
    - Remove scattered starlight, model the optical/FIR relation with intrinsic scatter, and subtract a scaled cirrus template.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - A roughly linear optical/FIR relation requires about `20%` intrinsic scatter. FIR-based cleaning yields a practical `mu_V~28 mag/arcsec^2` limit in this field despite substantially deeper optical exposures.
    - Coarse resolution and source confusion drive the loss of depth. The coefficients and limiting depth are method- and field-dependent, not universal limits on low-surface-brightness astronomy.
  - **Position:** A concrete foreground-control test for interpreting faint halos and tidal structures.

- [2610.01541](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01541) Divya Rana et al. `Weighing Galaxies Inside-Out: Small-Scale Lensing and the Stellar Mass Problem`
  - **Question:** Can Euclid-like weak lensing distinguish stellar mass-to-light normalizations?
  - **Data:**
    - Flagship-based DESI-like BGS lens mocks over `9000 deg^2`, Euclid-like background galaxies and image simulations.
  - **Method:**
    - Subtract foreground lens light with GALFIT; fit stellar plus NFW mass profiles over `10-100 h^-1 kpc`, with an inner cut tied to lens size.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The forecast full-sample stellar mismatch precision is `8.5%`; lens-light subtraction reduces the tested bias by about an order of magnitude.
    - A `20%` change in assumed inner halo slope shifts the stellar mismatch parameter by `25%` in a sensitivity test. Idealized blending and a fixed NFW profile prevent treating the forecast as achieved IMF accuracy.
  - **Position:** A complementary stellar-mass probe whose precision depends on mass decomposition and realistic image validation.

- [2610.00513](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00513) Shivan Khullar et al. `Star Formation Mitosis: The Effects of Particle Splitting on the Star Formation Outcome`
  - **Question:** Does adaptive particle splitting introduce artificial fragmentation or change star formation?
  - **Data:**
    - About 220 GIZMO cloud-collapse simulations with varied cloud mass, resolution, feedback and sink/stellar-population prescriptions.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare splitting runs with uniformly high-resolution controls and independent turbulent random seeds.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - No artificial fragmentation is found in the tests, and total stellar masses remain similar. Individual mass spectra and histories still differ, generally within the variation across turbulent seeds.
    - This validates the tested cloud problems, not arbitrary refinement schemes; the `0.1-10 M_sun` intermediate mass-resolution regime remains difficult for either star-formation prescription.
  - **Position:** A useful numerical control for interpreting resolved star-formation predictions.

- [2610.00578](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00578) Yasmine J. Meziani et al. `The Impact of Simulation Resolution on Dwarf Galaxy Stellar Stream Populations in FIRE`
  - **Question:** How much of the simulated stellar-stream population depends on numerical resolution?
  - **Data:**
    - Two FIRE-2 m12i runs sharing initial conditions, with initial baryonic particle masses `7070` and `880 M_sun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify systems above 100 star particles; classify debris and match progenitors across resolutions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The higher-resolution run contains `22` streams versus `14`, but above a common stellar-mass threshold the comparison is `16` versus `14`.
    - Matched objects on similar orbits can survive longer at higher resolution. This single-host comparison also changes orbital histories and completeness, so raw count differences do not isolate disruption physics.
  - **Position:** Demonstrates why convergence tests need both matched objects and matched physical selection thresholds.
