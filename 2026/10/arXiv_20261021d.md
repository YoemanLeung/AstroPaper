## Key Findings

- The inferred abundance of UV-faint [C II] emitters at z~7 depends on how a UV-selected sample is mapped onto the population. The stellar-mass-based luminosity function exceeds the UV-based estimate by about 0.5 dex near log(L_[CII]/Lsun)=8.5; the difference is conditional on scaling relations and the input mass function. [2610.27052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27052) Thomas Herard-Demanche.
- Massive dusty galaxies need not already contain compact stellar cores. Leviathan has a half-mass radius of 3.1 +/- 0.4 kpc at z=4.051; continued star formation in its present distribution would require additional structural change to produce a compact quiescent descendant. [2610.27101](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27101) William M. Baker.
- Morphology is not a direct formation-channel label. JADES requires an extra contribution beyond major mergers for many massive clumpy systems, while FIREbox HR shows that projected shapes can mislead when intrinsic-distribution assumptions or triaxiality constraints are inadequate. [2610.28515](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28515) David Puskas; [2610.26969](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26969) Haojie Geng.
- Warm gas is detected in 13/17 low-richness group centrals. Filamentary systems often combine disturbed motions with cooling-to-eddy timescale ratios near 1-2, supporting condensation without uniquely excluding mergers or recycling. [2610.27169](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27169) V. Olivares.
- A successful quenched mass function does not uniquely validate an AGN feedback prescription. In a toy model, steep quenching with black-hole scatter around 0.6-0.9 dex and gentler quenching with smaller scatter can yield similar slopes. [2610.27061](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27061) Antonio J. Porras-Valverde.

## Dusty galaxy growth and the reionization-era census

- [2610.27052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27052) Thomas Herard-Demanche et al. `The ALMA REBELS Survey: The [C II] Luminosity Function of UV-Selected Galaxies at z~7`
  - **Question:** How much of the z~7 [C II] population is missed by bright UV selection?
  - **Data:**
    - ALMA REBELS and pilots: 27 >4-sigma [C II] detections from 42 line-search targets; detected redshifts `z=6.50-7.68`.
    - ALPINE: lower-redshift detections and upper limits, combined with published UV luminosity and stellar mass functions.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit UV-[C II] and mass-[C II] relations with measurement errors, scatter, and ALPINE upper limits; propagate these into luminosity functions.
    - Compare with a stepwise estimate normalized through UV completeness and accessible line-detection volumes.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The mass-based estimate is about `0.5 dex` higher near `log(L_[CII]/Lsun)=8.5`; the inferred missing fraction is conditional, not a blind-survey count.
    - Integrating the fiducial function above `log(L_[CII]/Lsun)=8.1` gives `log(rho_SFR/[Msun yr^-1 Mpc^-3])=-2.23 (+0.35/-0.21)`, about `63%` of the adopted cosmic reference; this is not the obscured fraction.
    - Excluded REBELS non-detections, uncertain faint-end slope, scaling relations, mass-function systematics, and cosmic variance limit the census.
  - **Position:** Extends ALPINE luminosity-function work into reionization and motivates blind line surveys and intensity-mapping constraints.

- [2610.27101](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27101) William M. Baker et al. `Leviathan: a very massive, extended sub-millimetre galaxy spectroscopically confirmed at z = 4.05`
  - **Question:** Can a very massive dusty galaxy at z>4 assemble without a compact stellar core?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST RUBIES spectroscopy, PRIMER/MINERVA imaging, and Herschel/SCUBA-2/ALMA photometry: 31 bands; `z=4.0514 +/- 0.0017`.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit integrated and spatially binned spectral energy distributions with Prospector; compare stellar light, stellar mass, and dust radii.
    - Use spectroscopy for the redshift prior: the slit samples only `16%` of F444W light.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Stellar mass is `log(Mstar/Msun)=11.34 (+0.15/-0.22)` and 100-Myr-averaged star formation is `410 (+130/-90) Msun/yr`.
    - Half-light, half-mass, and dust radii are approximately `4.3`, `3.1 +/- 0.4`, and `2.2-2.5 kpc`; the galaxy is extended relative to compact quiescent systems.
    - A compact component below the `~1.5 kpc` matched resolution remains possible; dust-temperature gradients and SED assumptions affect interpretation. Its eventual group/cluster role is unresolved.
  - **Position:** Contrasts with the much more compact GN20.2b at similar redshift and mass; demonstrates diversity in dusty progenitor structures.

## What high-redshift morphology can establish

- [2610.28515](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28515) David Puskas et al. `The clumpy fraction of galaxies at z=1-9 with JADES: evidence for a mass-dependent transition in clump origin`
  - **Question:** How do clumpy galaxy fractions evolve with mass, redshift, and wavelength?
  - **Data:**
    - JADES NIRCam: 24,064 galaxies at `z=1-9`, `log(Mstar/Msun)>=8`, with redshift, mass, and imaging-quality cuts.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify off-centre intensity peaks with dendrograms at rest-frame `0.2`, `0.4`, and `0.55 micron`.
    - Use injected clumps to set bin-dependent 50%-completeness limits; compare a separate probability-weighted statistic and major-merger expectations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Blue/optical clumpy fractions fall from `~55%` at `z=1-2` to `~5%` at `z=8-9`; the UV fraction is `~12%` in the highest bin.
    - At `z=1-2`, blue/optical fractions rise from `~35%` in the lowest mass bin to `~85%` above `10^10 Msun`; massive systems often exceed major-merger predictions.
    - Injection recovery does not measure purity. Weighting detected clumps cannot restore galaxies with entirely missed clumps; highest-redshift optical coverage is partial, and formation-channel inference depends on observability timescales.
  - **Position:** Builds on Guo+2015 clump selection and JADES merger-rate work; supports mixed origins rather than assigning origins to individual peaks.

- [2610.26969](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26969) Haojie Geng et al. `Inferring the shapes of low-mass galaxies at z>6 from FIREbox HR. Where are the disks?`
  - **Question:** When can projected JWST sizes and axis ratios recover intrinsic dwarf-galaxy shapes?
  - **Data:**
    - FIREbox HR mock images at `z=6.3-15` and `log(Mstar/Msun)=6.5-9.5`; stellar particles supply comparison shapes.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit point-spread-function-convolved Sersic profiles and compare with robust three-dimensional stellar-particle shape estimates.
    - Model logit-transformed ellipticity/triaxiality and log size jointly, allowing all correlations; test informative priors and sample size.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The simulated population is mainly prolate/triaxial with a spheroidal component; a Gaussian model in untransformed shape variables can bias recovery.
    - Triaxiality is weakly constrained for `N<=1000`; fully free correlations need much larger samples, of order `10000` in the tests.
    - Noise is deliberately suppressed, the point-spread function is known, mass and light shapes differ, and the small simulation volume limits the highest-redshift inference; this does not establish a universal absence of disks.
  - **Position:** Tests the assumptions behind Zhang+2019 and Pandya+2024 shape inference using a controlled simulated population.

## Gas supply, black-hole pairs, and quenching

- [2610.27169](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27169) V. Olivares et al. `A MUSE Study of Stellar and Ionized-Gas Kinematics in Central Group Galaxies`
  - **Question:** Which gas-supply channels operate in low-richness group-dominant galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - MUSE: 17 observed members of the 27-object low-richness CLoGS subsample; complementary X-ray and radio measurements.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit stellar continua and emission-line kinematics; compare gas morphology and stellar/gas position angles.
    - Combine cooling/eddy timescale estimates with integrated line shifts and dispersions as a diagnostic of ordered versus disordered motion.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Warm gas appears in `13/17` systems: nine ordered disk/ring-like systems and four filamentary systems with disturbed motions.
    - Most filamentary systems approach `t_cool/t_eddy~1-2`, except NGC 5903, consistent with cooling-driven condensation.
    - Integrated velocity cancellation, interaction signatures, and approximate turbulence scales prevent a unique gas-origin assignment; alignment alone cannot establish internal recycling.
  - **Position:** Extends earlier CLoGS work to lower-richness groups and tests the chaotic cold accretion picture alongside external gas acquisition.

- [2610.27086](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27086) M. V. Zanchettin et al. `Dual and lensed AGN at sub-arcsecond separations uncovered by ERIS`
  - **Question:** How reliably can Gaia-selected sub-arcsecond multiples reveal dual active galactic nuclei?
  - **Data:**
    - VLT ERIS-SPIFFIER adaptive-optics spectroscopy of 34 Gaia Multi-Peak candidates, plus optical spectra and photometry.
  - **Method:**
    - Decompose each spectral channel with shared-shape Moffat profiles to reduce component cross-contamination.
    - Compare emission profiles, redshifts, attenuation, and image geometry to classify dual nuclei, lenses, and stellar projections.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Six systems are classified as dual AGN and seven as lensed quasars; 18 are AGN-plus-star projections, one is single, and two have unclassified secondaries.
    - Dual systems span `z=0.68-1.57` and projected separations `2.2-6.7 kpc`; quoted `290-950 km/s` offsets are broad-line shifts, not precise systemic differences.
    - The sample favors bright unobscured sources and has observing/guide-star constraints; these yields are not population dual-AGN fractions.
  - **Position:** Extends the Mannucci+2022 Gaia Multi-Peak approach with spatially resolved spectroscopy separating pairs from lenses.

- [2610.27061](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27061) Antonio J. Porras-Valverde et al. `How does a galaxy's quenching probability depend on its black hole's mass?`
  - **Question:** Can the quenched stellar mass function identify the black-hole quenching law?
  - **Data:**
    - Parametric central-galaxy evolution experiments compared with observed mass-function slopes and published simulation/semi-analytic populations.
  - **Method:**
    - Grow galaxies along a star-forming sequence, assign scattered black-hole masses, and vary the power-law mass dependence of quenching probability.
    - Measure quenched mass-function slopes across the scatter/quenching grid.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Large black-hole scatter washes out differences between quenching laws; steep laws with scatter `~0.6-0.9 dex` can resemble gradual laws with less scatter.
    - Joint scatter and mass-function constraints are needed. The toy model assumes fixed per-galaxy offsets, uncorrelated scatters, and instantaneous irreversible quenching; it does not identify a causal feedback mode.
  - **Position:** Extends the Peng mass-quenching framework and cautions against validating feedback physics with a mass-function match alone.

- [2610.27076](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27076) Christopher Tiede et al. `Balmer breaks in accreting super-massive black hole binaries`
  - **Question:** Could a truncated circumbinary disk generate a Balmer break useful for binary searches?
  - **Data:**
    - Analytic disk and population models; illustrative LSST and Roman time-domain detectability windows, with no new binary detections.
  - **Method:**
    - Model gas pile-up at the cavity edge and local hydrogen excitation/ionization; estimate the spectral edge with a simplified atmosphere.
    - Weight candidate binary parameter space by residence time and an assumed mass distribution.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Favorable systems have total mass `>=~10^6 Msun` and periods of months to years; predicted low-redshift periodic-source fractions with strong breaks are a few to `~10%` for assumed feeding rates.
    - The opacity diagnostic is not an emergent flux ratio. Non-equilibrium radiative transfer, vertical structure, magnetic fields, and the persistence of non-accreting phases remain untested.
    - Little red dots share some spectral features but their weak variability and prominent broad lines complicate this explanation.
  - **Position:** Offers a complementary spectroscopic test of compact-binary candidates; the population fractions remain conditional forecasts.
