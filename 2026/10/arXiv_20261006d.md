## Key Findings

- Molecular gas can occur far into a galaxy halo: 19 H2 absorption transitions are detected at a projected 139 kpc from a starbursting LIRG. Wind-CGM cooling is plausible, but a single sight line, unresolved components, and saturated Lyalpha prevent a unique formation history. [2610.06662](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06662) Brad Koplitz.
- Internal radiation can help assemble heavy-seed progenitors after initial fragmentation. Three runs of one nearly primordial halo develop atomic-cooling collapse under external `J21=1-100`; subsequent growth to approximately `10^5 Msun` and black-hole formation remain conditional on continued gas supply. [2610.03877](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03877) Masaki Kiyuna.
- High-redshift clump demographics depend on the spatial decomposition and selection function. Compact-clump mass functions and giant-clump composition together favor a low-mass break, while neither resolves whether giant clumps are long-lived structures or blended associations. [2610.03866](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03866) Boris S. Kalita; [2610.03867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03867) Boris S. Kalita.
- Quiescence is not a unique gas state or an irreversible event. Simba permits CO conversion biases, obscured star formation, and rejuvenation in different selections; TNG-Cluster predicts repeated BCG rejuvenation before lasting suppression. These are model-dependent, complementary results rather than a universal evolutionary sequence. [2610.06680](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06680) E. Savitch; [2610.06634](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06634) Sunna Gottschewski.
- Broad lines need component-aware interpretation. In the Toothbrush cluster, separated bulk flows yield much smaller dispersions than a single-component fit; QSO broad-wing differences likewise require geometry and selection checks before conversion to feedback power. [2610.05351](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05351) Anjali Madangarli; [2610.03925](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03925) Gabriel R. Hauschild-Roier.

## Molecular gas, dust, and quenching

- [2610.06662](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06662) Brad Koplitz et al. `Forming Molecular Hydrogen in a Galaxy Halo Through Star Formation Wind Interactions`
  - **Question:** Can wind-CGM interactions produce molecular gas far from a star-forming disk?
  - **Data:**
    - HST/COS spectra of QSO J0057+1446 probe the minor-axis halo of a starbursting LIRG at projected `139 kpc`; Hectochelle and archival optical data constrain absorption and neighboring galaxies.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit Voigt profiles to H2, HI, and metals; fit rotational excitation and test ionization models.
    - Compare transport and formation scenarios, including a simplified nonequilibrium cooling network.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The 19 H2 transitions span `J=0-3`, with `log[N(H2)/cm^-2]=16.52 +/- 0.05` and a single-temperature fit of `245 +25/-21 K`.
    - Wind-CGM cooling can reproduce the molecular fraction, but the calculation is a feasibility example. Saturated Lyalpha allows strongly different HI columns, and COS does not resolve the likely multiple clouds; the inferred molecular fraction is correspondingly uncertain.
  - **Position:** Extends molecular-absorption studies into the outer CGM without establishing how common in situ molecule formation is.

- [2610.06680](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06680) E. Savitch et al. `Unveiling the Origins of the Molecular Gas Reservoirs in Recently Quenched Massive Galaxies`
  - **Question:** Why can apparently quenched galaxies retain substantial inferred molecular reservoirs?
  - **Data:**
    - Simba cosmological simulation: galaxies with `Mstar>3e9 Msun`, examined through their histories and synthetic emission.
  - **Method:**
    - Track descendants and rejuvenation using star-formation thresholds.
    - Model CO emission with SLICK/DESPOTIC; compare simulated gas masses with a fixed Milky Way conversion.
    - Test UBV-selected systems using synthetic infrared and FUV star-formation indicators.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Rejuvenation preferentially involves gas-rich transitioning systems. A Milky Way CO conversion can overestimate gas masses when the modeled conversion factor is lower.
    - Dust-rich color-selected systems can hide ongoing star formation. The mechanisms use different selections and need not explain the same objects; their relative importance depends on Simba's subgrid physics and emission modeling.
  - **Position:** Places rejuvenation and two observational biases in one simulation framework, complementing EAGLE and FIREbox post-starburst studies.

- [2610.06639](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06639) Chayan Mondal et al. `PAH emission from star-forming and quiescent galaxies at z=0.5-0.8 probed by JWST/MIRI`
  - **Question:** How do PAH emission and abundance change with galaxy mass, ionization, and quiescence?
  - **Data:**
    - GOODS-South: 224 galaxies at `z=0.5-0.8`, with JWST/MIRI, NIRCam, HST, UVIT, and Herschel photometry; 73 have the MUSE lines used for O32.
  - **Method:**
    - Construct an F1280W-to-continuum PAH diagnostic and fit 38-band SEDs with CIGALE.
    - Compare against O32 and metallicities inferred from the mass-SFR-metallicity relation.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - PAH strength rises with stellar mass and declines with O32; the latter has Spearman `rho=-0.79` in the line-selected subset.
    - A transition near `12+log(O/H)~8.45` is calibration dependent, not a direct metallicity threshold measurement. Several dust parameters are fixed in the SED fits; weaker excitation and grain destruction both remain possible for quiescent galaxies.
  - **Position:** Connects intermediate-redshift PAH diagnostics to local and higher-redshift dust studies while exposing limitations of PAH-based population comparisons.

## Early black-hole seeds and resolved high-redshift structure

- [2610.03877](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03877) Masaki Kiyuna et al. `Internal Radiative Feedback Triggers Global Collapse and Direct-Collapse Black Hole Formation in Metal-free Atomic-Cooling Haloes`
  - **Question:** Can internal stellar radiation enable massive seed progenitors without an extreme external FUV field?
  - **Data:**
    - Three radiation-hydrodynamic realizations of one atomic-cooling halo with external `J21=1, 10, 100`; star formation starts near `z~13` and `Mhalo~1.5e8 Msun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Follow GADGET-3 gas dynamics, nonequilibrium chemistry, FUV/EUV transfer, and sink growth for approximately `1-2 Myr`.
    - Compare initial molecular fragmentation with the subsequent atomic-cooling collapse; extrapolate later stellar growth separately.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Internal FUV suppresses further molecular cooling and helps a warm reservoir collapse, feeding protostars at roughly `0.1-1 Msun/yr` and reaching masses of order `10^4 Msun` during the runs.
    - Final growth to `~10^5 Msun`, collapse to black holes, and the connection to Little Red Dots are conditional. The runs fix an effectively primordial metallicity and omit the later supernova, enrichment, and black-hole feedback needed to test survival of the reservoir.
  - **Position:** Tests a less restrictive irradiation route to heavy-seed progenitors; the abundance estimate is an extrapolation, not a simulated population census.

- [2610.03866](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03866) Boris S. Kalita et al. `SCALES. I. Bridging Lensed Clump - Giant Clump Scales in Lensed Galaxies at z=1-4`
  - **Question:** How does separating compact and extended light change inferred clump demographics?
  - **Data:**
    - UNCOVER/MEGASCIENCE NIRCam imaging of 23 Abell 2744-lensed galaxies at `z=1-4`; 18 have spectroscopic redshifts and five use photometric redshifts.
  - **Method:**
    - Apply difference-of-Gaussian decomposition, simultaneous PSF-convolved component fitting, and 13-band BAGPIPES SED fits.
    - Propagate lensing uncertainties and test detection with injection-recovery experiments.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The analysis identifies 599 compact clumps and 146 extended components. Above the fitted mass-function break near `10^7.38 Msun`, the compact-clump slope is `2.19 +/- 0.06`.
    - Redshift trends weaken in a high-detectability subset. F070W selection misses some old or dusty clumps; host selection favors clump-rich systems, and the extended component is a scale-separated background rather than an independently established physical object.
  - **Position:** Provides an explicit bridge between lensed compact-clump measurements and lower-resolution giant-clump surveys.

- [2610.03867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03867) Boris S. Kalita et al. `SCALES. II. The Internal Composition of Giant Star-Forming Clumps at z=1-4`
  - **Question:** Does the stellar mass distribution inside giant clumps require special constituent populations?
  - **Data:**
    - The same 23 lensed galaxies, with 44 giant clumps assembled by associating compact clumps and extended components.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare dominant and remaining clump masses with 2,000 within-host mass reshufflings.
    - Redistribute the available low-mass and extended-component mass to test continuation of a slope-two mass function.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Observed mass partitions are consistent with random sampling of the host clump population. The available mass budget cannot restore an unbroken slope of approximately two below `~10^7.4 Msun` within the tested construction.
    - Association geometry and incompleteness matter, and a compact clump can enter more than one association. The result does not choose between long-lived giant structures and transient or blended complexes.
  - **Position:** Supplies a constituent-mass constraint for competing giant-clump models rather than a lifetime or dynamical-binding measurement.

## Cluster assembly and satellite populations

- [2610.06634](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06634) Sunna Gottschewski et al. `The star-forming past and quenching of brightest cluster galaxies in TNG-Cluster`
  - **Question:** When do BCGs quench, and how is that history linked to black-hole feedback?
  - **Data:**
    - TNG-Cluster: 352 present-day BCGs, with galaxy properties measured within `30 physical kpc` and progenitor histories traced backward.
  - **Method:**
    - Classify quenching and rejuvenation from sSFR histories; compare cumulative kinetic feedback energy with BCG binding energy.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At `z=0`, 329 BCGs are quenched and 23 are star forming; the population averages approximately 2.1 rejuvenations.
    - First and final quenching occur at characteristic lookback times of `9.9` and `7.0 Gyr`, with broad distributions. First quenching follows initial kinetic injection by about `2.3 Gyr` and occurs when cumulative injected energy is comparable to binding energy.
    - These thresholds and times describe TNG's feedback model and aperture definitions, not an observationally calibrated universal quenching clock.
  - **Position:** Extends the TNG kinetic-feedback picture to the assembly histories of the most massive central galaxies.

- [2610.06660](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06660) Frazer R. Pearce et al. `Disentangling the assembly of the central and diffuse stellar components in galaxy clusters through dynamical friction and tidal stripping`
  - **Question:** Can assembly histories define BCG and intracluster-light populations more physically than an aperture alone?
  - **Data:**
    - Three Hundred cluster simulations: retrospective tracking in 107 clusters and a progenitor-fate classification applied to 261 clusters; 45 higher-resolution counterparts provide a comparison.
  - **Method:**
    - Track exclusive stellar memberships through merger trees and classify central delivery versus stripping using progenitor histories.
    - Compare spatial and velocity distributions with conventional aperture selections.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Typically only `3-5` infall events contribute half the final BCG stellar mass. Many accreted galaxies instead survive or supply diffuse light.
    - BCG and ICL stars remain spatially interleaved despite different assembly histories. Particle thresholds, progenitor apertures, and subgrid modeling affect the split; the higher-resolution comparison also changes the galaxy-formation implementation.
  - **Position:** Offers a dynamical interpretation of the BCG-ICL division without requiring a sharp observable radial boundary.

- [2610.03981](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03981) Lucas S. Mandacaru Guerra et al. `The Merian Survey: A Statistical Census of Bright Satellites of Sub-Milky Way-Mass Galaxies`
  - **Question:** How does bright satellite abundance vary across the sub-Milky Way host-mass regime?
  - **Data:**
    - Merian medium-band imaging and HSC photometry: 357 central hosts with `10^9.5<Mstar/Msun<10^10.5` at `z=0.07-0.09`, with star-forming satellite candidates above approximately `10^7.5 Msun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Select emission-line candidates, inspect blends, and estimate local projected contamination; compare abundance and scaled radial profiles with TNG50 and other surveys.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The 471 projected candidates yield a background-subtracted population of `148 +/- 7`. Satellite abundance increases with host stellar mass.
    - Membership is statistical, selection favors star-forming satellites, and inner-radius incompleteness complicates comparisons with simulations and total satellite populations.
  - **Position:** Fills the host-mass interval between dwarf and Milky Way satellite censuses using emission-line selection.

## Interpreting gas motions and feedback

- [2610.03925](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03925) Gabriel R. Hauschild-Roier et al. `The differences in the Narrow Line Region of nearby QSOs 1 and QSOs 2: II - Signatures of kinetic feedback`
  - **Question:** Do type 1 and type 2 QSOs have different narrow-line-region outflow signatures?
  - **Data:**
    - SDSS DR16 spectra: 1,541 type 1 and 688 type 2 QSOs at `z=0.4-0.5`; luminosity cuts define two comparison subsamples.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit narrow and broad emission-line components with PyQSOFit and compare nonparametric velocity widths, blueshifts, and approximate outflow indicators.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Narrow components are similar, while type 1 broad components show stronger blueshifts; broad dispersions are approximately `30%` larger in the high-luminosity subset but similar in the low-luminosity subset.
    - The SDSS aperture integrates roughly `16-19 kpc`, leaving the NLR unresolved. Orientation, sample selection, and physical assumptions in the outflow conversion prevent a unique evolutionary interpretation or precise absolute kinetic powers.
  - **Position:** Couples integrated NLR kinematics to the companion excitation analysis as a statistical test of obscuration and feedback geometry.

- [2610.05351](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05351) Anjali Madangarli et al. `A Brush with Turbulence: Probing the ICM Dynamics in the Merging Toothbrush Cluster using XRISM`
  - **Question:** Does the disturbed Toothbrush ICM owe its broad lines mainly to turbulence or superposed bulk flows?
  - **Data:**
    - XRISM/Resolve: `166.7 ks` in the southern X-ray-bright region, analyzed over the full array and four quadrants.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit single- and multiple-component thermal models in `3-8 keV`, including spatial-spectral mixing and PSF uncertainty tests.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - A two-component full-array model is favored by `Delta AIC=8`, with bulk separation `1635 +165/-110 km/s` and component dispersions around `245-310 km/s`; a single component gives about `750 km/s`.
    - Inferred turbulent pressure fractions are approximately `3-6%` under isotropy. They exclude coherent bulk flows and may miss large-scale, low-emissivity motions; they do not measure the total nonthermal energy budget.
  - **Position:** Demonstrates how velocity-component separation changes merger-ICM energetics and cautions against interpreting all line broadening as turbulence.
