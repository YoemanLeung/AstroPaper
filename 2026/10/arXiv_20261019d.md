## Key Findings

- Radio active galactic nuclei (AGN) show stronger clustering than star-forming galaxies in a population-mixture model. The cosmic microwave background (CMB) lensing cross-signal is detected at `4.3 sigma` by a fitted-amplitude test, but the bias evolution law remains unresolved. [2610.23567](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23567) Joel Hamlett.
- Little red dots (LRDs) with representative bias `b~3-8` can occupy massive halos; randomly activated, dynamically negligible Poisson seeds instead predict `b~1`. If seed activity follows massive hosts, clustering cannot distinguish primordial from astrophysical seeds. [2610.23806](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23806) Gert Hutsi.
- For simulated `z~10-15` galaxies with `M_UV~-20`, favored dust prescriptions predict only `0.5-1 microJy` at the observed wavelength corresponding to rest-frame `88 microns`. Existing `3-sigma` limits of `10-20 microJy` therefore leave substantial room for dust; UV-brighter targets are more promising. [2610.22766](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22766) Yurina Nakazato.
- Two spectroscopically confirmed `z~14` galaxies are consistent with survey-specific extreme-value predictions. Some photometric candidates reach `~4 sigma` conditional on redshift and emission model; star formation history shifts predictions by nearly `1 dex` at very high redshift. [2610.23016](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23016) Christopher C. Lovell.
- In COSMOS, the mean star formation rate relative to the main sequence decreases with X-ray power at `1.0<z<1.8`; the lower-redshift positive trend is weak. The infrared-selected sample has no fitted mean shift, but the body discusses a width trend, so a blanket null result is inappropriate. [2610.23349](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23349) Dustin T. Mason.

## Clustering, environments, and the galaxy-matter connection

- [2610.23567](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23567) Joel Hamlett et al. `Galaxy bias of radio AGN and star-forming galaxies from MIGHTEE and CMB lensing`
  - **Question:** Can radio clustering and CMB lensing separate AGN and star-forming galaxy environments without classifying every source?
  - **Data:**
    - MeerKAT MIGHTEE DR1 over approximately `20 deg^2`, with `S_1.4GHz>40 microJy`, and ACT DR6 CMB lensing; Spitzer colors define broad low- and high-redshift subsets.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit the radio-lensing cross-spectrum and two radio auto-spectra, including masks, completeness, redshift distributions, and shot noise.
    - Infer the evolving population mixture from radio luminosity functions; compare bias laws with and without an external star-forming-galaxy prior.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - For `b(z)=b0/D(z)`, where `D(z)` is linear matter growth, the AGN normalization is `2.60(+0.65/-0.91)` and the star-forming normalization `0.52(+0.47/-0.34)` without that prior; with it they become `1.96(+0.37/-0.40)` and `0.90+/-0.06` (Table 4; central 68% intervals).
    - The cross-signal is `4.3 sigma` from the fitted-amplitude likelihood comparison, or about `3.5 sigma` from the null chi-square tail. Bias-law evidence is inconclusive; flux selection, redshift representativeness, and the assumed mixture limit environmental interpretation.
  - **Position:** Separates population clustering statistically; luminosity selection complicates environmental evolution.

- [2610.23806](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23806) Gert Hutsi et al. `Clustering of JWST LRDs. Implications for PBHs and other early-forming seeds`
  - **Question:** Does LRD abundance and clustering identify the origin of their black-hole seeds?
  - **Data:**
    - Published JWST LRD abundance and clustering at `z=4-8`, summarized as number density `~10^-5-10^-4 h^3 Mpc^-3` and bias `~3-8`; these are heterogeneous literature ranges, not a new precision measurement.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine a minimum halo mass and duty cycle with the Sheth-Mo-Tormen mass function and bias; compare with matter and seed power spectra for a subdominant Poisson primordial-black-hole population.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Halo thresholds `~10^10.5-10^12 h^-1 Msun` accommodate the adopted bias range; the duty cycle adjusts abundance. Here `h=H0/(100 km s^-1 Mpc^-1)`.
    - Randomly activated seeds predict shot-noise-subtracted `b~1`. Host-dependent activation restores halo-like clustering and removes this seed-origin discriminator. Conflicting clustering estimates and selection differences preclude treating the adopted range as established redshift evolution.
  - **Position:** Tests activation and host occupation; seed origin remains degenerate when halos govern activity.

- [2610.23828](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23828) Kimiko Queen et al. `Revisiting the Mass and Scale Dependence of the "Lensing is Low" Effect with DECADE`
  - **Question:** Does the lensing deficit around massive galaxies persist equally on small and large scales?
  - **Data:**
    - BOSS LOWZ lenses at `0.2<=z<=0.35`, divided into three mass bins using three catalogs; DECADE source shapes replace SDSS.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure excess surface density with source-redshift distributions, shear corrections, random subtraction, and jackknife covariance.
    - Reuse the earlier lensing predictions and fit observed-to-predicted amplitude against logarithmic separation, excluding `r_p<0.25 h^-1 Mpc`.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - All nine overlapping sample definitions favor increasing amplitude ratio with scale, with slopes up to `~0.14` and individual significances `1.3-3.8 sigma`.
    - Small-scale deficits persist; large-scale agreement improves but remains imperfect. The samples overlap; neither a feedback mechanism nor cosmological tension is established.
  - **Position:** Revisits the Lange et al. comparison with updated source data.


## Dust and extreme galaxies at cosmic dawn

- [2610.22766](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22766) Yurina Nakazato et al. `Dust in Galaxies at z=10-15: Attenuation and Re-emission Predictions from the FirstLight Simulations`
  - **Question:** Which dust prescriptions jointly explain ultraviolet attenuation and weak far-infrared emission in the earliest galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - FirstLight: `208` snapshots of `99` galaxies at approximately `z=10-15` (selection starts at `z>=9.5`), spanning stellar masses `10^6.5-10^9.5 Msun`; only two snapshots lie near `z=14` and one near `z=15`.
  - **Method:**
    - Post-process with three-dimensional SKIRT radiative transfer, stellar and nebular emission, four dust-to-metal prescriptions, and three grain distributions: `2496` calculations.
    - Compare ultraviolet slopes, attenuation curves, dust masses, and predicted continuum with JWST and ALMA constraints.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Metallicity-dependent dust abundance with Small Magellanic Cloud-like grains best spans the compared constraints; a fixed dust-to-metal ratio `0.4` overpredicts many dust-mass limits.
    - Predicted peak-temperature proxies reach `60-80 K` in starbursts. Typical `M_UV~-20` sources remain faint; current continuum depths favor intrinsic `M_UV<-21.5` targets. Temperature is a spectral-peak proxy, not a unique physical dust temperature.
    - Fixed grain distributions, prescribed dust growth, and sparse highest-redshift sampling limit generalization; flatter attenuation need not imply larger grains.
  - **Position:** Connects UV and infrared predictions to bright-target follow-up strategies.

- [2610.23016](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23016) Christopher C. Lovell et al. `How bright can a galaxy be? Extreme value predictions for high-redshift survey observables`
  - **Question:** How exceptional are bright high-redshift candidates when tested in observed flux rather than inferred stellar mass?
  - **Data:**
    - Published COSMOS-Web, MIDIS/NGDEEP, ASTRODEEP-JWST, and multi-sightline samples, compared using their own survey areas and photometric bands.
  - **Method:**
    - Map halo extreme-value distributions through a stellar-conversion model and Synthesizer emission models; vary star formation history, stellar populations, metallicity, and initial mass function.
    - Compute upper-tail probabilities at each published redshift; photometric-redshift posteriors are not marginalized.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The two confirmed `z~14` sources have exceedance probabilities `0.88` and `0.48`. Some photometric candidates reach `~4 sigma` under fiducial assumptions, motivating redshift confirmation rather than a cosmological rejection.
    - COSMOS-Web candidates lie at least `1 dex` below the fiducial `+3-sigma` bound, but the faintest explored star formation history brings the brightest candidate to that bound. Typical burstiness shifts the predicted maximum by `<~0.1 dex`; stronger assumed variability changes it more.
    - The most massive halo is the baseline host of the brightest galaxy; a mass-independent stellar fraction and fixed-redshift treatment limit the test.
  - **Position:** Extends extreme-value tests into flux space with explicit emission assumptions.


## AGN fuel, star formation, and molecular-gas experiments

- [2610.23349](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23349) Dustin T. Mason et al. `Comparing Infrared & X-ray-selected Active Galactic Nuclei host galaxy star formation rates to the galaxy main sequence`
  - **Question:** Does AGN power change the distribution of host star formation relative to the main sequence in the same way for X-ray and infrared selection?
  - **Data:**
    - COSMOS2020/Chandra/Spitzer and super-deblended far-infrared data: `323` X-ray AGN at `0.8<z<1.2`, `498` at `1.0<z<1.8`, and `246` infrared AGN in the latter interval.
    - Star formation upper limits comprise approximately `74%`, `64%`, and `52%`, respectively; the redshift intervals overlap.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit AGN-aware spectral energy distributions and the log distribution of `R_MS=SFR/SFR_main-sequence` with detections and censored likelihood terms.
    - Vary mean and width with AGN power, converting infrared to X-ray luminosity where needed; test selection with mock spectra.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The higher-redshift X-ray mean slope is `-0.35+/-0.12` per dex in X-ray power (Table 4). The lower-redshift positive slope is much weaker; neither determines feedback causality.
    - The infrared mean is fitted as constant, but Section 4.2.2 discusses a narrowing distribution. The abstract's blanket null, model labels, and Table 3's non-normalized higher-redshift X-ray probabilities are internally inconsistent; exact model odds should not be reused without clarification.
    - Infrared results remain sensitive to upper-limit-dominated low-power bins and the luminosity conversion; obscuration is not directly isolated as the cause.
  - **Position:** Compares censored distributions; selection and manuscript inconsistencies limit quenching claims.

- [2610.22449](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22449) Ilaria Ruffa et al. `Resolving the AGN Feeding and Feedback Cycle with ALMA2040`
  - **Question:** What observing capability would turn resolved AGN molecular-gas case studies into a statistical feeding and feedback census?
  - **Data:**
    - An ALMA2040 white paper using published nearby systems and a GHOST simulation of the NGC 3100 CO(3-2) model moved to `z=0.05`.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare matched `2 h` current-ALMA and illustrative `255 x 12-m` compact-array observations at similar resolution.
    - Specify multi-line diagnostics, longer baselines, and diffuse-emission recovery.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Simulated channel noise falls from `0.65` to `0.11 mJy/beam`: about `5.9x` fixed-time sensitivity or `35x` thermal-noise observing speed. The `~400 pc` beam demonstrates disc-scale recovery, not resolved `50 pc` clouds.
    - The broader `~10x` sensitivity and `~9 mas` at `230 GHz` are design requirements. The illustrative `300` targets in `600 h` excludes overheads and is not a complete multi-line survey forecast.
  - **Position:** Quantifies a molecular-gas experiment separately from proposed instrument requirements.

- [2610.22860](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22860) Pedro Naethe Motta et al. `GASTRO-BH: Effects of a Central Black Hole in GSE-like Dwarf-galaxy Mergers Around Milky Way-like Hosts`
  - **Question:** Can a Milky Way-like merger fuel rather than starve a dwarf galaxy's central black hole?
  - **Data:**
    - ChaNGa dwarf/host merger and isolated simulations with low/high stellar feedback; seeds are `10^5 Msun` in merger runs and `8 x 10^3 Msun` in higher-resolution isolated runs.
  - **Method:**
    - Follow gas, star formation, and angular-momentum-aware Bondi-Hoyle accretion; compare orbital passages and black-hole-free controls.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Merger black holes grow by factors `135` and `110`, with post-pericenter accretion followed by suppressed star formation.
    - Feedback coupling was tuned for sustained growth; seed mass, resolution, and unresolved accretion matter. Jets and kinetic winds are absent. This is a possible fueling channel, not an omega Centauri reconstruction.
  - **Position:** Tests merger-driven fueling in dwarf companions with calibration-dependent growth.
