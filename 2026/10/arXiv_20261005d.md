## Key Findings

- Group X-ray luminosity functions shift toward brighter characteristic luminosities across redshift, but the simplified completeness model leaves intrinsic population evolution uncertain. The AmicOne diffuse-emission search remains a non-detection. [2610.02470](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02470) Ghassem Gozaliasl.
- AGN subtraction can enable more useful host photometry than a single smooth profile for clumpy galaxies. The 66-object SED subset, PSF degeneracies and underestimated profile errors matter when using the inferred host properties. [2610.02465](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02465) Callum Dewsnap.
- The 123 SFACT Green Pea candidates extend emission-line analog work toward fainter luminosities. Their secure redshifts do not remove residual AGN contamination or supply reliable masses, sSFRs or ionizing escape fractions. [2610.03572](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03572) Kristin N. Baker.
- Dwarf interactions show diverse color/SFR responses, while most selected compact dwarf groups are compatible with binding under assumed halos. Neither result supplies a universal merger clock: separation trends and halo-dependent decay estimates carry different limitations. [2610.02583](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02583) Marko Micic; [2610.02587](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02587) Marko Micic.
- Resolved gas observations expose geometric ambiguity: HI absorption samples only illuminated foreground gas in TXS 1033+026, and the Antennae shows how superposed velocity structures can mislead coarse maps. [2610.03300](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03300) J. N. H. S. Aditya; [2610.03346](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03346) Akhil Lasrado.

## Group environments and hierarchical assembly

- [2610.02470](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02470) Ghassem Gozaliasl et al. `X-ray Characterization of Galaxy Groups and Hickson Groups in COSMOS-Web to z~3.8 I. X-ray Luminosity Function Evolution`
  - **Question:** How does the X-ray luminosity distribution of JWST-selected groups change with redshift?
  - **Data:**
    - COSMOS-Web: 1,678 AMICO groups and 993 independently selected Hickson compact groups over `0.45 deg^2`, extending to `z~3.8`; catalogs can overlap.
    - Combined Chandra and XMM-Newton imaging provides uniform aperture measurements.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure background-subtracted fluxes with a first-pass `300 kpc` aperture, refining detections to a scaling-relation radius.
    - Apply `SNR>=2`, estimate the detected-population luminosity function with `1/V_max`, and fit Schechter functions in four redshift bins.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Detect 499 AMICO groups and 332 compact groups. Fitted characteristic luminosities rise by approximately `1.9` and `2.1 dex`, respectively, from the lowest to highest bin.
    - Richness and central stellar-mass checks do not explain the trend, but a single flux threshold assumes complete detection above that limit; unmodeled sensitivity and selection biases preclude a precision intrinsic-evolution interpretation.
    - AmicOne has no significant diffuse detection; the whole-box `3-sigma` limit is `log10(L_X/erg s^-1)<44.12`. Appendix A releases individually detected groups only, despite broader measurements in the analysis.
  - **Position:** Extends earlier COSMOS X-ray group work with JWST group selection; the catalog is a starting point for environmental studies, with quality flags and selection modeling essential.

- [2610.02583](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02583) Marko Micic et al. `DESI Interacting Dwarfs Survey: Multiple Evolutionary Channels of Dwarf Galaxies Driven by Major Dwarf Interactions`
  - **Question:** Do major dwarf interactions follow the same star-formation sequence across mass and tidal environment?
  - **Data:**
    - DESI EDR: 1,192 isolated dwarf pairs; the major-interaction analysis uses 461 pairs with mass ratio `<3`.
    - Stellar masses span `log10(M*/M_sun)=6.5-9.5`; redshifts span `0.015<z<1.5`. Separate non-isolated samples test external tides.
  - **Method:**
    - Select pairs within `100 kpc`, require spectroscopic redshift proximity, and split by mass and redshift.
    - Compare median SFR and UV-to-near-IR colors in separation bins; contrast weak and strong external tidal environments.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Low-mass, low-redshift pairs show enhanced SFR and bluer colors toward `10-25 kpc`. At `<10 kpc`, colors suggest different dust and recent-star-formation behavior depending on environment.
    - The isolated major-pair bins contain 85 low-mass/low-redshift, 251 high-mass/low-redshift, and 125 high-mass/high-redshift pairs. Close-pair uncertainties grow substantially.
    - Dust buildup, gas removal/reaccretion, and quenching are interpretations of statistical color/SFR trends; projected separation is an imperfect clock and these are not direct gas-flow measurements.
  - **Position:** Provides a larger observational framework for dwarf pre-processing, while retaining the distinction between cross-sectional trends and individual evolutionary tracks.

- [2610.02587](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02587) Marko Micic: `The Largest Catalog of Dwarf Galaxy Groups: Transient Structures or Building Blocks of the Universe?`
  - **Question:** Are compact dwarf associations compatible with gravitationally bound groups?
  - **Data:**
    - DESI EDR: 74 isolated groups with 238 galaxies, three to six members per group, at `0.02<z<0.33`.
    - Members lie within `100 kpc` of the projected centroid, with no non-dwarf neighbor within `1 Mpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure gapper velocity dispersions and projected crossing times; test stellar-only binding.
    - Infer group halos from a stellar-to-halo mass relation and NFW potentials; propagate velocity, stellar-mass and halo-model uncertainties in 5,000 realizations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Median velocity dispersion is `53 km s^-1` and median crossing time is `0.37 Gyr`; a crossing time is not a merger time.
    - 69/74 groups favor binding with model-based `P_bound>=0.68`, including 66/74 with `P_bound>=0.95`. These probabilities test projected consistency with assumed halos, not complete three-dimensional orbital binding.
    - All 69 bound-favored groups admit orbital-decay times below a Hubble time if satellites retain SHMR-inferred halos; only 20 do so in the stellar-only satellite-mass limit.
  - **Position:** Expands the observational sample of isolated dwarf assembly, with halo assumptions and tidal stripping controlling the dynamical interpretation.


## AGN hosts and gas kinematics

- [2610.02465](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02465) Callum Dewsnap et al. `Separating the Optical to Near-Infrared Light of AGN and Their Host Galaxies`
  - **Question:** How reliably can multi-band imaging separate AGN light from host-galaxy structure and stellar emission?
  - **Data:**
    - 96 X-ray-selected EGS/CEERS sources imaged in up to 13 HST and JWST bands; 39 spectroscopic and 57 photometric redshifts.
    - 66 objects have sufficient photometry for separate host and AGN SED modeling.
  - **Method:**
    - Use AstroPhot joint Sersic-plus-PSF fits, empirical PSF alternatives and neighbor modeling.
    - Subtract the unresolved component, measure nonparametric host properties, and compare CIGALE fits from aperture and profile photometry.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Aperture photometry generally gives better host SED fits for galaxies with clumps or spiral arms. Host masses and SFRs broadly overlap massive non-AGN galaxies.
    - Joint fits can trade nuclear light against host concentration; profile-fit uncertainties are underestimated, and HST Sersic indices are poorly constrained in this sample.
    - AGN disk luminosities are consistent with bolometric-to-X-ray ratios of roughly `5-20`, treating disk luminosity as bolometric. Dust/torus luminosities are unreliable given wavelength coverage and decomposition systematics.
  - **Position:** A practical test of complementary parametric and nonparametric measurements, with empirical failure modes relevant to JWST AGN-host work.

- [2610.03300](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03300) J. N. H. S. Aditya et al. `Merger remnant in TXS 1033+026 - I: Halpha and HI 21-cm observations suggest counter-rotating gas`
  - **Question:** How are neutral and ionized gas arranged around the radio core of this merger remnant?
  - **Data:**
    - eMERLIN L/C-band continuum and HI absorption; Gemini GMOS-N IFU spectroscopy of TXS 1033+026 at `z_core=0.05012+/-0.00011`.
    - HI channels resolve `6.9 km s^-1`; the optical cube has `0.6 arcsec` seeing.
  - **Method:**
    - Map HI optical depth and position-velocity structure against resolved radio emission.
    - Fit the Halpha velocity field with a PSF-convolved four-ring warped-disk model and compare rotation senses.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Halpha is consistent with a warped kiloparsec-scale disk. HI velocity components suggest counter-rotation, but a more complex warped or helical configuration remains possible.
    - Continuum-weighted integrated HI optical depth is `90+/-3 km s^-1`, using the flux actually covered by absorption.
    - Absorption traces only foreground gas illuminated by the radio source; the recovered geometry cannot describe all gas around the nucleus.
  - **Position:** Links merger-driven gas rearrangement to possible AGN fueling without claiming a unique three-dimensional inflow geometry.


## Extreme emission-line analogs

- [2610.03572](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03572) Kristin N. Baker et al. `Searching for Green Pea Galaxies in the SFACT Survey`
  - **Question:** Can deep narrowband selection extend Green Pea samples to fainter emission-line galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - SFACT broadband and narrowband imaging plus Hydra spectra: three windows at `z=0.31-0.34`, `0.38-0.41`, and `0.48-0.51`.
  - **Method:**
    - Estimate [O III] equivalent widths photometrically because faint spectral continua make spectroscopic EWs unreliable.
    - Combine `EW_5007,phot>=300 Angstrom` with filter-dependent colors where usable and `0.4<=log10([O III]/Hbeta)<0.9`.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The final sample has 123 candidates: 64, 44, and 15 in the three redshift windows. NB695 selection has no effective color cut, so the selection functions differ.
    - At `z=0.31-0.34`, candidate surface density is `2.95 deg^-2`; deeper observations explain access to lower-luminosity systems rather than establishing population evolution.
    - Spectroscopic redshifts do not guarantee pure star-forming classifications: Halpha/[N II] coverage is absent and residual Seyfert contamination remains. [O III]-based SFRs are calibration dependent; reliable stellar masses and sSFRs are not yet available.
  - **Position:** Builds a fainter low-redshift comparison set for extreme high-redshift emitters; it does not measure ionizing escape fractions.


## Feedback and magnetic-field modeling

- [2610.03544](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03544) Cami Delgado et al. `Baryonic Imprints on DM Halos: the concentration-mass relation and its dependence on 28 model parameters in the CAMELS suite`
  - **Question:** Can the baryonic dependence of halo concentration be compressed into a tractable model?
  - **Data:**
    - 2,048 CAMELS SB28 IllustrisTNG simulations varying 23 astrophysical and five cosmological parameters.
    - ROCKSTAR total-matter NFW concentrations cover approximately `10^11-10^14.5 M_sun/h` and `z=0-3`.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit kernel-localized multivariate regressions with a `0.2 dex` mass kernel and bootstrap uncertainties.
    - Decompose the mass-dependent astrophysical response slopes into principal components and approximate them with cubic polynomials.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Three components capture roughly `95%` of the normalized astrophysical-slope variation. Stellar feedback dominates responses near Milky-Way halo masses; AGN responses become important at higher masses.
    - Feedback channels couple nonlinearly, so changing a stellar-wind parameter can affect concentration indirectly through AGN gas supply.
    - The fitted concentration describes total matter, not only dark matter. The basis is calibrated to TNG and is not established as universal across galaxy-formation models.
  - **Position:** Offers a compact nuisance model for baryonic effects in halo analyses, with cross-model calibration still needed.

- [2610.02389](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02389) Muhammed Irshad et al. `Collapse-accelerated small-scale dynamos in the first stars and galaxies`
  - **Question:** How does gravitational collapse change kinematic and nonlinear magnetic-field growth?
  - **Data:**
    - Analytic/semi-analytic first-star minihalo and first-galaxy atomic-cooling-halo models, with scans of turbulence, dynamo and collapse parameters.
  - **Method:**
    - Evolve turbulent decay in supercomoving variables with compression, followed by scale-by-scale magnetic saturation.
    - Remove flux-freezing amplification before comparing dynamo growth times; stop at protostellar density or rotational support.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Collapse yields speed-ups `>10` in part of parameter space, especially when a stationary dynamo is slow enough for collapse to matter but still efficient enough to amplify beyond flux freezing.
    - A fiducial first-galaxy realization reaches approximately `75.6 microG` on a `53.3 pc` coherence scale; these are conditional model outputs, not observations or unique predictions.
    - Spherical homologous collapse, simplified isothermal thermodynamics and incomplete ion-neutral treatment limit the predictions; the magnetic spectrum is not explicitly evolved.
  - **Position:** Extends collapse-dynamo theory into nonlinear saturation and supplies parameter-dependent initial conditions for subsequent simulations.

- [2610.03346](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03346) Akhil Lasrado et al. `A multi-scale view of molecular gas and magnetic fields in the Antennae galaxies`
  - **Question:** Which unresolved gas structures contribute to the polarization measured across a merger?
  - **Data:**
    - Antennae CO(3-2) from APEX/LAsMA and ALMA, combined with SOFIA/HAWC+ `154 micron` dust polarization.
    - ALMA resolves approximately `55 pc`; the polarization comparison probes kiloparsec scales.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare matched-resolution CO intensity and velocity dispersion with polarization.
    - Use channel-wise rolling Hough transforms to measure gas orientations, alignment with the inferred magnetic field, and line-of-sight coherence.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Velocity-coherent structures in the overlap region align strongly with the measured field; CO orientation coherence correlates modestly with polarization fraction, `rho~0.5`.
    - The interpretation assumes molecular structures trace local field directions. Coarse polarization is sensitive to much smaller gas structures, but does not directly resolve their magnetic fields.
    - Line-of-sight superposition can mimic rotation or outflow-like moment-map patterns; diffuse emission sensitivity and smoothing choices affect the comparison.
  - **Position:** Provides a nearby resolved test for interpreting coarse high-redshift polarimetry and merger kinematics.
