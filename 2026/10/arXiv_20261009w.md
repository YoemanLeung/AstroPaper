## Key Findings

- Cold-gas inventories need both phase information and environment assignment. Outer-halo H2 absorption establishes a molecular phase far from a galaxy; MUSE-ALMA Haloes shows that a sight line can also intersect reservoirs associated with multiple galaxies. Neither gives a unique gas-origin history. [2610.06662](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06662) Brad Koplitz; [2610.10117](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10117) T. O'Beirne.
- Environment influences activity in different ways: COSMOS2020 SFR trends depend strongly on population mix, while COSMOS-Web radio AGN fractions remain elevated at fixed stellar mass. These are complementary observables, not evidence for one universal triggering mechanism. [2610.07710](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07710) Sina Taamoli; [2610.10719](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10719) F. Ubertosi.
- Selection and calibration can mimic physical evolution. CAPERS measures a concrete high-redshift selection tradeoff; the UVLF synthesis exposes correlated data and method differences; the group XLF still requires a forward selection model to isolate intrinsic evolution. [2610.12322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12322) Callum T. Donnan; [2610.12121](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12121) A. Fontana; [2610.02470](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02470) Ghassem Gozaliasl.
- Feedback changes where baryons reside and when hot gas appears, but the answer depends on the quantity and boundary being measured. Cosmic baryon shares, baryon-fraction closure, total-mass compensation and virial-scaled CGM histories are not interchangeable. [2610.07140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07140) Mohammadreza Ayromlou; [2610.08931](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08931) Victor Rufo-Pastor.
- Early black-hole formation has multiple viable conditional pathways. Internal radiation enables rapid progenitor growth in one halo, while cluster-based seeding changes early assembly in a rare overdensity; neither calculation by itself establishes a cosmological seed abundance. [2610.03877](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03877) Masaki Kiyuna; [2610.12398](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12398) Yihao Zhou.

## Multiphase gas and absorber environments

- [2610.10112](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10112) Celine Peroux et al. `MUSE-ALMA Haloes XIV: The ALMA Large Programme Data Release`
  - **Question:** What resolved molecular-gas data are available for absorption-selected environments?
  - **Data:**
    - The multi-facility MUSE-ALMA Haloes survey; its Cycle 10 ALMA Large Programme targeted `39` galaxies at `z~0.5`.
  - **Method:**
    - Calibrate, self-calibrate where possible, and release naturally and Briggs-weighted cubes at `15` and `60 km/s`.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The release enables joint CO, optical-IFU and absorption analyses. The `39` targets are not the full `79`-galaxy parent sample or a count of detections.
  - **Position:** A reusable link between CGM sight lines and resolved galaxy gas; heterogeneous coverage and absorber selection govern representativeness.

- [2610.10117](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10117) T. O'Beirne et al. `MUSE-ALMA Haloes XVI: a census of the condensed baryons in z~0.5 HI absorption-selected galaxies`
  - **Question:** Can absorber-selected galaxies close their condensed-baryon budget?
  - **Data:**
    - MUSE, ALMA, HST and quasar spectra for `79` galaxies associated with `32` absorbers along `19` sight lines.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine stellar and molecular masses with empirical HI estimates, absorber columns and dynamical constraints.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Seventeen absorbers have multiple associated galaxies, so inferred HI reservoirs can overlap; absorption need not identify one isolated galaxy.
    - The steeper decline of molecular fraction with stellar mass is tentative. HI is inferred rather than emission-detected, its estimator already depends on stellar mass, and CO nondetections limit the comparison.
  - **Position:** Places galaxy gas fractions in their group and CGM context without treating empirical HI estimates as independent measurements.

- [2610.06662](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06662) Brad Koplitz et al. `Forming Molecular Hydrogen in a Galaxy Halo Through Star Formation Wind Interactions`
  - **Question:** Can molecular hydrogen form in the outer CGM of a starburst?
  - **Data:**
    - HST/COS spectroscopy of a background quasar at projected separation `139 kpc` from GAL J0057+1447.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit HI, H2 and metal absorption; test ionization and shock-cooling models against alternative origin scenarios.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Nineteen H2 transitions give `log[N(H2)/cm^-2]=16.52+/-0.05`. Wind-CGM cooling is a plausible route to the cold molecular phase.
    - Saturated Lyalpha leaves the HI column highly degenerate. One sight line and unresolved cloud structure prevent a unique reconstruction of the gas origin.
  - **Position:** Direct molecular absorption far from a galaxy complements emission-based inventories of its central gas.

- [2610.07273](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07273) Abhisek Mohapatra et al. `Multiple H I 21-cm absorbers towards blazar PKS 1158+007`
  - **Question:** Do multiple cold-HI absorbers trace a common overdense region?
  - **Data:**
    - MeerKAT spectra toward PKS 1158+007, optical spectra and HSC/DESI photometric galaxy redshifts.
  - **Method:**
    - Validate absorption against RFI and imaging artifacts; compare metal-line limits and integrate galaxy redshift probabilities.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Two HI absorbers occur at `z=0.5305` and `0.5653`; photometric galaxy counts suggest an overdensity near the absorber interval.
    - The metal-poor interpretation assumes the radio and optical beams probe the same gas. Spin temperature, covering fraction and broad galaxy photo-z distributions leave metallicity and environment membership uncertain.
  - **Position:** A cold-gas environment candidate for follow-up rather than a confirmed three-dimensional association or unique metallicity measurement.

- [2610.07068](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07068) S.Zarattini et al. `EMIR confirmation of the high-z Lyalpha blob SHARDS20018464`
  - **Question:** Does near-infrared spectroscopy establish the redshift of a compact Lyalpha blob?
  - **Data:**
    - Four hours of GTC/EMIR K-band spectroscopy of SHARDS20018464.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify two candidate [O III] lines and compare their flux ratio with the atomic expectation.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The measured ratio `2.92` supports the doublet identification, but the individual redshifts are `3.61` and `3.57`. The quoted mean `3.59+/-0.01` does not remove this inconsistency.
    - Treat the high-redshift interpretation as supported but requiring line-centroid verification before using a precise systemic redshift.
  - **Position:** A useful compact-nebula follow-up case; this review flags the internal redshift tension explicitly.

## Environment, activity and quenching

- [2610.10719](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10719) F. Ubertosi et al. `Prevalence of radio Active Galactic Nuclei in galaxy groups since z=3.5 in the COSMOS-Web field`
  - **Question:** Does group environment enhance radio AGN activity at fixed stellar mass?
  - **Data:**
    - VLA-COSMOS 3 GHz radio-excess AGN matched to COSMOS-Web galaxies and groups at `0.1<=z<=3.5` and `10<=log(Mstar/Msun)<=11.4`.
  - **Method:**
    - Assign group membership at association probability `>=0.5`; estimate environment-specific luminosity functions and radio-completeness-corrected AGN fractions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Group radio AGN fractions average about twice the field values at fixed stellar mass; significance varies across redshift bins. Fractions peak within `0.2 R200`.
    - The `3-30` space-density enhancement depends on the adopted group volume. Cosmic variance in one `~0.45 deg^2` field and photometric membership limit generalization; a fueling transition near `z~1.5` is an interpretation.
  - **Position:** Extends radio-environment tests beyond the lower-redshift group and cluster samples discussed in the manuscript.

- [2610.07710](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07710) Sina Taamoli et al. `The Role of the Cosmic Web in Galaxy Evolution Across 0.4<z<4`
  - **Question:** How do star-formation trends change across the cosmic web?
  - **Data:**
    - COSMOS2020 photometry and redshift probability distributions, with mass-complete comparisons over `0.4<z<4`.
  - **Method:**
    - Classify density fields into field, filament and cluster environments; separate central/satellite and star-forming populations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At `2<z<4`, median SFR rises mildly, by `~0.1 dex`, from field to cluster. Low-redshift suppression weakens greatly in star-forming-only samples.
    - Population mixing therefore explains much of the late-time trend; photo-z environments and SED-derived quantities limit causal interpretation.
  - **Position:** Connects large-scale environment with the changing quenched fraction rather than assuming every trend shifts the star-forming sequence.

- [2610.08999](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08999) Euclid Collaboration et al. `Euclid Quick Data Release (Q1). Exploring the complexity of quenching processes across time, environment, and mass through recently quenched galaxies`
  - **Question:** Do fast and slow quenching populations follow the same environmental path?
  - **Data:**
    - Euclid Q1 photometry across `~63.1 deg^2`; UVJ-selected populations over `0.2<z<3`.
  - **Method:**
    - Classify transition populations, compare morphology and SED properties, and examine environments only to `z~1.5`.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Fast-quenching candidates retain more star-forming-like morphologies; slow-quenching candidates resemble quiescent galaxies and have clearer dense-environment tails above `z~0.5`.
    - Photometric classes and fitted histories support a population-level scenario; they do not uniquely measure physical quenching mechanisms.
  - **Position:** Adds a large-area transition-population view to environmental trends, with a narrower redshift reach for the environment test.

- [2610.02470](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02470) Ghassem Gozaliasl et al. `X-ray Characterization of Galaxy Groups and Hickson Groups in COSMOS-Web to z~3.8 I. X-ray Luminosity Function Evolution`
  - **Question:** How do COSMOS-Web groups populate the X-ray luminosity function?
  - **Data:**
    - Chandra/XMM-Newton maps at the positions of `1678` AMICO groups and `993` Hickson compact groups, extending to `z~3.8`.
  - **Method:**
    - Apply uniform aperture photometry with `SNR>=2` detection, refine detected-source apertures, and fit luminosity functions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - There are `499` CW-All and `332` CW-HCG detections; the two catalogs overlap and must not be summed as independent groups.
    - Characteristic luminosity rises by `~1.9-2.1 dex` across the fitted bins. Independent richness checks argue against a simple richer-host explanation, but intrinsic evolution still requires a forward-modeled selection function.
  - **Position:** A group-scale X-ray catalog and population baseline; targeted aperture detections are not an independent blind source catalog.

## Feedback and gas accounting

- [2610.07140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07140) Mohammadreza Ayromlou et al. `And Then There WEre Baryons (ATWEB): The Baryon Budget of the Universe in Halos and the IGM over 13 Billion Years of Cosmic Time`
  - **Question:** Where do simulated baryons reside, and how far does feedback redistribute matter?
  - **Data:**
    - IllustrisTNG, EAGLE and SIMBA hydrodynamic simulations and corresponding gravity-only comparisons.
  - **Method:**
    - Separate cosmic baryon shares from enclosed baryon fractions; compare closure and total-mass compensation radii.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At `z=0`, `72-83%` of baryons lie outside the selected halos in this census, which adopts `M200c>=10^10 Msun` for its global budget.
    - Restoring the enclosed cosmic baryon fraction and restoring the gravity-only total mass are distinct conditions. Feedback prescriptions, halo definitions and the mass floor affect the inferred budget.
  - **Position:** A framework for connecting halo gas deficits to large-scale matter redistribution, with explicitly different accounting measures.

- [2610.08931](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08931) Victor Rufo-Pastor et al. `Early onset of the hot circumgalactic medium around Milky Way galaxies`
  - **Question:** Does stellar feedback advance the onset of a hot circumgalactic atmosphere?
  - **Data:**
    - Six VINTERGATAN Milky Way halo zooms, including three feedback-free runs with fixed metallicities.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare cooling/free-fall times and thermal structure with a virial-shock model using both physical and virial-scaled radii.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Feedback produces a hot corona around `z~3-4`, earlier than the reference shock-model expectation near `z~1` for `0.1 Zsun`.
    - Growing virial boundaries can mimic prolonged CGM evolution. The small suite and omitted AGN feedback limit transfer to other halo populations.
  - **Position:** Shows why present-day temperature alone does not identify the heating history, and why radial conventions matter.

- [2610.12159](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12159) Doga Tolgay et al. `Modeling CO Line Luminosity Using FIRE Simulations: I. Methodology and Validation of Molecular Gas and CO Abundance`
  - **Question:** How reliably can CO and molecular gas be predicted from simulated galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - FIRE-2 galaxy suites, Milky Way clouds and sight lines, xCOLD GASS, PHIBSS2 and ASPECS comparisons.
  - **Method:**
    - Use SKIRT radiation fields and a five-parameter CLOUDY grid; interpolate line emission and species abundances per gas cell.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Changing assumed cloud metallicity from solar to three times solar changes summed predicted CO luminosity by about an order of magnitude.
    - At `z=0`, molecular masses overlap xCOLD GASS scatter for `SFR>=0.3 Msun/yr`; lower-SFR coverage is insufficient for a decisive test. High-redshift comparisons remain limited by CO conversion and survey selection.
  - **Position:** Provides a physical forward-model component for CO surveys and intensity mapping; galaxy-wide CO-luminosity validation is deferred to the companion paper.

## Earliest galaxies and black-hole seeds

- [2610.12322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12322) Callum T. Donnan et al. `JWST CAPERS: Spectroscopic evaluation of very high redshift galaxy candidates`
  - **Question:** Which photometric choices most reliably select very high-redshift galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST CAPERS NIRSpec/PRISM and NIRCam photometry: `327` targeted candidates, `183` secure spectroscopic redshifts, and `48` confirmed at `z>=8.5`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure emission-line or Lyman-break redshifts; compare template, SNR, break-strength and chi-squared cuts with a UV-luminosity-function prior.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The LF prior raises the defined photometric-redshift success fraction from `~35%` to `~77%`, while sample-relative completeness falls from `~79%` to `~69%`.
    - The highest redshift measured by this analysis is `12.98`. The `144` targets without secure redshifts and deliberately heterogeneous candidate selection prevent interpreting these fractions as population-wide purity or completeness; LF inference still needs selection simulations.
  - **Position:** Turns photometric selection choices into spectroscopically testable tradeoffs; the prior changes redshift probabilities rather than merely imposing another quality cut.

- [2610.12121](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12121) A. Fontana et al. `Too many or too bright? Status and perspectives in the study of the UV Luminosity Function at z>10`
  - **Question:** What evolution of the UV luminosity function is supported beyond redshift nine?
  - **Data:**
    - Published JWST UVLF measurements over `8.5<z<14.5`, spectroscopic lower limits, and competing candidate/upper-limit results at `z~17`.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit a smoothly evolving Schechter function and separate redshift bins; compare UV luminosity densities and alternative galaxy-formation prescriptions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - A four-parameter descriptive fit gives `alpha~-2.3`, `Mstar_UV~-21.7` and a normalization decline of `~0.17 dex` per unit redshift.
    - Shared imaging makes many literature points non-independent; inter-study scatter of `~0.1-0.2 dex` and parameter degeneracy limit physical interpretation. The evidence above `z~15` does not yet decide between continued smooth evolution and a sharp decline.
  - **Position:** A useful synthesis of JWST abundance constraints and model degeneracies, rather than a new independent census.

- [2610.03877](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03877) Masaki Kiyuna et al. `Internal Radiative Feedback Triggers Global Collapse and Direct-Collapse Black Hole Formation in Metal-free Atomic-Cooling Haloes`
  - **Question:** Can internal stellar radiation trigger massive-seed progenitor growth under weak external FUV?
  - **Data:**
    - Radiation-hydrodynamic calculations of one nearly metal-free atomic-cooling halo with external `J21=1-100`.
  - **Method:**
    - Follow chemistry, internal radiation and sink growth for `~1-2 Myr`; compare global collapse and fragmentation.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Internal radiation suppresses H2 cooling and enables massive protostars to grow to `~10^4 Msun` while still accreting.
    - Larger final stars and black holes are extrapolations. The calculations stop before the final fate and omit supernova feedback that could terminate the gas supply.
  - **Position:** A conditional alternative to requiring an extreme external radiation field, not a completed direct-collapse black-hole calculation.

- [2610.12398](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12398) Yihao Zhou et al. `ACORN I. Massive Black Hole Seeding and Tidal Disruption Events from Star Clusters in Cosmological Simulations`
  - **Question:** How does star-cluster-based seeding change early black-hole assembly?
  - **Data:**
    - Four ASTRID-based runs of one constrained `5-sigma` overdensity, evolved to `z=6`, comparing cluster, gas and halo seeding.
  - **Method:**
    - Populate unresolved star clusters, assign collision-motivated seed masses, evolve clusters and tidal disruptions, and compare runs with/without recoil.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Allowing multiple seeds per halo produces a clustered population and faster early assembly. In the dominant halo, different prescriptions converge near `10^8.6 Msun` by `z=6` as gas accretion regulates growth.
    - This is a controlled rare-environment experiment, not a cosmological merger-rate forecast. Cluster dynamics are subgrid, numerical mergers occur on kiloparsec scales, and recoil assumes zero spin.
  - **Position:** Connects seed formation to local cluster properties instead of prescribing one fixed-mass seed per eligible halo.
