## Key Findings

- **Early enrichment remains observable after reionization.** [2610.16509](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16509) Sarah E. I. Bosman measures exceptionally low carbon in neutral gas at `z=5.14`; the elemental pattern does not uniquely identify Population III enrichment.
- **A large catalogue still needs a selection model.** [2610.15901](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15901) Rashmi Gottumukkala releases uniform JWST fits, but heterogeneous targeting and flagged non-galaxy/lensed sources prevent direct demographic use without additional selection and magnification treatment.
- **Frequent mergers need not dominate mass assembly.** [2610.17575](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17575) Hao Fu finds predominantly in-situ stellar growth across several models despite substantial merger activity; accreted mass fractions and event rates must be kept distinct.
- **Gas-tracer interpretation depends on the surrounding population and the gas model.** [2610.17200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17200) Lurdes Ondaro-Mallea finds satellite-induced feedback biases in mock stacks; [2610.18046](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18046) Kyle A. Oman finds excessive simulated circumgalactic H I despite successful integrated scaling relations.
- **Production is not the same as enrichment.** [2610.17218](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17218) Devesh Nandal produces abundant internal nitrogen but ejects little before collapse under its adopted wind prescription. Interior yields alone cannot predict nebular abundances.

## Nearly pristine gas and the first enrichment sources

- [2610.16509](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16509) Sarah E. I. Bosman et al. `Nearly pristine gas in a galaxy 1 billion years after the Big Bang`
  - **Question:** How pristine can neutral gas remain after reionization?
  - **Data:**
    - Gemini/GHOST: 5.8 hr on the background quasar J1941-4500, with resolving power R=57,000; C II, O I and Si II absorption in UMP-DLA-1.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit unsaturated metal lines with thermal and turbulent broadening.
    - Jointly fit the DLA, a nearby Lyman-limit absorber and the quasar continuum; compare 15 continuum/window choices.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At `z=5.139987`, `log N_HI/cm^-2=20.542 (+0.071/-0.014)` and `[C/H]=-4.05 (+0.03/-0.08)` on the adopted Asplund solar scale.
    - The cooling discriminant is `D_trans=-3.85+/-0.03`. A top-heavy initial mass function is favored, but dust cooling can still permit some low-mass stars. C/O/Si alone cannot distinguish Population III from extremely metal-poor Population II enrichment.
  - **Position:** Extends metal-poor DLA abundance work into the regime below the fine-structure cooling threshold.

- [2610.17218](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17218) Devesh Nandal et al. `From Giant Envelopes to Rapidly Contracting Cores in Rotating Population III Stars from 500 to 1000 M_sun`
  - **Question:** Does primary nitrogen made inside a very massive primordial star actually enrich its surroundings?
  - **Data:**
    - GENEC: nonmagnetic zero-metallicity models of 500-1000 solar masses at initial v/v_crit=0.1; one faster-rotating 1000-solar-mass comparison through helium exhaustion.
  - **Method:**
    - Evolve rotational transport, burning and prescribed surface mass loss.
    - Track interior inventories separately from cumulative ejecta and test specific angular momentum against circularization requirements.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The fiducial `1000 M_sun` model contains `62.1 M_sun` of nitrogen at helium exhaustion, yet its terminal cumulative wind contains only `1.8e-3 M_sun` of nitrogen in `20.2 M_sun` of lost material.
    - The inner `300 M_sun` fails the adopted inside-out circularization test, despite rapid core spin-up. These are hydrostatic progenitors, not collapse simulations; uncertain winds or deeper stripping could change enrichment.
  - **Position:** Connects primordial stellar evolution to observable abundance patterns while separating synthesized material from escaped yields.


## Galaxy growth and reusable JWST measurements

- [2610.15901](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15901) Rashmi Gottumukkala et al. `The DAWN JWST Archive: A spectroscopically-informed population study of the physical properties of galaxies at z>2`
  - **Question:** What can uniform spectrophotometric modelling reveal about recent star formation and dust beyond cosmic noon?
  - **Data:**
    - DAWN JWST Archive: roughly 12,000 high-quality PRISM spectra with NIRCam counterparts at 2<z<14.4; roughly 9,000 spectra enter the main analysis after exclusions.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit BPASS-based BAGPIPES models with a non-parametric continuity star-formation history.
    - Fit flexible emission-line residuals and calibrate spectra to photometry; derive star-formation rates averaged over 3, 10 and 100 Myr.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The average 90th-percentile SFR range across mass bins broadens from `1.36 dex` at 100 Myr to `2.2 dex` at 10 Myr; these are distribution widths, not individual measurement errors.
    - The catalogue includes flagged little red dots, broad-line AGN and lensing-field sources excluded from the main analysis. Heterogeneous targeting, high-redshift outshining and stellar-library/SFH assumptions limit demographic interpretations.
  - **Position:** Provides a reproducible DJA/BAGPIPES resource for comparing stellar populations across public spectroscopy programmes.

- [2610.17575](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17575) Hao Fu et al. `Mergers may not be the main channel for galaxy stellar mass growth at redshift z>=5: Predictions from cosmological hierarchical models and simulations`
  - **Question:** Do high close-pair fractions imply that mergers dominate stellar mass assembly at cosmic dawn?
  - **Data:**
    - DECODE, TNG100, EAGLE and L-Galaxies, compared with HST/JWST pair statistics and stellar-mass-density measurements at approximately z=5-11.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare merger trees and calibrated merger timescales across model families.
    - Track accreted stellar mass separately from stars formed in the main progenitor; distinguish close-pair observability times from subhalo infall times.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The model merger-accreted stellar-mass density lies roughly an order of magnitude below total cosmic stellar-mass density, with a wider gap at `z>=8`.
    - Typical ex-situ fractions are `<=10-20%`, with model/mass dependence and a high-redshift L-Galaxies exception. Its major-merger rates are much higher than the other models; agreement on in-situ dominance does not imply agreement on event rates.
  - **Position:** Offers a counterpoint to interpreting JWST close pairs as direct measures of merger-dominated growth.


## Gas tracers, feedback and numerical interpretation

- [2610.17200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17200) Lurdes Ondaro-Mallea et al. `Interpreting the stacked kinetic SZ signal II: the degeneracy between satellite galaxies and baryonic feedback`
  - **Question:** How strongly can satellite galaxies bias feedback inferred from stacked gas profiles?
  - **Data:**
    - FLAMINGO feedback variants with DESI-like bright, luminous-red and emission-line galaxy samples at z=0.25, 0.7 and 1.1; ACT-like beam and observational covariances.
  - **Method:**
    - Stack projected gas optical depth using compensated aperture filters and split central/satellite contributions.
    - Fit mixed-population mock profiles with a central-only emulator at fixed sample mean halo mass.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Satellites supply approximately `50-80%` of the signal despite being a minority of the galaxies, because they occupy more massive haloes.
    - Central-only fits underestimate gas fractions by about a factor of `2` for the bright sample and `40%` for luminous red galaxies. No significant emission-line-sample bias is detected at current errors; these are mock-analysis results, not a direct refit of DESI/ACT data.
  - **Position:** Extends stacked-gas modelling to sample occupation, making clustering and lensing useful controls on feedback inference.

- [2610.17577](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17577) Shyam H. Menon et al. `Non-equilibrium chemistry and radiation hydrodynamics in metal-poor and metal-rich interstellar gas`
  - **Question:** When does chemical equilibrium misrepresent the coolants and absorption tracers of interstellar gas?
  - **Data:**
    - KROME/VETTAM/FLASH benchmark calculations, one-zone collapse and 3D turbulent gas over primordial to solar metallicity; a fiducial network of 37 species and 206 reactions.
  - **Method:**
    - Couple non-equilibrium chemistry to radiation transport and thermal evolution.
    - Compare turbulent and static photodissociation-region predictions against diffuse-ISM carbon and CO absorption measurements.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - OH and water become important dense-gas coolants at `Z<=0.01 Z_sun`. The turbulent solar-metallicity test reproduces both C I and CO columns more closely than its matched static slab.
    - The result supports turbulent non-equilibrium chemistry as a remedy for excess C I, but broader conditions remain to be tested. Fixed dust assumptions, omitted ice/nitrogen chemistry and prescribed cosmic rays limit applicability.
  - **Position:** Tests a proposed dynamical explanation of the neutral-carbon discrepancy in static gas models.

- [2610.18046](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18046) Kyle A. Oman et al. `Atomic hydrogen in z=0 galaxies in the COLIBRE simulations`
  - **Question:** Does a multiphase cosmological simulation reproduce both galaxy H I statistics and extended neutral gas?
  - **Data:**
    - COLIBRE at z=0, principally a 200-comoving-Mpc box with gas particle mass 1.84e6 solar masses, with lower/higher-resolution comparisons and H I survey benchmarks.
  - **Method:**
    - Use coupled gas chemistry and dust rather than assigning H I in post-processing.
    - Compare mass/size statistics and mock 21-cm maps; test numerical resolution and shielding/radiation assumptions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Broad H I scaling relations agree without H I entering the calibration, but the highest-resolution run overproduces sources above `M_HI~3e9 M_sun` by about a factor of `2`.
    - Excess outer H I around massive galaxies is associated with too many neutral clouds; their numbers and masses decline at higher resolution. Jeans-length shielding is questionable for pressure-confined clouds, so integrated success does not validate CGM morphology.
  - **Position:** Provides a useful H I benchmark with a clearly identified boundary between galaxy-scale agreement and circumgalactic reliability.


## Early black holes and little red dots

- [2610.18263](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18263) Hannah Uebler: `Massive black holes in the first billion years with JWST`
  - **Question:** Which conclusions about early black-hole growth survive the current measurement uncertainties?
  - **Data:**
    - Literature review of JWST AGN masses, direct mass checks and hosts; no new uniform sample.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare broad-line masses with direct constraints.
    - Assess multiwavelength properties, dense gas and host scaling relations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Detected systems often lie above local black-hole/stellar-mass relations; lower-mass black holes are preferentially missed and both masses remain method dependent.
    - Radio upper limits can still match radio-quiet AGN. Limited rest-frame time baselines weaken variability tests; local calibrations and possible scattering in little red dots prevent a unique seed-growth interpretation.
  - **Position:** A synthesis guiding independent mass and host measurements.

- [2610.17559](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17559) Tao An et al. `A radio counterpart of the local little-red-dot J092359+415736`
  - **Question:** What powers the radio counterpart of the local little red dot J092359+415736?
  - **Data:**
    - Archival FIRST, LoTSS, RACS/VAST and VLASS imaging at 0.144-3 GHz over 1994-2024 for the z=0.323 broad-line AGN.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit beam-matched amplitudes at the Gaia position with background and calibration-error tests.
    - Compare spectral/temporal behavior with star formation, transients, scintillation and accretion-powered emission.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The LoTSS detection reaches `42 sigma`; FIRST is approximately `2.6` times fainter than the later spectral level. The separate VLASS rise is only `2.3 sigma`.
    - Radio emission would require `~202 M_sun/yr` from star formation, versus an unobscured H-alpha upper limit of `~3.34 M_sun/yr`. Accretion is favored, but survey beams do not resolve a nuclear component or distinguish a jet, corona and wind; high-z extrapolation assumes no evolution.
  - **Position:** Adds a nearby temporal test to the diversity of radio-detected little-red-dot analogues.


## Survey measurement methods

- [2610.16007](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16007) Hyeonguk Bahk et al. `TractorJAX: GPU-Accelerated SPHEREx Forced Photometry and the Trade-offs of Regularization and External Priors`
  - **Question:** Do regularization or external flux priors make deeper forced-photometry catalogues more useful?
  - **Data:**
    - SPHEREx QR2 imaging in COSMOS and eight cluster fields, DESI Legacy Survey priors, 7,781 bright spectroscopic comparison sources and controlled A2537 simulations.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare magnitude-truncated and full catalogues with identical forward-model families.
    - Evaluate matched-source photometric redshifts and fluxes, including the response of the calibration procedure.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Under the stated reliability cut, full-depth regularization retains `1357` sources versus `2253` for the truncated fit; matched-source redshift scatter rises by `17%`.
    - External priors recover `2182` sources but raise paired normalized-median-absolute-deviation scatter by `0.0015`. These field/calibration-dependent tests do not establish a universal magnitude cut; good calibrated redshifts alone do not certify accurate fluxes.
  - **Position:** A practical end-to-end validation example for blended survey photometry, beyond image residuals or formal signal-to-noise.
