## Executive View

The read-first paper is [2605.13966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13966) Katherine Chworowsky. It uses the CAPERS JWST/NIRSpec Treasury data to revisit massive galaxies at `z>3.5` with joint spectra and photometry. The strongest practical lesson is that photometry-only fits overestimate stellar masses by `0.29-0.51 dex` relative to spectro-photometric fits, while the most massive galaxies often require shallow, gray attenuation curves. This directly affects the high-z massive-galaxy abundance problem.

The most important object paper is [2605.14922](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14922) Kana Takechi. DREAMS JWST/NIRSpec spectroscopy of MACS0416-Y1 at `z=8.312` finds broad Hbeta consistent with an AGN, low metallicity of about `0.15 Z_sun`, a small dust mass near `10^6 M_sun`, and very hot dust, `T_dust=91(-35,+62) K`. The key warning is that the ALMA [O III] `88 micron` to JWST [O III] `5007 A` ratio cannot be explained by a single homogeneous ionized region.

The best reionization-era population paper is [2605.14313](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14313) Yuanhang Ning. Its `14` luminous LAEs at the end of reionization are mostly low-mass, young, bursty systems with high Lyalpha escape fractions. The paper argues that luminous LAEs need not be massive evolved galaxies; many look more like efficient ionizing engines with low dust, blue UV slopes, high `xi_ion`, and burst-enhanced Halpha.

Two AGN/LRD items should be kept together. [2605.13946](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13946) Ryan W. Pfeifle builds a WISE plus DeCaLS sample of spatially resolved mid-IR dual AGNs and finds `13` confirmed duals plus `63` high-confidence candidates. [2605.14233](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14233) Kevin Park reports `8` low-redshift DESI Little Red Dot analogs at `z=0.2-0.45`, with broad Balmer lines, low-ionization spectra, compact morphology, and little or no long-term variability.

The methods papers are unusually useful today. [2605.13967](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13967) Ely D. Kovetz shows that bursty star formation can boost high-z line-intensity-mapping shot noise by factors of a few to more than ten. [2605.15096](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15096) Bozhidara Stoyanova shows that SPS model choice can shift SED-inferred stellar masses by up to about `0.6 dex`. [2605.15048](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15048) M. Orte-Garcia updates empirical electron-temperature relations for direct-method metallicity work.

## High-Redshift Galaxies, Dust, and Reionization

- [2605.13966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13966) Katherine Chworowsky et al. `Massive Galaxies Form Early and Gray: Stellar Assembly and Dust Attenuation at z>3.5 from CAPERS`
  - **Question:** how do massive galaxies at `z>3.5` assemble stellar mass, and what dust attenuation curves are required when JWST/NIRSpec spectra are fitted jointly with photometry?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST: CAPERS Cycle 3 Treasury NIRSpec prism spectroscopy, using EGS, PRIMER-COSMOS, and PRIMER-UDS observations obtained from 2024-12 to 2026-01.
    - Imaging: multi-band HST/ACS and JWST/NIRCam photometry tied to the spectroscopic fields.
    - Sample: `148` galaxies selected photometrically with `log(M_*/M_sun)>9.5` and `z>3.5`, after removing LRDs, AGN, and quality failures.
    - Selection/fitting: Dense-Basis photometric preselection followed by BAGPIPES spectro-photometric fitting.
  - **Method:**
    - Cross-match CAPERS spectra with photometric catalogs and rescale spectra for slit losses to match photometry.
    - Remove LRDs using red power-law continua plus Halpha excess and remove likely AGN before fitting the massive-galaxy sample.
    - Fit flexible non-parametric star-formation histories and dust attenuation curves with variable slopes.
    - Compare inferred assembly histories with SC-SAM and FLARES counterparts.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Photometry-only fitting overestimates stellar masses by `0.51 dex` in the lower-mass bin and `0.29 dex` in the higher-mass bin relative to joint spectro-photometric fitting.
    - Slit-loss rescaling raises inferred masses by about `0.5 dex` and SFRs by about `0.4 dex`, making spectrophotometric calibration a major systematic.
    - The most massive systems, especially `log(M_*/M_sun)>10.5`, often require shallow or gray attenuation curves, likely from high optical depth, large grains, or complex geometry.
    - A massive quiescent galaxy, CAPERS-EGS-21360, has `z=4.55`, `log M_*=10.31+-0.02`, and `log sSFR=-9.43+-0.09 yr^-1`.
    - The observed galaxies form systematically earlier than matched SC-SAM and FLARES analogs, although later assembly becomes more similar.
  - **Position:** central for JWST high-z mass-function work because it links early massive-galaxy claims to spectroscopy, dust-curve choice, slit losses, and LRD/AGN cleaning.

- [2605.14922](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14922) Kana Takechi et al. `DREAMS. JWST Spectroscopy of a z=8.3 Galaxy with an ALMA Dust Continuum Detection: Early Dust, Very High Tdust, and a Multi-wavelength [OIII] Ratio Discrepancy`
  - **Question:** what powers the ALMA dust continuum in MACS0416-Y1 at `z=8.312`, and how should combined JWST optical and ALMA far-infrared line diagnostics be interpreted?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST: new deep DREAMS NIRSpec MSA medium-grating spectrum plus archival NIRSpec IFU.
    - JWST imaging: NIRCam F150W and F444W.
    - ALMA: Band 7 dust continuum and [O III] `88 micron`, with `27.52 hr` continuum and `18.89 hr` [O III] on-source time.
    - Target: MACS0416-Y1, the highest-redshift ALMA dust-continuum detection to date.
  - **Method:**
    - Reduce and align JWST MSA, IFU, NIRCam, and ALMA data.
    - Fit emission-line profiles and search for broad Hbeta and broad [O III] components.
    - Use MEx diagnostics, LRD-style checks, direct electron-temperature metallicity from [O III] `4363 A`, and strong-line cross-checks.
    - Combine JWST [O III] `5007 A`, ALMA [O III] `88 micron`, [C II], and dust continuum constraints.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Broad Hbeta with FWHM near `1100 km/s` is interpreted as broad-line AGN activity and is spatially associated with the western dust peak.
    - The [O III] `4363 A` detection gives `Te([O III])=17300+-1500 K` and `12+log(O/H)=7.86(-0.08,+0.09)`, about `0.15 Z_sun`.
    - The dust mass is small, around `10^6 M_sun`, with low `M_dust/M_gas` and `M_dust/M_metal` ratios.
    - The dust temperature is very high, `T_dust=91(-35,+62) K`, plausibly due to low dust content, compact star formation, and AGN heating.
    - The [O III] `88 micron/5007 A` ratio of `0.26+-0.06` cannot be reproduced by a single homogeneous ionized nebula, so optical and far-infrared [O III] lines likely trace different dusty and dust-poor regions.
  - **Position:** a top-priority early-dust and early-AGN paper because it shows that JWST plus ALMA diagnostics can be physically non-co-spatial at `z>8`.

- [2605.14313](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14313) Yuanhang Ning et al. `An Updated Characterization of Luminous Lyalpha emitters at the End of Reionization`
  - **Question:** are luminous LAEs at `z~6` massive evolved galaxies, or can they be low-mass young starbursts with high Lyalpha escape?
  - **Data:**
    - Spectroscopy: Magellan/M2FS observations with about `36 hr` effective integration.
    - Imaging: JWST/NIRCam broad and medium bands, especially F410M for rest-optical constraints.
    - Sample: `14` luminous LAEs, with `13` at `z~5.7` and one at `z~6.6`.
    - Regime: Lyalpha luminosities `>10^42.6 erg/s`, mostly rest-frame equivalent widths `>100 A`, and `M1500` from about `-20.2` to `-18.2`.
  - **Method:**
    - Use BAGPIPES SED fitting with NIRCam medium-band constraints to separate nebular emission and Balmer-break effects.
    - Measure UV slopes, Halpha, ionizing photon production efficiency, Lyalpha FWHM, Lyalpha-stellar offsets, and Lyalpha escape fractions.
    - Compare the systems with starburst relations and known LyC leaker analogs.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - About half the sample has `M_*<10^8 M_sun`, ages below `10 Myr`, and negligible dust.
    - The stellar masses span roughly `7.3<log(M_*/M_sun)<8.6`, with median below `10^8 M_sun`.
    - All objects have `f_esc(Lyalpha)>0.2`, and about half exceed `0.4`.
    - The galaxies have blue UV slopes and high ionizing efficiency, with `log xi_ion,0` around `25.2-26`.
    - Dust content appears more important than neutral-gas line width in suppressing Lyalpha transfer and increasing Lyalpha offsets.
  - **Position:** highly relevant to reionization because it argues that luminous LAEs can be young, efficient, low-mass ionizing engines rather than only rare massive systems.

- [2605.13982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13982) Shyam H Menon et al. `Lyman-alpha Radiation Pressure in Dense Star Clusters: Implications for Star Formation and Winds at Cosmic Dawn`
  - **Question:** can Lyalpha radiation pressure regulate dense star-cluster formation and launch winds under cosmic-dawn conditions?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: post-processed radiation-hydrodynamic dense star-cluster-forming clouds.
    - Radiative transfer: COLT Monte Carlo Lyalpha calculations.
    - Regime: surface densities `Sigma_*>=10^3 M_sun pc^-2` and dust abundances `Z_d=0-0.1 Z_d,sun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Post-process dense cloud simulations with Lyalpha transfer and measure force multipliers, Eddington ratios, and gas response.
    - Separate dense filaments from lower-density bulk gas to identify where Lyalpha pressure couples most efficiently.
    - Compare Lyalpha pressure with UV and infrared radiation pressure across dust abundance.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Lyalpha pressure has only a mild, about `10%`, effect on gas-to-star conversion for `Z_d>=0.01 Z_d,sun`, leaving high efficiencies `epsilon_*>=60%`.
    - Even dust-free cases retain `epsilon_*>=25%`, still above local efficiencies below `10%`.
    - The densest filaments with `n>=10^4 cm^-3` remain sub-Eddington, but lower-density gas can reach `f_Edd>1` and often `>10`.
    - Lyalpha pressure can launch winds within `<=4 Myr`; force multipliers can be as high as `~500` in dust-free cases.
  - **Position:** useful theory context for cosmic-dawn feedback because it separates high-efficiency cluster formation from wind launching in surrounding gas.

## AGN, LRDs, and Lensing

- [2605.13946](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13946) Ryan W. Pfeifle et al. `The Hunt for Red Dual AGNs I: Spatially-Resolved Mid-IR Dual AGNs in the DeCam Legacy Survey`
  - **Question:** can WISE mid-infrared colors plus DeCaLS merger morphology uncover spatially resolved, obscured dual AGNs beyond late-stage optical selections?
  - **Data:**
    - Infrared: AllWISE point-source catalog with W1-W2 AGN color selection.
    - Imaging: DeCaLS grz morphology for resolved galaxy pairs and merger signatures.
    - Spectroscopy: Gemini, Keck/Palomar, SOAR, SDSS, and DESI follow-up or archival redshifts for about two-thirds of the sample.
    - Sample: `146` morphologically selected candidates plus `11` archival spectroscopic candidates, for `157` total.
  - **Method:**
    - Build an all-sky mid-IR AGN parent sample with WISE signal-to-noise and contamination cuts.
    - Match WISE AGN pairs and require projected separations `r_p<=130 kpc` and, where available, velocity offsets `|Delta v|<700 km/s`.
    - Morphologically classify DeCaLS pairs and use optical spectra/BPT diagnostics to rank dual-AGN confidence.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - `76/157` candidates are at consistent redshift with `r_p<=130 kpc`.
    - The sample contains `13` Rank 1 confirmed dual AGNs and `63` strong Rank 0.5 candidates.
    - Confirmed and high-confidence candidates span `14.5-129 kpc`, with median separation `47.8(-18.0,+46.0) kpc`; more than half are wider than `50 kpc`.
    - About `39.4%` of Rank 1 plus 0.5 systems with redshifts have at least one additional companion, indicating a strong multi-merger component.
    - Requiring initial DESI spectroscopy would have reduced the candidate sample by about `86%` and the Rank 1 sample by about `78%`.
  - **Position:** important for obscured SMBH growth and merger-triggered AGN work because it finds many wide-separation systems that optical-only pipelines would miss.

- [2605.14233](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14233) Kevin Park et al. `A new sample of Little Red Dots at z<0.45 in DESI DR1: Broad Balmer lines, low ionization spectrum and no variability`
  - **Question:** do low-redshift analogs of Little Red Dots exist, and what do they imply about LRD spectra, variability, and evolution?
  - **Data:**
    - DESI: DR1 emission-line search rather than a compact-red photometric selection.
    - Sample: `8` LRD analogs at `z=0.2-0.45`.
    - Time domain: multi-year light curves for `7/8` objects over `4-17` rest-frame years.
  - **Method:**
    - Search DESI spectra for broad Balmer-line and low-ionization features resembling high-z LRDs.
    - Check compact morphologies and long-term variability to compare with high-redshift LRD phenomenology.
    - Estimate effective survey volume and low-redshift number density.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The analogs show broad Balmer lines, steep Balmer decrements, compact morphologies, and low-ionization spectra.
    - They lack or have weak high-excitation Type I AGN lines such as strong He II or [Ne V].
    - Long-term variability is weak or absent, about `0.0-0.1 mag` over multi-year rest-frame baselines.
    - The number density is only `1.6e-9 Mpc^-3`, around `10,000` times lower than during the first Gyr.
    - The rarity of luminous red LRD analogs at `z<1` may reflect lower gas feeding rates at later epochs and higher metallicity.
  - **Position:** useful for LRD interpretation because it offers nearby spectral analogs while emphasizing that the phenomenon is much rarer at low redshift.

- [2605.15189](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15189) Adi Zitrin `Strong Gravitational Lensing with JWST`
  - **Question:** what has JWST changed in strong-lensing science, and which lensing use cases matter most for galaxy and AGN studies?
  - **Data:**
    - Review scope: JWST imaging and spectroscopy of strong-lens fields, cluster lenses, galaxy-scale lenses, lensed high-redshift galaxies, lensed AGN, and transient/microlensing cases.
    - Context: systems where JWST resolution and infrared depth reveal source structure or lens constraints unavailable from pre-JWST data.
  - **Method:**
    - Review lens-modeling applications, high-redshift magnification work, source-plane reconstruction, cluster mass mapping, and time-domain lensing opportunities.
    - Summarize how JWST imaging and spectroscopy change the accessible redshift, surface-brightness, and source-size regimes.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - JWST is making strong lensing more central to high-z galaxy and AGN studies by combining magnification, source structure, and rest-frame optical spectroscopy.
    - Cluster lenses remain especially valuable for faint reionization-era systems, while galaxy-scale lenses sharpen quasar-host and compact-galaxy studies.
    - Lens modeling uncertainty remains a limiting systematic and must be propagated into physical interpretation.
  - **Position:** review rather than a primary result, but useful context for lensed JWST samples, magnification bias, and high-z source reconstruction.

## Survey, Environment, and Physical-Inference Systematics

- [2605.13967](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13967) Ely D. Kovetz et al. `When galaxies burst: enhanced shot-noise for line-intensity mapping in the JWST era`
  - **Question:** how does bursty star formation change line-intensity-mapping shot noise and its use for high-redshift large-scale-structure work?
  - **Data:**
    - Framework: Munoz et al. 2026 burstiness model constrained by UV luminosity functions, clustering, and Halpha/UV behavior at `z~4-6`.
    - Reference lines: Halpha, [C II], CO(1-0), and UV continuum with different response timescales.
    - Survey context: SPHEREx, EXCLAIM, CCAT/FYST, COMAP, and related LIM programs.
  - **Method:**
    - Analytically propagate stochastic star-formation histories through line-specific stellar-population kernels.
    - Derive multiplicative shot-noise boosts and cross-line correlation coefficients.
    - Combine with halo mass functions and line-luminosity models to estimate redshift and halo-mass dependence.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At `z~6`, mass-integrated shot-noise boosts are about `B_Halpha~6.8`, `B_[CII]~3.6`, `B_CO~2.9`, and `B_UV~2.6`.
    - The Halpha boost can grow to about `12.5` by `z~10`.
    - Per-halo Halpha boosts can reach `~63` at `10^10 M_sun`, `18` at `10^11 M_sun`, and `6.2` at `10^12 M_sun`.
    - Burstiness can improve line auto-spectrum detectability but raises a stochastic floor that complicates BAO and clean clustering measurements.
    - Cross-line shot noise can probe burst coherence time; Halpha x CO is especially sensitive.
  - **Position:** important for LSS and high-z survey interpretation because it turns JWST-era burstiness into a measurable LIM systematic and signal.

- [2605.14930](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14930) Rhys Jordan et al. `Identifying group galaxies merging with massive clusters using machine learning`
  - **Question:** can supervised machine learning identify galaxy groups in cluster infall regions from projected observable features?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: The Three Hundred plus MultiDark Planck 2.
    - Sample: `304` simulated cluster regions with `14.9<=log10(M_cluster/M_sun)<=15.6`.
    - Galaxies: `300,781` total, including `56,513` main-cluster galaxies, `18,148` group galaxies, and `226,120` neither.
    - Environment: `258` clusters host at least one group, with `603` groups total.
  - **Method:**
    - Label galaxies in 3D as main cluster, group, or neither using simulation substructure information.
    - Project the simulation into mock observational phase space.
    - Train a Random Forest on `24` features including radius, velocity, neighbor distances, local densities, and stellar mass.
    - Compare performance with friends-of-friends and Gaussian Mixture Modelling.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - In 3D, the model reaches `87%` accuracy/weighted precision, with group completeness `45%` and group purity `78%`.
    - In projected 2D, overall accuracy and weighted precision are `81%`, while group completeness and purity are `30%` and `76%`.
    - Group-central recovery is `337/460=73%` in the observational-style test set.
    - Beyond the cluster boundary, group completeness is about `40%` at about `80%` purity.
    - Removing target stellar mass lowers group completeness and purity by about `8%` each.
  - **Position:** useful for protocluster and cluster-infall work because it targets preprocessing and group accretion rather than only the cluster core.

- [2605.15096](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15096) Bozhidara Stoyanova et al. `Impact of stellar population models on the estimated physical properties of galaxies`
  - **Question:** how much do stellar population synthesis model choices shift SED-inferred stellar masses and SFRs?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `17,230` ASTRODEEP-JWST galaxies with spectroscopic redshifts.
    - Models: BC03, CB19, BPASS single-star, and BPASS binary-star SPS/SSP templates.
    - Fitting: CIGALE SED modeling with otherwise fixed assumptions.
    - Validation: synthetic catalogs generated from each model and refit with all models.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit observed photometry while varying only the SPS model family.
    - Build synthetic catalogs preserving best-fit parameters and photometric uncertainties.
    - Compare offsets in `M_*`, SFR, redshift dependence, BIC preferences, and main-sequence placement.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Observed stellar masses vary by up to about `0.6 dex` and SFRs by up to about `0.4 dex` across model pairs.
    - Synthetic tests show BC03 versus BPASS single-star mass offsets of about `0.55-0.56 dex`.
    - BC03 and CB19 are broadly consistent, while BPASS differs substantially from the BC03/CB19 family.
    - Mass offsets exceed typical fitting and photometric uncertainties; SFR offsets are closer to normal fitting errors.
    - BIC preference for BC03 is strongest around `z~1` and weakens toward `z>=6`, partly because rest-frame wavelength coverage changes.
  - **Position:** essential SED-systematics context for any high-z mass or SFR claim, including the CAPERS massive-galaxy result above.

- [2605.15048](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15048) M. Orte-Garcia et al. `The DESIRED electron temperature relations in star-forming regions of the local Universe`
  - **Question:** which empirical electron-temperature relations are reliable when only one auroral diagnostic is measured?
  - **Data:**
    - Database: DESIRED-E with more than `3000` spectra containing direct electron-temperature determinations.
    - Selected sample: `699` spectra, including `513` H II regions and `186` star-forming galaxies.
    - Diagnostics: [N II], [O II], [O III], [S II], [S III], and [Ar III].
    - Metallicity span: `12+log(O/H)=7.12-8.92`.
  - **Method:**
    - Recompute densities and temperatures with PyNeb 1.1.18 and updated atomic data.
    - Fit Te-Te relations using orthogonal distance regression.
    - Quantify total and intrinsic dispersion and identify safe validity ranges.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Low-ionization relations involving [O II] and [S II] show the largest intrinsic dispersions because they are sensitive to density and recombination effects.
    - [N II]-based relations are preferred for estimating low-ionization temperatures when only [O III] or [S III] is available.
    - [S III]-[O III] is calibrated with `224` points and gives `Te([S III])=(0.953+-0.015) Te([O III]) + (580+-150) K`.
    - [N II]-[O III] gives `Te([N II])=(0.763+-0.032) Te([O III]) + (2530+-280) K`.
    - The [N II]-[O III] relation should be limited to `Te([O III])<14000 K`, and [N II]-[S III] to `Te([S III])<16000 K`.
  - **Position:** local calibration, but important for JWST direct-method metallicities because high-z work often has only partial auroral-line coverage.

- [2605.15069](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15069) Keyi Ding et al. `Probing the IMF in the Early Universe -- Direct measurements in the Bootes I UFD with JWST/NIRCam`
  - **Question:** does low-metallicity early-universe-like star formation require a low-mass IMF different from the Milky Way?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST: NIRCam imaging of the Bootes I ultra-faint dwarf galaxy.
    - Stellar sample: about `10,000` member stars resolved down to `<=0.15 M_sun`.
    - Regime: Bootes I has `[Fe/H]~-2.4` and is treated as a local fossil analog of low-mass galaxies at `z>=6`.
  - **Method:**
    - Perform DOLPHOT photometry, quality cuts, member selection, chip-offset correction, and artificial-star tests.
    - Use BaSTI stellar models and forward-model synthetic color-magnitude diagrams.
    - Compare single power-law, broken power-law, and lognormal IMF parameterizations with simulation-based inference.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - A single power-law IMF fails to match the luminosity function and is ruled out at `68%` confidence.
    - Broken power-law and lognormal forms match the observations and are consistent with the Milky Way IMF within `68%` confidence.
    - The broken-power-law break mass is around `0.47 M_sun`.
    - The result supports IMF universality down to `[Fe/H]~-2.4`, assuming Bootes I is a fair high-z fossil analog.
  - **Position:** not a direct high-z galaxy sample, but useful as a fossil constraint on stellar-population assumptions used in high-z mass and light modeling.
