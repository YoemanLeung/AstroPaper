## Executive View

The read-first paper is [2605.04144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04144) G. Quadri. It studies the Big Wheel, a giant massive disk in a `z~3.25` cosmic-web node, using JWST priors and ALMA CO(4-3) kinematics. The inferred halo mass is `log(M_h/M_sun)=12.11+0.29/-0.17`, the stellar mass is `log(M_*/M_sun)=11.00+0.11/-0.12`, and the stellar-to-halo mass ratio is `0.06+0.04/-0.03`, about three times the expected value from common SHM relations. This makes the object a useful stress test for high-z disk formation in overdense environments.

The strongest LRD/early-AGN pair is [2605.05074](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05074) Piero Madau and [2605.04685](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04685) Lulu Bao. Madau models LRDs as dust-intercepted, high-inclination counterparts of compact broad-line AGN at `z>4`, while Bao tests whether local AGN-hosting dwarf galaxies are true LRD analogues. Together they sharpen a key point: LRD-like SED shape alone is not enough to establish the same physical population.

The main theory and survey-support papers are [2605.04776](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04776) Ziyong Wu and [2605.04176](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04176) Yanhui Yang. Wu gives a two-phase SMBH-to-stellar mass evolution model with a broad peak at `z~7-10`. Yang shows that realistic black-hole dynamics in Astrid weaken AGN-driven matter-power suppression, which matters for feedback interpretations in precision cosmology.

The most useful environment and diagnostic papers are [2605.04645](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04645) Dakalo G. Phuravhathu, [2605.04925](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04925) J. H. Costa-Souza, and [2605.05174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05174) M. Polletta. They cover z>1 cluster radio halos, JWST/NIRSpec near-IR excitation diagnostics, and a compact ORC-like source in a group environment.

## Cosmic-Web Nodes, LRDs, and High-z SMBH Growth

- [2605.04144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04144) G. Quadri et al. `The galaxy-halo connection and the dynamical evolution of a giant disc in a massive node of the Cosmic Web at z~3`
  - **Question:** what halo mass, baryonic content, and formation pathway can explain the Big Wheel giant disk in a `z~3` cosmic-web node?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST photometry: priors on the Big Wheel stellar structure and baryonic components.
    - ALMA: Cycle 8 and Cycle 12 CO(4-3) kinematics and dust continuum for the Big Wheel at `z~3.25`.
    - Environment: the MQN01 cosmic-web node hosting the Big Wheel.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit a physically motivated dynamical model to the ALMA CO rotation curve.
    - Use priors from JWST SED fitting and CO luminosity to constrain stellar, gas, bulge, and halo components.
    - Compare the inferred stellar-to-halo mass ratio with empirical SHM relations.
    - Evolve an idealized galaxy model adiabatically for `2.5 Gyr` to test whether internal instabilities would erase the observed disk-like state.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The inferred halo mass is `log(M_h/M_sun)=12.11+0.29/-0.17`.
    - The inferred stellar mass is `log(M_*/M_sun)=11.00+0.11/-0.12`.
    - The stellar-to-halo mass ratio is `0.06+0.04/-0.03`, roughly `3` times higher than standard relations predict.
    - The disk remains stable against strong global instabilities in the `2.5 Gyr` idealized evolution test.
    - The halo spin estimate is high, with `lambda_BW=0.085` or `0.139` depending on the assumed angular-momentum retention.
    - The Big Wheel likely had unusually efficient star formation and no recent major merger, violent disk instability, or strong ejective feedback episode.
  - **Position:** this is the central high-z LSS paper today because it ties galaxy-halo connection, gas accretion, disk survival, and overdense cosmic-web environment into one object.

- [2605.05074](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05074) Piero Madau et al. `Little red dots as obscured little blue dots: relative abundances, luminosities, and black-hole masses`
  - **Question:** can LRDs be explained as dust-reddened, high-inclination views of the same compact broad-line AGN population that appears as little blue dots?
  - **Data:**
    - Observed UV luminosity function of compact broad-line AGN at `z>4`.
    - Photometric LRD luminosity functions from JWST samples.
    - Model comparison in rest-frame UV and optical magnitude bins.
  - **Method:**
    - Draw mock compact broad-line AGN from the observed parent UV luminosity function.
    - Forward-model accretion rate, anisotropic emission, viewing angle, and dusty circumnuclear cloud interception.
    - Apply the same photometric LRD color selection used in current samples.
    - Infer apparent LRD/BLAGN ratios and the implied black-hole mass function.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - A super-Eddington geometry with a thick flow, equatorial broad-line region, and modest dusty cloud covering can reproduce the observed LRD luminosity function.
    - The predicted UV LRD/BLAGN fraction rises from `3%` at `M_1500=-21` to about `20%` near `M_1500=-19`.
    - The apparent fraction is larger at optical wavelengths, reaching `26%` at `M_4500=-20` and `35%` at `M_6500=-21`.
    - The best-fitting cloud extinction is `<A_V>=2.8+0.0/-0.4 mag`.
    - The mean dust covering factor is `<C_dust>=0.23+0.27/-0.00`.
    - UV-selected LRDs host apparently more massive black holes than unobscured LBDs because dust attenuation removes lower-mass obscured systems from the UV-selected sample.
  - **Position:** this is a useful demographic test of LRD unification. It turns the LRD question from "new population or AGN?" into a luminosity-function and orientation-selection problem.

- [2605.04685](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04685) Lulu Bao et al. `Compact, AGN-hosting Dwarf Galaxies with "Little Red Dots"-like SEDs in the Local Universe`
  - **Question:** are local AGN-hosting dwarf galaxies with V-shaped SEDs physically useful analogues of high-z LRDs?
  - **Data:**
    - Local AGN-hosting dwarf galaxies compiled from `45` studies and DESI-identified sources.
    - JWST-era LRD comparison sample: `326` V-shaped LRDs from Akins et al. and Kokorev et al.
    - Multiwavelength photometry, SDSS spectra where available, and morphological fits for `533` local ADGs.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure UV and optical continuum slopes and host effective radii.
    - Use K-means clustering over SED shape, morphology, and color.
    - Compare star formation, metallicity, ionization, broad-line fractions, and black-hole-to-stellar mass placement between ADGs and LRDs.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - About half of the local ADGs show V-shaped SEDs and relatively compact morphologies.
    - The median effective radius of the local ADG sample is `~2100+/-1200 pc`, much larger than the `~100 pc` scale typical of LRDs.
    - The "Compact & V-shape" group is the closest local match but still has `R_e=1.6+0.6/-0.7 kpc`.
    - Local V-shaped compact ADGs are more evolved than high-z LRDs and show different ionization conditions.
    - High-z LRDs have much higher broad-line fractions than the local V-shaped ADG groups.
    - Local ADGs likely follow multiple evolutionary pathways rather than forming a direct low-z continuation of LRDs.
  - **Position:** this paper is an important caution for analogue work. It supports using local compact AGN dwarfs as comparison objects, not as one-to-one physical analogues of high-z LRDs.

- [2605.04776](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04776) Ziyong Wu et al. `Redshift Evolution of the Ratio of Supermassive Black Hole Mass to Stellar Mass`
  - **Question:** can early rapid SMBH growth and later declining duty cycle explain the elevated `M_BH/M_*` ratios seen in high-z galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - High-redshift zoom-in cosmological simulations down to `z=10`.
    - Eight simulations with different supernova feedback and SMBH accretion prescriptions.
    - Observational calibration of an effective duty cycle at `z<=6`.
  - **Method:**
    - Allow super-Eddington accretion before self-regulated SMBH growth.
    - Model later SMBH and host growth with halo assembly histories.
    - Adopt an effective Eddington-duty cycle `f_duty=0.0004(1+z)^3`.
    - Compare simulated and analytic tracks against observed `M_BH/M_*` constraints.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The simulations reproduce high-z `M_BH/M_*` only if super-Eddington accretion is allowed before the final self-regulated phase.
    - The ratio has a broad peak at `z~7-10`, reaching a few percent up to `~30%`.
    - The predicted sequence is `M_BH/M_*=(0.002, 0.003, 0.006, 0.016, 0.071, 0.156)` at `z=(0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 10)`.
    - The peak is driven by early SMBH mass e-folding times shorter than stellar-mass growth times.
    - The later decline occurs because galaxy growth dominates after the early rapid SMBH phase.
  - **Position:** this is directly relevant to JWST overmassive-BH and LRD interpretation. It gives a simple evolutionary track for when high `M_BH/M_*` ratios should be expected.

## Feedback, Matter Clustering, and Cluster Environments

- [2605.04176](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04176) Yanhui Yang et al. `Matter Clustering in Astrid: Reduced Baryonic Suppression from Realistic Black Hole Dynamics`
  - **Question:** does a more realistic treatment of black-hole dynamics weaken AGN-feedback suppression of the matter power spectrum?
  - **Data:**
    - Astrid hydrodynamical simulation and new Astrid-DMO dark-matter-only counterpart.
    - Controlled small-volume simulations with dynamical-friction and repositioning black-hole dynamics.
    - Fiducial and stronger kinetic AGN feedback variants.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure `P_hydro(k)/P_DMO(k)` to quantify baryonic effects.
    - Compare dynamical-friction black-hole dynamics against artificial black-hole repositioning.
    - Track cumulative kinetic feedback energy and AGN X-ray luminosity functions.
    - Test whether stronger feedback preserves agreement with galaxy stellar mass and AGN luminosity functions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Astrid shows no significant matter-power suppression at `z=0` and only mild suppression at `z=0.2`.
    - Black-hole repositioning artificially enhances black-hole mergers and boosts kinetic feedback.
    - In the small-volume comparison, repositioning releases about `2` times more kinetic feedback energy than dynamical friction at `z=1.5`.
    - Kinetic feedback is essential for suppression; thermal-only tests show no suppression over the same range.
    - Simply increasing feedback strength can suppress matter clustering but creates tension with the galaxy stellar mass function and AGN luminosity function.
  - **Position:** this is important for interpreting baryonic feedback in LSS cosmology. It argues that some simulations may overstate AGN-driven suppression because of black-hole numerics.

- [2605.04645](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04645) Dakalo G. Phuravhathu et al. `The MeerKAT Massive Distant Clusters Survey: a search for diffuse radio emission in 30 massive SZ-selected clusters at z > 1`
  - **Question:** how common are diffuse radio halos, relics, and mini-halos in the most massive SZ-selected clusters at `z>1`?
  - **Data:**
    - MMDCS sample: `30` massive ACT SZ-selected clusters at `z>1`.
    - MeerKAT L-band observations centered near `1.28 GHz`.
    - Mock halo injection simulations for non-detection upper limits.
  - **Method:**
    - Image full-resolution and point-source-subtracted MeerKAT maps.
    - Classify radio halos and non-detections.
    - Derive upper limits by injecting model halos into the MeerKAT data.
    - Fit and compare the `P_1.4GHz-M_500c` relation for high-z radio halos.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Diffuse radio halos are detected in `8/30` clusters, a `27%` detection rate.
    - `21` reliable clusters show no diffuse emission; one target is excluded due to poor data quality.
    - No cluster-scale radio relics or mini-halos are detected.
    - The detection rate is lower than at intermediate redshift but higher than the `less than 10%` occurrence commonly predicted at `z>1`.
    - Detected halos scatter around the MMDCS `P_1.4GHz-M_500c` relation, while non-detections lie near the lower envelope.
    - Surface-brightness dimming, inverse-Compton losses, and finite MeerKAT depth strongly shape the observed detection fraction.
  - **Position:** this is the main LSS-environment survey paper today. It provides a high-z cluster non-thermal baseline for merger activity, ICM turbulence, and magnetic-field amplification.

- [2605.05174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05174) M. Polletta et al. `A Compact Radio Ring with a Diffuse Envelope in LOFAR: Odd Radio Circle or Distinct Phenomenon?`
  - **Question:** is J1248+4826 a compact odd radio circle, a related diffuse radio source, or a distinct group-environment phenomenon?
  - **Data:**
    - LOFAR LoTSS 144 MHz and LoLSS 54 MHz imaging.
    - Multiwavelength data from DESI, Spitzer/IRAC, WISE, Herschel, GALEX, VLA, and WSRT.
    - A putative galaxy group at `z=0.2`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure radio morphology, radial profile, spectral index, and luminosity.
    - Model group-galaxy SEDs with CIGALE.
    - Compare the source with known ORCs, radio halos, and mini-halos.
    - Test active radio-galaxy, dying-radio-galaxy, virial-shock, and group-merger interpretations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - J1248+4826 has a ring radius of `~9 arcsec`, corresponding to `~30 kpc` if associated with the `z=0.2` group.
    - The diffuse envelope extends to `~1 arcmin`, or about `~200 kpc` total extent.
    - The diffuse component dominates the radio flux, with about `73%` of the total 144 MHz emission.
    - The ring is much smaller than typical ORCs; known ORC radii are usually `44-365 kpc`.
    - The most plausible explanation is remnant radio plasma re-accelerated by intragroup shocks, possibly driven by group interactions or mergers.
    - Active radio-galaxy and virial-shock interpretations are disfavored by morphology, spectrum, host offset, and scale.
  - **Position:** this is lower redshift, but it is useful for the environment side of diffuse plasma studies. It links group dynamics, fossil AGN plasma, and ORC-like radio morphology.

## AGN Diagnostics, Variability, and Spectral Methods

- [2605.04925](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04925) J. H. Costa-Souza et al. `Near-infrared diagnostic diagrams of the gas ionization sources in nearby galaxies: a JWST NIRSpec view`
  - **Question:** can JWST/NIRSpec near-IR line ratios separate star formation, AGN excitation, and shocks in dusty galaxy nuclei?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRSpec IFU high-resolution spectra of `9` local `z<0.1` U/LIRGs.
    - Near-IR lines including [Fe II], Pa beta, Br gamma, H2, [C I], and the 3.3 um PAH feature.
    - Circumnuclear regions including AGN-dominated, star-forming, and shock-dominated zones.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit stellar continua, emission lines, and PAH 3.3 um features.
    - Classify spaxels using the established `[Fe II]/Pa beta` versus `H2/Br gamma` diagram.
    - Compare new line-ratio diagrams with CLOUDY photoionization models.
    - Measure `W80` line widths to test kinematic separation of excitation classes.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Two proposed diagnostics are `[C I] 0.9852 um/Pa gamma` and `H2 1-0 O(5)/PAH 3.3 um`.
    - The `[C I]/Pa gamma` diagram is effective for identifying shock-excited gas but does not cleanly separate AGN and star formation.
    - The `H2/PAH 3.3 um` diagram separates star formation, AGN, and shocks more directly, but needs confirmation with larger samples.
    - Shock-excited regions have `W80` about `2.5` times larger than AGN-excited regions.
    - SF and AGN regions have similar mean `W80`, around `255 km/s` for [C I] and `266 km/s` for [Fe II].
    - H2 emission in AGN hosts is often a mixture of X-ray heating and shocks, not a single excitation mechanism.
  - **Position:** this is a practical JWST methods paper for obscured AGN and dusty starbursts. It helps interpret NIRSpec line diagnostics in high-z analogues and compact dusty sources.

- [2605.04781](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04781) Bence Becsy et al. `Statistical evidence for massive black hole recoils in active galactic nuclei`
  - **Question:** does the SDSS quasar population show a dust-reddening and broad-line velocity-offset correlation expected for recoiling SMBHs?
  - **Data:**
    - SDSS DR16 quasar catalogue and Wu & Shen emission-line measurements.
    - About `10^5` quasars after spectral quality and consistency cuts.
    - Broad H beta redshift, narrow-line redshifts from [O III], [O II], and Ca II, and `Delta(g-i)` color excess.
  - **Method:**
    - Use the H beta broad-line offset relative to narrow lines as a line-of-sight velocity proxy.
    - Use redshift-relative `Delta(g-i)` as a dust-column proxy.
    - Test correlations between `|Delta v|` and `Delta(g-i)`.
    - Run null tests using narrow-line-only offsets and alternative line estimators.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The correlation between `|Delta v|` and `Delta(g-i)` is modest but highly significant.
    - The Spearman coefficient is `r~0.12`; the Pearson coefficient is `r~0.13`; both have `p << 10^-10`.
    - The highly obscured quasar fraction increases with broad-line velocity offset.
    - Narrow-line-only tests do not reproduce the same positive correlation.
    - If the correlation is due to recoils, a rough comparison to simulations implies that up to `~50%` of quasars may have experienced a recent merger, but this inference is model-dependent.
  - **Position:** this is not high-z-specific, but it is relevant to SMBH merger demographics, AGN geometry, and future LISA-era massive black-hole interpretation.

- [2605.04804](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04804) Sofia Kankkunen et al. `Connecting the long-term variability behaviour of active galactic nuclei to their central engines`
  - **Question:** do long-term radio flare timescales in AGN trace the same central-engine parameters that set PSD bend timescales?
  - **Data:**
    - Long-term `37 GHz` Metsahovi Radio Observatory light curves.
    - `54` AGN sources, with `11` having constrained PSD bend timescales.
    - Black-hole masses, accretion-disk luminosities, Doppler factors, and jet parameters where available.
  - **Method:**
    - Decompose radio light curves into individual exponential flares.
    - Compare mean flare durations and separations with PSD bend timescales.
    - Use flare rise times as proxies for emission-region size.
    - Test Kendall rank correlations with `M_BH`, normalized accretion rate, luminosity, Lorentz factor, and redshift.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The inverse PSD bend frequency corresponds best to the mean duration of the brightest flares.
    - Flare separations also correlate in some sources but have larger scatter.
    - Mean flare duration and separation correlate positively with `M_BH/mdot`, suggesting a central-engine connection.
    - The flare-duration and flare-separation correlation has `tau~0.58` and `p~7.9e-10`.
    - Weak correlations between rise time and central-engine or jet quantities are likely partly driven by redshift.
  - **Position:** this is a useful AGN-variability support paper. It helps connect radio time-domain behavior to accretion and jet physics, but it is not a core high-z result.

- [2605.04434](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04434) Suk Kim et al. `A CNN-Transformer Denoiser for low-S/N Galaxy Spectra: Stellar Population Recovery in Synthetic Tests`
  - **Question:** can a hybrid CNN-Transformer denoiser recover stellar-population information from low-S/N galaxy spectra without spatial binning?
  - **Data:**
    - `90,000` synthetic training spectra from MILES SSP models.
    - `10,000` independent synthetic test spectra.
    - SAMI-like wavelength-dependent noise with `S/N=5-20`.
  - **Method:**
    - Train the Enhanced U-Net Transformer, a one-dimensional CNN-Transformer model.
    - Evaluate full-spectrum residuals and absorption-line recovery.
    - Run pPXF on noisy and denoised spectra to recover mass-weighted age and metallicity.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - EUT reduces full-spectrum RMS residuals by `96.5%` at `S/N=5` and `94%` at `S/N=20`.
    - Recovery rates are at least `99.8%`.
    - At `S/N=5`, mass-weighted age scatter improves from `0.41 dex` to `0.25 dex`.
    - At `S/N=5`, mass-weighted metallicity scatter improves from `0.45 dex` to `0.36 dex`.
    - At `S/N=20`, denoised and noisy pPXF results are broadly consistent.
    - Validation on observed spectra is still required before operational use.
  - **Position:** this is a methods paper. It is useful for IFU and low-surface-brightness stellar-population work, with possible relevance to future faint high-z spectral analyses after real-data validation.

## Lower-Priority or Outside-Profile Items

- [2605.04537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04537) Jianfei Liu et al. `Investigating the Effects of Bars on Star Formation and Nuclear Activity of Galaxies Using DESI Survey Data`
  - DESI DR1 plus Galaxy Zoo DESI analysis of `33,201` disk galaxies at `0.01<z<0.05`.
  - Strong bars enhance central SFR in low-mass galaxies but reduce sSFR in massive systems.
  - Barred galaxies show higher AGN incidence and a larger powerful-AGN fraction, but bar-structure correlations are weak.
  - Useful as low-z secular-evolution context, not central to the high-z profile.

- [2605.04138](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04138) Lina Necib et al. `Galactic Amnesia: The Information Washout of the Milky Way Merger History`
  - Uses mutual information in TNG50 Milky Way analogues, with FIRE-2 comparison, to quantify which present-day stellar chemodynamical observables retain merger-history information.
  - Gravitational potential and total energy preserve merger information longest; radial velocity reaches the noise floor within `~5 Gyr`.
  - Mainly Milky Way archaeology, so it is outside the main profile.

- [2605.04632](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04632) James Dempsey et al. `Revealing the cold skeleton of the Magellanic Clouds and the Magellanic Bridge with ASKAP`
  - GASKAP-HI pilot absorption survey with `3219` sightlines across the LMC, SMC, and Magellanic Bridge.
  - Finds `344` candidate cold-gas detections and a Magellanic Bridge cold neutral fraction `f_CNM=0.12+/-0.08`.
  - Useful for neutral-gas structure, but local and outside the primary high-z scope.

- [2605.04633](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04633) A. Feldmeier-Krause et al. `A spectroscopic map of the Galactic centre: Integrated light and dynamical modelling`
  - Tests triaxial orbit-based dynamical modeling on integrated-light kinematics of the Galactic centre.
  - Useful dynamical-method reference, but Milky-Way focused.

- [2605.04133](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04133) Venkatessh Ramakrishnan et al. `Shaping the future of Global Interferometric Arrays: Imaging Strong Gravity and Magnetic Fields`
  - ALMA2040 community report on future VLBI arrays for strong gravity, magnetic fields, and jet launching.
  - Relevant to AGN instrumentation, but not a galaxy-evolution result.

- [2605.04293](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04293) Daniel Verscharen et al. `Transport of electrons in tangled magnetic fields`
  - Broad review of electron transport in tangled magnetic fields.
  - Useful plasma-background reading for magnetic-field and cosmic-ray work, but not a direct `astro-ph.GA` galaxy report.

- [2605.04890](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04890) Jaume Zuriaga-Puig et al. `Sensitivity of the Cherenkov Telescope Array Observatory to Gamma-Ray Signals in Dwarf Irregular Galaxies`
  - CTAO forecast for gamma-ray and dark-matter signals in dwarf irregular galaxies.
  - Interesting for dark-matter indirect searches, but peripheral to the profile.

- [2605.04801](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04801) Dieter Horns et al. `Contribution of interstellar objects to local dark matter density`
  - Estimates how an optimistic population of interstellar objects could bias local dark-matter density estimates.
  - Galactic mass-budget topic, outside the high-z/LSS/IGM/AGN reading priority.
