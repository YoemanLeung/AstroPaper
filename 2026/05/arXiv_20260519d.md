## Executive View

The read-first paper is [2605.16483](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16483) Andrew Pontzen. It gives a clean physical explanation for why AGN feedback can remove gas from galaxy groups but not from massive clusters. In FLAMINGO, AGN outflows reach a nearly halo-mass-independent entropy ceiling of about `360-370 keV cm^2`. They escape only when that ceiling exceeds the entropy of newly accreting halo gas, which happens below a critical mass near `M_200m~10^13.7 M_sun` in the fiducial model.

[2605.16488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16488) Hiranya V. Peiris is the conceptual companion. It reframes black holes as halo-gas thermostats rather than engines that directly expel gas at all radii. Together these two papers are the strongest AGN-feedback package in today's listing, because they connect black-hole heating, entropy generation, buoyancy, and the halo-mass scale where baryon cycling changes regime.

For JWST-era AGN and Little Red Dots, [2605.16485](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16485) Ritik Sharma proposes a halo-driven growth phase in which black holes become over-massive before hot-halo suppression pushes them back toward local scaling relations. [2605.18161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18161) Paloma Guetzoyan adds an observational low-redshift analog space: X-ray selected red quasars are dust-reddened but also intrinsically X-ray weak, with more than half below `L_X=10^43.5 erg/s`.

The strongest CGM paper is [2605.16924](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16924) Sunil Malik. SPICE-RACS rotation measures show a `4.5 sigma` excess RM dispersion for Mg II absorber sightlines at median `z~1.14`, implying magnetized CGM gas with fields of order `0.4-0.8 microG` under the adopted geometry. This is directly relevant for baryon-cycle and CGM transport work.

For survey planning, [2605.17882](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17882) Yoshiaki Ono uses deep JWST/NIRCam imaging to test source confusion for GREX-PLUS. The result is practical: deep `4 micron` imaging remains useful to roughly `27 mag`, but blending and unresolved faint sources make exposure-time gains increasingly inefficient. [2605.17040](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17040) S. P. Willner reports JWST counterparts for nearly all micro-Jy radio sources in the NEP Time Domain Field, with a median redshift of `1.14` and AGN indicators in `66%` of hosts.

## AGN Feedback, Halo Gas, and LRDs

- [2605.16483](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16483) Andrew Pontzen et al. `The limits of feedback from active galactic nuclei`
  - **Question:** why does AGN feedback strongly deplete gas in galaxy groups but fail to remove gas efficiently from massive clusters?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: FLAMINGO cosmological hydrodynamic simulations with fiducial, strong-feedback, weak-feedback, thermal-feedback, and jet-feedback variants.
    - Halo regime: group and cluster halos spanning the mass scale where observed gas fractions rise toward the cosmic baryon fraction.
    - Observational context: X-ray, thermal-SZ, kinetic-SZ, and fast-radio-burst constraints on hot halo gas motivate the comparison.
  - **Method:**
    - Divide halo gas response into an inner shock-heating zone, an intermediate buoyancy zone, and an outer stalling or termination-shock zone.
    - Track gas inflow and outflow rates, entropy generation, and radial energy transport across halo mass.
    - Interpret the entropy ceiling with shock physics and compare it with the entropy of newly accreting halo gas.
    - Vary AGN feedback strength and injection geometry to test whether the critical mass shifts.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - AGN heating self-limits in the inner halo because gas that is already hot receives little additional entropy from later shocks.
    - Fiducial FLAMINGO outflows reach entropy ceilings of `360` and `370 keV cm^2` in the two quoted resolution variants.
    - Outflows escape when this ceiling exceeds the inflow entropy, which occurs for `M_200m<10^13.7 M_sun` in the fiducial model.
    - Stronger and weaker feedback shift the transition to about `10^14.0 M_sun` and `10^13.5 M_sun`, respectively.
    - Above the transition, cluster inflow entropy rises with the virial relation and AGN-driven gas stalls instead of being expelled.
  - **Position:** core AGN-feedback paper for baryon-cycle work because it turns gas depletion into an entropy-threshold problem rather than a simple energy-budget problem.

- [2605.16488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16488) Hiranya V. Peiris et al. `Thermostats, Not Engines: A New Picture of Halo Gas Regulation`
  - **Question:** should black holes be modeled as engines that push gas out directly, or as thermostats that set the entropy of halo gas near the center?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: FLAMINGO feedback variants, including isotropic thermal and collimated jet implementations.
    - Halo regime: the same group-to-cluster transition where halo gas fractions and star-formation histories change behavior.
  - **Method:**
    - Interpret AGN feedback through the entropy of gas leaving the inner halo rather than through total injected energy alone.
    - Compare virial-radius outflow entropy with halo-mass-dependent inflow entropy.
    - Test how injection geometry changes the solid angle and entropy of directly heated gas.
    - Connect halo gas regulation to later reincorporation and possible rejuvenation of massive galaxies.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Thermal feedback produces a nearly mass-independent entropy ceiling for gas at the virial radius.
    - Jet feedback changes the effective outflow entropy because directly heated gas occupies a restricted solid angle.
    - Virial shocks dominate above `M_crit~10^13.5-10^14 M_sun`, so AGN feedback can no longer remove most newly accreting gas.
    - The thermostat picture predicts that the most massive galaxies can regain fuel when halo growth lifts the inflow entropy above the feedback ceiling.
  - **Position:** best read with [2605.16483](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16483); it provides the conceptual language for interpreting AGN feedback in CGM/ICM and massive-galaxy evolution.

- [2605.16485](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16485) Ritik Sharma et al. `Halo-driven Origin and Suppression of Over-massive Black Holes and Little Red Dots`
  - **Question:** can over-massive black holes and Little Red Dots arise from a temporary halo-driven accretion phase before ordinary host-galaxy scaling relations are restored?
  - **Data:**
    - Model: steady spherical accretion in the combined gravitational potential of an NFW dark-matter halo and a central black hole.
    - Observational targets: JWST-era over-massive black-hole candidates, Little Red Dots, and local black-hole-host scaling relations.
  - **Method:**
    - Compute black-hole growth when halo gravity enhances cold gas inflow into the nucleus.
    - Follow how halo thermodynamics changes as the system crosses into a pressure-supported hot mode.
    - Track the predicted turnover in the `M_BH-M_*` and `M_BH-M_h` relations.
    - Interpret LRDs as the luminous phase of the same over-massive black-hole sequence.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Early halo gravity can drive black-hole growth above the local host-galaxy relation.
    - Hot-halo formation later suppresses inflow and moves the system back toward local black-hole-host scaling.
    - The model predicts a spurt-and-quench sequence: rapid black-hole growth first, then reduced accretion after halo thermodynamics changes.
    - LRDs and over-massive black holes become two observational views of the same transient growth channel.
  - **Position:** high-priority LRD/SMBH theory paper; useful for connecting JWST compact red sources to halo-scale gas physics, but more model-dependent than direct demographic measurements.

- [2605.18161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18161) Paloma Guetzoyan et al. `SDSS-V: Revealing a weak accretion state in X-ray selected red quasars`
  - **Question:** what accretion state, absorption geometry, and host-galaxy phase are traced by X-ray selected red quasars?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `380` red quasars from the eROSITA/SDSS-V collaboration across the German eROSITA hemisphere.
    - Spectroscopy: SDSS-V optical spectra.
    - Multi-wavelength data: optical to X-ray photometry for dust reddening, X-ray luminosity, alpha_OX, and host-galaxy properties.
  - **Method:**
    - Select red quasars from X-ray detections and optical spectroscopy.
    - Reconstruct intrinsic optical spectra with Independent Component Analysis.
    - Correct for dust reddening and host contamination.
    - Compare X-ray luminosity, optical luminosity, gas column, reddening, and host properties against blue-quasar controls.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Red quasars are intrinsically X-ray weak relative to blue quasars, with more than half at `L_X<10^43.5 erg/s`.
    - They are not simply optically faint; dust-corrected optical luminosities are higher on average than in the blue-quasar comparison sample.
    - X-ray columns are large compared with optical reddening, pointing to dust-free absorbing gas close to the black hole plus larger-scale dusty material.
    - The population traces a suppressed black-hole accretion state while host galaxies continue to grow.
  - **Position:** important AGN-evolution context for LRD and obscured-AGN work because it separates red optical colors from a simple high-X-ray-obscuration interpretation.

- [2605.17547](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17547) Dimitra Gerolymatou et al. `Population synthesis of active galactic nuclei based on the radiation-regulated unification model`
  - **Question:** can radiation-regulated AGN unification reproduce the cosmic X-ray background and local obscuration statistics with a self-consistent population model?
  - **Data:**
    - Observables: cosmic X-ray background, AGN number counts, column-density distribution, obscured fraction versus Eddington ratio, and Compton-thick fraction versus flux.
    - Model ingredients: active black-hole mass function, Eddington-ratio distribution, torus geometry, and Eddington-dependent spectra.
  - **Method:**
    - Use simulation-based inference to fit the AGN population model.
    - Generate X-ray emission and obscuration with the RefleX ray-tracing code.
    - Vary torus size, density, covering factor, and active-fraction evolution.
    - Compare predicted absorption demographics with observed X-ray constraints.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The model reproduces the X-ray background and several local absorption observables with a simple active-fraction evolution.
    - The inferred intrinsic Compton-thick fraction is `40+-3%`.
    - The fit constrains how torus covering factor depends on Eddington ratio.
    - Reflection and obscuration correlations become a diagnostic of torus geometry rather than only selection bias.
  - **Position:** useful AGN-population baseline for interpreting obscured fractions in red quasars, LRD analogs, and future X-ray selected samples.

## CGM, Radio Counterparts, and Survey Depth

- [2605.16924](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16924) Sunil Malik et al. `Magnetised CGM Gas at z~1 revealed by SPICE-RACS`
  - **Question:** can Faraday rotation from background quasars detect magnetized circumgalactic gas associated with intervening Mg II absorbers at `z~1`?
  - **Data:**
    - Radio polarization: SPICE-RACS DR2 rotation measures.
    - Absorbers: DESI DR1 quasar sightlines with Mg II absorption.
    - Initial sample: `2483` quasars with RMs, including `612` Mg II absorber sightlines and `1871` controls.
    - Clean sample: `757` quasars after Galactic RM, H I, and Halpha foreground cuts, including `191` absorber sightlines and `566` controls.
    - Redshift range: background quasars over `0.13<z<3.45`, with median absorber redshift `z~1.14`.
  - **Method:**
    - Subtract Galactic RM using an annulus-based foreground estimator.
    - Remove high-foreground sightlines using Milky Way H I and Halpha cuts.
    - Compare residual RM dispersion for Mg II absorber sightlines against matched control sightlines.
    - Infer CGM magnetic-field strengths under assumed gas path lengths and impact-parameter scales of roughly `20-150 kpc`.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Mg II absorber sightlines show excess residual RM dispersion of `4.13+-0.91 rad m^-2`.
    - The detection significance is `4.5 sigma`.
    - The implied CGM magnetic field is of order `0.4-0.8 microG` for the adopted geometry.
    - Substantial CGM magnetization is already present by `z~1`.
  - **Position:** strong CGM paper because it adds magnetic-field information to absorber-selected halo gas, complementing metallicity and ionization diagnostics.

- [2605.17040](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17040) S. P. Willner et al. `PEARLS: JWST Counterparts of Micro-Jy Radio Sources in the NEP Time Domain Field. II. All Four Spokes`
  - **Question:** what are the JWST/NIRCam counterparts and host-galaxy properties of micro-Jy radio sources in the NEP Time Domain Field?
  - **Data:**
    - Radio sample: `211` sources with `S(3 GHz)~5 microJy` in a `65 arcmin^2` field.
    - JWST imaging: NIRCam `4.4 micron` data from PEARLS in the North Ecliptic Pole Time Domain Field.
    - Counterparts: `206` radio sources matched to `4.4 micron` sources.
  - **Method:**
    - Match radio source positions to JWST/NIRCam detections.
    - Test counterpart recovery with a `0.3 arcsec` simple positional match.
    - Use aggressive deblending in the `4.4 micron` catalog to separate multiple peaks inside complex galaxy light distributions.
    - Classify likely radio origin from host properties and radio luminosity after accounting for the non-linear radio-SFR relation.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - JWST detects counterparts for nearly all radio sources in the field.
    - All but five counterparts are brighter than `23.5 AB` at `4.4 micron`.
    - The median redshift is `1.14`.
    - Radio emission is consistent with star formation in about `79%` of the sample, while the remaining `21%` may include dust-hidden star formation or AGN.
    - At least one AGN indicator is present in `66%` of radio hosts.
  - **Position:** useful JWST-radio bridge paper for AGN and dusty star-formation demographics; this entry is abstract-only because arXiv HTML was unavailable during the run.

- [2605.17882](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17882) Yoshiaki Ono et al. `Assessing the Impact of Source Confusion for GREX-PLUS based on Deep JWST NIRCam Imaging`
  - **Question:** how strongly will source confusion limit deep `4 micron` imaging with the proposed GREX-PLUS space telescope?
  - **Data:**
    - Mission concept: GREX-PLUS, a JAXA L-class candidate with a `1.0 m` primary mirror, `2-8 micron` wide-field camera, and `0.50 deg^2` field of view.
    - Input images: deep JWST/NIRCam imaging from JADES, GLASS, CEERS, and COSMOS-Web.
    - Simulated PSFs: `0.9 arcsec` and `1.2 arcsec` FWHM, including PSF wings and ghosts from the latest optical design.
  - **Method:**
    - Convolve JWST images to GREX-PLUS image quality and add mission-like noise properties.
    - Measure limiting magnitudes with random-aperture statistics.
    - Run Monte Carlo source recovery tests for completeness and blending.
    - Correct number counts for magnitude bias and incompleteness near the detection limit.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - GREX-PLUS simulated images become shallower than the input JWST fields because unresolved faint sources and bright-source PSF wings add confusion noise.
    - Blank-aperture limits improve down to roughly `27 mag` without a hard plateau at planned deep-survey depths.
    - Exposure-time gains become inefficient as confusion increases.
    - Blending can reduce completeness even above the nominal `5 sigma` depth.
    - Corrected galaxy number counts remain consistent with JWST counts near the detection limit.
  - **Position:** practical high-z survey-planning paper because it quantifies where future wide-field mid-infrared depth is limited by astrophysical crowding rather than detector noise alone.

## Galaxy Images, L-star Hosts, and Local AGN Anchors

- [2605.17546](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17546) Tianyue Yang et al. `Accelerating Redshift-Conditioned Galaxy Image Synthesis with One-step Generative Modeling`
  - **Question:** can one-step generative models synthesize redshift-conditioned galaxy images fast enough for survey-scale use while preserving key morphology statistics?
  - **Data:**
    - Dataset: GalaxiesML-64.
    - Metrics: ellipticity, semi-major axis, Sersic index, isophotal area, and morphology-distribution statistics.
  - **Method:**
    - Train and evaluate redshift-conditioned image-generation models.
    - Compare DDPM, DDIM, DEIS-AB2, DPM++2M, and pixel-MeanFlow samplers.
    - Measure fidelity against morphology and distributional metrics rather than image appearance alone.
    - Compare performance as a function of sampling cost.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Standard DDPM gives the best distributional fidelity but remains computationally expensive.
    - Second-order samplers are substantially more efficient than DDIM.
    - Pixel-MeanFlow provides single-step generation with competitive performance on several morphology statistics.
    - Fine structure still favors multi-step diffusion, but one-step generation is a credible acceleration route for large mock-image workflows.
  - **Position:** useful methods paper for survey simulations and selection-function studies, especially when fast morphology-conditioned image generation matters more than perfect visual realism.

- [2605.16501](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16501) Salvador E. Grimozzi et al. `Co-evolution of Supermassive Black Holes and their Host L-star galaxies: implications for Milky Way and M31`
  - **Question:** what drives the scatter in black-hole masses among L-star galaxies such as the Milky Way and M31?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: ARTEMIS and EAGLE.
    - Galaxy regime: L-star galaxies classified by low or high central black-hole-to-stellar mass ratio.
    - Comparison targets: Milky Way and M31 black-hole and host-galaxy properties.
  - **Method:**
    - Track low-BH-mass and high-BH-mass L-star galaxies from `z~2` to the present.
    - Compare gas fractions, star-formation histories, morphologies, and merger histories.
    - Separate black-hole growth by gas accretion from growth by black-hole mergers.
    - Relate present-day morphology to cumulative merger activity.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Low-BH-mass and high-BH-mass galaxies have similar properties at `z~2` but diverge toward low redshift.
    - High-BH-mass systems form stars earlier, grow black holes faster, experience stronger feedback, and quench more efficiently.
    - In high-BH-mass galaxies, black holes grow primarily by gas accretion, at about `90%` of the total.
    - In low-BH-mass galaxies, growth is split between gas accretion at about `60%` and black-hole mergers at about `40%`.
    - The different black-hole masses of the Milky Way and M31 can be explained by different merger histories.
  - **Position:** lower-redshift anchor for the AGN-feedback sequence; useful for connecting halo assembly history to black-hole scatter and host morphology.

- [2605.17993](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17993) M. N. Rosli et al. `Nuclear Activity and Host Galaxy Properties of Low-Luminosity AGN Identified from VLA Observations`
  - **Question:** how well do X-ray, optical, and infrared diagnostics recover low-luminosity AGN selected by compact radio emission?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `38` low-luminosity AGN from a sub-arcsecond `15 GHz` VLA survey.
    - Luminosity regime: `L_bol<10^42 erg/s`, with typical `log L_bol~39.3-41.9 erg/s`.
    - Comparisons: Swift-BAT AGN and multi-wavelength diagnostic selections.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare optical emission-line, X-ray, and infrared recovery rates for the radio-selected LLAGN sample.
    - Estimate black-hole masses, Eddington ratios, stellar masses, star-formation rates, and host morphologies.
    - Contrast host and accretion properties with higher-luminosity Swift-BAT AGN.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Optical diagnostics recover `84.2%` of the sample, X-rays recover `63.2%`, and infrared methods recover only `13.2%`.
    - LLAGN are about `4.1 dex` fainter in bolometric luminosity than Swift-BAT AGN.
    - They host black holes about `0.7 dex` less massive and accrete about `4.2 dex` lower in Eddington ratio.
    - Host galaxies show mixed morphologies, lower stellar masses, and reduced star-formation rates relative to Swift-BAT AGN hosts.
  - **Position:** useful local diagnostic anchor for weak accretion, but lower priority than the red-quasar and LRD entries because the sample is local and low-luminosity.
