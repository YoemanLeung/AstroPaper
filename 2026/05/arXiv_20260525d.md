## Executive View

The read-first entry is [2605.23049](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23049) Jie Song. It combines `34,182` SDSS galaxies and `863` JWST/JADES galaxies to build a consistent stellar and nebular dust-attenuation census over `z~0-7`. The useful result is a mass-driven attenuation relation with little redshift evolution at fixed mass and a transition near `M_*~10^9 M_sun`.

The cosmic-dawn companion is [2605.22914](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22914) A. Ferrara. It interprets UV-blue and UV-red `z>10` galaxies in an attenuation-free model where radiation-driven outflows redistribute dust. It offers an evolutionary sequence from a dust-obscured red phase to a UV-bright blue phase, while compact sources remain harder to explain without AGN.

The environment side is strong. [2605.23457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23457) Daria Zakharova uses TNG50 to isolate filament effects on low-mass galaxies, separating altered accretion, starvation, and cosmic-web stripping. [2605.23314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23314) Haoran Dou shows that group infall both pre-processes galaxies and partly protects them after cluster entry.

For gas and survey infrastructure, [2605.23662](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23662) Daichi Kashino measures ALMA dust continuum for massive `z~1.5` galaxies and finds main-sequence scatter driven by both gas fraction and depletion time. [2605.23399](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23399) D. Espada introduces ALMA CO-CAVITY, a resolved CO survey of `41` void galaxies.

## Dust, Super-Early Galaxies, and Cosmic Noon Gas

- [2605.23049](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23049) Jie Song et al. `SDSS+JWST Census of Stellar and Nebular Dust Attenuation at z ~ 0-7: Mass Dependence and Redshift Evolution`
  - **Question:** how do stellar and nebular dust attenuation depend on stellar mass from the local Universe to `z~7`?
  - **Data:**
    - SDSS: `34,182` galaxies at `z~0.1`.
    - JWST/JADES: `863` galaxies at `z~1.5-7`.
    - Supplementary data: GALEX and rest-frame UV-optical spectra.
  - **Method:**
    - Derive nebular attenuation from Balmer decrements.
    - Derive stellar attenuation from UV-optical spectral modeling with multiple attenuation curves.
    - Compare mass trends across redshift with consistent methods.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At fixed `M_*`, stellar and nebular attenuation show little significant redshift evolution from `z~0` to `z~7`.
    - Both attenuation measures rise from `A_V~0.2-0.4` at `M_*<~10^9 M_sun` to `A_V~1` near `M_*~10^11 M_sun`.
    - The transition near `M_*~10^9 M_sun` controls the stellar-to-nebular color-excess ratio, shifting from `~1.0` toward `~0.44`.
  - **Position:** high-priority JWST calibration paper because dust corrections affect essentially every high-redshift SFR and stellar-population inference.

- [2605.22914](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22914) A. Ferrara et al. `No Blue without Red: Evolutionary Properties of Super-Early Galaxies`
  - **Question:** can one dust-outflow framework explain both blue and red luminous galaxies at `z>10`?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `32` spectroscopically confirmed super-early galaxies.
    - Focus: UV colors, sizes, halo masses, star-formation efficiencies, metallicities, and outflow extents.
  - **Method:**
    - Apply the attenuation-free model in which radiation-driven outflows move dust to large radii.
    - Infer physical properties and test an evolutionary path from obscured to UV-bright stages.
    - Compare compact sources against model expectations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Typical inferred halos have `log M/M_sun~10.7`.
    - Star-formation efficiencies are moderate, around `0.01-0.05`.
    - The model produces a red obscured phase followed by a UV-bright blue phase as outflows clear the center.
    - Compact sources with `r_e<~150 pc` remain difficult for the model and may require AGN-dominated emission.
  - **Position:** important cosmic-dawn interpretation paper because it connects dust geometry, outflows, and the diversity of JWST `z>10` colors.

- [2605.23662](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23662) Daichi Kashino et al. `Gas Fraction and Depletion Time Drive the Main-Sequence Scatter in Massive Galaxies at z~1.5`
  - **Question:** what drives the star-forming main-sequence scatter in massive cosmic-noon galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - ALMA Band 7: dust continuum for `57` massive star-forming galaxies at `1.45<z<1.70`.
    - Parent sample: FMOS-COSMOS, selected near the main sequence.
  - **Method:**
    - Infer dust masses and molecular gas masses using metallicity-dependent gas-to-dust ratios.
    - Compare gas fraction, SFE, and offset from the main sequence.
    - Test the integrated Schmidt-Kennicutt relation.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - ALMA detects `55/57` targets above `3 sigma`.
    - Molecular gas ratios span `0.11-2.8`, with median `0.65`.
    - Gas fraction and SFE both scale roughly as `(sSFR/sSFR_MS)^0.5`.
    - The main-sequence scatter is driven by both gas content and depletion time.
  - **Position:** useful cosmic-noon gas-regulation paper because it tests whether gas supply and efficiency jointly shape SFR scatter.

## Environment, Filaments, and Cluster Infall

- [2605.23457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23457) Daria Zakharova et al. `Cosmic web stripping and starvation of low-mass filament galaxies in TNG50`
  - **Question:** how do filaments alter low-mass galaxy gas reservoirs when group and cluster effects are controlled?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: TNG50-1.
    - Sample: low-mass galaxies with `8<=log(M_star/M_sun)<=10` in filaments and field environments.
  - **Method:**
    - Match galaxies by stellar and halo mass.
    - Exclude group and cluster effects.
    - Track gas-disc sizes, asymmetries, accretion histories, and filament-entry times.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Integrated masses and quenched fractions are similar between filament and field controls.
    - Filament galaxies have smaller and more asymmetric cold-gas disks.
    - Early filament entry can alter gas and dark-matter accretion geometry or produce starvation.
    - Late filament entry can trigger hydrodynamical gas removal analogous to ram pressure.
  - **Position:** strong cosmic-web environment paper because it identifies gas morphology as a sensitive tracer before global properties diverge.

- [2605.23314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23314) Haoran Dou et al. `The dual effect of group-scale environments on galaxy quenching during cluster infall: pre-processing and protection`
  - **Question:** how does group accretion change quenching during cluster infall?
  - **Data:**
    - Cluster sample: `25` low-redshift X-ray luminous massive clusters.
    - Galaxy sample: large spectroscopic sample around those clusters.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify substructures with the Blooming Tree algorithm.
    - Separate group-accreted galaxies from isolated infallers.
    - Measure quiescent fraction along an `R-V` infall proxy.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The quiescent fraction rises toward cluster centers after a transition near `d_R~2.5`.
    - Group galaxies are more quenched at early infall, consistent with pre-processing.
    - After cluster entry, their quenching increase is delayed relative to isolated galaxies, consistent with group-scale protection.
  - **Position:** relevant cluster-infall result because it shows that groups are not only pre-processing sites but also buffers inside clusters.

- [2605.23399](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23399) D. Espada et al. `ALMA CO-CAVITY I. Resolved Molecular Gas in Void Galaxies`
  - **Question:** how do molecular gas distributions in void galaxies compare with galaxies in denser environments?
  - **Data:**
    - ALMA: CO(1-0) survey of `41` void galaxies.
    - CAVITY: optical IFU data for pixel-by-pixel comparisons.
  - **Method:**
    - Produce cubes, moment maps, position-velocity diagrams, and matched-resolution surface-density maps.
    - Compare global scaling relations with unresolved and resolved literature surveys.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Integrated void-galaxy scaling relations are compatible with denser-environment samples.
    - The molecular-gas main sequence has the smallest reported scatter, `0.21 dex`.
    - The dataset enables future resolved tests of how isolation affects molecular gas and star formation.
  - **Position:** high-value environment data release because void galaxies provide a clean large-scale-environment control sample.

## Radio, H I, and Survey Classification

- [2605.23750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23750) Tong Pan et al. `Probing the environments of FRI and FRII radio galaxies in LoTSS DR2 with galaxy clusters`
  - **Question:** does cluster-scale environment drive the FRI/FRII radio morphology split?
  - **Data:**
    - LoTSS DR2: morphologically classified radio galaxies at `z<0.4`.
    - DESI Legacy Imaging Survey: galaxy cluster catalogue.
  - **Method:**
    - Build volume-limited and luminosity-redshift paired radio samples.
    - Cross-match radio galaxies to clusters within `|Delta z|<0.01` and `<2 R_500`.
    - Compare cluster association, BCG fractions, radial distributions, and cluster richness/mass trends.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - FRIs are more often cluster-associated than FRIIs: `48.6%` versus `30.6%` in the volume-limited sample.
    - At `L_144>10^26 W Hz^-1`, the difference strengthens to `55.6%` versus `19.0%`.
    - Once inside clusters, FRIs and FRIIs occupy similar large-scale environments.
  - **Position:** useful AGN-environment paper because dense intracluster gas may suppress stable FRII structures without fully determining morphology.

- [2605.23686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23686) Chen Xu et al. `The FAST Hundred-Deg^2 HI Deep (HD^2) Survey: Early Results from the Pilot Survey`
  - **Question:** what H I yield does a deep FAST field produce in a DESI/HSC-overlap region?
  - **Data:**
    - FAST HD2 pilot: `10 deg^2` overlapping HSC-SSP and DESI EDR SV3.
    - Sensitivity: `0.45 mJy/beam` at `4.8 km/s` in the pilot field.
  - **Method:**
    - Build H I detections from the pilot cube.
    - Cross-match with optical imaging and spectroscopy.
    - Compare yield against shallower H I surveys.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The pilot identifies `339` H I sources at `z<0.09`.
    - The surface density is about `34` detections per square degree.
    - The survey is positioned to support joint H I, optical, and LSS studies in the DESI footprint.
  - **Position:** lower-redshift than the core profile, but useful for gas-rich galaxy and survey-overlap planning.

- [2605.23388](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23388) Sara Jamal et al. `Performance analysis of extragalactic classifications in Gaia Data Release 4`
  - **Question:** how reliable will Gaia DR4 quasar and galaxy classifications be for extragalactic sample construction?
  - **Data:**
    - Gaia DR4 Discrete Source Classifier outputs from astrometry, photometry, and XP spectra.
    - Optional CatWISE2020 mid-infrared information.
  - **Method:**
    - Evaluate classifier completeness and purity versus magnitude and sky position.
    - Compare Gaia-only and Gaia+infrared models.
    - Provide quality-cut recommendations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - For `G<20`, best classifiers reach at least `88%` completeness and `96%` purity for quasar/galaxy classes.
    - At `20<=G<20.5`, performance drops to at least `55%` completeness and `71%` purity.
    - Adding infrared photometry increases faint extragalactic completeness by `9-29` percentage points with a modest purity cost.
  - **Position:** survey-infrastructure entry, relevant for building clean quasar and galaxy samples.
