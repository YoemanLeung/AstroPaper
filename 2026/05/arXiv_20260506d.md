The 2026-05-06 listing contains 23 new submissions and 5 cross submissions. I weighted the ranking toward the public profile themes: high-redshift galaxies, reionization, IGM/CGM gas flows, quasars/AGN and feedback, large-scale structure, protocluster or environment work, JWST/NIRCam/MIRI studies, and survey-scale tools that affect those topics.

## High-z Galaxies, Reionization, and JWST Discovery Space

### 2605.03008 Aleyna Doven et al. [Environmental Quenching of High-Redshift Galaxies: Interpreting JWST Observations with Simulations]

- Question: Can environmental quenching explain low-mass quenched galaxies at z>3 discovered by JWST, and do current simulations produce physically plausible analogues?
- Data:
  - Simulations: L-GALAXIES, IllustrisTNG/TNG300, SIMBA, and TNG-Cluster.
  - Population scale: more than half a million simulated galaxies at z=5.
  - Observational context: JWST low-mass quenched systems with `M_* < 10^10 Msun`, including objects such as GS-z5-Q1 and COS-z5-Q1.
- Method:
  - Classifies simulated galaxies into quenched/star-forming and central/satellite populations.
  - Compares stellar mass, host halo mass, halocentric distance, gas fractions, and environmental exposure.
  - Tracks ram-pressure effects and the future fates of simulated analogues.
  - Tests whether simulated low-mass quenched satellites occupy the same parameter space as JWST high-z quenched candidates.
- Conclusion:
  - Quenched systems at z=5 are overwhelmingly satellites even though satellites are less than 10% of the simulated galaxy population.
  - Satellite quenching is stronger in more massive host halos, at smaller halocentric distance, and under stronger ram pressure.
  - Simulated analogues, especially in L-GALAXIES, broadly match the stellar masses and environments of JWST low-mass quenched galaxies.
  - About 90% of these analogues merge or disrupt by z=0, while rejuvenation is rare at less than 10%.
  - The physical picture is environmental gas removal or starvation in early overdense regions, not isolated mass quenching.
- Position:
  - This is the most profile-matched paper today. It links JWST high-z quiescent galaxy discoveries to protocluster-like satellite processing and gives a concrete simulation baseline for interpreting early low-mass quenching.

### 2605.03635 Zihan Wang et al. [The JWST early galaxy crisis resolved by a reionization degeneracy]

- Question: Is the apparent overabundance of bright z>10 JWST galaxies a real crisis, or can it be resolved by the degeneracy between star-formation efficiency and ionizing escape fraction during reionization?
- Data:
  - JWST UV luminosity functions at z=9, 10, 11, and 12.5.
  - Global reionization observables that constrain the product of escape fraction and star-formation efficiency.
  - Scatter models for galaxy UV output, including Gaussian, log-normal, and duty-cycle burst prescriptions.
- Method:
  - Uses the degeneracy ridge between `f_esc` and `f_*,0` from reionization constraints.
  - Adds JWST UV luminosity-function shapes to constrain the normalization of star-formation efficiency independently.
  - Profiles the likelihood across different assumptions for luminosity scatter.
  - Reconstructs `f_esc(z)` over z=7-12 and compares constant-efficiency and evolving-efficiency pathways.
- Conclusion:
  - Global reionization data constrain the product `f_esc x f_*,0`, not the two factors independently.
  - The high-efficiency "crisis" threshold is excluded at 4.5 sigma for duty-cycle burst models and above 3 sigma for all three scatter prescriptions tested.
  - A constant-efficiency scenario gives `f_esc ~ 10-16%` at z=7-12, naturally connecting to lower-redshift direct detections.
  - An evolving-efficiency scenario can require `f_esc ~ 6%` at z=12, which is harder to reconcile with expectations for porous, low-metallicity ISM conditions.
  - JWST Cycle 3-4 luminosity functions should distinguish the main pathways at better than 2 sigma.
- Position:
  - This is a high-priority theory guardrail for interpreting JWST bright-galaxy counts. It is especially useful when connecting UV luminosity functions, ionizing escape, and reionization history.

### 2605.03442 Julia Dima et al. [High-Redshift Gravitational Lens Discoveries in JWST NIRCam Using AnomalyMatch]

- Question: Can a semi-supervised anomaly-matching approach find rare high-redshift gravitational lenses in JWST NIRCam archives with only a small initial set of known lenses?
- Data:
  - JWST NIRCam Level 3 products from ESA Datalabs.
  - ASTRODEEP and COSMOS-Web fields covering about 0.74 deg^2.
  - 614,015 NIRCam cutouts, including 314,449 from ASTRODEEP and 299,566 from COSMOS-Web.
  - Initial training set of 11 known lenses.
- Method:
  - Uses AnomalyMatch with iterative human-in-the-loop training.
  - Starts from known lenses, searches for morphologically similar anomalies, and retrains over multiple cycles.
  - Applies expert grading into high-confidence, intermediate, and lower-confidence lens candidates.
- Conclusion:
  - Finds 58 unique lens candidates: 16 Grade A, 16 Grade B, and 26 Grade C.
  - Reports 37 previously uncatalogued systems, including 9 new Grade A lenses.
  - The highest-redshift newly discovered system has a photometric redshift around z=2.1.
  - The method works despite starting from only 11 labelled examples, showing that JWST lens searches can benefit strongly from semi-supervised anomaly discovery.
- Position:
  - This is a useful JWST archive-mining paper. It is not a physical high-z galaxy interpretation paper, but it improves access to rare magnified systems that can become high-z galaxy and CGM targets.

### 2605.02997 Lucas C. Kimmig et al. [Built to Rest: The Evolving Star-Forming Main Sequence Requires Episodic Quiescence or Late Assembly]

- Question: Can galaxies evolve along the observed star-forming main sequence from high redshift to the present without overproducing the stellar masses and ages of present-day descendants?
- Data:
  - Analytical models for main-sequence evolution from z=6 to z=0.
  - Observed star-formation histories and mean ages of local galaxies.
  - Milky Way and Andromeda as reference descendants.
- Method:
  - Evolves galaxies along empirical star-forming main-sequence parameterizations.
  - Uses number-density and descendant-mass constraints to test whether continuous main-sequence growth is plausible.
  - Compares predicted histories with Local Group constraints.
- Conclusion:
  - Low-mass galaxies with `M_* >= 10^8 Msun` already on the main sequence at z>3 naturally evolve into `M_* ~ 10^11 Msun` systems by today.
  - Intermediate-mass galaxies with `M_* ~ 10^10 Msun` must either assemble late at z<2 or spend more than 1 Gyr at least 0.3 dex below the main sequence.
  - Milky-Way-mass descendants require star-formation rates about 0.3 dex below the main sequence since z=3.
  - The Milky Way likely experienced quenching or near-quiescence with `Delta_SFMS <= -0.5 dex`.
- Position:
  - This is broader galaxy-evolution context rather than a direct high-z observational result. It is useful for interpreting whether high-z star-forming populations can be simple progenitors of present-day disks.

## Gas Flows, Feedback, and Analogue Environments

### 2605.02999 S. Weng et al. [Peering down the barrel with DESI DR2: 10 000+ inflows at z < 0.6 reveal how galaxies accrete cold gas]

- Question: How common are cold-gas inflows in low-redshift galaxies, and what do they imply about galaxy accretion, orientation, and minor-merger feeding?
- Data:
  - DESI DR2 spectra of 15.6 million galaxies at z<0.6.
  - Down-the-barrel Na I D absorption.
  - Galaxy orientation and host-property measurements.
- Method:
  - Fits local continua around Na I D and models absorption components.
  - Uses nested sampling and Bayesian evidence ratios to identify systemic and flow components.
  - Separates low-velocity inflows, systemic absorption, and outflows.
  - Tests flow velocity trends with inclination, stellar mass, and stellar velocity dispersion.
- Conclusion:
  - Produces catalogues of 50,088 moderate-evidence and 27,420 strong-evidence absorbers.
  - About 50% of velocity components have `v_flow < -50 km/s`, about 30% are within 50 km/s of systemic, and about 20% have `v_flow > 50 km/s`.
  - Edge-on galaxies show strong evidence for low-velocity inflows of roughly 20 km/s, consistent with radial inflows in simulations.
  - In early-type galaxies, inflow velocity correlates more strongly with stellar velocity dispersion than with stellar mass, pointing to accreting satellites or minor mergers.
- Position:
  - This is a low-z paper, but it is a major baryon-cycle reference. It gives a DESI-scale empirical baseline for cold accretion signatures that can be compared with CGM and high-z inflow discussions.

### 2605.03016 Kylie Yui Dan et al. [Resolving the Multiphase Outflow, Shock Signatures, and PAHs in the AGN-Starburst Composite ULIRG F10565+2448 with JWST MIRI/MRS]

- Question: What drives and regulates the multiphase outflow in the AGN-starburst composite ULIRG F10565+2448, and how do shocks and PAHs trace the feedback structure?
- Data:
  - JWST MIRI/MRS spectroscopy from 4.9 to 27.9 um.
  - F10565+2448, a starburst-AGN composite ULIRG with SFR around 150 Msun/yr.
  - Warm-ionized lines including [Ne II], [Ne III], [Ne V], [Fe II], molecular hydrogen lines, and PAH features.
- Method:
  - Reduces and analyzes MIRI/MRS spectral cubes.
  - Separates disk and outflow components in warm-ionized and warm-molecular gas.
  - Uses H2 excitation, [Fe II]/Pfund-alpha shock diagnostics, and PAH ratio gradients.
  - Compares outflow velocities with disk kinematics and escape velocity.
- Conclusion:
  - Finds an unresolved warm-ionized outflow with velocity up to about `-520 km/s`.
  - Finds a warm molecular nuclear outflow near `-150 km/s` and a resolved warm-molecular outflow with `-280 < v50 < -110 km/s`.
  - Most warm molecular outflowing gas is near or only slightly above disk velocity and below the escape speed of at least about 300 km/s; only about 0.01% of the H2 S(1) flux exceeds escape.
  - The outflow is hotter than the disk, about `507 +/- 25 K` versus `329 +/- 5 K`.
  - The warm ionized outflow mass is about `5e6 Msun` with a rate near `3 Msun/yr`; the warm molecular outflow mass is about `7e6 Msun` with total rate around `3.4 Msun/yr`.
  - Both star formation and AGN or jet-related processes are needed for the energetics; pure radiation pressure is insufficient.
- Position:
  - This is a strong JWST/MIRI feedback case study. It is most useful as a nearby analogue for interpreting AGN-starburst composite systems and multiphase feedback coupling.

### 2605.03154 James M. Sullivan et al. [ArkenstoneBH. A model for high-specific energy black hole feedback in cosmological simulations]

- Question: How can cosmological simulations represent high-specific-energy black-hole feedback that couples weakly to dense ISM gas but strongly affects the CGM?
- Data:
  - Subgrid model tests in AREPO and isolated galaxy simulations.
  - Arkenstone Hot implementation, with future Arkenstone Cold extensions planned for multiphase cold clouds.
  - Comparisons against no black-hole feedback and IllustrisTNG-style feedback.
- Method:
  - Launches high-specific-energy wind or jet particles tied to black-hole accretion.
  - Hydrodynamically decouples the feedback carriers and recouples them near the ISM/CGM boundary or by specified recoupling criteria.
  - Uses targeted refinement in outflow regions to better capture CGM energy deposition.
  - Varies wind velocity, feedback efficiency, and recoupling prescriptions.
- Conclusion:
  - The model captures hot, low-mass, high-specific-energy feedback that mostly heats the CGM instead of immediately coupling to cold dense ISM gas.
  - Star formation is suppressed primarily through preventative feedback: reducing gas inflow from the CGM into the ISM.
  - The recoupling prescription strongly controls the outcome because the location of energy deposition is decisive.
  - Higher velocity or higher feedback efficiency broadens the heated CGM volume and suppresses star formation more strongly.
  - The paper sets up a future full multiphase ArkenstoneBH framework including cold clouds.
- Position:
  - This is a methods paper, but it is directly relevant to AGN feedback, quenching, and CGM thermodynamics. It is useful when comparing how different simulation models create quenched galaxies.

### 2605.03676 A. Arroyo-Polonio et al. [Not So Isolated: Green Pea Galaxies in Overdense Environments revealed by VLT/MUSE]

- Question: Are Green Pea galaxies isolated compact starbursts, or do they preferentially live in gas-rich overdense environments that may sustain extreme emission?
- Data:
  - VLT/MUSE observations of 24 Green Pea galaxies at z~0.2.
  - Emission-line diagnostics including Halpha, Hbeta, [O III], and [O II].
  - Companion searches within `|Delta v| <= 500 km/s`.
- Method:
  - Searches each MUSE cube for emission-line galaxies near the Green Pea redshift and over a wider velocity range for field comparison.
  - Derives extinction, star-formation rate, stellar mass, age, and metallicity with line diagnostics and SED-based tools.
  - Estimates group dynamical masses from companion phase-space information.
- Conclusion:
  - Detects 22 emission-line galaxies and 11 companions within the companion velocity window.
  - Infers a companion fraction of `33 +11/-8%`, corresponding to about a 1 dex number-density excess relative to the field.
  - Companions are usually at projected separations near 100 kpc, so the data do not point to widespread ongoing major interactions.
  - Green Peas are young, with ages around 230 Myr, while companions are older at about 1.6 Gyr.
  - Dynamical masses are around `2.6e12 Msun`, roughly 3 dex above the stellar masses, suggesting large dark-matter or neutral-gas reservoirs.
  - Green Peas may be transient starbursts in overdense, gas-rich regions, possibly maintained by gas accretion.
- Position:
  - This is a useful low-z analogue paper for Lyman-alpha and LyC-emitting galaxies. It supports the idea that extreme emission can be tied to environment and gas supply rather than only internal compactness.

## Stellar Populations, Lensing Systematics, and Lower-Priority Tools

### 2605.03000 Aliza G. Beverage et al. [Are Nucleosynthetic Yields Universal? Interpreting the Multi-Elemental Abundances of Quiescent Galaxies over Cosmic Time Using Milky Way Stars]

- Question: Can empirical abundance patterns from Milky Way stars explain multi-element abundances in quiescent galaxies across cosmic time without invoking radically different nucleosynthetic yields?
- Data:
  - Quiescent galaxies from SDSS at z~0, LEGA-C at z~0.7, and JWST/SUSPENSE at z~2.
  - Multi-element abundance measurements: 14 elements for SDSS, 11 for LEGA-C, and 6 for SUSPENSE.
  - Milky Way APOGEE stars as empirical yield calibrators.
- Method:
  - Splits Milky Way disk stars into high-Ia and low-Ia abundance sequences.
  - Uses Mg and Fe to infer the relative contribution of core-collapse supernovae and Type Ia supernovae.
  - Predicts quiescent-galaxy abundance ratios from `[Mg/H]` and `[Mg/Fe]`.
  - Tests the direction and amplitude of possible top-heavy IMF effects.
- Conclusion:
  - Empirical Milky Way-based yields reproduce alpha and Fe-peak abundances with median offsets of about 0.03 dex in SDSS, 0.06 dex in LEGA-C, and 0.06 dex in SUSPENSE.
  - The overall mismatch is around 0.05 dex, much better than the roughly 0.23 dex mismatch from theoretical yield tables.
  - Mg and Fe alone encode much of the core-collapse versus Type Ia enrichment balance for alpha and Fe-peak elements.
  - Failures occur for N, Sr, Ba, and z~2 C, which are tied to AGB enrichment.
  - A top-heavy IMF shifts core-collapse yields by about 0.05-0.2 dex in the direction that improves the agreement.
- Position:
  - This is relevant for high-z quiescent galaxy interpretation. It gives a practical abundance-calibration framework for comparing JWST stellar-population measurements with lower-redshift baselines.

### 2605.03905 F. Avila-Vera et al. [Microlensing time-scales and flux magnification probabilities of a sample of 204 lensed quasars]

- Question: What microlensing timescales and high-magnification probabilities should be expected for known lensed quasars?
- Data:
  - 204 known lensed quasar systems with sufficient data for lens modeling.
  - Automatic lens models and microlensing magnification maps.
  - Quasar source-size assumptions scaled to recent measurements.
- Method:
  - Computes convergence and shear for lensed images.
  - Builds microlensing maps and derives source-crossing and Einstein-radius-crossing timescales.
  - Compares predicted magnification histograms with observed microlensing distributions.
- Conclusion:
  - The mean source-crossing time is `2.59 +/- 0.07 yr`.
  - The mean Einstein-radius-crossing time is `11.29 +/- 0.05 yr`.
  - For microlens mass fraction `alpha=0.2`, the model histogram matches the observed mean microlensing magnification.
  - The average quasar half-light radius is `5.4 +/- 2.7` light-days.
  - A typical lensed quasar image has about a 9% probability of a high-magnification event with `Delta m <= -0.32`.
- Position:
  - This is not central for high-z galaxy selection, but it is useful for quasar-lensing systematics, variability interpretation, and magnification uncertainty.

### 2605.03024 E. Tanchon et al. [Uncovering the multi-scale structure of dust distribution in nearby galaxies]

- Question: How does mid-infrared dust emission in nearby galaxies separate into compact and diffuse structures across spatial scales?
- Data:
  - High-resolution JWST/MIRI imaging of nearby star-forming galaxies.
  - Mid-infrared bands from 7.7 to 21 um.
- Method:
  - Applies constrained diffusion decomposition to split compact and diffuse emission components over scale.
  - Compares PAH and dust-emission structure across wavelength and environment.
- Conclusion:
  - PAH emission shows a transition scale around 300 pc.
  - PAH fraction is weaker on smaller scales, consistent with PAH destruction in HII regions.
  - Dust-emission probability distributions show a transition around 200 pc from compact power-law structures to diffuse log-normal ISM.
- Position:
  - This is a lower-priority nearby-galaxy paper, but it provides useful JWST/MIRI physical intuition for spatially resolved dust and PAH interpretation.

### 2605.04028 Amit Mondal et al. [A Fuzzy Set Framework for Red/Blue/Green Valley Galaxies]

- Question: Can fuzzy galaxy classification reduce contamination among red-sequence, blue-cloud, and green-valley populations?
- Data:
  - SDSS DR18 volume-limited sample of 88,579 galaxies.
  - Observables include `u-r`, sSFR, and D4000.
- Method:
  - Builds Gaussian mixture membership functions.
  - Uses a fuzzy minimum operator to assign graded membership rather than hard population boundaries.
  - Tests morphology, AGN fraction, and clustering trends under the fuzzy classification.
- Conclusion:
  - The method reduces contamination in transition populations.
  - It gives clearer green-valley morphology trends.
  - It does not find a large AGN-fraction difference between the fuzzy classes.
  - Fuzzy red galaxies show enhanced large-scale clustering.
- Position:
  - This is low priority for the profile, but it may be useful when defining transition samples or comparing classification schemes.
