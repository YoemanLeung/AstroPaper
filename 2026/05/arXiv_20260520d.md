## Executive View

The read-first paper is [2605.20121](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20121) Maya Steen. It tests whether JWST-detected galaxy proximate damped Lyman-alpha absorbers can trace reionization. TECHNICOLOR DAWN predicts an inside-out-middle topology: dense source regions ionize early, lower-density IGM follows, and the CGM can remain partially neutral to `z=5.5`. The useful caution is that DLA strength mostly follows halo or stellar mass, not simply local neutral fraction.

The strongest high-redshift galaxy paper is [2605.19661](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19661) Stephen Eales. COSMOS-Web plus SCUBA-2 stacking finds that massive galaxies already show a dust-based SFR sequence across morphology at `2<z<4.5`, from roughly `280 M_sun/yr` in irregulars to `80 M_sun/yr` in spheroids. This is a direct statement that quenching was already coupled to the early Hubble sequence.

The main reionization-simulation companion is [2605.18939](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18939) Aaron Smith. Lumina light cones show that patchiness boosts the line-of-sight CMB optical depth to `tau_LOS=0.0550`, about `7%` above the volume-weighted estimate and close to the mass-weighted value. The result matters for CMB optical-depth interpretation and future patchy-reionization forecasts.

For environment and LSS, [2605.18963](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18963) Caitlin Igel is the strongest observational result. Three million HSC galaxies show `>5 sigma` evidence that structure depends on environment at fixed stellar mass, with cluster-scale effects dominating at `z<0.5` and broader large-scale effects appearing for massive galaxies at `z>=0.5`.

The strongest CGM/feedback result is [2605.18987](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18987) Michael Messere. High-specific-energy supernova outflows can make dwarf-galaxy CGM regulation preventive rather than simply ejective: hot gas carries the energy, warm gas carries the mass, and after `~5 Gyr` the CGM baryon fraction falls to `~0.1` while about `75%` of expected baryon accretion is prevented.

## Reionization, High-Redshift Galaxies, and Dust

- [2605.20121](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20121) Maya Steen et al. `Galaxy Proximate Damped Lyman-Alpha Systems and H I Reionization Topology in TECHNICOLOR DAWN`
  - **Question:** can galaxy proximate damped Lyman-alpha absorbers be used as an observational tracer of H I reionization progress?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: TECHNICOLOR DAWN cosmological hydrodynamic simulation.
    - Redshifts: snapshots at `z=10`, `z=8`, `z=6`, and `z=5.5`.
    - Observational motivation: JWST detections of PDLAs in foreground gas near `z>5` galaxies.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare neutral fraction as a function of overdensity to classify the reionization topology.
    - Measure foreground H I column density around simulated halos as a proxy for DLA strength.
    - Test correlations among H I column density, halo mass, stellar mass, gas fraction, local neutral fraction, and volume-averaged neutral fraction.
    - Estimate observational feasibility for current JWST samples and larger future PDLA samples.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The simulation predicts an inside-out-middle topology: dense source regions ionize early, lower-density regions ionize next, and intermediate or self-shielded gas remains neutral later.
    - The CGM remains significantly neutral at `z=5.5`, after the IGM has become more ionized.
    - High H I column density is not mainly a sign of high local gas fraction or high local neutral fraction.
    - H I column density is tied most strongly to halo or stellar mass, with an additional dependence on volume-averaged neutral fraction.
    - The fitted relation gives `b=0.407+-0.001` for halo mass and `b=0.242+-0.001` for stellar mass in the reported log-column model.
    - Galaxy PDLAs can trace reionization only when halo or stellar mass is estimated precisely enough.
  - **Position:** high-priority reionization paper because it turns JWST PDLA detections into a testable CGM/IGM topology diagnostic rather than treating them as direct neutral-fraction measurements.

- [2605.19661](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19661) Stephen Eales et al. `COSMOS-Web: Star formation along the early Hubble sequence and the evolution of dust over the redshift range 0<z<12`
  - **Question:** when did quenching and dust buildup become tied to the early Hubble sequence?
  - **Data:**
    - Galaxy catalog: COSMOS-Web DR1 with photometric redshifts, stellar masses, SFRs, and ML-MORPHO classifications.
    - Submillimetre map: S2COSMOS SCUBA-2 `850 um` image with `640 hr` of data and median noise `1.2 mJy/beam` over `1.6 deg^2`.
    - Samples: galaxies split into `14` redshift intervals over `0<z<12`, five stellar-mass bins, and four morphology classes.
  - **Method:**
    - Stack SCUBA-2 fluxes at COSMOS-Web galaxy positions.
    - Convert mean `850 um` fluxes into dust masses and bolometric dust luminosities with multiple dust-temperature models.
    - Infer dust-obscured SFRs and compare them with COSMOS-Web UV-to-near-IR SED estimates.
    - Combine stacking results with COSMOS-Web stellar-mass functions to model dust-density evolution.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Mean dust-based SFR rises with redshift for all morphology classes.
    - Massive galaxies with `M_*~10^11 M_sun` reach SFRs `>~80 M_sun/yr` at `2<z<4.5`.
    - At `2<z<4.5`, mean SFR declines along the Hubble sequence from `~280 M_sun/yr` in irregulars to `~80 M_sun/yr` in spheroids.
    - High-redshift spheroid SFRs can be reproduced with a starvation model with depletion time `~10^8.2 yr`.
    - Dust-to-stellar mass ratio increases with redshift to `z~8`, and the cosmic dust-density relation is tracked over `0<z<12`.
  - **Position:** important COSMOS-Web result because it links JWST morphology, submillimetre dust emission, and early quenching soon after the Hubble sequence appears.

- [2605.18939](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18939) Aaron Smith et al. `The Lumina Project: CMB Optical Depth Fluctuations from Patchy Reionization`
  - **Question:** how does patchy reionization bias CMB optical-depth predictions relative to global ionization histories?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: Lumina radiation-hydrodynamical run with `2 x 6000^3` resolution elements in a `500 cMpc` box.
    - Outputs: high-cadence Cartesian fields and on-the-fly light cones tracking H II, He II, and He III down to `z=3`.
    - Observable: line-of-sight Thomson optical-depth maps and global mass- and volume-weighted ionization histories.
  - **Method:**
    - Compute `tau_CMB` from volume-weighted global histories, mass-weighted global histories, and explicit light-cone integrations.
    - Measure sightline-to-sightline scatter and non-Gaussianity in `tau_LOS`.
    - Coarse-grain subvolumes to test the scale where density-ionization correlations are erased.
    - Decompose angular statistics into hydrogen and helium auto- and cross-contributions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The light-cone mean is `tau_LOS=0.0550`, compared with `tau_CMB,V=0.0515` and `tau_CMB,m=0.0544`.
    - Patchiness therefore boosts the light-cone value by about `7%` relative to the volume-weighted proxy but only about `1%` relative to the mass-weighted prediction.
    - Sightlines show non-Gaussian dispersion of about `5.0%` after variance-minimizing retiling, or `6.5%` without it.
    - Coarse-graining on scales larger than `~3 cMpc` suppresses the density-ionization correlation and biases `tau_CMB` low.
    - H II and He II optical-depth structures are strongly coherent, while He III shows scale-dependent differences from AGN-driven helium reionization.
  - **Position:** strong companion to the earlier Lumina infrastructure paper because it turns the simulation into a CMB-facing observable for patchy H I and He II reionization.

## CGM, ICM, and Feedback

- [2605.18987](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18987) Michael Messere et al. `How High-Specific-Energy Winds Regulate the Circumgalactic Medium of Dwarf Galaxies`
  - **Question:** can supernova-driven high-specific-energy winds regulate the dwarf-galaxy CGM through preventive feedback?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: cosmological zoom-ins of ten dwarf halos with the ENZO adaptive-mesh code.
    - Halo masses: `M_h~10^10-10^11 M_sun`.
    - Time sampling: `332` outputs per zoom-in, spaced by `~42 Myr`, down to `z=0`.
    - Feedback model: discrete supernovae with high-resolution treatment of hot outflows.
  - **Method:**
    - Track mass and energy flow between ISM, CGM, and IGM.
    - Separate hot outflows from warm outflows by temperature.
    - Measure CGM cooling, baryon fraction, `t_cool/t_ff`, and gas inflow/outflow rates.
    - Post-process ion columns for H I, low ions, and O VI with Trident.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Supernova shock heating sustains the inner CGM near `T_vir` by `z~0`.
    - Hot outflows with `T>3 x 10^5 K` have about `10x` larger specific energy than warm outflows and dominate the energy budget.
    - Warm outflows dominate the mass budget by about `5-10x` and are more readily recycled.
    - Around `~5 Gyr`, the system transitions toward preventive feedback as `t_cool/t_ff` rises toward `~10` in the outer CGM and nearby IGM.
    - After `5-8 Gyr`, the CGM baryon fraction falls to `~0.1`, preventing about `75%` of the expected baryon accretion rate.
    - H I and low-ion profiles broadly agree with observations, but O VI remains underpredicted.
  - **Position:** high-value CGM paper because it links dwarf feedback mode, baryon retention, and ion-column failures in one physically resolved wind model.

- [2605.18989](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18989) J. Martin et al. `High-resolution X-ray spectroscopy with XRISM/Resolve reveals super-Solar abundance ratios in Virgo/M87`
  - **Question:** does Virgo/M87 have an ICM abundance pattern different from other cool-core clusters, and what does that imply about enrichment and AGN transport?
  - **Data:**
    - X-ray spectroscopy: XRISM/Resolve observations of four Virgo-core regions: center, east, northwest, and southwest.
    - Energy band: `1.7-11 keV`.
    - Supporting data: Chandra imaging for spatial structure and comparison with XMM-Newton/EPIC measurements.
    - Elements measured: Si, S, Ar, Ca, Cr, Fe, and Ni.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit single-temperature, multi-temperature, and multi-abundance plasma models.
    - Compare X/Fe ratios across the four pointings.
    - Test robustness against temperature structure, bandpass limits, and atomic-database choices.
    - Interpret abundance patterns in terms of AGN uplift, stellar enrichment, and mixing.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The central, eastern, and southwestern pointings show systematically super-Solar X/Fe ratios for Si, S, Ar, Ca, Cr, and Ni.
    - The northwest pointing, less affected by AGN activity, lies closer to Solar ratios and traces the more relaxed cluster-wide ICM.
    - Multi-temperature and multi-abundance fits indicate that cool, metal-rich gas uplifted by AGN coexists with hotter ambient gas.
    - The super-Solar pattern is not removed by the restricted Resolve bandpass or temperature modeling.
    - M87 may reflect enrichment by an old stellar population plus limited cold gas, but the current microcalorimeter cluster sample is still small.
  - **Position:** useful ICM/AGN-feedback anchor because it uses XRISM to connect metal transport, AGN uplift, and cluster-core enrichment on spatially resolved scales.

## Environment, LSS, and Survey Inference

- [2605.18963](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18963) Caitlin Igel et al. `Environmental Sculpting of Galaxy Structure at Fixed Stellar Mass: A Multi-Scale Analysis Across Cosmic Time using 3 Million HSC Galaxies`
  - **Question:** does environment affect galaxy structure beyond the dominant dependence on stellar mass?
  - **Data:**
    - Imaging sample: `~3 million` HSC-SSP galaxies with `m<23`.
    - Redshift range: `0.3<=z<0.7`.
    - Stellar-mass limit: `log(M/M_sun)>=8.9` at the lowest redshifts, with mass completeness limits rising by redshift bin.
    - Structure catalog: GaMPEN bulge-to-total light ratio posteriors anchored to rest-frame `~450 nm`.
    - Environment tracers: `10 cMpc` large-scale overdensity maps and CAMIRA cluster associations within `~2 cMpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Define a mass-independent structural statistic by subtracting the median bulge-to-total ratio at fixed stellar mass and redshift.
    - Propagate structural-parameter posteriors through a Bayesian correlation analysis.
    - Test large-scale environment and cluster-scale membership separately.
    - Split results by redshift, stellar mass, and star-forming/quiescent state.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Galaxy structure depends on environment at fixed stellar mass with more than `5 sigma` confidence when all tests are combined.
    - The environmental dependence is real but secondary to stellar mass.
    - At `z<0.5`, large-scale overdensity has no significant monotonic correlation, but cluster members are more bulge dominated than field galaxies at fixed mass.
    - At `z>=0.5`, massive galaxies with `log(M/M_sun)>=10.25` show bulge enhancement across both cluster and large-scale environments.
    - Star-forming and quiescent subsamples show flatter internal trends, implying coupled morphology and star-formation transformations.
  - **Position:** strong LSS-environment paper because it controls stellar mass carefully and separates cluster-specific effects from broader cosmic-web-scale effects.

- [2605.19323](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19323) Zilan Xiao et al. `Correlation between baryonic process and galaxy assembly bias`
  - **Question:** which baryonic processes drive galaxy assembly bias in semi-analytic galaxy mocks?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation backbone: UNIT dark-matter simulation.
    - Galaxy model: Galacticus semi-analytic model.
    - Mock suite: hundreds of galaxy mocks spanning parameters for gas cooling, star formation, stellar feedback, outflow reincorporation, and AGN feedback.
  - **Method:**
    - Quantify assembly bias with shuffling methods at fixed halo mass.
    - Separate effects from halo concentration and local environment at `5` and `10 h^-1 Mpc` scales.
    - Use Random Forest regression to identify which baryonic parameters control the assembly-bias signal.
    - Compare stellar-mass-selected and SFR-selected galaxy samples.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Environment-induced GAB is often close to the total GAB, but baryonic physics can change which secondary halo property dominates.
    - For stellar-mass-selected galaxies, gas cooling and disk stellar feedback dominate the GAB signal.
    - For SFR-selected galaxies, the dominant process shifts from star formation to gas cooling as number density increases.
    - The result gives direct guidance for empirical GAB parameterizations in upcoming galaxy-survey analyses.
  - **Position:** useful LSS-theory paper because it connects assembly-bias phenomenology to specific baryonic parameters rather than treating GAB as an abstract nuisance term.

- [2605.20027](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20027) S. Voskresenskaia et al. `ComPACT: Mass-Redshift Properties of the galaxy cluster catalogue`
  - **Question:** what are the redshift, mass, and completeness properties of the CNN-selected ComPACT SZ cluster catalog?
  - **Data:**
    - Cluster catalog: `2962` SZ-selected candidates from ComPACT.
    - Microwave maps: combined ACT+Planck and Planck Compton-y maps.
    - Optical/IR data: DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys and WISE photometry.
    - Simulations: cluster injections into real microwave maps for completeness.
  - **Method:**
    - Cross-match candidates with published SZ, X-ray, and optical cluster catalogs.
    - Estimate missing redshifts with literature values, the Zaznobin method, and zCluster.
    - Derive masses from ACT+Planck and Planck y maps using SZ scaling relations.
    - Evaluate catalog completeness through injected-cluster recovery.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - About `~60%` of ComPACT candidates are confirmed as galaxy clusters.
    - Confirmed redshifts span `0.007<z<1.7`, including about `116` new measurements.
    - Masses are obtained for `56%` of the sample, spanning `(0.25-13.1) x 10^14 M_sun` and including `158` new mass determinations.
    - Five previously unreported clusters have `M_500c>6 x 10^14 M_sun` at `z>0.7`.
    - Those five systems increase the known population of such massive high-redshift clusters by about `10%`.
  - **Position:** relevant LSS infrastructure paper because it demonstrates how deep-learning SZ selection can expand the high-mass, high-redshift cluster census.

- [2605.18959](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18959) Aritra Ghosh et al. `Hyrax: An Extensible Framework for Rapid ML Experimentation and Unsupervised Discovery in the Era of Rubin, Roman, and Euclid`
  - **Question:** what ML infrastructure is needed to make next-generation survey discovery systematic rather than ad hoc?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey context: Rubin-LSST, Roman, Euclid, HSC, DES, ZTF, and DECam data products.
    - Demonstration data: `~4 x 10^5` Rubin LSST DP1 galaxies in one unsupervised representation-learning example.
    - Science cases: mergers and low-surface-brightness galaxies, cluster-scale lensed arcs, transients, distant Solar System objects, and semi-resolved dwarfs.
  - **Method:**
    - Provide modular Python workflows for data acquisition, training, inference, experiment comparison, and visualization.
    - Integrate vector databases for similarity search in learned latent spaces.
    - Support two- and three-dimensional interactive latent-space exploration.
    - Demonstrate unsupervised and supervised workflows across five astronomical use cases.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Unsupervised DP1 galaxy embeddings recover merger and low-surface-brightness candidates missed by reference catalogs.
    - Hybrid density-based clustering identifies cluster-scale gravitational-lens candidates in DP1 data.
    - The framework supports multimodal transient classification, shift-and-stack filtering, and semi-resolved dwarf detection.
    - Astronomy-specific ML infrastructure becomes the bottleneck-reducer for survey-scale discovery, not just model architecture.
  - **Position:** useful survey-methods paper because it is directly aimed at Rubin/Roman/Euclid-scale galaxy and lens discovery workflows.

## AGN Accretion State

- [2605.18958](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18958) Riccardo Middei et al. `Directly tracking the re-brightening of a supermassive black hole accretion disk`
  - **Question:** can an AGN accretion disk be observed as it recovers from a weak, disk-suppressed state into a radiatively efficient state?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: nearby type-1 Seyfert ESO 511-G030 at `z=0.0223`.
    - Monitoring: six years of simultaneous Swift UVOT and XRT observations.
    - Sample size: `82` Swift epochs with optical/UV and X-ray coverage.
    - Context: archival XMM-Newton and Swift states showing prior high and low X-ray phases.
  - **Method:**
    - Track UV and X-ray light curves and time-resolved spectral energy distributions.
    - Test whether the brightening is caused by obscuration changes or true disk recovery.
    - Fit the time-resolved relation between UV disk luminosity and X-ray coronal luminosity.
    - Compare the transition with disk-corona behavior in luminous quasars and stellar-mass black holes.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - UV flux from the disk rises by more than an order of magnitude over less than `3 yr`.
    - X-ray and UV fluxes both increase by about a factor of `~10` during the monitoring campaign.
    - The behavior is inconsistent with a simple uncovering event because the X-ray spectra remain unobscured and the UV trend does not match reddening removal.
    - Above an Eddington ratio near `~0.01`, UV and X-ray emission follow the quasar-like disk-corona relation.
    - Below that threshold, the relation flattens, consistent with inner-disk evaporation into a hot flow.
  - **Position:** useful AGN-physics paper because it catches a SMBH accretion-state transition in real time and connects local Seyfert monitoring to quasar disk-corona scaling.
