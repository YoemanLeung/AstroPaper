## Reionization, IGM Transmission, and High-z Quasar Gas

- [2605.02539](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02539) Weida Hu et al. `Using Lyman-alpha Transmitted Spectrum to Probe IGM Transmission and Identify Ionized Structures in Cosmic Reionization`
  - **Question:** can high-S/N galaxy spectra measure individual-sightline IGM transmission and identify ionized structures during late reionization?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRSpec prism: `143` galaxies at `5<z<7` from CAPERS and JADES.
    - GOODS-S: the high-S/N `z=6.335` galaxy GS-18846 and other galaxies in the `5.75<z<6` overdensity.
    - Lyman-alpha context: known GOODS-S LAEs including GS-9422 and galaxy overdensity measurements from the same redshift slice.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit galaxy SEDs to predict the intrinsic continuum blueward of Lyman-alpha.
    - Compare predicted and observed flux between Lyman-beta and Lyman-alpha to estimate `T_IGM`.
    - Convert transmission to effective optical depth and compare with quasar-based measurements.
    - Map the high-transmission interval against the spatial and redshift distribution of GOODS-S galaxies.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - GS-18846 gives `T_IGM=0.17+/-0.02` at `5.75<z<6`, about an order of magnitude above prior average transmission.
    - The structure GS-z6IS spans `~110 cMpc` along the line of sight and at least `21 x 17 cMpc^2` across the spectroscopic footprint.
    - The same interval contains `64` galaxies, compared with `10-27` galaxies in adjacent bins.
    - GS-9422 has total Lyman-alpha transmission of `83+/-2%`, supporting a large ionized region rather than only source-intrinsic Lyman-alpha differences.
  - **Position:** the week's strongest reionization-IGM paper because it moves Lyalpha visibility toward direct IGM-transmission mapping with galaxy spectra.

- [2605.05673](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05673) Raymond P. Remigio et al. `Detection of an Extended Lyalpha Halo around a z=6.64 Broad Absorption Line Quasar with the Keck Cosmic Web Imager`
  - **Question:** can KCWI red-channel observations detect extended Lyalpha halos around quasars beyond the usual MUSE redshift limit?
  - **Data:**
    - Keck/KCWI: red-channel integral-field observations centered near observed `9500 Angstrom`.
    - Target: the `z=6.64` BAL quasar J0910-0414.
    - ALMA: prior [C II] redshift and compact host-galaxy kinematics.
  - **Method:**
    - Subtract the quasar PSF from the KCWI cube.
    - Build smoothed signal-to-noise masks for extended Lyalpha.
    - Measure halo flux, size, velocity centroid, velocity dispersion, and radial surface-brightness profile.
    - Compare Lyalpha halo properties with compact ALMA [C II] and FIR continuum sizes.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Extended Lyalpha is detected to a radius of `~11 pkpc`.
    - The halo luminosity is `L_Lyalpha=(2.3+/-0.1) x 10^43 erg/s`.
    - The halo contributes `55%` of the total detected Lyalpha flux inside the mask.
    - The Lyalpha halo is roughly `8` times larger than the [C II] region and `18` times larger than the FIR continuum region.
    - The Lyalpha velocity field shows ordered, rotation-like structure but with a different orientation and lower dispersion than the compact [C II] host core.
  - **Position:** a direct high-z quasar-CGM result and the best Friday addition to the weekly reionization set.

- [2605.00763](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00763) Romain A. Meyer et al. `Life After the Quasar: Overmassive Black Holes and Remnant Ionised Bubbles in and Around Two z~6.6 Galaxies`
  - **Question:** can rare double-peaked Lyman-alpha emitters at `z~6.6` be recently faded quasars with remnant ionized bubbles?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRSpec IFU: rest-frame `2000-7000 Angstrom` spectroscopy of COLA1 and NEPLA4.
    - Line diagnostics: Hbeta+[O III] and Halpha+[N II] complexes.
    - Ancillary photometry: Subaru-HSC, HEROES, and JWST constraints for stellar-population fits.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit narrow, outflow, and broad Balmer components.
    - Estimate black-hole masses from broad Halpha and Hbeta virial relations.
    - Model ionized-bubble requirements and residual neutral fractions for blue-side Lyman-alpha transmission.
    - Compare quasar and double-peaked-LAE number densities to infer duty-cycle limits.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - COLA1 and NEPLA4 have broad Balmer components with FWHM `3947+/-263 km/s` and `4114+/-305 km/s`.
    - The inferred black-hole masses are `log10(M_BH/Msun)=8.28+/-0.05` and `8.24+/-0.06`.
    - The black-hole-to-stellar mass ratios are `0.22` and `0.10`, far above the local relation.
    - Current Eddington ratios are low, `lambda_Edd~0.05` and `0.04`.
    - The galaxy ionizing output alone leaves residual neutral fractions `14-36x` too high for the observed blue Lyman-alpha peaks.
    - A recent quasar phase ending within `<1 Myr` can supply the required remnant ionization.
  - **Position:** a key bridge between JWST broad-line AGN, overmassive black holes, and Lyman-alpha transmission during reionization.

- [2605.00978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00978) Gene C. K. Leung et al. `Discovery of Quasar Variability and Early Accretion Disk Signatures at Cosmic Dawn`
  - **Question:** can quasar variability at `z>6` constrain early SMBH accretion-disk structure?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: the strongly lensed quasar J043947.08+163415.7 at `z=6.51`.
    - WISE/NEOWISE: W1 and W2 light curves from unWISE.
    - Support data: 2MASS, UHS, HST/WFC3, JWST/NIRSpec IFU, XMM-Newton, and eROSITA.
    - Parent search: `531` known quasars at `z>5.3`.
  - **Method:**
    - Calibrate WISE light curves with nearby Gaia stars.
    - Construct multiwavelength light curves from rest-UV/optical to X-rays.
    - Build a variable SED from bright-minus-faint states.
    - Compare the variable SED slope and inter-band lag limits with thin-disk predictions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - J0439+1634 varies significantly in WISE W1 at `10.9 sigma`.
    - The variable SED has slope `-1.58+/-0.25`, consistent with a geometrically thin, optically thick disk.
    - The quasar has Eddington ratio `(60+/-10)%`.
    - WISE W2-W1 reverberation yields a `90%` upper limit of `<160 days` observed-frame.
  - **Position:** the main weekly cosmic-dawn AGN variability paper because it makes early accretion-disk reverberation observationally plausible.

## JWST Galaxy Demographics, Metallicity, and Quenching

- [2605.05327](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05327) A. Gimenez-Alcazar et al. `Shape of Direct-Method Mass-Metallicity Relation with JWST: Fast-Track Nitrogen and Helium Enrichment`
  - **Question:** how do auroral-line selection, star-formation history, and secondary abundances shape the direct-method MZR at high redshift?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRSpec: medium-resolution spectra from the public DAWN JWST Archive.
    - Sample: `286` star-forming galaxies with [O III] 4363 detections over `z=0.8-9.1`.
    - Stacks: galaxies with and without individual auroral-line detections.
  - **Method:**
    - Select star-forming galaxies using auroral-line diagnostic diagrams.
    - Derive direct-method oxygen abundances, N/O, and He/H.
    - Fit stellar masses with SED modeling and derive SFRs from Balmer lines.
    - Stack spectra by stellar mass to test selection bias.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The sample spans `log(M_*/M_sun)=6.77-10.5` and `12+log(O/H)=6.9-8.4`.
    - The fitted MZR slope is `gamma=0.38+/-0.09`.
    - Stacks without individual auroral-line detections are `~0.2-0.3 dex` more metal-rich at fixed stellar mass.
    - Auroral-line detections preferentially select high-EW, high-sSFR galaxies in the low-metallicity envelope.
    - Several stacked bins show enhanced N/O and He/H.
  - **Position:** the week's best JWST metallicity-systematics paper because it defines the selection function of direct-method high-z abundance samples.

- [2605.03008](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03008) Aleyna Doven et al. `Environmental Quenching of High-Redshift Galaxies: Interpreting JWST Observations with Simulations`
  - **Question:** can environmental quenching explain low-mass quenched galaxies at `z>3` discovered by JWST?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: L-GALAXIES, IllustrisTNG/TNG300, SIMBA, and TNG-Cluster.
    - Population scale: more than half a million simulated galaxies at `z=5`.
    - Observational context: JWST low-mass quenched systems with `M_*<10^10 Msun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Classify simulated galaxies by quenched state and central/satellite status.
    - Compare stellar mass, host halo mass, halocentric distance, gas fractions, and ram pressure.
    - Track future fates of simulated analogues.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Quenched systems at `z=5` are overwhelmingly satellites even though satellites are less than `10%` of simulated galaxies.
    - Satellite quenching is stronger in massive host halos and at smaller halocentric distance.
    - Simulated analogues broadly match the stellar masses and environments of JWST low-mass quenched candidates.
    - About `90%` merge or disrupt by `z=0`, while rejuvenation is rare at `<10%`.
  - **Position:** the week's best high-z quenching paper because it connects early quiescence to overdense satellite processing rather than only internal mass quenching.

- [2605.03635](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03635) Zihan Wang et al. `The JWST early galaxy crisis resolved by a reionization degeneracy`
  - **Question:** is the apparent overabundance of bright `z>10` JWST galaxies a real crisis, or can it be resolved by the degeneracy between star-formation efficiency and escape fraction?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST UV luminosity functions at `z=9`, `10`, `11`, and `12.5`.
    - Global reionization observables constraining integrated ionizing emissivity.
    - Scatter models for UV luminosity, including duty-cycle bursts.
  - **Method:**
    - Use the degeneracy ridge between `f_esc` and `f_*,0` from reionization constraints.
    - Add JWST UV luminosity-function shapes to constrain star-formation efficiency independently.
    - Reconstruct `f_esc(z)` over `z=7-12`.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Reionization data constrain the product `f_esc x f_*,0`, not the factors independently.
    - The high-efficiency crisis threshold is excluded at `4.5 sigma` for duty-cycle burst models and above `3 sigma` for all tested scatter prescriptions.
    - A constant-efficiency scenario gives `f_esc~10-16%` at `z=7-12`.
    - Cycle 3-4 luminosity functions should distinguish the main pathways at better than `2 sigma`.
  - **Position:** a high-priority theory guardrail for interpreting JWST bright galaxies and reionization emissivity.

- [2605.02493](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02493) Vaidik Prasal et al. `Morphological and Star Formation Properties of Cosmic Noon Massive Quiescent Galaxies`
  - **Question:** what morphologies and radial star-formation histories characterize massive quiescent galaxies at `2<z<3`?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRCam: UNCOVER Treasury and MegaScience imaging of Abell 2744.
    - HST: archival ACS and WFC3 photometry.
    - Sample: `14` massive quiescent galaxies with `M_*>10^10 Msun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit stellar populations with BAGPIPES.
    - Measure non-parametric and parametric morphologies.
    - Perform resolved SED modeling to recover radial sSFR and quenching profiles.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Most systems are intermediate type or S0-like with median Sersic index `n~4`.
    - `11/14`, or `~79%`, show positive radial sSFR gradients.
    - Mean sSFR rises by about `2 dex` from `R/R_e=0.5` to `R/R_e=4.5`.
    - Inner regions formed `~0.5 Gyr` earlier than outer regions and quenched more rapidly.
  - **Position:** a strong JWST cosmic-noon bridge to the high-z quenching theme because it combines morphology and resolved star-formation histories.

## Cosmic-Web Nodes, LRDs, and Overmassive Black Holes

- [2605.04144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04144) G. Quadri et al. `The galaxy-halo connection and the dynamical evolution of a giant disc in a massive node of the Cosmic Web at z~3`
  - **Question:** what halo mass, baryonic content, and formation pathway can explain the Big Wheel giant disk in a `z~3` cosmic-web node?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST photometry: priors on stellar structure and baryonic components.
    - ALMA: CO(4-3) kinematics and dust continuum for the Big Wheel at `z~3.25`.
    - Environment: the MQN01 cosmic-web node.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit a dynamical model to the ALMA CO rotation curve.
    - Use JWST SED and CO priors to constrain stellar, gas, bulge, and halo components.
    - Compare stellar-to-halo mass ratio with empirical relations.
    - Evolve an idealized galaxy model for `2.5 Gyr`.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The inferred halo mass is `log(M_h/M_sun)=12.11+0.29/-0.17`.
    - The inferred stellar mass is `log(M_*/M_sun)=11.00+0.11/-0.12`.
    - The stellar-to-halo mass ratio is `0.06+0.04/-0.03`, roughly `3` times higher than standard relations.
    - The disk remains stable in the `2.5 Gyr` evolution test.
    - The halo spin estimate is high, with `lambda_BW=0.085` or `0.139`.
  - **Position:** the week's main high-z LSS galaxy paper because it ties disk survival, gas accretion, and galaxy-halo connection to an overdense node.

- [2605.05074](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05074) Piero Madau et al. `Little red dots as obscured little blue dots: relative abundances, luminosities, and black-hole masses`
  - **Question:** can LRDs be explained as dust-reddened, high-inclination views of compact broad-line AGN?
  - **Data:**
    - Observed UV luminosity function of compact broad-line AGN at `z>4`.
    - Photometric LRD luminosity functions from JWST samples.
    - Rest-frame UV and optical magnitude bins.
  - **Method:**
    - Draw mock compact broad-line AGN from the parent UV luminosity function.
    - Forward-model accretion rate, anisotropic emission, viewing angle, and dusty cloud interception.
    - Apply photometric LRD color selection.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - A super-Eddington geometry with modest dusty covering can reproduce the observed LRD luminosity function.
    - The UV LRD/BLAGN fraction rises from `3%` at `M_1500=-21` to about `20%` near `M_1500=-19`.
    - Optical fractions reach `26%` at `M_4500=-20` and `35%` at `M_6500=-21`.
    - The best-fitting cloud extinction is `<A_V>=2.8+0.0/-0.4 mag`.
    - The mean dust covering factor is `<C_dust>=0.23+0.27/-0.00`.
  - **Position:** a key LRD demographics paper because it converts the interpretation problem into an orientation and luminosity-function test.

- [2605.04685](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04685) Lulu Bao et al. `Compact, AGN-hosting Dwarf Galaxies with "Little Red Dots"-like SEDs in the Local Universe`
  - **Question:** are local AGN-hosting dwarf galaxies with V-shaped SEDs physical analogues of high-z LRDs?
  - **Data:**
    - Local AGN-hosting dwarf galaxies compiled from `45` studies and DESI sources.
    - JWST-era comparison: `326` V-shaped LRDs.
    - Morphological fits for `533` local ADGs.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure continuum slopes and effective radii.
    - Use K-means clustering over SED shape, morphology, and color.
    - Compare star formation, metallicity, ionization, broad-line fraction, and black-hole placement.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - About half of local ADGs show V-shaped SEDs and compact morphologies.
    - Their median effective radius is `~2100+/-1200 pc`, much larger than the `~100 pc` scale typical of LRDs.
    - The closest local group still has `R_e=1.6+0.6/-0.7 kpc`.
    - Local V-shaped compact ADGs are more evolved and have different ionization conditions than high-z LRDs.
  - **Position:** an important caution for analogue work: local compact AGN dwarfs are comparison objects, not one-to-one LRD analogues.

- [2605.04776](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04776) Ziyong Wu et al. `Redshift Evolution of the Ratio of Supermassive Black Hole Mass to Stellar Mass`
  - **Question:** can early rapid SMBH growth and later declining duty cycle explain elevated `M_BH/M_*` ratios at high redshift?
  - **Data:**
    - High-redshift zoom-in simulations down to `z=10`.
    - Eight simulations with varied feedback and SMBH accretion prescriptions.
    - Observational calibration of an effective duty cycle at `z<=6`.
  - **Method:**
    - Allow super-Eddington accretion before self-regulated SMBH growth.
    - Model later SMBH and host growth with halo assembly histories.
    - Adopt `f_duty=0.0004(1+z)^3`.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - High-z `M_BH/M_*` requires super-Eddington accretion before the final self-regulated phase.
    - The ratio peaks broadly at `z~7-10`, reaching a few percent up to `~30%`.
    - The predicted sequence is `M_BH/M_*=(0.002, 0.003, 0.006, 0.016, 0.071, 0.156)` at `z=(0,1,2,3,5,10)`.
  - **Position:** directly relevant to JWST overmassive-BH and LRD interpretation.

## Feedback, Outflow Tracers, and AGN Diagnostics

- [2605.05298](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05298) Jack M. M. Neustadt et al. `Kinematic Stratification in Extremely Red Quasars Revealed by JWST`
  - **Question:** what gas phases and viewing geometry explain the complex rest-optical spectra of extremely red quasars?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRSpec IFU: six ERQs from the Q3D ERS program.
    - ALMA: observed-frame `240 GHz` data for most of the sample.
    - Archival support: SDSS, X-Shooter, WISE, VLA, and FIRST.
  - **Method:**
    - Decompose Balmer, [O III], [N II], [S II], O I, and Fe II profiles into velocity templates.
    - Use Balmer decrements and line ratios to infer obscuration, density, and ionization.
    - Fit UV-to-MIR SEDs with a torus plus scattered-light model.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - ERQ spectra require BLR-like clouds, low-density NLR-like gas, and high-velocity low-density outflows.
    - J0834 shows [O III]-dominated gas that is more blueshifted and lower density than the bulk Balmer-emitting gas.
    - The UV continuum and UV lines are dominated by scattered light.
    - Red optical and NIR lines include both scattered and obscured components.
  - **Position:** one of the week's best obscured-AGN interpretation papers because it separates scattered, obscured, and outflowing components in JWST spectra.

- [2605.05650](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05650) Antonia Fernandez-Figueroa et al. `Differences between emission and absorption tracers of spatially resolved outflows in clumpy z~0.1 star-forming galaxies`
  - **Question:** do emission and absorption diagnostics measure the same outflow phase in spatially resolved star-forming galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Keck/LRIS: three clumpy DYNAMO galaxies at `z~0.1`.
    - HST: narrowband imaging for Halpha flux calibration.
    - Tracers: Halpha and Mg II emission, Mg II absorption, and Na I D absorption.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit narrow and broad components in emission and absorption.
    - Define outflow velocity as `v_out=v-2 sigma`.
    - Compare resolved outflow velocities with SFR and `Sigma_SFR`.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Mg II absorption gives much higher velocities than Halpha emission, with mean abstract values `-560+/-30 km/s` versus `-124+/-3 km/s`.
    - The resolved sample yields `v_out=-291 km/s` in Halpha and `v_out=-651 km/s` in absorption.
    - Mg II absorption is systematically offset from emission tracers by `~0.4 dex`.
    - The difference is best explained by tracer-density and measurement-systematics differences, not simply ionization state.
  - **Position:** essential tracer-systematics context for interpreting high-z outflows from mixed emission and absorption diagnostics.

- [2605.03016](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03016) Kylie Yui Dan et al. `Resolving the Multiphase Outflow, Shock Signatures, and PAHs in the AGN-Starburst Composite ULIRG F10565+2448 with JWST MIRI/MRS`
  - **Question:** what drives and regulates the multiphase outflow in the AGN-starburst composite ULIRG F10565+2448?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/MIRI MRS: `4.9-27.9 um` spectroscopy.
    - Target: a starburst-AGN composite ULIRG with SFR around `150 Msun/yr`.
    - Lines: [Ne II], [Ne III], [Ne V], [Fe II], H2, and PAH features.
  - **Method:**
    - Separate disk and outflow components in warm ionized and molecular gas.
    - Use H2 excitation, [Fe II]/Pfund-alpha, and PAH ratio gradients to test shocks.
    - Compare outflow velocities with disk kinematics and escape velocity.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The warm-ionized outflow reaches `~ -520 km/s`.
    - The resolved warm-molecular outflow spans `-280 < v50 < -110 km/s`.
    - Only about `0.01%` of the H2 S(1) flux exceeds escape velocity.
    - Warm ionized and molecular outflow rates are each about `3 Msun/yr`.
    - Both star formation and AGN or jet-related processes are required; pure radiation pressure is insufficient.
  - **Position:** the week's best JWST/MIRI feedback case study.

- [2605.04925](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04925) J. H. Costa-Souza et al. `Near-infrared diagnostic diagrams of the gas ionization sources in nearby galaxies: a JWST NIRSpec view`
  - **Question:** can JWST/NIRSpec near-IR line ratios separate star formation, AGN excitation, and shocks?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRSpec IFU high-resolution spectra of `9` local `z<0.1` U/LIRGs.
    - Lines: [Fe II], Pa beta, Br gamma, H2, [C I], and PAH `3.3 um`.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit stellar continua, emission lines, and PAH features.
    - Classify spaxels with established and new near-IR line-ratio diagrams.
    - Compare line ratios with CLOUDY photoionization models and `W80` kinematics.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - New diagnostics include `[C I] 0.9852 um/Pa gamma` and `H2 1-0 O(5)/PAH 3.3 um`.
    - `[C I]/Pa gamma` identifies shocks but does not cleanly separate AGN and star formation.
    - `H2/PAH 3.3 um` more directly separates star formation, AGN, and shocks.
    - Shock-excited regions have `W80` about `2.5` times larger than AGN-excited regions.
  - **Position:** a practical JWST line-diagnostic paper for obscured AGN, dusty starbursts, and compact high-z analogues.

## LSS, Cluster Environments, and Cosmic-Web Tools

- [2605.04176](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04176) Yanhui Yang et al. `Matter Clustering in Astrid: Reduced Baryonic Suppression from Realistic Black Hole Dynamics`
  - **Question:** does realistic black-hole dynamics weaken AGN-feedback suppression of the matter power spectrum?
  - **Data:**
    - Astrid hydrodynamical simulation and Astrid-DMO counterpart.
    - Controlled small-volume simulations with dynamical-friction and repositioning black-hole dynamics.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure `P_hydro(k)/P_DMO(k)`.
    - Compare black-hole dynamical friction against artificial repositioning.
    - Track kinetic feedback energy and AGN X-ray luminosity functions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Astrid shows no significant suppression at `z=0` and only mild suppression at `z=0.2`.
    - Repositioning boosts kinetic feedback by about `2x` at `z=1.5` in controlled runs.
    - Kinetic feedback is essential for suppression, but stronger feedback creates GSMF and AGN luminosity-function tension.
  - **Position:** important for LSS cosmology because black-hole numerics can change inferred baryonic suppression.

- [2605.04645](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04645) Dakalo G. Phuravhathu et al. `The MeerKAT Massive Distant Clusters Survey: a search for diffuse radio emission in 30 massive SZ-selected clusters at z > 1`
  - **Question:** how common are diffuse radio halos, relics, and mini-halos in massive SZ-selected clusters at `z>1`?
  - **Data:**
    - MMDCS sample: `30` massive ACT SZ-selected clusters at `z>1`.
    - MeerKAT L-band observations centered near `1.28 GHz`.
    - Mock halo injection simulations for non-detection limits.
  - **Method:**
    - Image full-resolution and source-subtracted MeerKAT maps.
    - Classify radio halos and non-detections.
    - Fit and compare the `P_1.4GHz-M_500c` relation.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Diffuse radio halos are detected in `8/30` clusters, a `27%` detection rate.
    - `21` reliable clusters show no diffuse emission.
    - No cluster-scale relics or mini-halos are detected.
    - The detection rate is higher than the commonly predicted `less than 10%` occurrence at `z>1`.
  - **Position:** the week's strongest high-z cluster non-thermal survey result.

- [2605.06587](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06587) Shenming Fu et al. `Constraining Galaxy Cluster Triaxiality via Weak Lensing -- I. Preparation for the Rubin Data Beyond Leading Order`
  - **Question:** can stacked weak-lensing multipoles measure projected cluster-halo ellipticity in preparation for Rubin LSST?
  - **Data:**
    - DES Y3 redMaPPer clusters, metacalibration shapes, and DNF photometric redshifts.
    - Baseline selection: `pcen0>0.9` and `0.2<z<0.65`.
    - Final baseline sample: `9962` clusters.
  - **Method:**
    - Align stacks by satellite-galaxy major axis.
    - Measure monopole and quadrupole weak-lensing profiles.
    - Fit a second-order elliptical NFW expansion.
    - Correct for orientation dilution and member contamination.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The DES Y3 ensemble has projected ellipticity `epsilon=0.310+0.017/-0.016`.
    - The projected axis ratio is `q=0.527+0.018/-0.019`.
    - No strong richness or redshift dependence is detected.
    - Rubin LSST Y10 should reduce the ellipticity error bar by about `82%`.
  - **Position:** a useful LSS-method paper because triaxiality affects cluster mass calibration and cosmic-web environment interpretation.

- [2605.01994](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01994) Kritti Sharma et al. `A Detection of Baryon Underdensity in Cosmic Voids with Fast Radio Bursts`
  - **Question:** can FRB dispersion measures detect baryon underdensities in cosmic voids?
  - **Data:**
    - CHIME/FRB catalog and SDSS BOSS voids.
    - Void stacking and line-of-sight dispersion-measure residuals.
  - **Method:**
    - Cross-correlate FRB dispersion residuals with void locations.
    - Compare with foreground and localization systematics tests.
    - Interpret the stacked signal as a missing-baryon tracer.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The void stack gives a `3.2 sigma` baryon-underdensity signal.
    - FRB sightlines can detect not only overdense cosmic-web gas but also baryon deficits in voids.
    - Larger localized FRB samples should turn this into a sharper cosmic baryon census.
  - **Position:** a useful weekly cosmic-web support result and a complement to cluster/filament baryon studies.
