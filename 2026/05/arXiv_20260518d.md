## Executive View

The read-first paper is [2605.15263](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15263) Albert Sneppen. It addresses the main tension in the AGN interpretation of Little Red Dots: if they host accreting black holes, why are they not detected in deep X-ray data? The answer is restrictive, not generic. The models require both Compton-thick gas columns around `N_H~10^25 cm^-2` and intrinsically weak X-ray emission, with `k_bol,X>=30`, similar to high-accretion-rate narrow-line AGN. A normal broad-line AGN X-ray SED would still be too bright.

The strongest high-redshift galaxy result is [2605.15273](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15273) Leindert A. Boogaard. It reports a stellar bar in GN20, an extreme gas-rich disk at `z=4.055`. The important point is not only that a bar exists early, but that the galaxy is both baryon dominated and gas dominated: roughly `70+-30%` baryon dominance over dark matter and `75+-25%` of the baryonic mass in gas. This challenges the idea that early bars require gas-poor disks.

The strongest environment paper is [2605.15712](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15712) Shuaiyi Li. COSMOS2025 plus JWST/NIRCam WFSS identifies PC J1001+0214 at `z=2.96`, with `131` members and overdensity `delta=2.66`. The protocluster already shows accelerated stellar mass assembly, with a `+0.2 dex` shift in the member stellar-mass distribution, but not yet strong quenching.

Two survey/inference papers are worth keeping close. [2605.15264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15264) Kyle Finner uses HST/CANDELS near-infrared weak lensing to find `12` shear-selected overdensities over `0.23 deg^2`, a useful Roman precursor. [2605.15893](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15893) Flaminia Fortuni forward-models TNG100 and EAGLE through CANDELS-like observations and argues that simulations underproduce faint compact galaxies with bright cores at `z>3`.

For reionization and CGM work, [2605.15310](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15310) Oliver Zier introduces Lumina, a `500 cMpc` radiation-hydrodynamic simulation following H I, He I, and He II reionization to `z=3`. [2605.15275](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15275) Daniel R. Piacitelli shows that simulated dwarf galaxies underproduce O VI reservoirs, likely because oxygen production and metal transport are insufficient in low-mass systems.

## LRDs, Quasars, and AGN Feedback

- [2605.15263](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15263) Albert Sneppen et al. `On the quenching of LRD X-ray emission by both Compton-thick gas and high accretion rates`
  - **Question:** can the dense-gas LRD model explain why high-redshift Little Red Dots remain undetected in deep X-ray observations?
  - **Data:**
    - Models: Sirocco radiative-transfer sequences previously tuned to reproduce LRD optical and near-infrared spectra.
    - Observational constraints: Chandra upper limits for JWST AGN/LRD candidates, typically `L_2-10keV<~(2-4)e41 erg/s`.
    - LRD scale: bolometric luminosities around `10^43-10^45 erg/s` and inferred black-hole masses around `10^6 M_sun` after electron-scattering corrections.
  - **Method:**
    - Map Balmer-break strength to gas column density in the surrounding cocoon.
    - Compute X-ray transmission through the same gas structures used to fit optical/NIR LRD spectra.
    - Compare a normal broad-line AGN X-ray SED with a narrow-line Seyfert 1-like high-accretion SED.
    - Vary metallicity, intrinsic X-ray bolometric correction, observed bandpass, and redshift.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The X-ray non-detections are hard to satisfy with obscuration alone.
    - Consistency requires Compton-thick columns near `N_H~10^25 cm^-2`, moderate metallicity around `0.05-0.1 Z_sun`, and intrinsically weak X-ray emission with `k_bol,X>=30`.
    - A typical broad-line AGN X-ray SED would remain detectable even behind the gas columns inferred from optical spectra.
    - Very low metallicity would make LRDs easier to detect in X-rays, implying that the modeled LRDs are unlikely to be chemically pristine.
  - **Position:** central LRD/AGN paper because it converts X-ray non-detection from a qualitative objection into a quantitative constraint on gas column, metallicity, and accretion state.

- [2605.15555](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15555) Sakiko Obuchi et al. `A VLBA-resolved Jet Associated with Super-Eddington Accretion in a Radio-loud Quasar at z=3.4`
  - **Question:** can a super-Eddington high-redshift quasar sustain a resolved, collimated radio jet?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: eFEDS J084222.9+001000, ID830, a radio-loud quasar at `z=3.4351`.
    - VLBA: observations at `1.6`, `4.9`, and `8.2 GHz`, with `5.6 hr` total observing and `3.6 hr` on target.
    - Ancillary context: eROSITA and VLA/FIRST selection; prior estimates of `M_BH=(4.40+-0.72)e8 M_sun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Image the parsec-scale radio structure with VLBA.
    - Measure spectral properties, core-jet morphology, relativistic beaming constraints, and jet physical scale.
    - Compare the object with lower-redshift radio-loud narrow-line Seyfert 1 analogs.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The source hosts a well-collimated core-jet structure extending about `745 pc`.
    - It is among the most distant known radio-loud quasars with a resolved jet associated with super-Eddington accretion.
    - The inferred jet speed is `v>=0.19c`, with viewing angle `<=79 deg` and little Doppler boosting.
    - The jet must have been sustained for at least `10^3-10^4 yr`.
  - **Position:** important for high-z AGN feedback because it shows that collimated radio jets can coexist with super-Eddington accretion early in cosmic history.

- [2605.15444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15444) Harshda Saxena et al. `Clusters Hiding Under Millimeter Sources (CHUMS) I: Extreme CHUMS`
  - **Question:** how much can central AGN emission hide galaxy clusters from SZ-selected millimeter surveys?
  - **Data:**
    - Cluster sample: three clusters with bright AGN in their central galaxies.
    - CARMA: `30` and `90 GHz` observations used to estimate contaminating AGN flux.
    - ACT: `90` and `150 GHz` maps where the clusters are non-detections before AGN correction.
    - X-ray: Chandra pressure profiles used to estimate the expected SZ signal.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure central AGN emission with CARMA and subtract it from ACT millimeter maps.
    - Use X-ray pressure profiles to subtract expected SZ signal and infer AGN flux at `150 GHz`.
    - Test the role of temporal variability using time-asynchronous observations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - In all three cases, AGN emission overwhelms the SZ decrement and turns real clusters into ACT non-detections.
    - After removing the AGN signal, the clusters become high-SNR detections.
    - The sample shows low fractional variability, so static contamination is already sufficient to bias selection.
    - SZ cluster completeness and cosmological selection functions need explicit treatment of radio/mm-bright central AGN.
  - **Position:** useful for LSS and cluster cosmology because it identifies an AGN-driven incompleteness mode in millimeter cluster surveys.

## High-Redshift Galaxies, Morphology, and Protoclusters

- [2605.15273](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15273) Leindert A. Boogaard et al. `A stellar bar hidden in an extreme gas-rich disk galaxy at z=4.055`
  - **Question:** can a gas-rich early disk form a stellar bar, or do early bars require gas-poor stellar-dominated disks?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: GN20, an extreme star-forming disk galaxy at `z=4.055`, about `1.5 Gyr` after the Big Bang.
    - Observations: simultaneous constraints on stellar light, gas, and dust, including JWST-era morphology and multi-wavelength gas/dust information.
    - Mass budget: baryon dominance over dark matter of about `70+-30%`; gas fraction within the baryonic mass about `75+-25%`.
  - **Method:**
    - Perform morphological analysis of the stellar distribution to identify the bar.
    - Estimate baryonic and dynamical masses and test whether the system is baryon dominated.
    - Compare the result with expectations from gas-rich disk simulations and analytic bar-formation arguments.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - GN20 hosts a stellar bar despite being extremely gas rich.
    - The result breaks the simple interpretation that high-redshift bars form only after early disks consume most of their gas.
    - Gas-rich, baryon-dominated disks can support rapid bar formation in the early Universe.
    - Bars may contribute to early central mass buildup, gas inflow, and possibly fast quenching.
  - **Position:** high-priority morphology result because it ties JWST high-z structure directly to gas-rich disk dynamics and early galaxy transformation.

- [2605.15712](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15712) Shuaiyi Li et al. `Early Emergence of Environmental Effects: Accelerated Galaxy Assembly in a z=2.96 Protocluster in the COSMOS Field`
  - **Question:** are environmental effects already visible in a `z~3` protocluster before strong quenching begins?
  - **Data:**
    - Field: COSMOS.
    - Photometry: COSMOS2025 multi-wavelength catalog over the central COSMOS-Web area.
    - Spectroscopy: JWST/NIRCam wide-field slitless spectroscopy from COSMOS-3D.
    - Structure: PC J1001+0214 at `z=2.96`, overdensity `delta=2.66`, with `131` members, including `21` spectroscopic and `110` high-fidelity photometric members.
  - **Method:**
    - Search COSMOS2025 photometric redshift slices for overdensities.
    - Validate the structure with JWST/NIRCam WFSS redshifts, primarily He I `10830 A` emission.
    - Compare protocluster members with mass-complete coeval field galaxies in stellar mass, SFR, quiescent fraction, and size-mass relation.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Protocluster members show a statistically significant `+0.2 dex` shift in stellar-mass distribution relative to the field.
    - Low-to-intermediate mass members with `log(M_*/M_sun)<=9.7` show SFR enhancement of `+0.11` to `+0.15 dex`.
    - The quiescent fraction remains extremely low and indistinguishable from the field.
    - Size-mass trends tentatively suggest enhanced compactness at fixed stellar mass.
    - The structure is in a growth-dominated phase: environment accelerates assembly before widespread quenching.
  - **Position:** strongly aligned with protocluster and cosmic-web interests because it separates early environmental growth from later environmental shutdown.

- [2605.15327](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15327) Samane Raji et al. `Witnessing the rapid growth of disk galaxies over cosmic time using JWST and HST`
  - **Question:** how have Milky-Way-like disk galaxies grown since `z~1` when size is measured by the star-formation edge rather than effective radius?
  - **Data:**
    - Imaging: HST and JWST data in the GOODS-South field.
    - Photometry: `22` bands used to derive radial age and metallicity profiles.
    - Sample: two disk galaxies at `z~1` with stellar masses around `4e10 M_sun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Define the galaxy edge as the outermost radius where star formation has occurred or is occurring.
    - Derive surface-brightness, age, metallicity, and stellar-mass-density radial profiles.
    - Correct for PSF effects, inclination, Milky Way extinction, and cosmological dimming.
    - Compare `z~1` disks with local disk galaxies.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The age profiles show a U-shape and metallicity declines with galactocentric radius.
    - The age-profile turnover occurs near the galaxy edge, suggesting stellar migration contributes stars beyond the edge.
    - Since `z~1`, the inner `8 kpc` grows little in mass, while the outskirts increase by about `300%`.
    - Disk growth is therefore stronger in the outer parts than effective-radius evolution alone would imply.
  - **Position:** useful morphology and structure paper, especially for interpreting inside-out disk growth with JWST/HST radial diagnostics.

- [2605.15361](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15361) Ilia V. Chugunov et al. `Clumps in spiral galaxies at z<=3: Disentangling two spatial modes of star formation`
  - **Question:** how are star-forming clumps related to spiral arms in galaxies from low redshift to cosmic noon?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `159` spiral galaxies over `0.1<=z<=3.3`.
    - Imaging: HST and JWST observations.
    - Clumps: `3003` clumps identified in residual images after modeling spiral arms.
  - **Method:**
    - Use prior photometric models of spiral arms to subtract spiral structure and identify clumps separately.
    - Measure clump luminosities, masses, sizes, positions, colors, and SED-fit properties.
    - Compare clumps in spiral arms and inter-arm regions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Clump luminosities, masses, and sizes are smaller than commonly reported, likely because spiral arms are modeled separately.
    - Clumps are spatially concentrated toward spiral arms, and clump/spiral properties correlate.
    - Clumps in arms are smaller but brighter than inter-arm clumps, while colors are similar.
    - Spiral arms likely stimulate clump formation without strongly changing clump star-formation properties.
  - **Position:** lower priority than the `z=4.055` bar paper, but useful for separating clump-driven and spiral-driven star formation in high-z disk morphology.

## Reionization, CGM, and Simulation Systematics

- [2605.15310](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15310) Oliver Zier et al. `Introducing the Lumina project: large-volume radiation-hydrodynamic simulations of the epochs of hydrogen and helium reionization`
  - **Question:** can one large-volume radiation-hydrodynamic simulation self-consistently follow galaxies, AGN, H I reionization, and He II reionization to `z=3`?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: Lumina, a `500 cMpc` box with `2 x 6000^3` resolution elements.
    - Mass resolution: baryonic `3.6e6 M_sun` and dark matter `1.9e7 M_sun`.
    - Physics: AREPO, IllustrisTNG galaxy formation, and GPU-accelerated M1 radiation transport in six frequency bins.
    - Initial conditions: separate baryon and dark-matter transfer functions plus baryon-dark-matter streaming velocity.
  - **Method:**
    - Couple galaxy and AGN radiation sources to radiation hydrodynamics.
    - Follow H I, He I, and He II ionization and thermal histories.
    - Produce snapshots, halo catalogs, light-cones, high-cadence reionization outputs, black-hole outputs, and power spectra.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Hydrogen reionization is late and mainly stellar-driven, with median sub-volumes fully ionized by `z~5.2` and residual neutral patches to `z~4.75`.
    - He II reionization is AGN-driven and nearly complete by `z=3`.
    - The Thomson optical depth agrees with Planck, and the IGM thermal history and photoionization background are broadly consistent with observations.
    - The large volume improves statistics for rare sources, environments, and cosmic variance.
  - **Position:** major reionization/IGM simulation infrastructure, especially valuable for linking galaxy and AGN populations to H I and He II topology.

- [2605.15275](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15275) Daniel R. Piacitelli et al. `The Simulated Oxygen Shortage (SOS): Mapping the Missing OVI in Simulated Dwarf Galaxies to Subgrid Physics`
  - **Question:** why do simulations underpredict O VI column densities and O VI masses in the CGM of dwarf galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation suites: Marvelous Massive Dwarfs plus Marvel-ous Dwarfs, and public FIRE-2 simulations.
    - Observational comparison: O VI reservoirs inferred around low-mass galaxies.
    - CGM phase: O VI generally traces cool/warm diffuse gas at large radii.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare simulated O VI column densities and masses with observationally inferred O VI reservoirs.
    - Track the oxygen budget produced over each galaxy's star-formation history.
    - Test effects of ultraviolet background choice, CGM thermal state, feedback implementation, metal mixing, and metal transport.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Both simulation suites underproduce O VI despite different feedback and metal-mixing prescriptions.
    - O VI self-selects gas near `log T/K~4.5`, `log n_gas/cm^-3~-5.0`, and `log Z/Z_sun~-1`.
    - UV background changes and plausible thermal-structure changes do not close the observational gap.
    - The deficit likely points to underproduction of oxygen and insufficient metal transport to large radii in low-mass galaxies.
  - **Position:** relevant to CGM modeling because it ties missing O VI to subgrid yields, star formation, and feedback transport rather than only ionization corrections.

- [2605.15893](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15893) Flaminia Fortuni et al. `Probing the faint-end of simulated galaxy counts at z>3`
  - **Question:** does the faint-end mismatch between observed and simulated near-infrared galaxy counts come from forward modeling or from simulation physics?
  - **Data:**
    - Observations: CANDELS fields, with focus on GOODS-South and near-infrared counts.
    - Simulations: TNG100 and EAGLE light cones.
    - Forward modeling: FORECAST mock images matched to CANDELS-like observing conditions.
  - **Method:**
    - Generate ten independent light-cone and mock-image realizations.
    - Compare input-universe catalogs and detected mock-image catalogs with observed counts.
    - Test field dependence, redshift dependence, completeness, image depth, stellar mass budget, and multi-band SED modeling.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The faint-end deficit appears in all CANDELS fields and emerges at `z>3` in both TNG100 and EAGLE.
    - Completeness corrections show that the missing population is not simply below the detection threshold.
    - Artificially deeper mock images recover some counts near the peak but overpredict the faintest counts.
    - Simulations underproduce faint compact galaxies with bright central cores and overproduce diffuse low-surface-brightness systems.
  - **Position:** important for JWST-era survey interpretation because it shows that simulated high-z faint counts can fail even after observational forward modeling.

## Lensing and Survey Infrastructure

- [2605.15264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15264) Kyle Finner et al. `Near-IR Weak-lensing Measurements in the CANDELS Fields. II. Mass Mapping and Overdensity Characterization`
  - **Question:** can deep near-infrared HST imaging detect low-mass shear-selected overdensities and preview Roman weak-lensing science?
  - **Data:**
    - Fields: CANDELS COSMOS, UDS, EGS, GOODS-N, and GOODS-S.
    - Area: `0.23 deg^2`.
    - Imaging: HST/WFC3 F160W-quality near-infrared mosaics.
    - Source density: about `170` weak-lensing source galaxies per square arcminute.
  - **Method:**
    - Build PSF models and measure galaxy shapes from CANDELS near-infrared images.
    - Construct mass maps and identify weak-lensing overdensity peaks.
    - Estimate masses and compare with multi-wavelength information and literature X-ray detections.
    - Stack tangential shear to recover the average density profile.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The analysis finds `12` shear-selected overdensities with `M_200=(0.2-2.2)e14 M_sun`.
    - The median mass is `5.5e13 M_sun`; the redshift range is `0.22<z<0.9`, with mean `z=0.68`.
    - Seven overdensities have diffuse X-ray emission whose centroids agree with the weak-lensing peaks.
    - Stacking gives an NFW-like profile with concentration `4.9+-2.1` and `M_200=1.3+-0.3e14 M_sun`.
  - **Position:** useful LSS and Roman-pathfinder paper because it demonstrates low-mass mass-selected structure detection from deep near-infrared space imaging.
