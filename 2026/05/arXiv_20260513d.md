## Executive View

The read-first paper is [2605.11064](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11064) Antonio Matteri. It asks whether clustering can discriminate among proposed explanations for the unexpectedly abundant bright JWST galaxies at `z>10`. The key result is that faint samples around `M_UV~-16` all predict similar galaxy bias, `b~7`, while bright samples around `M_UV<-18` become diagnostic: the primordial-black-hole scenario stays near luminosity-independent `b~7`, whereas attenuation-free, feedback-free-burst, and bursty-star-formation models rise toward `b~14` at `M_UV~-19`.

The best direct LSS/quasar-environment methods paper is [2605.11077](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11077) Jiamu Huang. It uses FLAMINGO-10k mocks matched to `25` ASPIRE JWST/NIRCam WFSS quasar fields at `z~6.6` and shows that Poisson pair-count errors understate the real covariance by large factors. For halo-mass inference, Poisson errors make the posterior too narrow by `~1.5-3`, and the nominal `1 sigma` interval contains the truth only `~20%` of the time in coverage tests.

The most relevant IGM/AGN absorber paper is [2605.12188](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12188) Patrick Petitjean. It reclassifies proximate metal absorbers in SDSS DR16 and argues that PDLAs are not only companion-galaxy gas; part of the population traces cold dense clouds in a quasar-driven inflow/outflow cycle. The scale is large: `164,901` selected quasars, `5,373` metal-ProxSys candidates, `4,257` confirmed metal-line systems, and `13%` Ghostly or coronagraphic DLA classes.

Two practical survey papers are worth keeping. [2605.12503](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12503) Jui-Kuan Chan finds `19,685` hidden DESI DR1 LAEs at `z~2-3.5` using a CNN with `95.2%` purity and `95.9%` completeness. [2605.11099](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11099) S. L. Ahad presents a zero-install browser tool for comparing Euclid, LSST, Roman, DESI Legacy, HSC, KiDS, and other imaging footprints.

The JWST/LRD interpretation paper is [2605.12141](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12141) Fabrice Martins. CMFGEN non-LTE expanding-atmosphere models at `L=10^10 L_sun` reproduce many LRD-like spectral properties but have a clear limitation: they struggle to make both a genuine Balmer break and strong emission lines without dust or more complex geometry.

## Cosmic-Dawn Galaxies and High-Redshift Clustering

- [2605.11064](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11064) Antonio Matteri et al. `Clustering constraints on super-early galaxy formation scenarios`
  - **Question:** can galaxy clustering break the degeneracy among models that all reproduce the high JWST UV luminosity function at `z>10`?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: Shin-Uchuu dark-matter-only halo catalog at `z~11`.
    - Resolution and volume: `L_box=140 cMpc/h`, `M_DM=8.97e5 M_sun/h`, resolving `~10^8 M_sun` atomic-cooling halos with about `100` particles.
    - Observational comparison: bias measurements from Dalmasso et al. and Weibel et al. for JWST high-redshift galaxy samples.
  - **Method:**
    - Populate halos with four luminosity-function-matched scenarios: attenuation-free, feedback-free burst, bursty star formation, and primordial-black-hole models.
    - Compute the two-point correlation function for flux-limited samples across `-20<M_UV<-16`.
    - Fit power-law clustering and convert to galaxy bias as a function of UV magnitude threshold.
    - Compare predicted `b(M_UV)` against current JWST clustering constraints.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Faint samples near `M_UV~-16` are weak discriminants because all models predict `b~7`.
    - Bright samples near `M_UV<-18` are diagnostic because non-PBH scenarios place bright galaxies in more massive halos and drive bias upward.
    - At `M_UV~-19`, the PBH model remains near `b~7`, while the other model families reach `b~14`.
    - Current data do not rule out any model at significant confidence because observational and theoretical uncertainties are still large.
    - A complete sample around `M_UV<-18` would probe the knee of `b(M_UV)` and maximize model separation.
  - **Position:** directly useful for JWST cosmic-dawn interpretation because it shifts the super-early galaxy debate from abundance fitting alone to measurable large-scale clustering.

- [2605.11077](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11077) Jiamu Huang et al. `The Impact of Cosmic Variance and Satellites on JWST Clustering Measurements at Redshift around 6`
  - **Question:** how much do cosmic variance, correlated radial bins, and satellite treatment affect JWST/WFSS halo-mass inference for `z~6` quasars and [O III] emitters?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: FLAMINGO-10k dark-matter-only box, approximately `(2.8 cGpc)^3`, using `10080^3` CDM particles.
    - Survey model: `25` ASPIRE JWST/NIRCam WFSS pointings around `z~6.6` quasars.
    - Geometry: each ASPIRE pointing covers about `2.2 arcmin x 5.1 arcmin`, or `~3.8 x 8.8 cMpc/h` transverse size at `z~6.6`, with line-of-sight depth `~485 cMpc/h` over `z=5.3-7.0`.
  - **Method:**
    - Generate `1000` ASPIRE-like mock realizations with real field coverage, spectral sensitivity, and flux limits.
    - Assign quasars and [O III] emitters using step-function HOD thresholds over `log M_h,min(QSO)=11.0-12.5` and `log M_h,min([O III])=10.5-11.0`.
    - Measure [O III] emitter auto-correlation and quasar-[O III] emitter cross-correlation.
    - Build mass-dependent covariance matrices and compare Bayesian inference against diagonal Poisson likelihoods.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Poisson pair-count errors underestimate correlation-function uncertainty by about a factor of `~3`.
    - Halo-mass uncertainties are too narrow by `~1.5-3` if Poisson errors replace the full covariance matrix.
    - At fiducial ASPIRE masses, `log M_h,min(QSO)=12.2` and `log M_h,min([O III])=10.6`, the Poisson posterior is about `1.8` times narrower.
    - In `1000` mock coverage tests, covariance-based `1 sigma` regions contain the truth in `~65%` of realizations, while Poisson `1 sigma` regions contain it in only `~20%`.
    - The inferred quasar halo mass is robust to whether central and satellite [O III] emitters share the same mass threshold.
  - **Position:** essential methods context for ASPIRE, EIGER-like, and future JWST/Roman high-z environment measurements because small pencil-beam fields are cosmic-variance dominated.

- [2605.12503](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12503) Jui-Kuan Chan et al. `Unveiling Hidden Lyman Alpha Emitters in the DESI DR1 Data`
  - **Question:** can machine learning recover high-redshift LAEs in DESI DR1 spectra that the standard DESI pipeline misidentifies or misses?
  - **Data:**
    - DESI: DR1 ELG spectra, with blue-arm wavelength coverage used over `3600-5550 Angstrom`.
    - Training basis: visually inspected DESI LAE and non-LAE spectra, plus supplementary QSOs and low-z ELGs.
    - Training split: `4,592` spectra for training, `984` for validation, and `984` for testing.
    - Independent check: DESI-HETDEX LAEs used to test completeness behavior.
  - **Method:**
    - Build a CNN that detects Lyalpha emission and estimates the Lyalpha redshift from the line peak.
    - Use candidate-selection routes for LAEs misidentified as QSOs and LAEs misidentified as low-z [O II] emitters.
    - Apply post-processing to separate star-forming LAEs from AGN-like cases using C IV/Lyalpha and other line checks.
    - Run the final model on roughly `2 million` DESI DR1 galaxy spectra.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The test-set performance is `95.2%` purity and `95.9%` completeness for LAE detection.
    - The final catalog contains `19,685` LAEs at `z~2-3.5`.
    - About `82%` of the final LAE sample lacks the correct Redrock redshift.
    - The LAEs are mostly bright-end systems with `L_Lyalpha>10^43 erg/s`.
    - The composite spectrum detects weak metal absorption and emission features, including C IV and Mg II, useful for gas-flow and LyC-leakage diagnostics.
  - **Position:** highly practical for DESI-II and LAE large-scale-structure work because it recovers a population hidden by low-z galaxy and QSO template assumptions.

## Quasar Feedback, Obscured Sources, and Galaxy Quenching

- [2605.12188](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12188) Patrick Petitjean et al. `Proximate damped Lyman-alpha systems as tracers of quasar feedback`
  - **Question:** are proximate DLAs only companion-galaxy absorbers, or do some trace dense cold gas in the immediate quasar feedback cycle?
  - **Data:**
    - SDSS DR16Q: `750,414` quasars in the parent catalog.
    - Selected sample: `164,901` quasars with `z_em>2.11`, low BAL absorption index, and sufficient S/N.
    - Absorber search: metal-line systems within `+-3000 km/s` of the quasar redshift.
    - Confirmed systems: `5,373` candidates, `4,257` confirmed metal-line ProxSys after visual inspection.
  - **Method:**
    - Select systems with multiple low-ionization and high-ionization metal transitions.
    - Classify proximate absorbers into Ghostly systems, coronagraphic DLAs, standard DLAs, sub-DLAs, and lower-equivalent-width classes.
    - Use velocity distributions, stacked spectra, Si II* absorption, high-ionization lines, and dust indicators to build a physical sequence.
    - Interpret the absorber kinematics in a chaotic-cold-accretion and multiphase-feedback framework.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Ghostly and coronagraphic DLA classes account for about `13%` of metal-rich ProxSys.
    - Standard DLAs cluster within `+-1000 km/s` of the quasar systemic redshift, consistent with host-galaxy gas.
    - Ghostly and Si II*-bearing systems show broader kinematics, including outflows to `~-2200 km/s` and inflowing or returning clouds to `+1800 km/s`.
    - Dense clouds can survive near the quasar if `n_H>~10^3 cm^-3`, consistent with Si II* constraints.
    - The absorber population is better described as a cold-cloud fountain with simultaneous inflow and outflow than as simple spherical clearing.
  - **Position:** important for interpreting high-z quasar absorption environments because proximate neutral gas can be part of AGN feedback rather than only large-scale companion structure.

- [2605.12141](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12141) Fabrice Martins et al. `Non-LTE atmosphere models of very luminous sources and their applicability to Little Red Dots, quasi-stars, and similar objects`
  - **Question:** can expanding non-LTE atmosphere models reproduce the unusual spectra of JWST Little Red Dots without assuming a standard exposed AGN spectrum?
  - **Data:**
    - Model grid: CMFGEN non-LTE atmosphere spectra.
    - Luminosity scale: `L=10^10 L_sun`, typical of many LRD interpretations.
    - Inner-boundary temperatures: `5000-12000 K`.
    - Geometry: spherical, 1D, expanding wind-like atmospheres with several metallicities.
    - Comparisons: stacked LRD spectra and individual objects such as GN 9771 and GLIMPSE 17775.
  - **Method:**
    - Compute line-blanketed, non-LTE radiative-transfer models with dense expanding atmospheres.
    - Compare continuum shape, Balmer/Paschen emission, Balmer decrement, and metal-line behavior with LRD spectra.
    - Test whether dust attenuation and wind-density changes can recover the observed red optical and blue UV structure.
    - Compare CMFGEN behavior with CLOUDY, SIROCCO, black-hole-star, and envelope models discussed in the LRD literature.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - CMFGEN models reproduce many LRD-like features, including broad hydrogen lines and several helium, oxygen, iron, and calcium lines.
    - Most models do not create a sharp genuine Balmer break; the coolest, lower-density cases can produce one but then weaken line emission.
    - Matching most LRD SEDs and Balmer decrements requires dust attenuation with `A_V~1.9-2.7`.
    - The absorbed fraction can be high, `~75-93%`, but is argued to remain compatible with current IR limits for many LRD stacks.
    - Spherical models struggle to make symmetric Balmer lines and a genuine Balmer break plus strong emission lines at the same time.
  - **Position:** useful for LRD interpretation because it identifies which spectral features can arise from dense extended atmospheres and which still demand dust, clumpiness, or non-spherical geometry.

- [2605.11101](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11101) Pallavi Patil et al. `Tracing Radio AGN-Driven Quenching in Post-Starburst Galaxies at Cosmic Noon`
  - **Question:** are radio-mode AGN directly linked to rapid quenching in cosmic-noon post-starburst galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Field: UKIDSS UDS.
    - Galaxy sample: photometrically selected post-starburst, quiescent, and star-forming galaxies over `0.5<z<3`.
    - VLA: `1.4 GHz` imaging with A/B/C configurations and `~1.8 x 1.6 arcsec` synthesized beam.
    - Main catalog: `88,147` cross-matched sources, including `1,943` PSBs; mass-complete analysis above `10^10.2 M_sun`.
    - Ancillary data: Chandra, Spitzer/IRAC, ALMA `870 micron`, and optical/NIR UDS photometry.
  - **Method:**
    - Cross-match VLA radio components to the UDS K-band selected catalog.
    - Apply a luminosity threshold of `L_1.4GHz>=10^24 W/Hz` to isolate radio-AGN-like sources.
    - Stack radio non-detections by redshift and stellar mass.
    - Compare radio luminosities with SED-derived SFRs and far-IR-radio expectations.
    - Check X-ray, MIR, and submillimeter counterparts to separate AGN from obscured star formation.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Only `0.8+-0.3%` of PSBs are radio detected above `L_1.4GHz>=10^24 W/Hz`.
    - The fraction rises to `5+-2%` for massive PSBs with `M_*>10^11 M_sun`, comparable to massive quiescent galaxies but below massive star-forming galaxies.
    - Detected PSBs have radio luminosities exceeding star-formation expectations by a median factor of `37`.
    - Their compact radio morphologies, usually `<15-40 kpc`, favor weak low-luminosity AGN rather than large radio jets.
    - Stacking yields only a `3.9 sigma` signal in the highest-mass PSB bin, supporting a short and weak radio-AGN duty cycle after or during quenching.
  - **Position:** relevant to cosmic-noon quenching because it argues against powerful current radio jets as the main shutdown mechanism for most PSBs, while leaving room for prior or weak maintenance-mode AGN.

## Nebular Physics, First-Star Enrichment, and Survey Infrastructure

- [2605.11377](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11377) Yifei Jin et al. `The Internal Nebular Attenuation Curve of Three-Dimensional Turbulent HII regions`
  - **Question:** how do turbulent density fields inside H II regions affect hydrogen-line attenuation curves and Balmer-decrement interpretation?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: ATHENA++ turbulent boxes with `125^3` cells.
    - Turbulence: Mach numbers `0.997`, `2.484`, `4.739`, and `8.791`, with multiple driving wavenumbers.
    - Radiative transfer: latest M3D photoionization and dust Monte Carlo code.
    - Outputs: projected hydrogen-line maps and attenuation maps.
  - **Method:**
    - Generate turbulent density fields with controlled Mach numbers.
    - Illuminate the boxes with photoionization radiative transfer to form 3D H II regions.
    - Propagate emission-line photons through gas and dust absorption plus dust scattering.
    - Infer internal attenuation curves from attenuated hydrogen lines and compare against the intrinsic line ratios.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Local hydrogen line ratios vary within each H II region because the gas is neither pure Case A nor pure Case B.
    - The global intrinsic `Halpha/Hbeta` ratio is `3.02-3.03`, not the standard Case B value `2.86`, mainly because the regions are cooler than `10,000 K`.
    - Higher Mach numbers increase clumpiness and spatial attenuation variations.
    - The integrated selective attenuation curve is nearly independent of Mach number because heavily obscured dense regions contribute little to the observed global flux.
    - Spatial attenuation maps still encode turbulence and are smoother at low Mach number and more filamentary at high Mach number.
  - **Position:** useful for JWST nebular-line work because it cautions that Balmer-decrement corrections can be biased by local H II-region physics even when the global curve appears stable.

- [2605.11647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11647) Aron Kordt et al. `Metal Enrichment by the First Stars Exploding at the Lower Energy Limit of Pair-Instability Supernovae`
  - **Question:** can low-energy Pop III pair-instability supernovae enrich second-generation stars above the EMP regime, thereby hiding PISN abundance signatures from EMP surveys?
  - **Data:**
    - Cosmological radiation-hydrodynamic simulations of Pop III star formation and metal enrichment.
    - PISN model: low-energy lower-limit case with `M=140 M_sun` and `E_SN=5e51 erg`.
    - Comparison: previous high-energy PISN simulations with `E_SN=1e53 erg`.
    - Tracers: Monte Carlo metal particles following PISN ejecta and second-generation star-forming gas.
  - **Method:**
    - Follow Pop III formation, radiative feedback, PISN explosions, metal transport, gas recollapse, and second-generation star formation.
    - Compare recollapse times, gas retention, halo metallicities, and second-generation stellar metallicities.
    - Place the simulated descendants in the EMP abundance/metallicity regime.
    - Compare the implication with the observed absence of secure PISN patterns in EMP stars.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Low-energy PISNe unbind less gas and yield shorter recollapse times than high-energy PISNe.
    - Second-generation stars still form at low metallicities compatible with EMP surveys.
    - The median stellar metallicity is lower than in the high-energy case because the explosion energy is `~20` times smaller, although the reduction is partly offset by less dilution volume.
    - If Pop III PISNe were common, their odd-even abundance signatures should appear among EMP stars.
    - The absence of robust PISN-enriched EMP stars therefore still favors PISNe being rare rather than merely hidden at high metallicity.
  - **Position:** relevant to cosmic-dawn chemical enrichment because it strengthens EMP archaeology as a constraint on the high-mass tail of the Pop III IMF.

- [2605.11099](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11099) S. L. Ahad et al. `Survey Footprint Explorer: A Browser-Based Interactive Tool for Visualizing and Cross-Matching Astronomical Survey Footprints`
  - **Question:** can survey-footprint comparison and source cross-matching be done in a zero-install browser tool for major imaging programs?
  - **Data:**
    - Built-in footprints: `13` survey products spanning X-ray to near-infrared.
    - Included high-value surveys: Euclid DR1, LSST WFD, Roman HLWAS, Roman HLTDS, DESI Legacy DR9, DES, HSC, KiDS, UNIONS, eRASS1, and ACT Legacy.
    - Area range: `7.7 deg^2` to `21,524.4 deg^2`.
  - **Method:**
    - Encode footprints as multi-order coverage maps.
    - Render an interactive globe and an equirectangular map fully in client-side JavaScript.
    - Compute overlap and "cross-match only" display modes locally in the browser.
    - Allow user catalog upload and append membership columns for selected footprints.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The tool runs without a server or local installation.
    - Euclid DR1, LSST WFD, and Roman HLWAS overlap checks are supported as a direct use case.
    - Custom source catalogs and custom MOC footprints remain browser-local.
    - The tool is useful for fast field-planning and multi-survey overlap triage rather than for deep survey simulation.
  - **Position:** lower scientific priority than the high-z papers, but operationally useful for planning cross-survey galaxy, quasar, and LSS samples.
