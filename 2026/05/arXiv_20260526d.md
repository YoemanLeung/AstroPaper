## Executive View

The read-first entry is [2605.24112](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24112) Xuejian Shen. Lumina is used to model high-redshift AGN demographics from luminous quasars to JWST Little Red Dots at `z>=3`, tying black-hole growth to the evolving IGM and He II reionization in a `500 cMpc` radiation-hydrodynamic box.

The strongest galaxy-formation entry is [2605.24104](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24104) Jenna Samuel. BonFIRE and CampFIRE produce early-galaxy populations across `M_*~10^4-10^10 M_sun`, with clustered bursty star formation, high halo-scale efficiencies in massive halos, and a faint-end UVLF turnover near `M_UV~-14`.

Several entries are immediately useful for JWST interpretation. [2605.24264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24264) A. J. Barger uses A2744 JWST+ALMA data to separate dusty star-forming galaxies, LRDs, and quiescent systems with rest-frame red colors. [2605.25130](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25130) Ravi Jaiswar quantifies how SED-fitting choices can move `xi_ion` by more than `1 dex` for individual sources. [2605.25691](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25691) Borja Perez-Diaz introduces [Ne III]/[O III] as an electron-temperature route when [O III]4363 is too faint.

The LSS and dark-matter side includes [2605.24174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24174) Charlie Mace on SIDM core-collapse modeling for substructure lensing and [2605.24099](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24099) Maria Luisa Buzzo on Fornax dark-matter-deficient dwarf analogs.

## High-Redshift AGN, LRDs, and Early Galaxies

- [2605.24112](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24112) Xuejian Shen et al. `The Lumina Project: The Demographics of Active Galactic Nuclei from Quasars to Little Red Dots at z>=3`
  - **Question:** can one cosmological model describe high-redshift quasars, faint LRDs, black-hole growth, and IGM ionization?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: Lumina radiation-hydrodynamic box of `(500 cMpc)^3`.
    - Resolution: `2 x 6000^3` elements.
    - Population: galaxies and SMBHs across hydrogen and helium reionization.
  - **Method:**
    - Predict AGN luminosity functions from hard X-rays to mid-infrared.
    - Map simulated SMBHs to canonical AGN and LRDs with bolometric and extinction/absorption corrections.
    - Assign SMBHs with `M_BH<=10 M_seed~10^7 M_sun` to an LRD phase with `30%` duty cycle.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The empirical framework reproduces observed LRD luminosity functions and clustering.
    - Pre-JWST quasar luminosity functions are recovered when the bright end includes `~0.3 dex` log-normal bolometric scatter.
    - The same AGN population dominates the modeled He II reionization.
  - **Position:** top AGN/LRD entry because it places JWST LRDs inside a cosmological black-hole and IGM framework.

- [2605.24104](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24104) Jenna Samuel et al. `Resolving galaxy formation in the early Universe with BonFIRE and CampFIRE`
  - **Question:** what early galaxy populations emerge in high-resolution FIRE-3 simulations at `z>=6`?
  - **Data:**
    - BonFIRE: `L~40 cMpc`, baryon mass resolution `~5 x 10^4 M_sun`.
    - CampFIRE: `L~5 cMpc` at `~800 M_sun` and `~6 x 10^3 M_sun` baryon resolution.
    - Dynamic range: `M_*~10^4-10^10 M_sun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine large-volume statistics with higher-resolution zoomed volume through resampling.
    - Measure star-formation efficiencies, compactness, UV luminosity functions, and luminosity variability.
    - Include first results from a simple Pop III model.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Early galaxy formation is clustered and bursty.
    - Halo-scale star-formation efficiencies reach `10-30%` in high-mass halos.
    - Some low-mass halos exceed `1%` efficiency and host ultra-compact galaxies with narrow age spreads.
    - UVLFs agree broadly at `M_UV>-19`, turn over near `M_UV~-14`, and slightly overpredict brighter galaxies.
  - **Position:** high-priority early-galaxy simulation paper because it tests whether burstiness and high efficiency can reproduce JWST-era demographics.

- [2605.24264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24264) A. J. Barger et al. `A Redshift-based Red Selection of Dusty Star-forming Galaxies`
  - **Question:** can rest-frame red colors separate dusty star-forming galaxies, LRDs, and quiescent systems in JWST cluster fields?
  - **Data:**
    - Field: A2744 massive lensing cluster.
    - JWST: `1.5-4.44 micron` imaging.
    - ALMA: complete `870 micron` and/or `1.2 mm` observations.
  - **Method:**
    - Select `z=1.5-5.5` galaxies with rest-frame `f_J/f_V>3`.
    - Cross-match against ALMA detections.
    - Use compactness and UVJ colors to distinguish DSFGs, LRDs, and quiescent galaxies.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The red selection recovers `34/41` ALMA sources above `4.5 sigma`.
    - Luminous red sources are generally extended and mostly DSFGs.
    - Less luminous compact red sources correspond to LRDs.
    - LRDs and quiescent galaxies are not detected above `3 sigma` in the ALMA images.
  - **Position:** practical JWST+ALMA selection paper because it clarifies which red sources are dusty, quiescent, or LRD-like.

## Reionization Diagnostics and Metallicity Methods

- [2605.25130](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25130) Ravi Jaiswar et al. `Exploring biases in derived stellar parameters and the ionizing photon production efficiency`
  - **Question:** how much do data choices and SED models bias `xi_ion` and stellar parameters for EoR-like galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `z=3` EoR analog galaxies with reliable photometric coverage.
    - Methods: spectroscopic and photometric constraints across three SED-fitting codes.
  - **Method:**
    - Vary star-formation histories, stellar-population models, dust prescriptions, and photoionization assumptions.
    - Compare stellar masses, SFRs, and `xi_ion` from different data/model choices.
    - Test whether inferred `xi_ion` evolution depends on method.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Median stellar mass can vary by more than `0.6 dex`.
    - SFR can vary by more than `0.9 dex`.
    - Individual-source `xi_ion` can vary by more than `1.1 dex` between spectroscopic and photometric derivations.
    - Apparent redshift evolution in `xi_ion` depends on using a consistent derivation method.
  - **Position:** essential methods warning because EoR source budgets are sensitive to modeling choices, not only observed line or continuum fluxes.

- [2605.25691](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25691) Borja Perez-Diaz et al. `Beyond the [OIII]4363 auroral line: [NeIII]3868 as a direct tracer of electron temperature`
  - **Question:** can [Ne III]3868 recover electron temperatures when [O III]4363 is too faint?
  - **Data:**
    - Calibration sample: galaxies across `z~0-9` plus extragalactic H II regions.
    - Lines: [Ne III]3868, [O III]4959,5007, and [O III]4363 where available.
  - **Method:**
    - Use photoionization models to derive a relation between `O3Ne3=[O III]/[Ne III]` and electron temperature.
    - Combine O3Ne3 with O3O2 to account for ionization.
    - Compare inferred temperatures to direct [O III]4363 measurements.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - O3Ne3 plus O3O2 gives temperatures consistent with [O III]4363-based values within uncertainties.
    - The diagnostic targets cool or metal-rich regimes with `T_e~6000-13500 K`.
    - It provides a route around the weak [O III]4363 auroral line in metal-enriched systems.
  - **Position:** useful JWST metallicity-method paper because high-redshift abundance work often needs alternatives to faint auroral lines.

- [2605.25339](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25339) David C. Flynn et al. `A Unified [CII] Morpho-Kinematic Corpus for 31 Star-Forming Galaxies at z = 4.26-5.68: The High-z Kinematic Corpus Z1`
  - **Question:** can ALPINE [C II] kinematics be organized into a machine-readable high-redshift corpus?
  - **Data:**
    - ALMA ALPINE: `31` star-forming main-sequence galaxies at `z=4.26-5.68`.
    - Tier 1: `8` confirmed rotators with 3DBarolo tilted-ring fits.
    - Tier 2: `23` galaxies with morpho-kinematic classification.
  - **Method:**
    - Standardize rotation, dispersion, dynamical mass, stellar mass, SFR, disk criteria, and geometry fields.
    - Release JSON, CSV, JSONL, and per-galaxy archive products.
    - Demonstrate single-galaxy and population notebooks.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Tier-1 rotators have per-ring rotation curves with `2-3` rings per galaxy.
    - Applying one kinematic correction yields negative median values at high redshift, contrasting with positive local-disk values.
    - The corpus is best treated as a data product for reproducible high-z kinematic comparisons.
  - **Position:** data-infrastructure entry for high-redshift [C II] dynamics and RAG-style literature tools.

## Environment, Dark Matter, and Transients

- [2605.25557](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25557) Bahar Bidaran et al. `The Gas-Phase Mass-Metallicity Relation of Dwarf Galaxies Across Large-Scale Environments Using the CAVITY Parent Sample`
  - **Question:** does one dwarf-galaxy MZR apply across voids, filaments, and clusters?
  - **Data:**
    - CAVITY parent sample: dwarf galaxies with `8.9<log(M_star/M_sun)<9.5`.
    - Environment bins: `353` void, `311` filament, and `22` cluster dwarfs.
    - Spectroscopy: SDSS optical spectra measured with pyPipe3D.
  - **Method:**
    - Derive emission-line fluxes, gas-phase metallicities, and SFRs.
    - Fit MZR slopes by large-scale and local environment.
    - Compare isolated and non-isolated dwarfs inside voids and filaments.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The MZR slope steepens in voids, `0.28+/-0.03`, and flattens in clusters, `0.17+/-0.08`.
    - Isolated filament dwarfs resemble void dwarfs.
    - Non-isolated filament dwarfs resemble cluster dwarfs, consistent with pre-processing.
    - Part of the dwarf MZR scatter arises from environment.
  - **Position:** strong environment/metallicity result because it links cosmic-web context to low-mass chemical enrichment.

- [2605.24174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24174) Charlie Mace et al. `The Sensitivity of Substructure Lensing to SIDM Core-collapse Model Variation`
  - **Question:** how sensitive are substructure-lensing predictions to the modeling of SIDM core collapse?
  - **Data:**
    - Theory and simulations of self-interacting dark-matter subhalos.
    - Observable: two-point statistics of effective deflection-field divergence and curl.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare smoothly evolving density profiles with instantaneous collapse.
    - Test probabilistic collapse versus individual-halo evolution.
    - Vary initial and final density-profile parameters.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Small-scale lensing statistics are sensitive to most core-collapse modeling choices.
    - Detectability depends on the observational probe and scale.
    - SIDM constraints from substructure lensing need explicit model-variation accounting.
  - **Position:** useful dark-matter methods paper for interpreting future strong-lensing substructure measurements.

- [2605.24099](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24099) Maria Luisa Buzzo et al. `Dark matter-deficient twins: FCC 224 and FCC 240 as possible analogues of NGC 1052-DF2 and DF4`
  - **Question:** are FCC 224 and FCC 240 possible Fornax analogs of the dark-matter-deficient DF2/DF4 pair?
  - **Data:**
    - VLT/MUSE: integral-field spectroscopy of FCC 224 and FCC 240.
    - Tracers: stellar populations, internal kinematics, and globular-cluster systems.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure velocity dispersions and stellar-population ages.
    - Compare mass estimators with stellar-only, cuspy-halo, and cored-halo interpretations.
    - Compare GC luminosity functions and pair configuration to DF2/DF4.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Both systems have low velocity dispersions and luminous globular clusters.
    - Their inner dynamics are more naturally explained by stars alone than by standard dark-matter halos.
    - The pair is old, with mass-weighted stellar ages near `10 Gyr`.
    - Their projected separation, `75 kpc`, is much smaller than the DF2/DF4 separation.
  - **Position:** local dwarf result, but relevant to environmental pathways for dark-matter-deficient stellar systems.

- [2605.24088](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24088) Valeria Aparicio et al. `Discovery and Analysis of a Type II Supernova Candidate at z = 3.19 from JWST's COSMOS-Web Survey`
  - **Question:** what can JWST supernovae at `z>3` reveal about massive-star deaths in young, low-metallicity environments?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/COSMOS-Web: SN 2023aeaf at `z=3.195`.
    - Photometry: two epochs separated by about one rest-frame month.
    - Spectrum: SN plus host around `30` rest-frame days after discovery.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare photometry to core-collapse and Type Ia SN models.
    - Fit the host galaxy SED with Prospector.
    - Estimate progenitor and circumstellar-material properties from limited light-curve coverage.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The transient is most likely a Type II supernova.
    - Best-fitting progenitor mass is around `12 M_sun`, with `~0.5 M_sun` of circumstellar material.
    - The host has `log(M_*/M_sun)=9.04` and gas-phase metallicity `12+log(O/H)=7.82`.
  - **Position:** transient side entry, but useful because high-redshift core-collapse SNe are becoming JWST probes of early stellar populations.
