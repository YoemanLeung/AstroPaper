## Executive view

The read-first paper is [2605.02539](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02539) Weida Hu. It uses high-S/N JWST/NIRSpec prism spectra of `143` galaxies at `5<z<7` to measure IGM transmission directly from galaxy sightlines. The GOODS-S structure GS-z6IS has `T_IGM=0.17+/-0.02`, a line-of-sight scale of `~110 cMpc`, and projected extent of at least `21 x 17 cMpc^2`. This is a direct bridge between Lyman-alpha visibility, galaxy overdensities, and ionized structures near the end of reionization.

The strongest cosmic-dawn AGN paper is [2605.00978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00978) Gene C. K. Leung. It detects multiwavelength variability in the lensed `z=6.51` quasar J0439+1634 using WISE, near-IR imaging, HST, JWST, XMM-Newton, and eROSITA. The variable SED slope `-1.58+/-0.25` is consistent with a thin disk, and the WISE lag limit of `<160 days` observed-frame keeps high-z accretion-disk reverberation within reach of Rubin and Roman.

The best galaxy-evolution complements are [2605.01095](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01095) Zoke W. Sackih, [2605.02493](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02493) Vaidik Prasal, and [2605.01981](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01981) Zhuoqi Liu. Together they cover a possible `z~8` COSMOS overdensity, JWST morphology and inside-out quenching in cosmic-noon massive quiescent galaxies, and the diverse morpho-kinematic origins of quasar nebulae at `z~0.4-1.4`.

The support papers worth tracking are [2605.01380](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01380) Zihan Wang for reionization-model degeneracies, [2605.01994](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01994) Kritti Sharma for FRB constraints on void baryons, [2605.01000](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01000) Ilsang Yoon for SKA/ngVLA high-z radio-FIR forecasts, [2605.02663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02663) Rogemar A. Riffel for JWST/MIRI jet feedback, and [2605.01505](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01505) Itai Bromberg for CGM/IGM ionisation-state systematics.

## Reionization, cosmic dawn, and high-z galaxies

- [2605.02539](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02539) Weida Hu et al. `Using Lyman-alpha Transmitted Spectrum to Probe IGM Transmission and Identify Ionized Structures in Cosmic Reionization`
  - **Question:** can high-S/N galaxy spectra measure individual-sightline IGM transmission and identify ionized structures during late reionization?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRSpec prism: `143` galaxies at `5<z<7` from CAPERS and JADES
    - GOODS-S: the high-S/N `z=6.335` galaxy GS-18846 and other galaxies in the `5.75<z<6` overdensity
    - Lyman-alpha sample context: known GOODS-S LAEs including GS-9422 and galaxy overdensity measurements from the same redshift slice
  - **Method:**
    - Fit galaxy SEDs to predict the intrinsic continuum blueward of Lyman-alpha
    - Compare predicted and observed flux between Lyman-beta and Lyman-alpha to estimate `T_IGM`
    - Convert transmission to effective optical depth and compare with quasar-based measurements
    - Map the high-transmission interval against the spatial and redshift distribution of GOODS-S galaxies
    - Compare LAE equivalent-width distributions inside and outside the candidate ionized structure
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Current NIRSpec spectroscopy is deep enough to measure IGM transmission on selected individual galaxy sightlines.
    - GS-18846 gives `T_IGM=0.17+/-0.02` at `5.75<z<6`, about an order of magnitude above prior average transmission at similar redshift.
    - The structure GS-z6IS spans `~110 cMpc` along the line of sight and at least `21 x 17 cMpc^2` across the GOODS-S spectroscopic footprint.
    - The effective optical depth is `tau_eff=1.77+/-0.12` for GS-18846 versus `2.59+/-0.53` for other GOODS-S galaxies at `6.3<z<7`.
    - The same redshift interval contains `64` galaxies, compared with `10-27` galaxies in adjacent bins, so enhanced transmission is spatially associated with a galaxy overdensity.
    - GS-9422 has total Lyman-alpha transmission of `83+/-2%`, supporting a large ionized region rather than only source-intrinsic Lyman-alpha differences.
  - **Position:** high-priority reionization paper because it moves Lyman-alpha visibility from population statistics toward direct IGM-transmission mapping with JWST galaxy spectra

- [2605.00978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00978) Gene C. K. Leung et al. `Discovery of Quasar Variability and Early Accretion Disk Signatures at Cosmic Dawn`
  - **Question:** can quasar variability at `z>6` constrain early SMBH accretion-disk structure?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: the strongly lensed quasar J043947.08+163415.7 at `z=6.51`, observed `~850 Myr` after the Big Bang
    - WISE/NEOWISE: W1 and W2 light curves from the unWISE individual-image catalog
    - Near-IR and optical support: 2MASS, UHS, HST/WFC3 F125W and F160W, and JWST/NIRSpec IFU data
    - X-ray support: XMM-Newton and eROSITA measurements of the soft X-ray corona
    - Parent search: `531` known quasars at `z>5.3`, with `30` multi-epoch WISE-detected objects after quality cuts
  - **Method:**
    - Calibrate WISE light curves with nearby Gaia reference stars and search for significant mid-IR variability
    - Construct multiwavelength light curves from rest-UV/optical to X-rays
    - Build a variable SED by comparing bright and faint states
    - Compare the variable SED slope and inter-band lag limits with thin-disk predictions
    - Test alternative variability mechanisms including microlensing and broad-line contamination
  - **Conclusion:**
    - J0439+1634 varies significantly in WISE W1 at `10.9 sigma`, with rest-UV/optical and X-ray variability also detected.
    - The X-ray variability amplitude is much larger than the IR/optical amplitude, consistent with a compact corona plus a larger disk-emitting region.
    - The variable SED has slope `-1.58+/-0.25`, consistent with a geometrically thin, optically thick accretion disk.
    - The quasar has Eddington ratio `(60+/-10)%`, so thin-disk-like structure persists in a high-redshift, high-accretion regime.
    - WISE W2-W1 reverberation gives no significant delay and a `90%` upper limit of `<160 days` observed-frame, consistent with thin-disk expectations at this luminosity.
    - The WISE search found two quasars with `>=5 sigma` variability among the high-z multi-epoch subset, so upcoming Rubin and Roman samples should enable population-level high-z variability work.
  - **Position:** central cosmic-dawn AGN paper because it makes variability a practical probe of early SMBH accretion physics rather than only a low-redshift reverberation tool

- [2605.01095](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01095) Zoke W. Sackih et al. `Validating z > 7.5 Lyman Break Galaxy candidates in the COSMOS field with JWST/PASSAGE`
  - **Question:** do the brightest COSMOS-Web `z>=7.5` photometric candidates survive JWST slitless-spectroscopic validation?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/PASSAGE: one NIRISS pointing in field Par028
    - NIRISS grisms: F115W, F150W, and F200W, covering `~4.8 arcmin^2`
    - Target list: seven COSMOS-Web photometric candidates at `z>=7.5`
  - **Method:**
    - Extract slitless spectra for each candidate and inspect continuum breaks and line detections
    - Fit the confirmed source with BAGPIPES to infer the redshift
    - Compare the inferred surface density with wide-area photometric survey expectations
  - **Conclusion:**
    - One luminous LBG is confirmed at `z=7.962+0.003/-0.006` with `m_F150W=25.9 AB`.
    - The other six candidates are too faint in the continuum, with `m_F150W>=26 AB`, and show no robust emission lines.
    - One confirmation in `~4.8 arcmin^2` implies surface density `0.21+0.59/-0.17 arcmin^-2`.
    - That surface density is about `10x` higher than wide-area photometric-survey expectations, suggesting a possible COSMOS overdensity at `z~8`.
  - **Position:** useful high-z validation note because it converts a photometric COSMOS-Web overdensity hint into a spectroscopic, though still low-number, constraint

- [2605.01380](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01380) Zihan Wang et al. `A structural degeneracy explains reionization tensions and limits dark matter constraints`
  - **Question:** why do reionization analyses infer discrepant escape fractions and star-formation efficiencies from similar observables?
  - **Data:**
    - Reionization probes: Thomson optical depth, neutral fraction constraints, UV luminosity functions, and quasar proximity zones
    - Simulations: `230` dark-matter-only N-body simulations including self-interacting dark matter halo suites
    - Halo regime: galaxy-scale haloes spanning `10^9-10^11 Msun`
  - **Method:**
    - Write the ionizing emissivity as a product of escape fraction and peak star-formation efficiency
    - Demonstrate the exact parameter degeneracy for volume-integrated reionization-history observables
    - Propagate SIDM-driven halo-structure changes through emissivity-matched reionization models
    - Test which observables respond to topology rather than only total photon production
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Reionization-history probes constrain `f_esc x f_*,0`, not `f_esc` and `f_*,0` separately.
    - Factor-of-two-to-five disagreements in inferred `f_esc` and `f_*,0` can arise from this non-invertible parameter mapping.
    - Changes in dark-matter microphysics can be hidden when the effective ionizing emissivity is matched.
    - Spatial topology of ionized regions, especially 21 cm topology, is the observable class that can break the product degeneracy.
    - Reionization histories alone are therefore weak dark-matter probes unless paired with topology-sensitive measurements.
  - **Position:** important theory guardrail for JWST-era reionization interpretation because it separates emissivity constraints from physical constraints on escape fraction, SFE, and dark matter

- [2605.00990](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00990) Ioanna Koutsouridou et al. `NEFERTITI: Linking early galaxy formation to the assembly of the Milky Way`
  - **Question:** how do Pop III systems and early dwarfs connect far-field JWST candidates to the present-day metal-poor Milky Way?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: `31` high-resolution Caterpillar dark-matter zoom simulations of Milky Way analogues
    - Model: NEFERTITI galaxy-formation model with inhomogeneous ionization and chemical enrichment
    - Observational anchors: metal-poor Milky Way stars and the JWST Pop III candidate Hebe near `z~11`
  - **Method:**
    - Resolve minihaloes that host first stars and follow Pop III/Pop II star formation
    - Track chemical enrichment, supernova feedback, and descendant metallicity distributions to `z=0`
    - Separate in-situ and accreted stellar components as a function of `[Fe/H]`
    - Compare model Pop III systems with current JWST candidate properties
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Pop III star formation begins at `z~27`, peaks at `z~10-15`, and persists to `z<5`.
    - Present-day descendants span `[Fe/H]<-9` to `[Fe/H]~-1`, with the most metal-poor stars typically enriched by `1-4` low-energy supernovae.
    - About `90%` of the total Milky Way stellar mass forms in situ, but the accreted component dominates below `[Fe/H]<-1` and nearly all stars below `[Fe/H]<-3`.
    - Ultra-faint and classical dwarf analogues contribute `~25%` of stars at `[Fe/H]<-3`.
    - The model reproduces a Hebe-like Pop III system at `z~10.6`, supporting the interpretation that some JWST candidates may be nearly metal-free stellar systems.
  - **Position:** useful near-field/far-field bridge because it ties JWST primordial-galaxy candidates to recoverable signatures in local metal-poor stellar populations

## Cosmic-noon galaxies, quasar environments, and CGM gas

- [2605.02493](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02493) Vaidik Prasal et al. `Morphological and Star Formation Properties of Cosmic Noon Massive Quiescent Galaxies`
  - **Question:** what morphologies and radial star-formation histories characterize massive quiescent galaxies at `2<z<3`?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRCam: UNCOVER Treasury and MegaScience imaging of Abell 2744
    - HST: archival ACS and WFC3 photometry used with JWST bands
    - Catalog: UNCOVER SUPER DR3 photometry with broad and medium bands
    - Sample: `14` unique massive quiescent galaxies with `M_*>10^10 Msun` and `sSFR<0.2/t_age`
  - **Method:**
    - Fit stellar populations with BAGPIPES using combined HST and JWST photometry
    - Select quiescent galaxies from the SED posterior properties
    - Measure non-parametric morphology with statmorph
    - Fit parametric Sersic and bulge-disk models with pysersic
    - Perform spatially resolved SED modeling with piXedfit to recover radial sSFR, formation-time, and quenching-time profiles
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Most galaxies are intermediate type or S0-like systems with median Sersic index `n~4`, consistent with bulge-dominated structure.
    - The median Sersic index remains roughly constant over `z~1.5-4`, linking high central concentration to quiescence since at least `z~4`.
    - `11/14` galaxies, or `~79%`, show positive radial sSFR gradients.
    - The mean sSFR rises by about `2 dex` from `R/R_e=0.5` to `R/R_e=4.5`, supporting inside-out quenching.
    - Inner regions formed `~0.5 Gyr` earlier than outer regions and quenched more rapidly.
    - The mean half-mass radius is `R_e=1.95+/-0.13 kpc`, so these quiescent systems are compact.
  - **Position:** high-value JWST cosmic-noon paper because it combines morphology and resolved star-formation histories instead of relying only on integrated quiescent selection

- [2605.01981](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01981) Zhuoqi Liu et al. `A Comprehensive Study of Morphology and Kinematics in Extended Nebulae Around UV Luminous Quasars at z~1`
  - **Question:** what physical pathways produce extended emission-line nebulae around UV-luminous quasars at intermediate redshift?
  - **Data:**
    - MUSE: quasar-field IFU observations from CUBS and MUSEQuBES
    - Sample: `30` UV-luminous quasars at `z~0.4-1.4`
    - Emission lines: extended [O II] and/or [O III] nebulae detected around `27/30` quasars
    - Environment data: surrounding galaxy redshifts and group associations in the same fields
  - **Method:**
    - Map nebular morphology and gas kinematics with MUSE
    - Classify nebulae into irregular large-scale, compact host-scale, and complex systems
    - Measure spatial and kinematic association between nebulae and nearby group galaxies
    - Compare radio-jet orientations with nebular emission and kinematic structure
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The sample contains `11` irregular large-scale systems above `50 kpc`, `12` compact host-galaxy-scale nebulae, and `4` complex systems.
    - Ten nebulae have statistically significant spatial and kinematic associations with quasar-host group galaxies.
    - Most of the significant galaxy-nebula associations occur in irregular large-scale systems, supporting an interaction or group-environment origin.
    - Velocity offsets of nebular components reach up to `~1500 km/s`, so the kinematics are often disturbed.
    - Radio jets are present in `6` systems, but jet activity does not show a strong global correlation with nebular emission.
    - Roughly `30%` of systems show blueshifted-redshifted patterns consistent with rotation, but disturbed gas and feedback remain important.
  - **Position:** useful CGM/quasar-environment paper because it shows that giant quasar nebulae are not a single physical class

- [2605.01505](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01505) Itai Bromberg et al. `Non-Equilibrium Ionisation in Photoionised Haloes: Implications for Shock Stability and Absorption-Line Signatures`
  - **Question:** how do nonequilibrium ionisation and the UV background change halo-gas cooling, virial-shock stability, and CGM/IGM absorption signatures?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: one-dimensional spherical hydrodynamical calculations with an extended hydra code
    - Halo masses: `10^11-10^13 Msun`
    - Redshift range: `z=100` to `z=0`
    - Ion network: H, He, C, N, O, Ne, Mg, Si, S, and Fe
  - **Method:**
    - Follow dark-matter growth, gas dynamics, time-dependent ionisation, and cooling
    - Compare cases with and without a metagalactic UV background
    - Measure virial-shock stability thresholds under CIE and NEI assumptions
    - Compute O VI, C IV, and H I absorption columns through halo and surrounding gas
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Without a UV background, NEI enhances post-shock cooling because underionised gas cools efficiently.
    - Enhanced post-shock cooling reduces pressure support and raises the minimum halo mass for stable virial shocks.
    - A UV background pre-ionises the IGM, suppresses NEI, and restores the CIE-like shock-stability threshold.
    - Photoionised gas can produce substantial warm-ion columns beyond `Rvir`, including O VI columns comparable to observed values.
    - At `z>~3`, haloes can produce `N_CIV~10^13-10^15 cm^-2` and `N_HI~10^15-10^17 cm^-2` out to `~10 Rvir`.
  - **Position:** useful CGM/IGM systematics paper because it warns that absorption attributed to halo gas can include extended photoionised IGM contributions

- [2605.01105](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01105) Xinfeng Xu et al. `Resolving the Unresolved Galactic Winds in Multi-phase Models. I. Methodology and Application`
  - **Question:** can spatially integrated UV absorption profiles recover radial structure and mass-loading in multiphase galactic winds?
  - **Data:**
    - Outflow observations: high-quality rest-UV absorption-line data with velocity-resolved column-density profiles
    - Sample: `22` CLASSY galaxies with robust cool-phase outflow measurements
    - Model reference: Fielding and Bryan 2022 multiphase, multiscale wind model
  - **Method:**
    - Fit hydrogen column density as a function of velocity using moments of the UV absorption profile
    - Vary initial hot-phase mass-loading, initial cool-phase mass-loading, and initial cloud mass
    - Map cool-cloud velocity structure onto radius where the model velocity field is monotonic
    - Infer coupled hot and cool wind properties from cool absorption tracers
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Most galaxies are fit well by the multiphase wind model.
    - The initial cool-phase mass-loading factor is tightly constrained, while hot-phase loading and cloud mass are moderately constrained.
    - Inferred hot and cool initial mass-loading factors are mostly of order unity, with significant object-to-object scatter.
    - Cool-phase velocity rises rapidly around `1-2 r50` and then reaches a plateau.
    - The interaction between cool clouds and hot wind lets cool absorption indirectly constrain hot-wind properties.
  - **Position:** useful feedback-method paper because it turns integrated UV absorption into a model-conditional radial diagnostic for unresolved multiphase winds

## Survey forecasts, feedback, and cosmic-web baryons

- [2605.01000](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01000) Ilsang Yoon et al. `Prospects for Observing Galaxy Spectral Energy Distribution from the Radio to the far-Infrared in the Era of Next-Generation Radio Telescopes`
  - **Question:** which radio-to-FIR facilities can detect galaxy SEDs from cosmic noon to the reionization era and beyond?
  - **Data:**
    - Model SEDs: energy-balanced UV-to-FIR galaxy SEDs extended into the radio
    - Facilities: ALMA, SKA, and ngVLA, covering roughly `100 MHz-100 GHz`
    - Galaxy populations: UV-bright star-forming galaxies and dusty star-forming galaxies
    - Redshift range: forecasts up to `z~20`
  - **Method:**
    - Add radio emission components to panchromatic SEDs using radio-IR correlations
    - Include CMB dust heating, CMB background contrast, and inverse-Compton synchrotron dimming
    - Model anomalous microwave emission under extreme ISM assumptions
    - Compare predicted flux densities with ALMA, SKA, and ngVLA sensitivities
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Massive galaxies with `M_*>10^10 Msun` are detectable at all `0<z<20` in high-frequency bands above `90 GHz`.
    - The ngVLA high-frequency `~100 GHz` band can detect galaxies with `M_*>10^9-10^9.5 Msun` almost independently of redshift.
    - The SKA low-frequency window below `~1 GHz` can detect `M_*>10^10 Msun` dusty star-forming galaxies out to `z=5-7`.
    - CMB effects reshape high-z radio-FIR SEDs through synchrotron dimming and dust heating/contrast.
    - Anomalous microwave emission is negligible beyond the local universe, especially for `z>~0.1`.
  - **Position:** practical survey-planning paper for high-z dusty and UV-bright galaxies because it identifies where SKA/ngVLA add information beyond ALMA

- [2605.01994](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01994) Kritti Sharma et al. `Baryons in the Darkest Sites of the Universe`
  - **Question:** can FRB dispersion measures directly constrain the baryon content of cosmic void interiors?
  - **Data:**
    - CHIME/FRB: `3,455` extragalactic sightlines with `~15 arcmin` localizations
    - SDSS BOSS: `1,288` cosmic voids over `0.2<z<0.7`
    - tSZ support: existing thermal Sunyaev-Zel'dovich stacks on voids
  - **Method:**
    - Stack FRB dispersion measures as a function of angular distance from void centers
    - Calibrate the void baryon signal against the FRB DM-redshift relation
    - Fit effective-universe and uniform-density void models for electron underdensity
    - Combine FRB electron columns with tSZ temperature-sensitive constraints
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The stacked FRB data show a DM deficit toward void centers at `3.2 sigma`.
    - The effective-universe calibration gives `alpha_v=1.80+/-0.87`.
    - A uniform-density void model gives electron contrast `delta_e,v=-0.58+/-0.30`.
    - The inferred electron contrast corresponds to a `~60%` baryon underdensity relative to the cosmic mean.
    - Combining FRBs with tSZ stacks constrains the mean void gas temperature to `T_e <= (1.1+/-0.7) x 10^6 K`.
  - **Position:** important cosmic-web baryon paper because FRBs measure free-electron columns independent of gas phase, temperature, and metallicity

- [2605.02663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02663) Rogemar A. Riffel et al. `Jet-driven shocks and turbulence in radio-loud Active Galactic Nuclei observed with JWST MIRI/MRS`
  - **Question:** how do compact radio jets affect molecular and ionized gas in nearby radio-loud AGN hosts?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/MIRI MRS: seven nearby radio-loud AGN, 3C 293, 3C 305, Centaurus A, Cygnus A, IC 5063, NGC 1052, and M87
    - Molecular tracers: H2 rotational lines and PAH `11.3 um`
    - Ionized tracers: [Fe II], [Ne II], and [Ne III]
    - Geometry: spaxels separated along and perpendicular to the radio-jet axis within `+/-30 deg`
  - **Method:**
    - Measure H2/PAH ratios and H2 excitation temperatures across the MIRI fields
    - Compare shock-sensitive [Fe II]/PAH and ionization-sensitive [Ne III]/[Ne II] ratios
    - Measure gas turbulence with velocity-width diagnostics along and perpendicular to jets
    - Test whether H2 excitation tracks shocks, PAH suppression, or star-forming PDRs
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Radio jets enhance turbulence in both molecular and ionized gas, including regions perpendicular to the jet axis.
    - H2/PAH is higher along the jet for most sources, while [Fe II]/PAH is higher along the jet in all sources.
    - Strong correlations among H2/PAH, H2 excitation temperature, and [Fe II]/PAH identify shocks as the main H2 excitation mechanism in most galaxies.
    - Perpendicular turbulence is consistent with jet-ISM coupling that drives lateral cocoon expansion or outflows rather than only a narrow collimated channel.
    - Jet feedback affects nuclear and circumnuclear gas on scales of hundreds of parsecs to a few kiloparsecs.
  - **Position:** relevant AGN-feedback paper because JWST/MIRI resolves the shock/turbulence coupling that low-resolution mid-IR spectra could only infer statistically

- [2605.00982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00982) G. Torres-Rios et al. `Stellar mass and morphology segregation in pairs and multiplets in the cosmic web`
  - **Question:** do local companions or large-scale cosmic-web environment dominate stellar-mass and morphology segregation?
  - **Data:**
    - Galaxy sample: `25,309` galaxies at `0.02<z<=0.04` with `log(M_*/Msun)>=9.5`
    - LSS classes: `2,787` cluster galaxies, `16,984` NCNV galaxies, and `5,377` void galaxies
    - Local classes: `12,393` multiplets and `12,755` singlets
    - Pair subsamples: `199` void pairs and `739` NCNV pairs
  - **Method:**
    - Classify galaxies by void, cluster, or not-cluster-not-void large-scale environment
    - Classify galaxies by singlet or multiplet local environment
    - Compare stellar-mass and T-type morphology distributions at fixed LSS and local environment
    - Isolate pair centrals and satellites in void and NCNV environments
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Even in voids, `~22%` of galaxies have companions.
    - Void galaxies are less massive than galaxies in denser LSS environments regardless of local companion status.
    - The denser the large-scale environment, the higher the early-type fraction, even among singlets.
    - Multiplets are `14.1%` more likely to be early-type than singlets in the full sample.
    - Satellites in void pairs are less massive relative to their centrals than satellites in NCNV pairs.
    - Local environment alone is insufficient to explain mass and morphology distributions.
  - **Position:** useful LSS baseline because it separates cosmic-web effects from pair/group effects in a clean low-redshift sample

## Lower-priority but useful references

- [2605.01353](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01353) Yingfeng Liu et al. `Cross-Comparison of Galaxies Detected in the CSST Spectroscopic Survey and the SKA HI Survey`
  - **Question:** what overlap and complementarity should CSST optical spectroscopy and SKA HI surveys have at low redshift?
  - **Data:**

  - **Method:**

    - Partition cold gas into atomic and molecular components
    - Generate SKA HI data cubes and source catalogs with SOFIA-2
    - Apply CSST emission-line noise and selection functions
    - Measure HI stacking, baryonic Tully-Fisher relations, optical-HI cross-correlations, and galaxy bias
  - **Conclusion:**
    - CSST-selected emission-line galaxies can be stacked to probe HI below direct SKA detection thresholds.
    - Optical-HI cross-correlations provide a route to measuring gas-selected galaxy bias.
    - The framework is most relevant as a future low-z cold-gas calibration set rather than as a direct high-z result.
  - **Position:** lower-priority for this profile, but useful for future survey-synergy planning between optical emission-line and HI-selected galaxy samples

- [2605.01648](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01648) Chen MuLin et al. `A Short-timescale Negative Optical Continuum Lag in SDSS J083717.88+191647`
  - **Question:** can optical continuum reverberation lags reverse sign on short timescales in an AGN?
  - **Data:**
    - Zwicky Transient Facility: multi-band light curves of SDSS J083717.88+191647
    - Bands: g and r optical continuum monitoring
  - **Method:**
    - Separate the light curve into stabilizing and declining phases
    - Measure inter-band lags with JAVELIN using the g band as the reference
    - Interpret color evolution and lag reversal with a moving dust-cloud obscuration model
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The stabilizing phase gives a positive lag of `3.68+1.94/-2.78 days`.
    - The declining phase gives a negative lag of `-1.60+0.69/-0.54 days`.
    - Phase-dependent lag reversal is consistent with obscuration of outer, redder disk regions by a moving dust cloud.
  - **Position:** lower-priority but relevant to AGN variability systematics because dust and geometry can complicate continuum-lag interpretation

- [2605.02875](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02875) John W. Montano et al. `AGN STORM 2. XII. Ground-Based Optical Photometry and Lag Measurements of Mrk 817`
  - **Question:** how do optical continuum lags in Mrk 817 behave across a long multiwavelength AGN STORM 2 campaign?
  - **Data:**
    - Ground-based imaging: `1.4 yr` campaign in uBgVriz filters
    - Cadence: median `0.4 days` in g band
    - Reference light curve: Swift UVW2 at `1928 Angstrom`
  - **Method:**
    - Measure continuum lags with ICCF, JAVELIN, and PyROA
    - Compare wavelength dependence with `tau proportional to lambda^(4/3)` thin-disk reprocessing
    - Split the campaign into epochs with different luminosity and X-ray obscuration
  - **Conclusion:**
    - ICCF centroid lags range from `3.0+/-0.8 days` in u to `7.9+/-1.5 days` in z.
    - The ICCF lags are broadly consistent with `lambda^(4/3)`, but other methods give shorter and less power-law-like lags.
    - The lags exceed thin-disk predictions by factors of `~3-6`.
    - Lags vary by as much as a factor of `2` between campaign epochs.
  - **Position:** local AGN reverberation reference that helps interpret high-z quasar variability work by exposing disk-size and obscuration systematics
