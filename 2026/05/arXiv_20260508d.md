## Executive View

The read-first paper is [2605.05673](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05673) Raymond P. Remigio. It reports KCWI red-channel detection of an extended Lyalpha halo around the `z=6.64` BAL quasar J0910-0414. The halo reaches `~11 pkpc`, has `L_Lyalpha=(2.3+/-0.1) x 10^43 erg/s`, contributes `55%` of the total detected Lyalpha flux inside the mask, and has lower dispersion than the compact ALMA [C II] host core. This is directly aligned with reionization-era quasar environments and cool-gas mapping beyond the MUSE redshift limit.

The strongest JWST galaxy-evolution paper is [2605.05327](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05327) A. Gimenez-Alcazar. It compiles `286` JWST/NIRSpec auroral-line galaxies over `z=0.8-9.1` and shows that direct-method MZR samples are biased toward low-metallicity, high-EW, high-sSFR systems. The key practical result is that stacks without individual [O III] 4363 detections follow a similar slope but are `~0.2-0.3 dex` more metal-rich at fixed stellar mass.

The best AGN-feedback and outflow pair is [2605.05298](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05298) Jack M. M. Neustadt and [2605.05650](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05650) Antonia Fernandez-Figueroa. Neustadt uses JWST Q3D ERQ spectra to separate velocity- and density-stratified gas components, while Fernandez-Figueroa shows that spatially resolved absorption tracers in local clumpy star-forming galaxies reach much higher outflow velocities than emission tracers. Together they are useful reminders that line tracer choice controls the physical phase being measured.

The support papers to keep are [2605.06406](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06406) Y. I. Izotov for low-z high-`O32` LyC-leaker analogues, [2605.05548](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05548) Ivan Altunin for hard-EUV photon-budget limits in metal-poor He II emitters, [2605.06587](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06587) Shenming Fu for DES/Rubin cluster triaxiality methods, and [2605.06517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06517) I. O. Izviekova for isolated NLSy1 dust reverberation.

## Reionization-Era Quasar Environments and JWST Metallicity

- [2605.05673](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05673) Raymond P. Remigio et al. `Detection of an Extended Lyalpha Halo around a z=6.64 Broad Absorption Line Quasar with the Keck Cosmic Web Imager`
  - **Question:** can KCWI red-channel observations detect extended Lyalpha halos around quasars beyond the usual MUSE redshift limit?
  - **Data:**
    - Keck/KCWI: red-channel integral-field observations centered near observed `9500 Angstrom`.
    - Target: the `z=6.64` BAL quasar J0910-0414.
    - ALMA: prior [C II] redshift and compact host-galaxy kinematics from the ASPIRE context.
    - Environment: previous work reports a quasar-centered overdensity with Lyalpha and [C II] emitters.
  - **Method:**
    - Subtract the quasar PSF from the KCWI cube.
    - Build smoothed signal-to-noise masks for extended Lyalpha emission.
    - Measure halo flux, size, velocity centroid, velocity dispersion, and radial surface-brightness profile.
    - Compare Lyalpha halo properties with compact ALMA [C II] and FIR continuum sizes.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Extended Lyalpha is detected to a radius of `~11 pkpc`.
    - The measured halo flux is `(4.6+/-0.3) x 10^-17 erg/s/cm^2`, corresponding to `L_Lyalpha=(2.3+/-0.1) x 10^43 erg/s`.
    - The halo contributes `55%` of the total Lyalpha flux inside the mask because the nuclear Lyalpha region is heavily affected by BAL absorption.
    - The halo velocity is `203+/-15 km/s` relative to the [C II] systemic redshift, with `sigma_Lyalpha=242+/-9 km/s`.
    - The Lyalpha halo is roughly `8` times larger than the [C II] region and `18` times larger than the FIR continuum region.
    - The Lyalpha velocity field shows ordered, rotation-like structure but with a different orientation and lower dispersion than the compact [C II] host core.
  - **Position:** the most profile-aligned paper today because it directly probes cool gas around a reionization-era quasar and demonstrates that KCWI can extend Lyalpha-halo searches beyond the practical MUSE limit.

- [2605.05327](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05327) A. Gimenez-Alcazar et al. `Shape of Direct-Method Mass-Metallicity Relation with JWST: Fast-Track Nitrogen and Helium Enrichment`
  - **Question:** how do auroral-line selection, star-formation history, and secondary abundances shape the direct-method MZR at high redshift?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRSpec: medium-resolution spectra from the public DAWN JWST Archive.
    - Sample: `286` star-forming galaxies with [O III] 4363 auroral-line detections over `z=0.8-9.1`.
    - Photometry: DJA Mosaic v7 imaging used for SED fitting.
    - Stacks: galaxies with and without individual auroral-line detections.
  - **Method:**
    - Select star-forming galaxies using auroral-line diagnostic diagrams and remove likely AGN/shock contaminants.
    - Derive electron densities, temperatures, oxygen abundances, N/O, and He/H with direct `T_e` methods.
    - Fit stellar masses with Charlot & Bruzual models plus nebular emission.
    - Derive SFRs from extinction-corrected Balmer emission lines.
    - Stack spectra in stellar-mass bins to test auroral-line selection bias.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The auroral-line sample spans `log(M_*/M_sun)=6.77-10.5` and `12+log(O/H)=6.9-8.4`.
    - The fitted MZR slope is `gamma=0.38+/-0.09`.
    - Stacked galaxies without individual auroral-line detections have similar MZR slope but metallicities higher by `~0.2-0.3 dex` at fixed stellar mass.
    - Auroral-line detections preferentially select high-EW, high-sSFR galaxies in the low-metallicity envelope.
    - Non-detections appear more chemically evolved and closer to the expected FMR.
    - Several stacked bins show enhanced N/O and He/H, linking the MZR scatter to fast nitrogen and helium enrichment.
  - **Position:** a central JWST interpretation paper because it quantifies how the easiest direct-method galaxies are biased relative to the chemically more evolved population.

## AGN, Outflows, and Ionizing Sources

- [2605.05298](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05298) Jack M. M. Neustadt et al. `Kinematic Stratification in Extremely Red Quasars Revealed by JWST`
  - **Question:** what gas phases and viewing geometry explain the complex rest-optical spectra of extremely red quasars?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRSpec IFU: six ERQs from the Q3D ERS program.
    - ALMA: observed-frame `240 GHz` data for most of the ERQ sample.
    - Archival support: SDSS, X-Shooter, WISE, VLA, and FIRST photometry or spectra.
    - Main object: J0834 at `z=2.5928`, selected for clearly decomposable Balmer and [O III] profiles.
  - **Method:**
    - Decompose Balmer, [O III], [N II], [S II], O I, and Fe II spectral profiles into velocity templates.
    - Use Balmer decrements and line ratios to infer obscuration, density, and ionization differences.
    - Fit UV-to-MIR SEDs with a torus plus scattered-light model.
    - Use ALMA/FIR excess to estimate host-galaxy dust and star-forming components.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - ERQ spectra require BLR-like clouds, low-density NLR-like gas, and high-velocity low-density outflow components.
    - J0834 shows [O III]-dominated gas that is more blueshifted and lower density than the bulk Balmer-emitting gas.
    - The UV continuum and UV lines are dominated by scattered light, while red optical and NIR lines include both scattered and obscured components.
    - J0834 likely contains stratified outflow zones: a larger-scale, high-velocity, relatively unobscured [O III] component plus a more compact, slower, obscured component.
    - The SEDs are consistent with a significantly dust-obscured central source plus a small unobscured scattered component.
  - **Position:** high-value AGN physics for the profile because ERQs, LRDs, and obscured high-z AGN all require careful separation of scattered light, direct obscured emission, and outflow phases.

- [2605.05650](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05650) Antonia Fernandez-Figueroa et al. `Differences between emission and absorption tracers of spatially resolved outflows in clumpy z~0.1 star-forming galaxies`
  - **Question:** do emission and absorption diagnostics measure the same outflow phase in spatially resolved star-forming galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Keck/LRIS: spatially resolved spectroscopy for three clumpy DYNAMO star-forming galaxies at `z~0.1`.
    - HST: narrowband imaging used to flux-calibrate Halpha along the slits.
    - Tracers: Halpha and Mg II emission, Mg II absorption, and Na I D absorption.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit narrow ISM and broad outflow components to Halpha+[N II] emission.
    - Fit Mg II emission and absorption with components tied to local systemic velocities.
    - Define outflow velocity as `v_out=v-2 sigma`.
    - Compare resolved outflow velocities with SFR and `Sigma_SFR`.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Mg II absorption gives much higher outflow velocities than Halpha emission, with mean values `-560+/-30 km/s` versus `-124+/-3 km/s` in the abstract comparison.
    - In the detailed resolved sample, the paper reports `v_out=-291 km/s` in Halpha emission and `v_out=-651 km/s` in absorption.
    - Mg II absorption is systematically offset from emission-line tracers by `~0.4 dex`.
    - Mg II emission has velocities consistent with Halpha emission in the two galaxies where it is detected.
    - Na I D absorption in one galaxy matches Mg II absorption and is faster than Halpha emission.
    - The discrepancy is best explained by tracer-density and measurement-systematics differences, not simply ionization state.
  - **Position:** useful for high-z feedback interpretation because unresolved high-z spectra often mix emission and absorption diagnostics that do not trace the same gas phase.

- [2605.05548](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05548) Ivan Altunin et al. `A Multiwavelength Assessment Disfavoring the X-ray Binary Origin of He III Regions in Metal-Poor Star-Forming Dwarf Galaxies`
  - **Question:** can accreting X-ray binaries alone power nebular He II emission in metal-poor star-forming dwarf galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Chandra: `7` new and `36` archival observations for He II-emitting dwarfs.
    - Sample: `21` nearby `z<0.1` star-forming galaxies with He II emission and no Wolf-Rayet spectral signatures.
    - Optical spectra: stellar-population synthesis and line-flux constraints on the required EUV continuum.
  - **Method:**
    - Detect and fit X-ray sources in Chandra images.
    - Extrapolate X-ray spectral models into the `54-300 eV` EUV band.
    - Infer the ionizing photon flux required by the He II 4686 line.
    - Compare observed and required EUV fluxes with empirical XRB scaling relations and stellar-population models.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Only a small subset of observations contain enough extrapolated X-ray EUV flux to explain the observed He II emission.
    - Most detected X-ray sources are spatially or energetically insufficient to dominate the He II-ionizing budget.
    - X-ray luminosities are broadly consistent with empirical XRB scaling relations, so the deficit is not simply a shortage of XRBs.
    - Stellar and accreting compact-object populations alone cannot explain the He II line fluxes in most systems.
    - A multi-channel model involving soft and supersoft X-ray sources, stripped stars, shocks, and feedback-heated gas is more plausible.
  - **Position:** important for reionization analogues because hard-ionizing spectra in metal-poor dwarfs cannot be reduced to a simple local XRB scaling.

- [2605.06517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06517) I. O. Izviekova et al. `The 2MIG isolated AGNs. 3. Optical-IR variability and dust reverberation in the NLSy1 galaxies Mrk 42 and Mrk 493`
  - **Question:** how are the BLR and hot-dust scales arranged in isolated narrow-line Seyfert 1 galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - ZTF: optical `gri` light curves.
    - WISE/NEOWISE: mid-infrared `W1` and `W2` monitoring.
    - Swift and SDSS: UV, X-ray, and spectral context.
    - IAC80: high-cadence optical monitoring for Mrk 42.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure color-magnitude relations and flux-flux behavior.
    - Cross-correlate ZTF optical and NEOWISE MIR light curves.
    - Estimate BLR radii from literature reverberation and host-subtracted 5100 Angstrom luminosities.
    - Test intraday variability and flare substructure for Mrk 42.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Mrk 493 has `tau_obs(W1)=79.4+/-2.2 d`, giving `R_dust(W1)=0.0648 pc`.
    - Mrk 42 has `tau_obs(W1)=39.1+/-2.6 d`, giving `R_dust(W1)=0.0320 pc`.
    - Both systems have dust-to-BLR scale ratios of order `~6-7` when AGN-relevant BLR estimates are used.
    - Mrk 493 shows significant optical and MIR bluer-when-brighter behavior.
    - Mrk 42 shows an optical flare with internal `~47 d` modulation, but subtracting that modulation does not remove the dust lag.
  - **Position:** lower redshift, but useful as a clean isolated-host AGN reverberation benchmark for disc-BLR-dust radial stratification.

## LyC Analogues, LSS Tools, and Environment Methods

- [2605.06406](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06406) Y. I. Izotov et al. `Chemical composition and kinematics of ionised gas in low-mass star-forming galaxies with extremely high [OIII]/[OII] ratios`
  - **Question:** what abundances and ionized-gas kinematics characterize low-z compact star-forming galaxies with extremely high `O32` ratios?
  - **Data:**
    - VLT/Xshooter: spectrophotometric observations of `11` compact star-forming galaxies.
    - Redshift range: `z<0.085`.
    - Selection: extreme `[O III]5007/[O II]3727=11-42`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure strong optical and near-IR emission lines.
    - Derive direct-method oxygen abundances, helium abundance, and N/O, Ne/O, S/O, Cl/O, Ar/O, and Fe/O.
    - Measure line-dependent velocity dispersions after instrumental and thermal corrections.
    - Compare high-`O32` systems with lower-ionization compact star-forming galaxies.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The galaxies have low oxygen abundances, `12+log(O/H)=7.53-7.99`.
    - Stellar masses are low, around `M_*=10^6-10^8 Msun`.
    - The sample is consistent with promising LyC-leaker analogues because of extreme high `O32`.
    - `sigma(Hbeta)` increases with stellar mass and oxygen abundance and decreases weakly with `O32`.
    - Most emission lines have velocity dispersions similar to Hbeta, but He II 4686 and He I 10830 are broader, while [O II] and [S II] are narrower.
    - High N/O and Fe/O in high-`O32` systems should be treated cautiously because density-bounded or clumpy H II regions can bias ionization-correction assumptions.
  - **Position:** useful low-z analogue paper for reionization because it links extreme ionization, LyC-leaker selection, abundance systematics, and internal gas kinematics.

- [2605.06587](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06587) Shenming Fu et al. `Constraining Galaxy Cluster Triaxiality via Weak Lensing -- I. Preparation for the Rubin Data Beyond Leading Order`
  - **Question:** can stacked weak-lensing multipoles measure the projected ellipticity of galaxy-cluster halos in preparation for Rubin LSST?
  - **Data:**
    - DES Y3 redMaPPer clusters.
    - DES Y3 metacalibration shape catalog and DNF photometric-redshift catalog.
    - Baseline cluster selection: `pcen0>0.9` and `0.2<z<0.65`.
    - Final baseline sample: `9962` clusters out of `21092`.
  - **Method:**
    - Align cluster stacks by the projected satellite-galaxy major axis.
    - Measure monopole and quadrupole weak-lensing profiles.
    - Fit a second-order elliptical NFW expansion.
    - Correct for orientation dilution from satellite-halo misalignment and member contamination.
    - Validate with mock elliptical-halo shear catalogs and DES Y1 redMaPPer comparisons.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The DES Y3 cluster ensemble has projected ellipticity `epsilon=0.310+0.017/-0.016`.
    - The corresponding projected axis ratio is `q=0.527+0.018/-0.019`.
    - No strong dependence on richness or redshift is detected within the current uncertainties.
    - DES Y1 gives consistent ellipticity, `epsilon=0.309+/-0.027`.
    - Accounting for triaxiality can reduce the weak-lensing monopole-inferred mass by roughly `~5%` in representative elliptical NFW tests.
    - Rubin LSST Y10 should reduce the ellipticity error bar by about `82%` relative to DES Y3.
  - **Position:** a methods paper rather than a high-z galaxy result, but useful for LSS and cluster-environment systematics that will affect future cosmic-web and cluster-growth work.

- [2605.05570](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05570) Masanori Iye et al. `Spin Parity of Spiral Galaxies VI -- A Search for Dynamical Memory in the Spin Distribution of Galaxies in HSC WIDE Survey Regions`
  - **Question:** do spiral-galaxy winding parities in HSC WIDE reveal residual dynamical memory in galaxy spin directions?
  - **Data:**
    - HSC DR2: `49,494` S/Z-annotated spiral galaxies with spectroscopic redshifts.
    - Search volumes: `46,247` fiducial cubes in 3D supergalactic coordinates.
    - Scale range: cube sizes of `20-200 Mpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Count S- and Z-spirals in each cube.
    - Compute binomial probabilities for spin-parity imbalance.
    - Compare observed distributions with `1000` random spin assignments.
    - Test anomalous cube counts and hemispheric divisions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The observed cumulative distributions match the theoretical binomial expectation.
    - The number of anomalous cubes is consistent with random S/Z assignment.
    - The spin-vector distribution is statistically random within the tested HSC WIDE sample.
  - **Position:** lower priority for the profile, but a useful LSS spin-alignment null result.

- [2605.05972](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05972) Matias Gamez-Marin et al. `A statistical look on kinematic planes of satellite galaxies II: The physics behind their early formation in TNG50 MW/M31-like galaxies`
  - **Question:** what high-redshift cosmic-web process produces kinematically persistent satellite planes in Milky Way and M31 analogues?
  - **Data:**
    - TNG50: `190` MW/M31-like host-satellite systems.
    - KPP subset: `46` early kinematically persistent planes from prior work.
    - Local-volume tracking: deformation of Lagrangian volumes around each system.
  - **Method:**
    - Track principal collapse directions in the local high-z cosmic web.
    - Compare satellite orbital-pole clustering with local-volume axes.
    - Follow satellite distances and velocities relative to evolving collapse planes.
    - Test timescales for plane establishment and survival.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - About `67%` of KPPs align satellite orbital poles with the strongest-collapse direction.
    - About `20%` align with the intermediate axis, while major-axis alignments are rare.
    - Early satellite-plane formation is tied to anisotropic accretion and local cosmic-web collapse.
  - **Position:** relevant to LSS-galaxy dynamics, but secondary to the high-z and CGM papers today.
