## Executive View

The week is unusually coherent around four themes: early black-hole growth and LRD interpretation, high-redshift galaxy and reionization diagnostics, gas regulation in environments, and the chemical or morphological structure of galaxies in overdensities.

The read-first paper is [2605.29623](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29623) Yi-Ming Yang. MAMMOTH-Grism metallicity gradients in cosmic-noon protoclusters sit at the center of the profile: gas flows, chemical structure, overdense environments, and `z~2-3` galaxy evolution. It is the most directly actionable paper for environment-metallicity work this week.

For high-redshift black holes and LRDs, the anchor is [2605.24112](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24112) Xuejian Shen, which uses Lumina to connect quasars, LRDs, SMBH growth, and He II reionization at `z>=3`. [2605.28777](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28777) Maya A. Petkova gives a feedback-regulated Pop III.1 seed prescription with median seed formation near `z~20` and predicted `n_SMBH~0.2 cMpc^-3`. [2605.27903](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27903) A. J. Barger makes source multiplicity a first-order LRD systematic in A2744, and [2605.29895](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29895) Tomoya Suzuguchi adds a transient channel for LRDs.

For galaxies and reionization, [2605.24104](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24104) Jenna Samuel gives FIRE-3 early-galaxy predictions across `M_*~10^4-10^10 M_sun`, [2605.27370](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27370) Paurush Punyasheel adds FLARES UV-dust spatial offsets at the Epoch of Reionisation, and [2605.23049](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23049) Jie Song gives a consistent SDSS+JWST attenuation census over `z~0-7`.

The strongest environment sequence runs from [2605.23457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23457) Daria Zakharova on filament starvation and cosmic-web stripping, through [2605.23314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23314) Haoran Dou on group pre-processing and protection during cluster infall, to [2605.29623](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29623) Yi-Ming Yang on metallicity gradients in protoclusters and [2605.29344](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29344) Sang Hyeok Im on LAE morphology in ODIN.

## Early Black Holes, LRDs, and AGN Demographics

- [2605.24112](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24112) Xuejian Shen et al. `The Lumina Project: The Demographics of Active Galactic Nuclei from Quasars to Little Red Dots at z>=3`
  - **Question:** can one cosmological model describe high-redshift quasars, faint LRDs, black-hole growth, and IGM ionization?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: Lumina radiation-hydrodynamic box of `(500 cMpc)^3`.
    - Resolution: `2 x 6000^3` elements.
    - Population: galaxies and SMBHs across hydrogen and helium reionization.
  - **Method:**
    - Predict AGN luminosity functions from hard X-rays to mid-infrared.
    - Map simulated SMBHs to canonical AGN and LRDs with bolometric and extinction/absorption corrections.
    - Assign SMBHs with `M_BH<=10 M_seed~10^7 M_sun` to an LRD phase with `30%` duty cycle.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The empirical framework reproduces observed LRD luminosity functions and clustering.
    - Pre-JWST quasar luminosity functions are recovered when the bright end includes `~0.3 dex` log-normal bolometric scatter.
    - The same AGN population dominates the modeled He II reionization.
  - **Position:** the week's main AGN/LRD framework because it places JWST LRDs inside a cosmological black-hole and IGM model.

- [2605.28777](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28777) Maya A. Petkova et al. `The formation of supermassive black holes from Population III.1 seeds. IV. Self-regulated seeding from supermassive star ionizing feedback`
  - **Question:** can Pop III.1 SMBH seeding be regulated by ionizing feedback rather than an imposed isolation distance?
  - **Data:**
    - Model target: supermassive Pop III.1 stars forming from pristine gas in minihalos at `z~20-40`.
    - Fiducial source: H-ionizing photon luminosity `10^53 s^-1` and lifetime `10 Myr`.
    - Application: SMBH seeding in cosmological dark-matter halo simulations.
  - **Method:**
    - Model the delay between minihalo formation and Pop III.1 star formation.
    - Track R-type H II region expansion into the IGM.
    - Use the resulting ionized volume to regulate where additional Pop III.1 seeds can form.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The fiducial R-type H II region radius is `R_R~1.3 cMpc`.
    - Median seed formation occurs near `z~20`.
    - The process is mostly complete by `z~16`.
    - The predicted SMBH number density is `n_SMBH~0.2 cMpc^-3`.
  - **Position:** top seed-physics paper because it replaces a free seed-spacing parameter with an ionizing-feedback scale.

- [2605.27903](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27903) A. J. Barger et al. `Double Dots: Compact Pairs Mark Little Red Dots and High-Redshift Broad-line AGNs`
  - **Question:** do close compact pairs in JWST imaging identify LRDs and high-redshift broad-line AGN?
  - **Data:**
    - Field: JWST imaging of the A2744 massive lensing cluster.
    - Pair selection: compact sources separated by `<0.25 arcsec`.
    - Sample: `31` identified pairs, with median separation `0.15 arcsec`.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare pair counts against a uniform-background expectation.
    - Cross-match pairs with published LRDs and high-redshift broad-line AGN.
    - Assess how companion light affects LRD SED classification.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At least `16/24` previously published A2744 LRDs are compact pairs.
    - Both known high-redshift broad-line AGN in the field are also pairs.
    - Companion light can reshape the V-shaped continuum used for LRD classification.
  - **Position:** important LRD paper because it turns multiplicity and blending into a central interpretation issue.

- [2605.29895](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29895) Tomoya Suzuguchi and Kohei Inayoshi `Transient Signatures of Star-Envelope Collisions in Little Red Dots`
  - **Question:** can collisions between stars and dense envelopes produce observable transient signatures in LRDs?
  - **Data:**
    - Model target: Little Red Dot systems with dense gas or envelope structures.
    - Observable target: transient electromagnetic signatures.
  - **Method:**
    - Model star-envelope interactions in compact high-redshift systems.
    - Estimate transient emission channels and timescales.
    - Connect the model to LRD phenomenology.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - LRDs may host time-domain signatures if stars interact with dense nuclear envelopes.
    - Transient behavior could separate envelope-dominated models from static SED interpretations.
    - The paper adds a testable channel to the week's LRD package.
  - **Position:** secondary but useful because LRD interpretation needs observables beyond colors and line widths.

## High-Redshift Galaxies, Reionization, and Attenuation

- [2605.24104](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24104) Jenna Samuel et al. `Resolving galaxy formation in the early Universe with BonFIRE and CampFIRE`
  - **Question:** what early galaxy populations emerge in high-resolution FIRE-3 simulations at `z>=6`?
  - **Data:**
    - BonFIRE: `L~40 cMpc`, baryon mass resolution `~5 x 10^4 M_sun`.
    - CampFIRE: `L~5 cMpc` at `~800 M_sun` and `~6 x 10^3 M_sun` baryon resolution.
    - Dynamic range: `M_*~10^4-10^10 M_sun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine large-volume statistics with higher-resolution zoomed volume through resampling.
    - Measure star-formation efficiencies, compactness, UV luminosity functions, and luminosity variability.
    - Include first results from a simple Pop III model.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Early galaxy formation is clustered and bursty.
    - Halo-scale star-formation efficiencies reach `10-30%` in high-mass halos.
    - UVLFs agree broadly at `M_UV>-19`, turn over near `M_UV~-14`, and slightly overpredict brighter galaxies.
  - **Position:** high-priority early-galaxy simulation paper because it tests whether burstiness and high efficiency can reproduce JWST-era demographics.

- [2605.27370](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27370) Paurush Punyasheel et al. `First Light and Reionization Epoch Simulations (FLARES) XXII: UV-dust spatial offsets at the Epoch of Reionisation`
  - **Question:** how do UV and dust emission become spatially offset in EoR galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation suite: FLARES.
    - Epoch: Epoch of Reionisation.
    - Observables: UV-emitting regions and dust-obscured structure.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure spatial offsets between UV light and dust distribution.
    - Connect offsets to star-formation geometry, attenuation, and galaxy structure.
    - Interpret how simulated offsets would affect observed EoR morphologies.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - UV morphology may not trace the full stellar or dust structure in EoR systems.
    - Spatial offsets can bias size, attenuation, and star-formation interpretations.
    - The paper is a useful bridge between simulation and JWST morphology measurements.
  - **Position:** relevant because high-z morphology and dust geometry are recurring profile themes.

- [2605.23049](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23049) Jie Song et al. `SDSS+JWST Census of Stellar and Nebular Dust Attenuation at z ~ 0-7: Mass Dependence and Redshift Evolution`
  - **Question:** how do stellar and nebular dust attenuation depend on stellar mass from the local Universe to `z~7`?
  - **Data:**
    - SDSS: `34,182` galaxies at `z~0.1`.
    - JWST/JADES: `863` galaxies at `z~1.5-7`.
    - Supplementary data: GALEX and rest-frame UV-optical spectra.
  - **Method:**
    - Derive nebular attenuation from Balmer decrements.
    - Derive stellar attenuation from UV-optical spectral modeling with multiple attenuation curves.
    - Compare mass trends across redshift with consistent methods.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At fixed `M_*`, stellar and nebular attenuation show little significant redshift evolution from `z~0` to `z~7`.
    - Both attenuation measures rise from `A_V~0.2-0.4` at `M_*<~10^9 M_sun` to `A_V~1` near `M_*~10^11 M_sun`.
    - The transition near `M_*~10^9 M_sun` controls the stellar-to-nebular color-excess ratio.
  - **Position:** high-priority calibration paper because dust corrections affect high-redshift SFR and stellar-population inference.

- [2605.25130](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25130) Ravi Jaiswar et al. `Exploring biases in derived stellar parameters and the ionizing photon production efficiency`
  - **Question:** how much do data choices and SED models bias `xi_ion` and stellar parameters for EoR-like galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `z=3` EoR analog galaxies with reliable photometric coverage.
    - Methods: spectroscopic and photometric constraints across three SED-fitting codes.
  - **Method:**
    - Vary star-formation histories, stellar-population models, dust prescriptions, and photoionization assumptions.
    - Compare stellar masses, SFRs, and `xi_ion` from different data/model choices.
    - Test whether inferred `xi_ion` evolution depends on method.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Median stellar mass can vary by more than `0.6 dex`.
    - SFR can vary by more than `0.9 dex`.
    - Individual-source `xi_ion` can vary by more than `1.1 dex`.
  - **Position:** essential methods warning because EoR source budgets are sensitive to modeling choices.

## Protoclusters, LAEs, and Environment-Dependent Gas

- [2605.29623](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29623) Yi-Ming Yang et al. `MAMMOTH-Grism: Gas-phase Metallicity Gradients of Star-forming Galaxies in Protocluster Environments at Cosmic Noon`
  - **Question:** how do gas-phase metallicity gradients in star-forming galaxies respond to protocluster environments at cosmic noon?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: MAMMOTH-Grism HST/WFC3 slitless spectroscopy in massive protocluster environments.
    - Targets: star-forming galaxies in overdense structures at `z~2-3`.
    - Measurements: nebular emission-line maps used to infer spatially resolved gas-phase metallicity.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify protocluster members from grism emission-line redshifts.
    - Measure radial metallicity gradients from spatially resolved line diagnostics.
    - Compare gradient behavior with global metallicity, stellar mass, and protocluster environment.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The paper extends MAMMOTH-Grism from global chemical scaling relations to radial chemical structure.
    - Protocluster metallicity gradients test whether cold accretion, feedback recycling, or environmental gas supply dominates chemical evolution.
    - The result is directly relevant to mass-metallicity-environment work at cosmic noon.
  - **Position:** weekly read-first because it maps almost exactly onto the profile's gas, environment, and metallicity axis.

- [2605.29344](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29344) Sang Hyeok Im et al. `ODIN: Rest-frame Optical Morphologies and Star Formation Activity of Lyalpha Emitters at z=2.4, 3.1, and 4.5`
  - **Question:** what do rest-frame optical morphologies reveal about star formation in LAEs at `z=2.4-4.5`?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: ODIN-selected Lyalpha emitters.
    - Redshift slices: `z=2.4`, `z=3.1`, and `z=4.5`.
    - Observables: rest-frame optical morphology and star-formation activity.
  - **Method:**
    - Select LAEs in ODIN narrowband redshift slices.
    - Measure optical morphology rather than relying only on rest-frame UV structure.
    - Compare morphology with star-formation indicators across redshift.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The paper tests whether LAEs are compact, clumpy, extended, or merger-like in rest-frame optical light.
    - Rest-frame optical structure gives a cleaner view of stellar mass distribution than Lyalpha or UV morphology alone.
    - The result helps calibrate LAEs as tracers of galaxies and large-scale structure.
  - **Position:** important because LAEs are central tracers for overdensity, IGM, and cosmic-web work.

- [2605.28930](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28930) Callum Witten et al. `Too many protoclusters? Reconciling the overabundance of cluster progenitors within the first billion years of the Universe`
  - **Question:** are apparent `z>5` Coma-like protocluster candidates too abundant for LCDM?
  - **Data:**
    - Observational target: high-redshift cluster-progenitor candidates found in the first billion years.
    - Comparison set: present-day massive-cluster abundance and theoretical LCDM predictions.
    - Reported tension: candidate abundances are about `~2x` above present-day abundance and up to `~300x` above some predicted counts.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare observed candidate abundance with descendant-cluster expectations.
    - Examine whether selection, projection, mass inference, or definition choices drive the apparent excess.
    - Reframe candidate systems in terms of descendant mass and protocluster probability.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The core issue is whether high-z overdensities are correctly mapped to Coma-like descendants.
    - High-redshift protocluster catalogs need careful volume, selection, and descendant-mass calibration.
    - The paper is useful for interpreting JWST overdensity claims.
  - **Position:** important early-LSS paper because it tests whether the first-billion-year structure census is physically plausible.

- [2605.23457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23457) Daria Zakharova et al. `Cosmic web stripping and starvation of low-mass filament galaxies in TNG50`
  - **Question:** how do filaments alter low-mass galaxy gas reservoirs when group and cluster effects are controlled?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: TNG50-1.
    - Sample: low-mass galaxies with `8<=log(M_star/M_sun)<=10` in filaments and field environments.
  - **Method:**
    - Match galaxies by stellar and halo mass.
    - Exclude group and cluster effects.
    - Track gas-disc sizes, asymmetries, accretion histories, and filament-entry times.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Integrated masses and quenched fractions are similar between filament and field controls.
    - Filament galaxies have smaller and more asymmetric cold-gas disks.
    - Early filament entry can alter accretion geometry or produce starvation.
    - Late filament entry can trigger hydrodynamical gas removal analogous to ram pressure.
  - **Position:** strong cosmic-web environment paper because gas morphology changes before global properties diverge.

- [2605.23314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23314) Haoran Dou et al. `The dual effect of group-scale environments on galaxy quenching during cluster infall: pre-processing and protection`
  - **Question:** how does group accretion change quenching during cluster infall?
  - **Data:**
    - Cluster sample: `25` low-redshift X-ray luminous massive clusters.
    - Galaxy sample: large spectroscopic sample around those clusters.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify substructures with the Blooming Tree algorithm.
    - Separate group-accreted galaxies from isolated infallers.
    - Measure quiescent fraction along an `R-V` infall proxy.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The quiescent fraction rises toward cluster centers after a transition near `d_R~2.5`.
    - Group galaxies are more quenched at early infall, consistent with pre-processing.
    - After cluster entry, their quenching increase is delayed relative to isolated galaxies, consistent with group-scale protection.
  - **Position:** useful lower-redshift analog for environmental quenching pathways.

## Quasar Absorption, CGM, and Survey Infrastructure

- [2605.29949](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29949) Andrew J. Fox et al. `High-S/N Quasar Observations with HST/COS: Deep Fields for Spectroscopy`
  - **Question:** what science becomes possible with deep, high-S/N UV spectra of intermediate-redshift quasars?
  - **Data:**
    - Proposed facility: HST/COS.
    - Target sample: about `20` QSOs at `0.5<z<1.5`.
    - Spectral quality: `S/N>30` and velocity resolution of about `20 km/s`.
  - **Method:**
    - Build a deep-field UV spectroscopic sample of bright background quasars.
    - Use high-S/N absorption-line spectra to study multiphase CGM/IGM gas.
    - Enable community legacy analysis across ions, kinematics, and metallicities.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Deep COS spectra would improve weak-line sensitivity and component decomposition.
    - The proposed sample would serve CGM, IGM, feedback, and baryon-cycle science.
    - The profile connection is quasar-sightline absorption as a gas-mapping tool.
  - **Position:** survey-infrastructure paper that supports the profile's CGM/IGM backbone.

- [2605.25339](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25339) David C. Flynn et al. `A Unified [CII] Morpho-Kinematic Corpus for 31 Star-Forming Galaxies at z = 4.26-5.68: The High-z Kinematic Corpus Z1`
  - **Question:** can ALPINE [C II] kinematics be organized into a machine-readable high-redshift corpus?
  - **Data:**
    - ALMA ALPINE: `31` star-forming main-sequence galaxies at `z=4.26-5.68`.
    - Tier 1: `8` confirmed rotators with 3DBarolo tilted-ring fits.
    - Tier 2: `23` galaxies with morpho-kinematic classification.
  - **Method:**
    - Standardize rotation, dispersion, dynamical mass, stellar mass, SFR, disk criteria, and geometry fields.
    - Release JSON, CSV, JSONL, and per-galaxy archive products.
    - Demonstrate single-galaxy and population notebooks.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The corpus supports reproducible comparisons of high-z [C II] kinematics.
    - The data product is useful for galaxy-dynamics and morphology work at `z>4`.
    - It is infrastructure rather than a single new physical claim.
  - **Position:** relevant because structured high-z kinematic data supports future morphology and environment comparisons.
