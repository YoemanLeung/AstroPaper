## Executive View

The read-first paper is [2605.28777](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28777) Maya A. Petkova. It turns the Pop III.1 supermassive-star seed picture into a feedback-regulated SMBH seeding prescription, replacing a free isolation distance with H II region growth. The concrete numbers are useful for early black-hole abundance modeling: a fiducial ionizing luminosity of `10^53 s^-1`, an H II radius of `~1.3 cMpc`, median formation redshift `z~20`, completion by `z~16`, and SMBH number density `~0.2 cMpc^-3`.

The JWST LRD entry is [2605.27903](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27903) A. J. Barger. In A2744, close compact pairs with separations below `0.25 arcsec` frequently mark LRDs or high-redshift broad-line AGN. At least `16/24` known LRDs in the field are such pairs, implying that companion contamination can affect the V-shaped SED used for LRD classification.

For high-redshift quenching, [2605.27555](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27555) Adit H. Edward is the key observational paper. JWST/NIRSpec IFU spectroscopy resolves ages, [Fe/H], and alpha-enhancement in three ultra-massive compact quiescent galaxies at `z~3.5`, showing young alpha-enhanced cores and diverse quenching gradients before later mergers can erase chemical signatures.

The day is unusually dominated by the BlackHoleWeather package. The six related entries use group-scale, high-resolution hydrodynamic simulations to connect turbulent halo condensation, chaotic cold accretion, spin evolution, and jet feedback across the meso-scale bridge between kpc halo rain and sub-pc SMBH feeding.

## High-Redshift Seeds, LRDs, and Quenching

- [2605.28777](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28777) Maya A. Petkova et al. `The formation of supermassive black holes from Population III.1 seeds. IV. Self-regulated seeding from supermassive star ionizing feedback`
  - **Question:** can Pop III.1 SMBH seeding be regulated by ionizing feedback rather than an imposed isolation distance?
  - **Data:**
    - Model target: supermassive Pop III.1 stars forming from pristine gas in minihalos at `z~20-40`.
    - Fiducial source: H-ionizing photon luminosity `10^53 s^-1` and lifetime `10 Myr`.
    - Application: SMBH seeding in cosmological dark-matter halo simulations.
  - **Method:**
    - Model the delay between minihalo formation and Pop III.1 star formation.
    - Track R-type H II region expansion into the IGM.
    - Include redshift dependence of Stromgren spheres for longer-lived ionizing sources.
    - Use the resulting ionized volume to regulate where additional Pop III.1 seeds can form.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The fiducial R-type H II region radius is `R_R~1.3 cMpc`, nearly redshift-independent.
    - Median seed formation occurs near `z~20`.
    - The process is mostly complete by `z~16`.
    - The predicted SMBH number density is `n_SMBH~0.2 cMpc^-3`.
    - Binary SMBH systems may appear as dual AGN at `<=0.3%` for `z>6`, with merger rates relevant to LISA.
  - **Position:** top entry because it gives a quantitative early-SMBH seeding prescription tied to feedback, not only to an assumed seed spacing.

- [2605.27903](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27903) A. J. Barger et al. `Double Dots: Compact Pairs Mark Little Red Dots and High-Redshift Broad-line AGNs`
  - **Question:** do close compact pairs in JWST imaging identify LRDs and high-redshift broad-line AGN?
  - **Data:**
    - Field: JWST imaging of the A2744 massive lensing cluster.
    - Pair selection: compact sources separated by `<0.25 arcsec`.
    - Sample: `31` identified pairs, with median separation `0.15 arcsec`.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare pair counts against a uniform-background expectation.
    - Cross-match pairs with published LRDs and high-redshift broad-line AGN.
    - Use 2D spectroscopic analysis to identify which member hosts broad-line activity.
    - Assess how companion light affects LRD SED classification.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At least `16/24` previously published A2744 LRDs are compact pairs.
    - Both known high-redshift broad-line AGN in the field are also pairs.
    - A companion can mask or reshape the V-shaped continuum used for LRD classification.
    - Broad-line activity can originate in either the redder or bluer member.
    - Close pairs are effective markers of merger-linked high-redshift accretion.
  - **Position:** important LRD paper because it makes source multiplicity and blending a first-order interpretation issue.

- [2605.27555](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27555) Adit H. Edward et al. `MAGAZ3NE: Spatially Resolved Ages and Chemical Abundances of Ultra-Massive Quiescent Galaxies at z ~ 3.5 using JWST/NIRSpec IFU`
  - **Question:** what do resolved age, metallicity, and alpha-enhancement gradients reveal about early quenching in ultra-massive galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRSpec IFU: three ultra-massive compact quiescent galaxies at `z~3.5`.
    - Stellar masses: `log(M_*/M_sun)>11`.
    - Sizes: `R_e<~2 kpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure spatially resolved stellar age, [Fe/H], and `[alpha/Fe]`.
    - Compare central and outer gradients.
    - Test alpha-enhanced and solar-scaled stellar population templates.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Cores are uniformly young, with ages `~0.6-0.7 Gyr`.
    - Cores are alpha-enhanced, with `[alpha/Fe]~0.2-0.5`.
    - Two galaxies show positive age gradients and negative `[alpha/Fe]` gradients, consistent with rapid merger-driven quenching.
    - One galaxy shows a flatter age profile, consistent with more uniform quenching.
    - Alpha-enhanced templates shift metallicities by `~0.2-0.4 dex` relative to solar-scaled templates.
  - **Position:** high-priority JWST quenching paper because it resolves chemical clocks in massive dead galaxies when the Universe is only about two billion years old.

## Black-Hole Weather and Chaotic Cold Accretion

- [2605.27507](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27507) Filippo Barbani et al. `BlackHoleWeather -- Chaotic cold accretion across the meso-scale: Morphology and thermodynamics`
  - **Question:** how does turbulence shape multiphase morphology and SMBH feeding in group-scale chaotic cold accretion?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: 3D hydrodynamic hyper-zoom group-halo runs with cooling and driven subsonic turbulence.
    - Resolution: kpc to sub-pc scales.
    - Regimes: weak and strong stirring.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare turbulence-driven condensation in hot intragroup atmospheres.
    - Track gas across `8-10 dex` in temperature and density.
    - Measure cold-gas extent, thermodynamic distributions, and accretion boosts.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Strong stirring delays accretion but sustains extended filament-rich rain to kpc radii.
    - Weak stirring condenses earlier and keeps cold gas mostly within `100 pc`.
    - SMBH accretion is boosted by up to `100x` above the hot-mode Bondi baseline in both regimes.
    - Feeding depends more on coupling of multiphase structures to the center than on total condensed cold mass.
  - **Position:** central BlackHoleWeather entry because it defines stormy versus rainy CCA regimes.

- [2605.27504](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27504) Filippo Barbani et al. `BlackHoleWeather -- Chaotic cold accretion across the meso-scale: Variability and kinematics`
  - **Question:** how does turbulence regulate CCA variability, radial transport, and kinematics?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: GPU-accelerated 3D group-halo runs with cooling and driven subsonic turbulence.
    - Dynamic range: kpc to sub-pc.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure accretion histories, power spectra, radial inflow, and multiphase k-plots.
    - Use `C=t_cool/t_eddy` as a condensation diagnostic.
    - Compare stormy and rainy turbulence regimes.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - SMBH accretion remains super-Bondi and varies by up to `~2 dex`.
    - Strong stirring produces fragmented feeding and inflow enhancement at `0.1-1 kpc`.
    - Inner feeding rates stay similar across regimes, so accretion is not simply supply-limited by macro-scale weather.
    - `C~1` marks soft X-ray gas as the condensation gateway.
  - **Position:** useful companion because it gives observable kinematic and timing diagnostics for CCA.

- [2605.27502](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27502) Olmo Piana et al. `BlackHoleWeather -- Spin-coupled chaotic cold accretion across the meso-scale: Morphology and thermodynamics`
  - **Question:** how does meso-scale chaotic accretion deliver angular momentum to SMBH spin and jet orientation?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: GPU-accelerated group atmosphere runs with jet feedback and SMBH spin evolution.
    - Models: fixed-axis, direct, and hybrid spin prescriptions.
  - **Method:**
    - Couple resolved multiphase inflow to unresolved ISCO angular-momentum transfer.
    - Compare low- and high-turbulence group setups.
    - Track cold reservoirs, torque coherence, spin evolution, and feedback geometry.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Cold-gas reservoir size is nearly independent of jet-axis prescription.
    - Spin prescription controls inner feeding-feedback coupling, jet efficiency, and feedback geometry.
    - Low-spin SMBHs reorient more easily.
    - Stronger turbulence fragments inflow and enhances torque cancellation.
  - **Position:** key spin/feedback entry because it links multiphase CCA to where the next jet points.

- [2605.27508](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27508) Olmo Piana et al. `BlackHoleWeather -- Spin-coupled chaotic cold accretion across the meso scale: Variability and kinematics`
  - **Question:** does persistent halo stirring regulate SMBH spin by disrupting torque coherence?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: four 3D hydrodynamic runs in a `100 kpc` box down to sub-pc scales.
    - Comparison: driven-turbulence and interrupted-turbulence suites.
  - **Method:**
    - Use the hybrid SMBH spin model from the companion paper.
    - Track accretion variability, torque coherence, k-plots, and jet-axis drift.
    - Compare continuous stirring with decaying turbulence.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Persistent stirring disrupts mass and angular-momentum continuity across the meso-scale bridge.
    - In driven runs, radial accretion drops by `2-3` orders of magnitude toward pc scales.
    - Interrupted runs preserve connected gas channels and coherent torque delivery.
    - Jet-axis reorientation rates can be about two orders of magnitude higher in interrupted runs.
  - **Position:** sharpens the BlackHoleWeather picture by separating cold-gas production from coherent angular-momentum delivery.

- [2605.27503](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27503) Vieri Cammelli et al. `BlackHoleWeather -- Jet-regulated chaotic cold accretion across the meso scale: Morphology and thermodynamics`
  - **Question:** how do kinetic jets reshape CCA morphology and thermodynamics across group atmospheres?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: two turbulent radiatively cooling group atmospheres with self-regulated kinetic jet feedback.
    - Regimes: stronger and weaker initial turbulence.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare jet compression, entrainment, mixing, and condensation.
    - Track hot-warm-cold bridges, cocoon structure, and central gas reservoirs.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Jets prevent cooling inside the jet channel but allow condensation around the cone and jet-ambient interface.
    - Stronger turbulence produces extended filamentary condensation and burst-dominated fueling.
    - Weaker turbulence keeps condensation coherent and centrally confined.
    - Once condensation begins, SMBH fueling becomes super-Bondi.
  - **Position:** important AGN-feedback paper because it couples CCA to mechanical jet regulation instead of treating accretion as a cooling-only process.

- [2605.27511](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27511) Vieri Cammelli et al. `BlackHoleWeather -- Jet-regulated chaotic cold accretion across the meso scale: Variability and kinematics`
  - **Question:** when is cold gas dynamically connected to SMBH feeding in a jet-regulated CCA system?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: matched jet-regulated group atmospheres differing only in turbulent driving strength.
    - Diagnostics: Eddington ratios, power spectra, phase-separated mass fluxes, k-plots, and `C=t_cool/t_eddy`.
  - **Method:**
    - Track accretion histories from Bondi-like to super-Bondi states.
    - Separate cold-gas presence from radial transport to the sink.
    - Compare stormy, cloudy, and rainy phases.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Both runs become CCA-fed after precipitation begins.
    - Strong stirring creates an early stormy phase with extended condensation and bursty feeding, then a cloudy phase with weakened sink coupling.
    - The calmer run keeps a compact rainy state with a longer-lived central reservoir.
    - Meso-scale transport, not just cold-gas production, controls SMBH fueling.
  - **Position:** useful closing piece for the package because it defines diagnostics that distinguish visible cold gas from feeding-relevant cold gas.

## Gas, Environment, and Survey Infrastructure

- [2605.27517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27517) Jia Wei Teh et al. `TRINITY: A coupled model of winds, radiation, and photoionised gas in molecular clouds. I. Methods and validation`
  - **Question:** how do winds, radiation pressure, photoionized gas, gravity, and cloud structure jointly determine cloud dispersal and LyC leakage?
  - **Data:**
    - Model grid: clouds with masses `10^5-10^6.5 M_sun`.
    - Core densities: `10^3-10^4 cm^-3`.
    - Star-formation efficiencies: `0.01-0.30`.
  - **Method:**
    - Evolve a 1D thin shell under winds, supernovae, radiation pressure, photoionized pressure, and gravity.
    - Allow uniform, power-law, or Bonnor-Ebert cloud structures.
    - Track shell structure, hot-bubble cooling, photon absorption, and LyC escape.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Photoionized gas pressure enlarges the shell radius by about `17%` at `10 Myr` in the fiducial run.
    - At high efficiency, the energy-driven phase lasts under `1 Myr`.
    - Cloud structure controls whether shells disperse or re-collapse.
    - Bonnor-Ebert clouds disperse about `55%` later than homogeneous clouds.
  - **Position:** relevant to reionization modeling because LyC escape and cloud clearing depend on coupled pre-supernova feedback.

- [2605.28758](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28758) Chandan Watts et al. `Cluster-centric trends in bar size and pattern speed: the case of Abell 2199`
  - **Question:** how does cluster environment affect stellar bar size and pattern speed?
  - **Data:**
    - Cluster: Abell 2199.
    - Members: `578` spectroscopically confirmed galaxies.
    - Bar sample: `39` barred galaxies, with MaNGA pattern-speed estimates for `22` and robust values for `12`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure bar structure with isophotal ellipse fitting and three-component photometric decompositions.
    - Estimate pattern speeds with the Tremaine-Weinberg method.
    - Compare bar properties, stellar age, and angular momentum versus cluster-centric radius.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Abell 2199 shows star-formation-density and morphology-density relations despite being dynamically unrelaxed.
    - Early-type spirals have larger normalized bars toward the cluster center.
    - Pattern speed also varies with cluster-centric distance.
    - Inside `~0.5 R_vir`, barred galaxies tend to be older and lower in projected angular momentum.
  - **Position:** local environment result, useful as a resolved low-redshift analog for how dense environments reshape disk structure.

- [2605.28731](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28731) Michalina Maksymowicz-Maciata et al. `MIGHTEE-HI: HI catalogue of 293 sources for the COSMOS field and comparative study of 3-dimensional source finding methods`
  - **Question:** what H I sample and source-finding completeness does MIGHTEE provide in COSMOS?
  - **Data:**
    - MeerKAT MIGHTEE COSMOS data cubes.
    - Catalogue: `293` H I sources over `0.004<z<0.093`.
    - Ancillary data: optical to near-infrared photometry, stellar masses, and SFRs.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare PyBDSF, ProFound, SoFiA, and LESHI for 3D H I source finding.
    - Inject simulated galaxies into a MeerKAT cube in bins of mass, inclination, and distance.
    - Use the simulations to estimate completeness and detection bias.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Untargeted H I source counts depend strongly on source-finder choice.
    - The catalogue includes H I masses, velocity widths, and galaxy properties.
    - The completeness characterization supports future MIGHTEE and SKAO survey strategies.
  - **Position:** survey-infrastructure entry, useful for gas-rich galaxy demographics and COSMOS multi-wavelength context.

- [2605.27538](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27538) Arpan Pal et al. `VROOM-SBI: A Fast Simulation-Based Bayesian Inference Methodology for QU-Fitting`
  - **Question:** can Bayesian QU-fitting become practical for survey-scale Faraday inference?
  - **Data:**
    - Stokes `Q` and `U` spectra over VLA L-band frequency coverage.
    - Models: four standard depolarization models.
  - **Method:**
    - Use simulation-based inference with neural posterior estimation.
    - Compare results with classical QU-fitting and Faraday synthesis.
    - Release open code and trained models.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - VROOM-SBI gives results comparable to Faraday synthesis and classical QU-fitting.
    - The method gives a speedup of `~500` over classical QU-fitting.
    - The approach makes per-pixel Bayesian Faraday inference more feasible for large radio polarization surveys.
  - **Position:** methods entry relevant to CGM/IGM magnetism because Faraday statistics depend on scalable line-of-sight inference.

- [2605.05203](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05203) Lawrence Dam et al. `Non-conservation and time non-locality of biased tracers`
  - **Question:** how do ongoing formation and mergers break number conservation assumptions for biased tracers?
  - **Data:**
    - Theory: Lagrangian model of evolving tracer number density.
    - Physical process: environmentally dependent tracer loss by mergers.
  - **Method:**
    - Include source and sink terms in tracer number density.
    - Make the formation history nonlocal in time.
    - Derive the resulting linear bias for non-conserved tracers.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Non-conserved tracers debias faster than conserved tracers.
    - Large-scale power becomes increasingly suppressed relative to conserved predictions.
    - Current LSS models that assume number conservation can misestimate tracer evolution when formation and mergers remain active.
  - **Position:** relevant LSS theory because galaxy and halo samples used for clustering may not satisfy conserved-tracer assumptions.
