## Executive View

The read-first paper is [2605.13472](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13472) Connor Sant Fournier. It uses Synthesizer, BPASS, and FLARES to test whether rest-frame UV absorption indices can recover stellar metallicities for `5<=z<=10` galaxies. The useful result is practical: the `1719 A` index is the cleanest and most stable metallicity tracer, while `1370 A`, `1400 A`, `1425 A`, `1533 A`, and `1550 A` are also usable with joint fitting. Indices such as `1460 A` and `1501 A` are more vulnerable to bursts and short-timescale feedback.

The strongest survey-forecast paper is [2605.13843](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13843) Fatemeh Hafezianzadeh. It forward-models LSST luminosity functions with ASTRID over `0<=z<=2`, calibrating a dust attenuation prescription against SDSS at `z=0` and predicting ugrizy counts in `Delta z=0.1` slices. This is useful for LSST/Rubin planning, not because the dust model is final, but because it turns a large hydrodynamical simulation into directly comparable LSST apparent-magnitude catalogs.

The strongest AGN-gas paper is [2605.12973](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12973) Ruitian Li. GMRT spatially resolved H I maps of `8` local Seyfert hosts show that the galaxies still sit on the classical H I mass-size relation, while UGC 4503 has elevated H I velocity dispersion, `sigma0=14.87(-3.84,+6.14) km/s`. The result favors AGN stirring or turbulence injection in the atomic disk rather than global H I removal.

The strongest quasar/lensing paper is [2605.13514](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13514) Erik Solhaug. COOL J1153+0755 is a rare cluster lens containing two background quasars, each quadruply imaged by the same massive foreground system. The system combines a Type I quasar at `z=1.524`, a dusty Type II quasar at `z=1.939`, and maximum image separations near `26 arcsec`, making it valuable for wide-separation quasar-lens selection and quasar environment work.

Two methods papers are worth keeping close. [2605.13735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13735) Ren Kawase presents a PSF deconvolution method that can remove AGN point-source light while improving host-galaxy resolution in Subaru/HSC images. [2605.13219](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13219) Ling Wang shows that missing-band imputation can improve CSST photo-z performance only when the missingness pattern is treated correctly; imputing physical non-detections can make the result worse.

## High-Redshift Galaxy Diagnostics and Survey Forecasts

- [2605.13472](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13472) Connor Sant Fournier et al. `First Light And Reionization Epoch Simulations (FLARES) XXI: The UV Indices of Galaxies in the Early Universe`
  - **Question:** which rest-frame UV absorption indices can robustly trace stellar metallicity in galaxies during the reionization era?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: FLARES zoom simulations, with galaxy populations evaluated across `5<=z<=10`.
    - Spectral models: Synthesizer forward modeling with BPASS stellar population models.
    - Observational context: JWST/NIRSpec-accessible rest-frame UV indices for `5<z<10` galaxies.
    - Galaxy regime: FLARES reaches `M_*=10^8-10^11 M_sun` at `z=5`, covering the mass scale of most current JWST samples except the faintest limits.
  - **Method:**
    - Generate integrated stellar spectra and equivalent widths for UV indices including `1370 A`, `1400 A`, `1425 A`, `1460 A`, `1501 A`, `1533 A`, `1550 A`, `1719 A`, and `1853 A`.
    - Test index response to stellar metallicity, star-formation history, IMF choice, nebular emission, and spectral resolution.
    - Apply the index measurements to FLARES galaxies from `z=5` to `z=10`.
    - Identify which indices retain a monotonic and low-scatter metallicity relation under realistic galaxy histories.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Most UV indices increase monotonically with stellar metallicity, so a multi-index approach can recover enrichment trends in high-redshift spectra.
    - The `1719 A` index is the most stable single tracer because it keeps a tight positive correlation across redshift and is less affected by temporary emission or short-term stellar-population changes.
    - The `1370 A`, `1400 A`, and `1425 A` indices are also robust; `1533 A` and `1550 A` retain useful positive metallicity scaling.
    - The `1460 A` and `1501 A` indices are less reliable because bursty histories and feedback episodes perturb their equivalent widths.
    - Native BPASS spectral resolution is adequate for JWST/NIRSpec-style interpretation of these UV indices at `z~5-10`.
  - **Position:** directly aligned with JWST high-z galaxy work because it turns rest-UV absorption features into a calibrated stellar-metallicity diagnostic for reionization-era samples.

- [2605.13843](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13843) Fatemeh Hafezianzadeh et al. `The Galaxy Luminosity Functions in ASTRID: Predictions for LSST`
  - **Question:** can ASTRID produce LSST-ready luminosity functions and mock photometric catalogs after dust attenuation is calibrated against low-redshift data?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: ASTRID cosmological hydrodynamical simulation with a large `250 h^-1 Mpc` volume and `2 x 5500^3` particles.
    - Calibration data: SDSS luminosity functions at `z=0`.
    - Validation data: rest-frame `B`, `V`, `R`, and `I` luminosity functions at `z=0.5`, `1.0`, and `1.5`.
    - Forecast output: LSST ugrizy apparent-magnitude luminosity functions over `0<=z<=2` in `Delta z=0.1` slices.
  - **Method:**
    - Precompute dust-free stellar-population magnitudes on age and metallicity grids.
    - Apply a dust attenuation model where optical depth scales with metal surface density.
    - Calibrate the effective ISM dust coefficient to SDSS, adopting `kappa_ISM=2.9`.
    - Compare predicted luminosity functions, colors, number densities, and Schechter parameters against observed constraints.
    - Generate LSST apparent-magnitude catalogs and mock imaging-oriented outputs.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Dust-attenuated ASTRID reproduces broad observed luminosity-function evolution across optical bands through `z~1.5`.
    - LSST luminosity functions shift to fainter apparent magnitudes with increasing redshift, while the faint-end slope becomes more negative toward `z~2`.
    - Predicted galaxy counts rise strongly with survey depth, especially in the `g`, `r`, and `i` bands.
    - Low-redshift galaxies dominate the expected LSST number counts even in the deep 10-year survey.
    - ASTRID predicts about `84 arcmin^-2` at DP1-like limits, higher than the Rubin DP1 estimate of about `57 arcmin^-2`, so real LSST data should be used to recalibrate dust and selection details.
  - **Position:** useful for LSST/Rubin galaxy and LSS planning because it connects hydrodynamical galaxy formation to survey-depth apparent-magnitude statistics.

## AGN, Quasar Lenses, and Black-Hole Growth

- [2605.12973](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12973) Ruitian Li et al. `A Spatially Resolved HI Survey of Seyfert Galaxies: the Role of AGN Feedback in Shaping Atomic Gas Reservoirs`
  - **Question:** does Seyfert AGN feedback globally deplete atomic gas reservoirs, or does it mainly perturb the kinematics of the H I disk?
  - **Data:**
    - GMRT: spatially resolved H I 21-cm survey of `8` nearby Seyfert galaxies.
    - Observing program: `40` hours of GMRT time across two cycles.
    - H I detection: all `8` Seyfert hosts are detected.
    - Case study: UGC 4503 at `D=62.2 Mpc` with `log M_*=10.82`, `log M_HI=10.074+-0.045`, and `D_HI=63.688+-0.795 kpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Reduce and image the GMRT data with CASA and the CAPTURE-CASA6 pipeline.
    - Construct H I spectra, moment maps, masses, and diameters.
    - Compare Seyfert hosts to the Wang et al. H I mass-size relation and to SIMBA feedback variants.
    - Model the resolved H I kinematics of UGC 4503 with DysmalPy.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Seyfert hosts remain on the classical H I mass-size relation, `log D_HI=(0.466+-0.022) log M_HI-(2.921+-0.207)`, so AGN feedback does not erase the large-scale H I reservoirs.
    - UGC 4503 has elevated intrinsic H I velocity dispersion, `sigma0=14.87(-3.84,+6.14) km/s`, above the typical disk value of about `10+-2 km/s`.
    - UGC 4503 has `V/sigma=14.28(-4.17,+4.97)`, lower than ordinary rotation-dominated star-forming disks.
    - The atomic gas evidence favors AGN-driven turbulence or stirring over wholesale cold-gas removal.
    - Follow-up that combines GMRT, FAST, and optical IFU data can connect the atomic and ionized feedback phases.
  - **Position:** lower-redshift than the core high-z profile, but important for interpreting how AGN feedback couples to cold gas without necessarily producing immediate gas depletion.

- [2605.13514](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13514) Erik Solhaug et al. `COOL-LAMPS IX: A Rare Duo of Quasars Each Lensed by a Single Massive Galaxy Cluster`
  - **Question:** what is the nature of COOL J1153+0755, and why does it matter for wide-separation lensed quasars and quasar environments?
  - **Data:**
    - Discovery data: DECaLS imaging.
    - Follow-up: Magellan FourStar, FIRE, LDSS3, and IMACS; Nordic Optical Telescope/ALFOSC; archival Spitzer, Herschel, eROSITA, ALMA, HST/ACS, and JWST/NIRCam.
    - Lens system: primary cluster at `z_lens=0.4301` plus a secondary cluster near `z=0.5766`.
    - Sources: a broad-line Type I quasar at `z=1.524` and a dusty Type II quasar at `z=1.939`.
    - Image geometry: both quasars are quadruply imaged, with maximum image separations of `25.6 arcsec` and `26.0 arcsec`.
  - **Method:**
    - Select cluster-lens candidates with red-sequence and quasar-color information while avoiding a strict point-source morphology cut.
    - Use optical/NIR imaging and spectroscopy to identify cluster members, quasar redshifts, and source types.
    - Fit emission lines and broad-band SEDs to classify the quasars and estimate intrinsic luminosities.
    - Build cluster-scale lens models using ground-based data and then refine the interpretation with HST and JWST imaging.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - COOL J1153+0755 is the first known gravitational lens system where a single massive cluster quadruply images two distinct background quasars.
    - The two sources sample different AGN states: a Type I quasar at `z=1.524` and a dust-obscured Type II quasar at `z=1.939`.
    - Space-based constraints give magnification-corrected bolometric luminosities of `3.4(-0.7,+0.5)e45 erg/s` for the Type I quasar and `5.4(-0.8,+0.9)e45 erg/s` for the Type II quasar.
    - The system expands the tiny wide-separation lensed-quasar sample and demonstrates that morphology cuts can miss blended, extended, or cluster-crowded quasar lenses.
    - The field is valuable for time-delay cosmography, quasar-host studies, cluster mass modeling, and dense-environment quasar selection.
  - **Position:** strong quasar-environment and lensing target because it combines cosmic-noon AGN physics with cluster-scale magnification and selection-function lessons.

- [2605.13735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13735) Ren Kawase et al. `A New PSF Deconvolution Algorithm: Simultaneous Spatial Resolution Enhancement and Point Source Removal for Morphological Analysis of AGN Host Galaxies`
  - **Question:** can ground-based AGN imaging be deconvolved so that the host galaxy is both sharper and less contaminated by the unresolved AGN?
  - **Data:**
    - Imaging: Subaru/HSC `i`-band images from HSC-SSP DR3 in the COSMOS UltraDeep field.
    - Survey scale: `1.8 deg^2`, pixel scale `0.168 arcsec/pixel`, limiting magnitude about `27.4`, and typical seeing around `0.6 arcsec`.
    - Test set: artificial AGN inserted into real galaxy images and observed AGN host systems at `z~0-1`.
    - Comparison data: Hubble imaging used as a high-resolution benchmark.
  - **Method:**
    - Decompose each observed image into a smooth extended host component, a sparse point-source component, and noise.
    - Add a host smoothness prior, a point-source sparsity prior, and an overlap penalty that prevents AGN light from leaking into the host model.
    - Optimize the deconvolution and point-source removal simultaneously.
    - Compare reconstructed HSC host morphologies to Hubble-based expectations after accounting for PSF differences.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The reconstructed image reproduces the original HSC observation with very small residuals, with example RMSE near `4e-6`.
    - The central point source is removed without the strong over-subtraction or under-subtraction that commonly biases AGN-host morphology.
    - The effective FWHM improves from about `1.06 arcsec` in the HSC input to `0.57 arcsec` in the reconstruction, close to the `0.55 arcsec` Hubble-based comparison in the worked example.
    - The method delivers a factor of about `2-3` resolution improvement for host structure in favorable cases.
    - Wide-field ground-based AGN host morphology can therefore move closer to HST-like structural information for statistical samples.
  - **Position:** practical AGN-host tool for HSC and future LSST-era morphology studies where space-based imaging is unavailable for most objects.

- [2605.12599](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12599) Ecaterina Leonova et al. `One Merge to Rule Them All: From Galaxy Interactions to Black Hole Mergers Using Horizon-AGN`
  - **Question:** how can observable galaxy-pair selections be converted into galaxy merger rates and supermassive black-hole merger rates?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: Horizon-AGN galaxy and black-hole catalogs.
    - Redshift range: galaxies and merger histories followed to `z~6`.
    - Stellar-mass regime: galaxy samples spanning roughly `10^9-10^11.5 M_sun`.
    - Black-hole information: numerical and delayed SMBH merger prescriptions from the simulation merger trees.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify projected galaxy pairs using projected separation and redshift or velocity differences.
    - Optimize pair-selection thresholds with the Matthews Correlation Coefficient in stellar-mass, mass-ratio, and redshift bins.
    - Compare optimized cuts with fixed literature-style pair criteria.
    - Use merger trees to derive pair fractions, merger fractions, merger timescales, and galaxy merger rates.
    - Convert galaxy merger rates to SMBH merger rates with corrections for black-hole occupation, black-hole merger fraction, and delay times.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Pair fraction, merger fraction, merger timescale, and merger rate evolve strongly with stellar mass and redshift.
    - Massive and high-redshift galaxies show elevated merger activity relative to lower-mass and lower-redshift systems.
    - MCC-optimized selections recover physically connected mergers more reliably than fixed projected-distance cuts.
    - The volume-averaged SMBH merger rate peaks near cosmic noon, `z~2-3`.
    - Delayed SMBH merger prescriptions change the redshift and mass dependence, which matters for LISA and pulsar-timing-array interpretation.
  - **Position:** useful bridge between galaxy-evolution merger observables and black-hole coalescence forecasts, especially for cosmic-noon AGN growth.

- [2605.13469](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13469) Emma L. Elley et al. `The impact of flickering variability and magnetisation on the dynamics, stability and morphology of radio-loud AGN jets`
  - **Question:** how do jet-power flickering and magnetization jointly control radio-loud AGN jet morphology and stability?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: `4` three-dimensional relativistic MHD jet simulations with the PLUTO code.
    - Parameter grid: constant versus variable jet power, crossed with low and high magnetization.
    - Jet model: underdense toroidal-field jets with radius `0.4 kpc`.
    - Evolution scale: jets followed for `5 Myr` to lengths of about `50 kpc`.
    - Variability model: lognormal jet-power fluctuations with pink-noise behavior and standard deviation `0.33` in natural log power.
  - **Method:**
    - Inject steady and flickering relativistic jets into the same ambient setup.
    - Compare cocoon morphology, backflows, jet-beam continuity, magnetic-field structure, and simulated radio images.
    - Analyze the stability of the injected equilibrium and conditions for localized `m=1` kink modes.
    - Render simulated radio morphologies over time and viewing angle to compare with observed complex radio galaxies.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Constant high-magnetization jets can move away from the central axis for extended periods and produce strongly asymmetric cocoons.
    - Variable high-magnetization jets show shorter off-axis episodes but can develop discontinuous or broken jet-beam morphologies.
    - Flickering power plus high magnetization can trigger localized kink instabilities along the beam.
    - Low-magnetization jets are more dominated by hydrodynamic mixing and resemble earlier non-magnetized flickering-jet behavior.
    - Viewing angle and Doppler effects can make the same physical jet appear as a misaligned, asymmetric, or broken radio source.
  - **Position:** useful AGN-feedback context because complex radio morphology can encode jet magnetization and duty cycle, not only external environment or precession.

## Survey Methods, Low-Surface-Brightness Galaxies, and Photo-z Systematics

- [2605.13842](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13842) Hareesh Thuruthipilly et al. `From DES to KiDS: Domain adaptation for cross-survey detection of low-surface-brightness galaxies`
  - **Question:** can DES-trained deep-learning LSBG finders transfer to KiDS without retraining, preparing for LSST and Euclid-scale searches?
  - **Data:**
    - Training set: DES DR1 labeled sample of about `37,000` objects, including `18,532` LSBGs and contaminants.
    - Target survey: KiDS DR5 parent catalog with `1.38e8` sources and 9-band KiDS+VIKING photometry.
    - Pipeline counts: `322,278` preselected candidates, `28,971` deep-learning candidates, `22,716` Sersic-selected candidates, and `22,264` visually confirmed objects.
    - Final catalog: `20,180` KiDS LSBGs, with `4,913` objects in the redshift subsample and `434` UDGs.
  - **Method:**
    - Apply CNN, LSBG-DETR, and LSBG-ViT models trained on DES cutouts.
    - Use surface-brightness and scale normalization for unsupervised domain adaptation from DES to KiDS.
    - Fit structural properties with Galfitm and inspect candidates visually.
    - Cross-match redshift information and perform SED fitting and photo-z checks for the final samples.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - DES validation recall reaches about `95%`, and the model ensemble reaches `97%` recall with contamination near `4%`.
    - The KiDS catalog structural properties match expectations from DES and HSC LSBG samples, supporting the cross-survey transfer.
    - Photo-z performance for the LSBG sample reaches `sigma_NMAD=0.031`, comparable to ordinary KiDS galaxy results.
    - KiDS-LSBG colors are bimodal, with about `73%` blue systems and `27%` red systems.
    - Cluster LSBGs and UDGs are redder and more quenched; at `z<=0.03`, `72.7%` of cluster LSBGs are quenched.
  - **Position:** relevant survey-scale infrastructure for LSST/Euclid diffuse-galaxy work and environment studies, though not directly high-z.

- [2605.13219](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13219) Ling Wang et al. `Comparative analysis of missing data imputation methods for CSST survey: Impact on photometric redshift estimation performance`
  - **Question:** which missing-band imputation methods improve CSST photo-z estimates, and when should missing values not be imputed?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey simulation: CSST Cycle 6 simulated photometry and official pipeline products.
    - Bands: seven optical/NIR CSST bands.
    - Photo-z engine: EAZY, evaluated with outlier fraction, `sigma_NMAD`, and bias.
    - Realistic missingness: final output missing fraction about `36%`, rising to `44%` among objects with at least one missing value.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare KNN, random forest, CatBoost, recurrent neural networks, GAIN/US-GAN, GP-VAE, SAITS, iTransformer, and related imputation strategies.
    - Test ideal missing-completely-at-random cases with one to three missing bands and missing rates of `10%`, `20%`, and `30%`.
    - Evaluate whether the training data are complete or have the same missingness pattern as the test set.
    - Separate random missing values from physical non-detections below survey limits.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Under ideal random missingness with complete training data, KNN and SAITS improve photo-z performance strongly.
    - With three missing bands, KNN and SAITS can reduce the outlier fraction by about a factor of four relative to no imputation.
    - KNN degrades when the training set also has missing values, while SAITS remains more robust in missing-training regimes.
    - Matching the training missingness pattern to the realistic test missingness can reduce the outlier fraction by nearly half.
    - Imputing physical non-detections is detrimental; those values should be kept as censoring or limit information rather than filled as ordinary missing bands.
  - **Position:** relevant to LSS and galaxy-survey systematics because photo-z quality depends on missing-data treatment, not only filter depth or template choice.

## Fossil Enrichment and Local Anchors

- [2605.12810](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12810) Daisy Bissonette et al. `The Metallicity Distribution of the Ultra-Faint Dwarf Galaxy Segue 1`
  - **Question:** what does a deeper Segue 1 metallicity distribution imply for early chemical enrichment in the lowest-mass galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Spectroscopy: Keck/LRIS low-resolution `R~1000` spectra using the Ca II K absorption feature.
    - Sample: `40` member stars in Segue 1, including red-giant-branch and main-sequence-turnoff stars.
    - Final MDF context: more than a six-fold increase over the previously published Segue 1 metallicity sample.
    - Galaxy scale: Segue 1 has `M_*~500 M_sun`, placing it among the least massive ultra-faint dwarfs.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure Ca II K pseudo-equivalent widths and calibrate stellar metallicities.
    - Fit the metallicity distribution with Gaussian and chemical-evolution descriptions.
    - Compare the larger MDF to previous small-number high-resolution samples and to other UFDs.
    - Use the absence of extremely metal-poor detections to bound the surviving Pop III fraction.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Segue 1 has mean metallicity `[Fe/H]=-2.52+-0.10` and dispersion `sigma=0.59+-0.06 dex`.
    - The MDF is smooth and single-peaked, with no evidence for distinct subpopulations.
    - The nonzero metallicity spread reaffirms Segue 1 as a galaxy rather than a globular cluster.
    - The enrichment history is consistent with short-duration star formation, `<=1 Gyr`, before or around reionization.
    - The lack of stars below `[Fe/H]<-5` places only a weak upper limit on surviving Pop III stars because the sample remains small.
  - **Position:** not an extragalactic high-z observation, but useful fossil evidence for early enrichment in the smallest galaxy-scale systems.
