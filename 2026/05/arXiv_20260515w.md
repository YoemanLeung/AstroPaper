## Executive View

The week is dominated by a connected JWST high-redshift theme: massive early galaxies, low-mass metallicities, dust physics, and how much of the apparent tension with standard galaxy formation is observational or modeling systematics. [2605.13966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13966) Katherine Chworowsky is the top read because CAPERS shows that joint NIRSpec plus photometry fitting lowers massive-galaxy stellar masses relative to photometry-only estimates and often requires gray attenuation. [2605.06770](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06770) Tiger Yu-Yang Hsiao anchors the low-mass end with direct metallicities for lensed `z~6-8` galaxies down to `M_*~10^6-10^8 M_sun`.

The early-dust thread is unusually strong. [2605.14922](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14922) Kana Takechi finds a `z=8.312` ALMA dust-continuum galaxy with broad Hbeta AGN evidence, low metallicity, hot dust, and non-co-spatial optical/far-infrared [O III] diagnostics. [2605.09829](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09829) D. Burgarella argues that low-opacity supernova dust and porous geometry can explain many UV-bright `z>9` galaxies without requiring dust-free ISM or extreme star-formation efficiency. [2605.06782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06782) Shengdong Lu shows that COLIBRE underpredicts bright UV luminosities at `z=7-15` after dust attenuation, keeping the UV-bright galaxy problem active.

For reionization and clustering, [2605.14313](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14313) Yuanhang Ning characterizes luminous LAEs near the end of reionization as low-mass, young, bursty, and high-Lyalpha-escape systems. [2605.11064](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11064) Antonio Matteri shifts the super-early-galaxy debate from abundance matching to clustering, predicting that bright `M_UV<-18` samples can separate primordial-black-hole-like scenarios from bursty or attenuation-free models. [2605.11077](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11077) Jiamu Huang shows that ASPIRE-like JWST quasar-environment clustering is covariance-dominated, not Poisson-dominated.

The AGN/LRD cluster is also coherent. [2605.07976](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07976) Rosa M. Merida tests the black-hole-star model on The Cliff and finds a Bagpipes solution with a low-mass dusty host, blackbody-like component, and possible weak AGN UV leakage. [2605.14233](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14233) Kevin Park reports low-z DESI LRD analogs, while [2605.06791](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06791) Matthew Stepney uses SPHEREx to more than double the confirmed heavily reddened quasar census at cosmic noon.

Several survey and inference papers are immediately useful for future work. [2605.13967](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13967) Ely D. Kovetz turns burstiness into a line-intensity-mapping shot-noise correction. [2605.15096](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15096) Bozhidara Stoyanova quantifies `~0.6 dex` SPS-driven mass shifts. [2605.12503](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12503) Jui-Kuan Chan recovers `19,685` hidden DESI LAEs. [2605.10753](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10753) Han-Yue Guo uses latent diffusion to infer photometric-redshift PDFs and rest-frame spectra from photometry.

## High-Redshift Galaxies, Dust, and Reionization

- [2605.13966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13966) Katherine Chworowsky et al. `Massive Galaxies Form Early and Gray: Stellar Assembly and Dust Attenuation at z>3.5 from CAPERS`
  - **Question:** how robust are massive `z>3.5` stellar masses once JWST/NIRSpec spectra and photometry are fitted jointly?
  - **Data:** CAPERS JWST/NIRSpec prism spectroscopy in EGS, PRIMER-COSMOS, and PRIMER-UDS; HST/ACS and JWST/NIRCam photometry; `148` galaxies with `log(M_*/M_sun)>9.5` after LRD/AGN cleaning.
  - **Method:** Dense-Basis preselection, BAGPIPES spectro-photometric fitting, slit-loss rescaling, flexible non-parametric star-formation histories, variable dust attenuation curves, and comparison with SC-SAM/FLARES.
  - **Conclusion:** photometry-only fits overestimate masses by `0.29-0.51 dex`; slit-loss scaling shifts masses and SFRs upward; the most massive galaxies often need gray dust; and CAPERS galaxies appear to assemble earlier than simulation analogs.
  - **Position:** the week's anchor paper because it directly affects claims about high-z massive-galaxy abundance and formation timing.

- [2605.14922](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14922) Kana Takechi et al. `DREAMS. JWST Spectroscopy of a z=8.3 Galaxy with an ALMA Dust Continuum Detection`
  - **Question:** what powers MACS0416-Y1 at `z=8.312`, and can JWST optical and ALMA far-infrared lines be interpreted with one ionized-gas phase?
  - **Data:** DREAMS JWST/NIRSpec MSA medium-grating spectroscopy, archival NIRSpec IFU, NIRCam F150W/F444W imaging, and ALMA Band 7 dust continuum plus [O III] `88 micron`.
  - **Method:** joint line fitting, broad-line search, MEx/LRD checks, direct `Te` metallicity from [O III] `4363 A`, and cross-comparison of JWST [O III] `5007 A` with ALMA [O III] `88 micron`.
  - **Conclusion:** broad Hbeta with FWHM near `1100 km/s` supports AGN activity; `12+log(O/H)=7.86`; the dust mass is only about `10^6 M_sun`; `T_dust=91(-35,+62) K`; and the [O III] `88 micron/5007 A` ratio cannot arise from one homogeneous nebula.
  - **Position:** top early-dust paper because it shows that ALMA and JWST line diagnostics can trace different physical regions in a `z>8` galaxy.

- [2605.06770](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06770) Tiger Yu-Yang Hsiao et al. `A Glimpse of the Low-Mass End of the Direct Mass-Metallicity Relation at z~6-8`
  - **Question:** what does the direct-method MZR look like below `M_*~10^8 M_sun` during reionization?
  - **Data:** deep `~30 hr` JWST/NIRSpec G395M GLIMPSE-D spectroscopy of the lensed Abell S1063 field, with eight [O III] `4363/4364 A` emitters, combined with literature auroral-line detections for `21` galaxies.
  - **Method:** direct electron-temperature metallicities, SED-based stellar masses and SFRs, lensing magnification corrections, strong-line cross-checks, and MZR fitting over `M_*~10^6.7-10^9 M_sun`.
  - **Conclusion:** high-z low-mass galaxies are `~0.3-0.5 dex` more metal-poor than local galaxies at fixed mass; the slope is `0.25+-0.10`; scatter is about `0.2 dex`; and assuming too-low electron density can underestimate metallicity by `~0.5 dex` in very dense gas.
  - **Position:** essential companion to massive-galaxy work because it constrains feedback and enrichment in the opposite mass regime at similar epochs.

- [2605.14313](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14313) Yuanhang Ning et al. `An Updated Characterization of Luminous Lyalpha emitters at the End of Reionization`
  - **Question:** are luminous LAEs at `z~5.7-6.6` massive/evolved systems or young bursty Lyalpha leakers?
  - **Data:** `14` luminous LAEs, Magellan/M2FS spectroscopy with about `36 hr` integration, and JWST/NIRCam broad plus medium-band photometry.
  - **Method:** BAGPIPES SED fitting, F410M leverage on nebular/Balmer-break degeneracy, and measurement of Halpha, UV slopes, `xi_ion`, Lyalpha widths, velocity offsets, and Lyalpha escape fractions.
  - **Conclusion:** about half the sample has `M_*<10^8 M_sun`, ages below `10 Myr`, and low dust; all have `f_esc(Lyalpha)>0.2`; and dust content appears more predictive of Lyalpha suppression than line width alone.
  - **Position:** high priority for reionization because it supports luminous LAEs as efficient low-mass ionizing engines.

- [2605.09829](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09829) D. Burgarella et al. `Stardust Galaxies at z>9: A Dust-Origin Transition Behind the Excess of UV-Bright Galaxies`
  - **Question:** can a transition to low-opacity supernova dust explain UV-bright `z>9` galaxies without invoking dust-free ISM or extreme star-formation efficiency?
  - **Data:** JWST `z>9` UV-bright galaxy constraints, semi-empirical and semi-analytic intrinsic UV luminosity functions, and dust models for supernova-processed grains.
  - **Method:** combine supernova dust extinction laws, metallicity/dust-to-metal opacity scalings, porous or leaky geometries, and hybrid attenuation routes linking supernova-dominated and ISM grain-growth regimes.
  - **Conclusion:** an intrinsic far-UV dust opacity near `10^3 cm^2 g^-1` reproduces the low attenuation of many observed systems; the model suppresses mainly the brightest sources and can bring UVLFs closer to JWST without requiring extreme efficiencies.
  - **Position:** important because it offers a physical dust-composition explanation for the UV-bright galaxy excess.

- [2605.06782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06782) Shengdong Lu et al. `The galaxy ultraviolet luminosity function from z=7 to 15 in the COLIBRE simulations`
  - **Question:** do COLIBRE plus SKIRT radiative-transfer predictions match JWST UV luminosity functions from `z=7` to `z=15`?
  - **Data:** COLIBRE cosmological hydrodynamic simulations, SKIRT dust radiative transfer, and observed high-z UVLF compilations.
  - **Method:** derive dust-attenuated and intrinsic UV luminosities, fit Schechter functions, test redshift binning, photo-z scatter, Eddington bias, and dust assumptions.
  - **Conclusion:** COLIBRE matches stellar mass functions up to `z=12`, but dust-attenuated UVLFs are too faint at the bright end by about `1 mag` at `z=7` and `2.5 mag` at `z=15`; removing dust helps at `7<=z<=12` but not fully at `z=15`.
  - **Position:** keeps the high-z UV-bright tension alive and makes dust plus IMF/burstiness the key interpretation axis.

- [2605.13472](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13472) Connor Sant Fournier et al. `First Light And Reionization Epoch Simulations (FLARES) XXI: The UV Indices of Galaxies in the Early Universe`
  - **Question:** which rest-UV absorption indices reliably trace stellar metallicity in `5<=z<=10` galaxies?
  - **Data:** FLARES simulations with Synthesizer and BPASS spectra for high-redshift galaxies.
  - **Method:** measure UV indices including `1370 A`, `1400 A`, `1425 A`, `1533 A`, `1550 A`, `1719 A`, and `1853 A` and test sensitivity to SFH, IMF, nebular emission, and spectral resolution.
  - **Conclusion:** `1719 A` is the cleanest single metallicity tracer, while `1370 A`, `1400 A`, `1425 A`, `1533 A`, and `1550 A` are useful in combination; `1460 A` and `1501 A` are more burst-sensitive.
  - **Position:** practical JWST/NIRSpec tool for stellar metallicities in reionization-era galaxies.

- [2605.11064](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11064) Antonio Matteri et al. `Clustering constraints on super-early galaxy formation scenarios`
  - **Question:** can clustering distinguish models that all reproduce the high abundance of bright `z>10` JWST galaxies?
  - **Data:** Shin-Uchuu dark-matter-only halos at `z~11`, compared with current JWST clustering measurements.
  - **Method:** populate halos under attenuation-free, feedback-free burst, bursty, and primordial-black-hole scenarios and measure the UV-threshold-dependent bias.
  - **Conclusion:** faint samples near `M_UV~-16` all give `b~7`, but bright samples near `M_UV<-18` separate models; non-PBH scenarios rise toward `b~14` at `M_UV~-19`, while the PBH case stays near `b~7`.
  - **Position:** key LSS-facing diagnostic for the high-z galaxy abundance debate.

## AGN, LRDs, and Obscured Black-Hole Growth

- [2605.07976](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07976) Rosa M. Merida et al. `Testing the BH* Model: a UV-to-Optical Spectral Fitting of The Cliff`
  - **Question:** can the black-hole-star model reproduce the full JWST/NIRSpec PRISM spectrum of the LRD The Cliff at `z=3.55`?
  - **Data:** JWST/NIRSpec PRISM spectrum of The Cliff, with unresolved JWST imaging constraints.
  - **Method:** Bagpipes fitting with stellar, nebular, AGN, and blackbody components, testing host-dominated and AGN-leakage UV scenarios.
  - **Conclusion:** broad priors naturally yield a BH*-like solution; the host is low mass, `log(M_*/M_sun)~7.7`, star-forming, metal-poor, and dusty with `A_V~0.5`; weak AGN UV leakage is allowed but not uniquely required; and the inferred BH-to-stellar-mass ratio remains high relative to local scaling relations.
  - **Position:** one of the week's most relevant LRD papers because it tests a specific physical model against a flagship object rather than relying on phenomenology alone.

- [2605.14233](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14233) Kevin Park et al. `A new sample of Little Red Dots at z<0.45 in DESI DR1`
  - **Question:** do nearby LRD analogs exist, and are their spectra variable or AGN-like?
  - **Data:** DESI DR1 emission-line search yielding `8` analogs at `z=0.2-0.45`, with long-baseline light curves for `7/8`.
  - **Method:** spectral selection for broad Balmer and low-ionization features, compactness checks, variability tests, and volume-density estimation.
  - **Conclusion:** the analogs have broad Balmer lines, steep Balmer decrements, compact morphologies, weak high-ionization AGN lines, and little variability; their density is about `1.6e-9 Mpc^-3`, far below the early-universe LRD density.
  - **Position:** useful because it decouples some LRD spectral signatures from strictly high-redshift conditions while showing strong number-density evolution.

- [2605.06791](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06791) Matthew Stepney et al. `Hidden Monsters with SPHEREx I: A goldmine for heavily reddened quasars at cosmic noon`
  - **Question:** can SPHEREx spectrophotometry build a population-level sample of luminous heavily reddened quasars?
  - **Data:** bright `K_AB<18` red near-infrared quasar candidates, multi-wavelength photometry, and SPHEREx spectra.
  - **Method:** select `J-K` red AGN candidates, use SPHEREx spectrophotometry for confirmation and redshifts, and fit SEDs against blue quasars and Hot DOGs.
  - **Conclusion:** `77` new heavily reddened quasars are confirmed at `1.5<z<3.9`; this more than doubles the known luminous HRQ sample at `z>1.5`; `7` are at `z>3`; and HRQs are hot-dust poor relative to blue quasars at fixed optical luminosity.
  - **Position:** important for cosmic-noon obscured black-hole growth and feedback because it turns a rare population into a statistical sample.

- [2605.09078](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09078) Charalambia Varnava et al. `JWST observations and a model for the extremely luminous obscured quasar W2246-0526 at z=4.6`
  - **Question:** how should the SED of the hyperluminous obscured quasar W2246-0526 be decomposed when JWST/MIRI data are included?
  - **Data:** new JWST/MIRI-MRS data for W2246-0526 at `z=4.601`, plus multi-wavelength SED information.
  - **Method:** SMART radiative-transfer SED fitting with smooth and two-phase torus models and an added polar-dust component.
  - **Conclusion:** the inferred AGN fraction is `72-81%`, AGN luminosity is `4.2-7.2e14 L_sun`, polar dust luminosity is `1.6-1.7e14 L_sun`, and including polar dust changes AGN luminosity estimates.
  - **Position:** important for high-z obscured quasar energetics because polar dust can bias SED-based AGN and SFR estimates.

- [2605.13946](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13946) Ryan W. Pfeifle et al. `The Hunt for Red Dual AGNs I`
  - **Question:** how many spatially resolved mid-infrared dual AGNs can be found with WISE colors plus DeCaLS morphology?
  - **Data:** AllWISE, DeCaLS grz imaging, and optical spectroscopy from Gemini, Keck/Palomar, SOAR, SDSS, and DESI.
  - **Method:** select WISE AGN pairs, require projected separation `r_p<=130 kpc` and velocity consistency where available, then rank dual-AGN confidence with spectroscopy and BPT classifications.
  - **Conclusion:** `13` confirmed dual AGNs and `63` high-confidence candidates are found; more than half of confirmed/high-confidence candidates are separated by more than `50 kpc`; multi-merger environments are common.
  - **Position:** local-to-intermediate-redshift, but useful for obscured SMBH growth and merger-triggering selection functions.

- [2605.12141](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12141) Fabrice Martins et al. `Non-LTE atmosphere models of very luminous sources and their applicability to Little Red Dots`
  - **Question:** can dense expanding non-LTE atmospheres reproduce LRD-like spectra without a standard unobscured AGN?
  - **Data:** CMFGEN models at `L=10^10 L_sun`, with comparisons to stacked and individual LRD spectra.
  - **Method:** compute line-blanketed non-LTE expanding atmospheres and compare continuum, Balmer/Paschen emission, Balmer decrement, and metal lines.
  - **Conclusion:** the models reproduce many broad-line and continuum properties but struggle to produce both a genuine Balmer break and strong emission lines without dust or complex geometry.
  - **Position:** a useful counterpoint to BH* and low-z analog papers because it identifies what atmosphere models can and cannot explain.

## LSS, Surveys, and Inference Systematics

- [2605.11077](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11077) Jiamu Huang et al. `The Impact of Cosmic Variance and Satellites on JWST Clustering Measurements at Redshift around 6`
  - **Question:** how much do covariance and satellite treatment affect JWST/WFSS quasar-environment clustering at `z~6.6`?
  - **Data:** FLAMINGO-10k mocks matched to `25` ASPIRE JWST/NIRCam WFSS quasar fields.
  - **Method:** generate `1000` ASPIRE-like mocks, populate quasars and [O III] emitters with HOD thresholds, and compare full covariance likelihoods with diagonal Poisson errors.
  - **Conclusion:** Poisson errors understate correlation-function uncertainties by about a factor of `3`; halo-mass posteriors become `~1.5-3` times too narrow; and nominal Poisson `1 sigma` intervals contain the truth only about `20%` of the time.
  - **Position:** essential for any high-z environment measurement using small JWST fields.

- [2605.12503](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12503) Jui-Kuan Chan et al. `Unveiling Hidden Lyman Alpha Emitters in the DESI DR1 Data`
  - **Question:** can DESI DR1 spectra reveal LAEs that the standard pipeline misclassifies?
  - **Data:** DESI DR1 ELG spectra, training/validation/test samples of visually inspected LAEs and non-LAEs, and DESI-HETDEX checks.
  - **Method:** train a CNN over blue-arm spectra to detect Lyalpha and estimate line redshift, then apply it to about `2 million` DR1 galaxy spectra.
  - **Conclusion:** the model reaches `95.2%` purity and `95.9%` completeness and produces a catalog of `19,685` LAEs at `z~2-3.5`, with about `82%` lacking correct Redrock redshifts.
  - **Position:** immediately useful for LAE/LSS studies because it rescues a large hidden sample from survey-template failures.

- [2605.13967](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13967) Ely D. Kovetz et al. `When galaxies burst: enhanced shot-noise for line-intensity mapping in the JWST era`
  - **Question:** how does bursty star formation alter line-intensity-mapping shot noise?
  - **Data:** analytic burstiness model constrained by JWST-era UV/Halpha observations and clustering, applied to Halpha, [C II], CO, and UV tracers.
  - **Method:** propagate stochastic SFR histories through line response kernels and estimate shot-noise boost factors and cross-line correlations.
  - **Conclusion:** at `z~6`, boosts are about `B_Halpha~6.8`, `B_[CII]~3.6`, `B_CO~2.9`, and `B_UV~2.6`; Halpha boosts can reach `~12.5` by `z~10`.
  - **Position:** high-value LSS methods paper because burstiness changes both detectability and cosmological interpretation of LIM surveys.

- [2605.15096](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15096) Bozhidara Stoyanova et al. `Impact of stellar population models on the estimated physical properties of galaxies`
  - **Question:** how large are SPS-driven shifts in SED-inferred `M_*` and SFR?
  - **Data:** `17,230` ASTRODEEP-JWST galaxies with spectroscopic redshifts, fit with BC03, CB19, BPASS single, and BPASS binary templates.
  - **Method:** CIGALE fits to observed and synthetic catalogs, holding non-SPS assumptions fixed.
  - **Conclusion:** stellar masses shift by up to about `0.6 dex`, SFRs by up to about `0.4 dex`, and BC03/CB19 are much closer to each other than to BPASS.
  - **Position:** a core systematic for every high-z stellar-mass, SFR, and main-sequence claim in the week's reports.

- [2605.08342](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08342) Yuanye Lin et al. `Satellite Metallicity Enhancement I`
  - **Question:** what physical processes shape the radial gas-phase metallicity enhancement of satellite galaxies around massive clusters?
  - **Data:** DESI DR1 star-forming galaxies around massive clusters and EAGLE simulation comparisons.
  - **Method:** measure satellite metallicity enhancement versus projected cluster-centric distance, then decompose the profile with a non-equilibrium chemical-evolution model.
  - **Conclusion:** the profile has a central steep decline, a virial-radius plateau, and an extended downturn; enriched intracluster-medium inflow dominates the plateau, while mass loss and quenching shape the core behavior.
  - **Position:** strong environment paper because it connects DESI measurements, simulations, and chemical-evolution interpretation across cluster infall regions.

- [2605.10753](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10753) Han-Yue Guo et al. `Joint probabilistic inference of galaxy redshifts and rest-frame spectra from photometric fluxes with latent diffusion`
  - **Question:** can broadband photometry probabilistically infer both redshift PDFs and rest-frame spectra?
  - **Data:** DESI DR1 spectra for pretraining and broadband photometry for conditioning.
  - **Method:** pretrain a spender spectral autoencoder on `5 million` DESI spectra, then use a conditional diffusion model to infer redshift and spectral latent variables from photometry.
  - **Conclusion:** the model returns photometric-redshift PDFs with point estimates comparable to boosted trees and reconstructs rest-frame spectra whose residuals are near the observed-spectrum noise level for good-S/N cases.
  - **Position:** promising survey-inference infrastructure for LSST/Roman/CSST-scale photometric samples.

- [2605.09248](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09248) Yunting Wang et al. `How to Count Clustered Galaxies`
  - **Question:** how should confusion-limited submillimeter number counts be corrected when galaxies are clustered rather than Poisson-distributed?
  - **Data:** simulations plus deep Herschel-SPIRE GOODS-N maps at `250`, `350`, and `500 micron`.
  - **Method:** combine 1-point P(D) fluctuation analysis with 2-point clustering information to measure and correct clustering bias.
  - **Conclusion:** clustering inflates apparent `500 micron` counts by a factor of about `1.6` near `10 mJy` and mildly suppresses the faintest sub-mJy counts; effects are smaller at shorter SPIRE wavelengths.
  - **Position:** relevant to dusty-galaxy and background-fluctuation work, especially for future confusion-limited far-infrared/submillimeter surveys.

- [2605.14930](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14930) Rhys Jordan et al. `Identifying group galaxies merging with massive clusters using machine learning`
  - **Question:** can group galaxies in cluster infall regions be identified from projected observables?
  - **Data:** The Three Hundred plus MultiDark Planck 2 simulations, with `304` cluster regions and `300,781` galaxies.
  - **Method:** train a Random Forest on projected phase-space, neighbor, density, and stellar-mass features; compare with friends-of-friends and Gaussian Mixture Modelling.
  - **Conclusion:** projected group purity reaches `76%` with `30%` completeness, improving over classic group finders for the high-purity use case.
  - **Position:** useful for preprocessing and protocluster/cluster-infall studies.

- [2605.15048](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15048) M. Orte-Garcia et al. `The DESIRED electron temperature relations in star-forming regions of the local Universe`
  - **Question:** which Te-Te relations are safe when auroral-line coverage is incomplete?
  - **Data:** `699` DESIRED-E spectra with direct Te determinations across [N II], [O II], [O III], [S II], [S III], and [Ar III].
  - **Method:** recompute densities and temperatures with PyNeb and fit empirical Te relations with orthogonal distance regression.
  - **Conclusion:** [N II]-based relations are preferred for low-ionization temperatures; [S III]-[O III] and [N II]-[O III] calibrations are provided with validity limits.
  - **Position:** local calibration, but directly useful for JWST metallicity interpretation.
