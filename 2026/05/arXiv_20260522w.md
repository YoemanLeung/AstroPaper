## Executive View

The week has three dominant threads: Little Red Dots and black-hole growth, high-redshift galaxy formation and reionization diagnostics, and environment-dependent gas regulation from the CGM to clusters.

The read-first paper is [2605.20622](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20622) A. M. Sebastian. It connects high-redshift C IV and Mg II absorbers to MUSE LAEs in three `z~6` quasar fields, showing mild LAE overdensities and metal enrichment extending beyond individual virial radii. It is the week's strongest direct CGM/environment measurement in the core redshift range.

The Little Red Dot package is unusually coherent. [2605.15263](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15263) Albert Sneppen shows that LRD X-ray non-detections need both Compton-thick gas and intrinsically weak X-ray emission. [2605.16485](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16485) Ritik Sharma gives a halo-driven route to over-massive black holes. [2605.21589](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21589) Shinsuke Takasao adds a magnetized black-hole-envelope model, and [2605.21574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21574) Xiaojing Lin supplies a low-redshift DESI LRD comparison sample.

For high-redshift galaxies, [2605.15273](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15273) Leindert A. Boogaard reports a stellar bar in the gas-rich `z=4.055` disk GN20, while [2605.20121](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20121) Maya Steen turns galaxy PDLAs into a conditional reionization-topology probe. [2605.21599](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21599) Qiao Duan is the main data-release anchor, because JADES DR5 provides homogeneous physical-parameter posteriors for deep JWST fields.

The baryon-cycle and environment theme runs from [2605.16483](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16483) Andrew Pontzen and [2605.16488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16488) Hiranya V. Peiris on AGN-feedback entropy ceilings, through [2605.18987](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18987) Michael Messere on preventive dwarf-CGM regulation, to [2605.20590](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20590) Kazuki Daikuhara on mass-dependent star formation in the Spiderweb protocluster.

## LRDs, Quasars, and High-Redshift Black Holes

- [2605.15263](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15263) Albert Sneppen et al. `On the quenching of LRD X-ray emission by both Compton-thick gas and high accretion rates`
  - **Question:** why do JWST LRDs remain undetected in deep X-ray observations if they host accreting black holes?
  - **Data:**
    - Sirocco radiative-transfer models tuned to LRD optical and near-infrared spectra.
    - Chandra upper limits for LRD/AGN candidates.
  - **Method:**
    - Connect Balmer-break strength to gas column density.
    - Compute X-ray transmission through the same gas structures.
    - Compare normal broad-line AGN and high-accretion narrow-line Seyfert-like X-ray SEDs.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Obscuration alone is insufficient.
    - Viable models require columns near `N_H~10^25 cm^-2`, moderate metallicity, and weak intrinsic X-rays with `k_bol,X>=30`.
    - A normal broad-line AGN X-ray SED would still be too bright.
  - **Position:** the week's central LRD constraint because it converts non-detections into gas-column, metallicity, and accretion-state requirements.

- [2605.16485](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16485) Ritik Sharma et al. `Halo-driven Origin and Suppression of Over-massive Black Holes and Little Red Dots`
  - **Question:** can halo gravity temporarily create over-massive black holes before hot-halo suppression restores scaling relations?
  - **Data:**
    - Analytic accretion model in an NFW halo plus central black-hole potential.
    - Targets: JWST over-massive BH candidates, LRDs, and local scaling relations.
  - **Method:**
    - Model halo-enhanced cold gas inflow.
    - Follow the transition to hot, pressure-supported accretion suppression.
    - Compare with observed over-massive BH and LRD phenomenology.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Halo potential can drive an early phase of rapid BH growth.
    - Hot-halo formation can later suppress accretion and move systems back toward local host-BH relations.
    - LRDs can arise during the transitional, high-accretion stage.
  - **Position:** strong conceptual LRD companion to [2605.15263](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15263), because it links the accretion state to halo growth.

- [2605.21589](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21589) Shinsuke Takasao et al. `A Magnetized Black Hole Envelope Model for Little Red Dots`
  - **Question:** can a magnetized black-hole envelope explain LRD broad lines and X-ray weakness?
  - **Data:**
    - Model parameter space: `10^5-10^7 M_sun` black holes.
    - Observational targets: LRD broad Balmer lines and X-ray upper limits.
  - **Method:**
    - Model a rotating magnetized envelope around a rapidly accreting black hole.
    - Produce broad-line Doppler components from plasma clumps in the magnetosphere.
    - Estimate X-ray emission from shocks and coronae.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Broad components of a few thousand `km/s` can arise without standard virial BLR assumptions.
    - X-ray luminosities remain below `~10^41 erg/s`.
    - Local virial black-hole mass estimators may fail for LRDs.
  - **Position:** useful theory entry because it explains why LRD line widths need not map directly to standard BLR masses.

- [2605.21574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21574) Xiaojing Lin et al. `(LRDs)^2: The Low-ReDshift Little Red Dots Survey. II. DESI DR1 Sample`
  - **Question:** do low-redshift analogs of JWST LRDs exist?
  - **Data:**
    - DESI DR1: `27` LRDs at `z=0.2-0.9`.
    - Follow-up: near-infrared spectra for `18` objects.
  - **Method:**
    - Select compact red continuum objects.
    - Measure Balmer decrements, absorption, metallicity, BPT location, outflows, and variability.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The sample shares compact morphology, V-shaped continua, broad Balmer emission, and low metallicity with high-z LRDs.
    - Ionized [O III] outflows appear in `78%` of the sample.
    - Broad-line luminosity relations differ from local type-1 AGN calibrations.
  - **Position:** important because nearby LRDs make the high-z phenomenon testable with richer follow-up.

## High-Redshift Galaxies and Reionization

- [2605.15273](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15273) Leindert A. Boogaard et al. `A stellar bar in the gas-rich disk galaxy GN20 at z=4.055`
  - **Question:** can stellar bars exist in very gas-rich disks at early cosmic times?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: GN20 at `z=4.055`.
    - Observables: stellar structure and gas-rich disk constraints.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify a bar-like stellar feature.
    - Compare baryon and gas dominance against expectations for bar formation.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - GN20 is both baryon dominated and gas dominated.
    - The inferred baryon dominance is about `70+/-30%`.
    - Gas accounts for about `75+/-25%` of baryonic mass.
    - Early bars do not require gas-poor disks.
  - **Position:** important high-z morphology paper because it pushes secular structure formation into a gas-rich regime.

- [2605.20121](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20121) Maya Steen et al. `Galaxy Proximate Damped Lyman-Alpha Systems and H I Reionization Topology in TECHNICOLOR DAWN`
  - **Question:** can galaxy PDLAs trace H I reionization topology?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: TECHNICOLOR DAWN at `z=10`, `z=8`, `z=6`, and `z=5.5`.
    - Motivation: JWST foreground PDLA detections near `z>5` galaxies.
  - **Method:**
    - Track neutral fraction versus overdensity.
    - Measure foreground H I column density around halos.
    - Test dependence on halo mass, stellar mass, gas fraction, and global neutral fraction.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Reionization follows an inside-out-middle topology.
    - The CGM can remain significantly neutral at `z=5.5`.
    - DLA strength correlates more strongly with halo or stellar mass than with local neutral fraction alone.
    - PDLAs become reionization probes only if host mass is constrained.
  - **Position:** high-priority reionization paper because it turns a new JWST observable into a conditional topology diagnostic.

- [2605.18939](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18939) Aaron Smith et al. `The Lumina Project: CMB Optical Depth Fluctuations from Patchy Reionization`
  - **Question:** how much does patchiness bias CMB optical-depth estimates?
  - **Data:**
    - Lumina: `500 cMpc` radiation-hydrodynamic simulation with `2 x 6000^3` resolution elements.
    - Outputs: light cones tracking H II, He II, and He III.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare line-of-sight, volume-weighted, and mass-weighted optical depths.
    - Quantify fluctuations from patchy reionization.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Patchiness boosts the line-of-sight optical depth to `tau_LOS=0.0550`.
    - This is about `7%` above the volume-weighted estimate.
    - Mass-weighted estimates are closer to the light-cone result.
  - **Position:** useful reionization-simulation result because optical-depth interpretation depends on topology, not just the global ionized fraction.

- [2605.21599](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21599) Qiao Duan et al. `JWST Advanced Deep Extragalactic Survey (JADES) Data Release 5: stellar population catalogue for galaxies in GOODS-N and GOODS-S`
  - **Question:** what common physical-parameter baseline does JADES DR5 provide for deep JWST galaxy studies?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRCam and MIRI plus ancillary multi-wavelength imaging.
    - GOODS-N and GOODS-S.
    - Catalogue size: about `500,000` sources.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit SEDs with Prospector using flexible SFHs, dust, metallicity, nebular emission, and AGN/dust components.
    - Use an evolving main-sequence prior while retaining non-parametric flexibility.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Posterior summaries cover stellar masses, SFRs, SFHs, attenuation, metallicity, and AGN contributions.
    - About `350,000` galaxies at `z=1-9` benefit from the depth and wavelength coverage.
    - The catalogue supports statistical high-z galaxy growth and quenching studies.
  - **Position:** the week's main data-release anchor.

## CGM, Protoclusters, and Quasar ISM

- [2605.20622](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20622) A. M. Sebastian et al. `Connecting CGM enrichment with Lyman alpha emitters at 2.9 < z < 6.7`
  - **Question:** which faint galaxies are associated with C IV and Mg II absorbers at high redshift?
  - **Data:**
    - VLT/MUSE: three deep `z~6` quasar fields.
    - E-XQR-30: C IV and Mg II absorber catalogues.
    - Keck/NIRC2: near-IR imaging for stellar-mass limits.
  - **Method:**
    - Blindly detect LAEs at `2.9<z<6.7`.
    - Match LAEs and absorbers within `|Delta v|<1000 km/s` and `<250 pkpc`.
    - Compare absorber separations to virial radii and measure LAE overdensities.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The search detects `156` LAEs.
    - It finds `34` C IV and `14` Mg II galaxy-absorber associations.
    - No Mg II absorbers lie within LAE virial radii, while four C IV systems do.
    - LAE overdensity around absorbers is mild, with ratios of `1.7` for C IV and `1.9` for Mg II.
  - **Position:** the week's best direct CGM paper for the profile.

- [2605.20590](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20590) Kazuki Daikuhara et al. `Spider-Webb: enhanced star formation in low-mass galaxies within the Spiderweb protocluster revealed by JWST Pa beta narrow-band imaging`
  - **Question:** does the Spiderweb protocluster enhance star formation in low-mass galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRCam: F405N Pa beta and F410M continuum imaging.
    - Target: Spiderweb protocluster at `z=2.16`.
    - Sample: `40` Pa beta emitters after excluding the radio galaxy.
  - **Method:**
    - Select Pa beta emitters from narrow-band color excess.
    - Infer stellar masses, dust attenuation, and SFRs.
    - Compare against field Pa beta emitters.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Halpha cross-matching gives a protocluster membership fraction of `22/40`.
    - Emitters with `M_*<10^9 M_sun` have enhanced SFRs relative to the field.
    - Higher-mass emitters do not show the same significant offset.
  - **Position:** strong protocluster result because the environmental effect is mass-dependent.

- [2605.20698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20698) Fuxiang Xu et al. `CO(7-6) and [C I](2-1) survey in z > 6 quasars`
  - **Question:** what do CO, [C I], [C II], and dust reveal about molecular gas in `z~6` quasar hosts?
  - **Data:**
    - ALMA Band 3: `18` quasars at `z~6`.
    - Lines: CO(7-6), [C I](2-1), [C II], and dust continuum.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure line and continuum luminosities.
    - Estimate gas masses with multiple tracers.
    - Compare line ratios with PDR/XDR expectations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - CO(7-6) is detected in `15/18` systems.
    - [C I](2-1) is detected in `6/18` systems.
    - Many sources cannot be described by single-zone classical PDR heating.
    - Warm or highly excited molecular gas is common.
  - **Position:** key quasar-host ISM paper because it moves beyond [C II]-only gas estimates.

## AGN Feedback, Halo Gas, and Large-Scale Environment

- [2605.16483](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16483) Andrew Pontzen et al. `The limits of feedback from active galactic nuclei`
  - **Question:** why does AGN feedback remove gas from groups but stall in massive clusters?
  - **Data:**
    - FLAMINGO cosmological hydrodynamic simulations.
    - Halo range: group to cluster scales.
  - **Method:**
    - Track inflow, outflow, entropy generation, and radial energy transport.
    - Compare feedback variants and injection geometries.
    - Interpret the transition with an outflow entropy ceiling.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Fiducial outflows reach entropy ceilings around `360-370 keV cm^2`.
    - Outflows escape below `M_200m~10^13.7 M_sun`.
    - Above the transition, virial inflow entropy is too high and AGN-driven gas stalls.
  - **Position:** core baryon-cycle paper because it makes group/cluster gas fractions an entropy-threshold problem.

- [2605.16488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16488) Hiranya V. Peiris et al. `Thermostats, Not Engines: A New Picture of Halo Gas Regulation`
  - **Question:** should black holes be interpreted as halo-gas thermostats rather than engines?
  - **Data:**
    - FLAMINGO feedback variants, including thermal and jet implementations.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare outflow entropy with halo-mass-dependent inflow entropy.
    - Interpret feedback geometry and reincorporation through entropy.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Thermal feedback creates a nearly mass-independent entropy ceiling.
    - Jet geometry changes the effective heated solid angle.
    - Massive halos can regain fuel when virial entropy exceeds the feedback ceiling.
  - **Position:** best read with [2605.16483](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16483); it supplies the conceptual language for halo gas regulation.

- [2605.18987](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18987) Michael Messere et al. `Dwarf-galaxy CGM regulation by high-specific-energy supernova outflows`
  - **Question:** how do high-specific-energy outflows regulate dwarf-galaxy CGM baryons?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations of dwarf-galaxy outflows and CGM cycling.
  - **Method:**
    - Separate hot energy-carrying gas from warm mass-carrying gas.
    - Track long-term CGM baryon fractions and prevented accretion.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Hot outflows regulate by preventing later accretion, not only by ejecting gas already inside the halo.
    - After `~5 Gyr`, the CGM baryon fraction can fall to `~0.1`.
    - About `75%` of expected baryon accretion can be prevented.
  - **Position:** important CGM modeling result because it reframes dwarf feedback as preventive.

- [2605.18963](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18963) Caitlin Igel et al. `Galaxy structure and environment with HSC`
  - **Question:** how much does environment affect galaxy structure at fixed stellar mass?
  - **Data:**
    - HSC: about three million galaxies.
    - Environment measures across cluster and large-scale regimes.
  - **Method:**
    - Control for stellar mass.
    - Test structural dependence on environment and redshift.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Structure depends on environment at `>5 sigma`.
    - Cluster-scale effects dominate at `z<0.5`.
    - Larger-scale effects appear for massive galaxies at `z>=0.5`.
  - **Position:** useful wide-field environment benchmark for connecting structure to LSS.
