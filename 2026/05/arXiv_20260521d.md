## Executive View

The read-first paper is [2605.20622](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20622) A. M. Sebastian. It links high-redshift metal absorbers to Lyman-alpha emitters in three deep `z~6` quasar fields. The useful result is not a single host identification but a CGM topology statement: C IV absorbers often sit near faint LAEs, Mg II has lower covering fraction within LAE virial radii, and the LAE overdensity around absorbers is mild rather than extreme.

The strongest protocluster paper is [2605.20590](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20590) Kazuki Daikuhara. JWST/NIRCam Pa beta imaging of the Spiderweb protocluster at `z=2.16` shows enhanced star formation in low-mass emitters with `M_*<10^9 M_sun`, while more massive emitters remain consistent with the field. The mass dependence makes this a clean environment result rather than a generic protocluster starburst claim.

For cosmic-dawn quasar host galaxies, [2605.20698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20698) Fuxiang Xu is the main ISM paper. ALMA detects CO(7-6) in `15/18` `z~6` quasars and [C I](2-1) in `6/18`, then combines CO, [C I], [C II], and dust gas tracers. Many systems cannot be described by classical PDR heating alone, implying a large warm or highly excited molecular component.

The JWST dwarf-galaxy metallicity result is [2605.20810](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20810) Xianlong He. NGDEEP NIRISS extends the cosmic-noon MZR to `log(M_*/M_sun)=6.3` and finds a roughly constant low-mass slope near `beta~0.24`, while adding SFR does not reduce the scatter enough to establish an FMR. This is a useful calibration anchor for interpreting low-mass JWST samples.

The LSS and dark-matter side has three strong entries: [2605.21212](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21212) Aristeidis Amvrosiadis detects a lensing subhalo in ALMA continuum data; [2605.20393](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20393) Lucas Gabriel-Silva builds a photometric splashback mass-function framework; and [2605.20352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20352) Marta Reina-Campos shows that bright globular clusters in Abell 2744 correlate more strongly with mass maps than with other galactic components.

## High-Redshift CGM, Protoclusters, and Quasar ISM

- [2605.20622](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20622) A. M. Sebastian et al. `Connecting CGM enrichment with Lyman alpha emitters at 2.9 < z < 6.7`
  - **Question:** which faint galaxies are associated with high-redshift C IV and Mg II absorbers, and do metal absorbers mark galaxy overdensities?
  - **Data:**
    - VLT/MUSE: three deep archival `z~6` quasar fields, searched blindly for LAEs at `2.9<z<6.7`.
    - E-XQR-30: C IV and Mg II absorber catalogues along the same quasar sightlines.
    - Keck/NIRC2: near-IR imaging used to place stellar-mass upper limits on LAEs in the quasar fields.
  - **Method:**
    - Detect LAEs in MUSE datacubes with LSDCat and classify candidate lines with QtClassify.
    - Measure Lyman-alpha fluxes with pyPlatefit and account for the Lyman-alpha velocity offset when matching to absorbers.
    - Define galaxy-absorber associations with `|Delta v|<1000 km/s` and impact parameter `<250 pkpc`.
    - Estimate stellar-mass limits from NIRC2 non-detections and compare absorber separations to inferred virial radii.
    - Measure LAE overdensity ratios around metal absorbers by comparing real absorber redshifts with random-redshift counts.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The MUSE search detects `156` LAEs with `log L[erg/s]=41.3-43.2`.
    - C IV is linked to more LAE associations than Mg II, with `34` C IV associations and `14` Mg II associations under the adopted matching window.
    - Mg II has low covering fraction near the detected LAEs because no Mg II absorbers fall within LAE virial radii, while four C IV absorbers do.
    - Most associated absorbers sit at large normalized separations, often around or beyond `2 R_vir`, so metal enrichment extends beyond the immediate CGM of individual faint galaxies.
    - LAE overdensity around absorbers is mild, with ratios of `1.7` for C IV and `1.9` for Mg II, and the authors defer robust field-to-field constraints to REQUIEM.
  - **Position:** high-priority CGM paper because it connects high-redshift metal absorption to faint LAE environments rather than requiring every absorber to have a single luminous host.

- [2605.20590](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20590) Kazuki Daikuhara et al. `Spider-Webb: enhanced star formation in low-mass galaxies within the Spiderweb protocluster revealed by JWST Pa beta narrow-band imaging`
  - **Question:** does the Spiderweb protocluster enhance star formation in low-mass galaxies at cosmic noon?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRCam: Cycle 1 GO 1572 Spider-Webb imaging with F405N targeting Pa beta at `z=2.16` and F410M sampling the adjacent continuum.
    - Photometry: JWST F405N, F410M, F115W, F182M plus HST ACS/WFC and other ancillary imaging for SED fitting.
    - Comparison sample: JWST Emission Line Survey field Pa beta emitters selected with the same type of narrow-band technique.
  - **Method:**
    - Select Pa beta emitters from F410M-F405N color excess with an observed equivalent-width limit of about `20 Angstrom`.
    - Derive stellar masses and dust attenuation with CIGALE using a six-module SED setup.
    - Convert F405N-F410M line excess into Pa beta luminosity and SFR using `SFR_Pa beta = 8.48e-41 L_Pa beta,corr`.
    - Compare dust-corrected and dust-uncorrected SFR-M_* relations for Spiderweb and field emitters.
    - Check Halpha counterparts from existing narrow-band data to estimate protocluster membership.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The working sample contains `40` Pa beta emitters after excluding the Spiderweb radio galaxy.
    - The Halpha cross-match gives a membership fraction of `22/40`, or `0.55`, for the Pa beta emitter sample.
    - Low-mass emitters with `M_*<10^9 M_sun` have enhanced SFRs relative to field counterparts, and the excess remains without dust corrections.
    - Intermediate- and high-mass emitters with `M_*>10^9 M_sun` do not show the same significant offset from the field.
    - Starbursting low-mass galaxies are not concentrated only in the protocluster core, so mergers, interactions, and efficient gas supply across the structure are plausible drivers.
  - **Position:** strong protocluster paper because it uses a dust-insensitive recombination line to isolate a mass-dependent environmental effect in a well-studied `z=2.16` structure.

- [2605.20698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20698) Fuxiang Xu et al. `CO(7-6) and [C I](2-1) survey in z > 6 quasars`
  - **Question:** what are the molecular gas masses and excitation conditions in luminous quasar host galaxies at `z~6`?
  - **Data:**
    - ALMA: Band 3 observations of redshifted CO(7-6), [C I](2-1), and 3 mm continuum in `18` quasars at `z~6`.
    - ALMA program: 2019.1.00147.S, observed between 2019 October and 2020 March with `43-48` 12 m antennas.
    - Literature tracers: [C II] and far-IR luminosities used alongside the new CO, [C I], and dust measurements.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit line profiles and continuum maps to measure CO(7-6), [C I](2-1), and dust-continuum luminosities.
    - Estimate molecular gas masses separately from CO(7-6), [C I], [C II], and dust.
    - Use a hierarchical Bayesian cross-calibration model to infer consistent gas masses and conversion factors across the four tracers.
    - Compare `L_[CII]/L_[CI]`, `L_CO(7-6)/L_TIR`, and CO/[C I] ratios with PDR and XDR model grids.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - CO(7-6) is detected in `15/18` quasars, [C I](2-1) in `6/18`, and 3 mm continuum in `13/18`.
    - The four-tracer gas-mass analysis gives a self-consistent framework for using CO, [C I], [C II], and dust in high-redshift quasar hosts.
    - Only a subset of sources is compatible with pure PDR conditions, typically requiring dense gas with `n>10^4 cm^-3` and radiation fields of `G_0~10^3-10^4`.
    - Many quasars fall outside the classical PDR/XDR parameter space, so single-zone radiative heating does not fully describe their molecular ISM.
    - Elevated CO(7-6)/[C I] ratios indicate that a large fraction of the molecular gas is warm or highly excited.
  - **Position:** important cosmic-dawn quasar-ISM paper because it moves beyond [C II]-only gas estimates and directly tests whether PDR models can explain the warm molecular phase.

## JWST Dwarf Galaxies, Metallicity, and Black-Hole Seeds

- [2605.20810](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20810) Xianlong He et al. `Revisiting the mass metallicity relation and the fundamental metallicity relation of dwarf galaxies at cosmic noon with NIRISS`
  - **Question:** does the cosmic-noon mass-metallicity relation keep the same low-mass slope, and is there a measurable SFR dependence among dwarf galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRISS: NGDEEP wide-field slitless spectroscopy over `1.1<z<3.4`.
    - Sample: `183` galaxies with `log(M_*/M_sun)=6.3-10.2`.
    - Comparison: previous GLASS-JWST NIRISS sample of `50` galaxies, combined with NGDEEP for the largest NIRISS dwarf-galaxy sample to date.
  - **Method:**
    - Reduce NIRISS WFSS spectra with Grism Redshift and Line Analysis software and measure rest-frame optical emission lines.
    - Infer gas-phase metallicities from stacked spectra and metallicity calibrations.
    - Fit the MZR in stellar-mass bins and test redshift-bin dependence.
    - Test an FMR by adding SFR as a second parameter and checking whether metallicity scatter decreases.
    - Propagate systematic uncertainties from metallicity calibration, stellar masses, and SFR indicators.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The NIRISS sample extends the MZR to extremely low masses at cosmic noon, down to `log(M_*/M_sun)=6.3`.
    - The observed MZR slope stays near `beta=0.24+-0.03` across almost four dex in stellar mass.
    - At `M_*<10^8 M_sun`, the metal-loading factor becomes less dominant than gas fraction in regulating the MZR slope.
    - Adding SFR does not provide robust evidence for an FMR and does not reduce the metallicity scatter enough to establish a secondary SFR dependence.
    - The MZR slope remains robust near `beta~0.22` across tested assumptions about metallicity, stellar mass, and SFR.
  - **Position:** useful JWST calibration paper because it anchors low-mass metallicity evolution in the exact mass regime where high-redshift dwarf samples now dominate.

- [2605.20381](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20381) Viola Bocchi et al. `Formation of intermediate-mass black holes in young massive clusters detected with JWST: analytic mass estimates`
  - **Question:** can the dense high-redshift stellar systems detected by JWST form IMBH seeds through runaway stellar collisions?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST systems: the Cosmic Gems arc at `z~10.2` and Firefly Sparkle at `z~8.3`.
    - Cluster properties: stellar-system masses of `10^5-10^7 M_sun` and half-mass radii of `~0.4-15 pc`.
    - Model calibration: Fokker-Planck and analytic runaway-collision framework validated against direct N-body simulations of compact clusters.
  - **Method:**
    - Estimate core collapse and runaway-collision conditions for observed cluster masses and radii.
    - Compute final black-hole seed masses after stellar wind mass loss.
    - Vary metallicity to test how wind losses change the retained seed mass.
    - Compare predicted efficiencies with direct N-body expectations and high-redshift heavy-seed requirements.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Typical predicted IMBH masses range from `~10^2 M_sun` to `~4 x 10^3 M_sun`.
    - Formation efficiencies are at the few-percent level when expressed as seed mass divided by cluster mass.
    - Cosmic Gems compact clusters with `R_h~1 pc` can produce seed masses of `1600-2700 M_sun`.
    - Low metallicity, especially `Z<=0.02 Z_sun`, is critical because weaker winds preserve more runaway-collision mass.
    - Dense metal-poor clusters are viable heavy-seed channels for early SMBH growth, but the result remains an analytic estimate rather than a direct detection.
  - **Position:** relevant high-redshift black-hole seed paper because it ties JWST compact-cluster measurements directly to IMBH formation efficiencies and early SMBH timing constraints.

## Clusters, Dark Matter, and LSS

- [2605.20323](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20323) Yutaka Fujita et al. `Suppression of Radiative Cooling in Galaxy Cluster Cores by the Combination of AGN Heating and Sloshing`
  - **Question:** how do sloshing motions and AGN heating combine to regulate cooling flows in cool-core clusters?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: three-dimensional hydrodynamic models of galaxy-cluster cool cores evolved from isothermal initial conditions.
    - Sloshing setup: simple waves with amplitudes `alpha=0`, `0.15`, and `0.3` times the sound speed.
    - Wavelengths: `lambda=200`, `1000`, and `2000 kpc`.
    - Runtime: cluster cores evolved to `t=8 Gyr`.
  - **Method:**
    - Model AGN heating as thermal energy input that mimics cosmic-ray heating.
    - Compare simulations with and without AGN heating across the sloshing amplitude and wavelength grid.
    - Track gas cooling, dense-core displacement, temperature structure, and velocity-temperature correlations.
    - Interpret the results in the context of XRISM velocity and temperature maps of cool-core clusters.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Sloshing alone suppresses cooling but cannot stop it unless the core is strongly disrupted.
    - Longer-wavelength sloshing drives deeper mixing and stronger cooling suppression.
    - Sloshing can make cooler gas move faster than hotter gas, matching behavior now observed in some XRISM cluster data.
    - AGN heating efficiently heats dense central gas and can substantially delay or prevent a cooling flow.
    - Intermediate wavelengths can move the densest gas away from the AGN heating zone, weakening feedback and increasing net cooling relative to the no-sloshing case.
  - **Position:** useful ICM/AGN-feedback paper because it gives a concrete dynamical caveat for interpreting XRISM cluster-core turbulence as simple heating support.

- [2605.20352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20352) Marta Reina-Campos et al. `On the correlation between globular clusters and the distribution of dark matter in galaxy clusters: the case of Abell 2744`
  - **Question:** can globular-cluster positions provide an independent tracer of galaxy-cluster mass structure?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: Abell 2744, a massive interacting galaxy cluster.
    - Tracer population: bright globular clusters distributed within the inner `40%` of the cluster virial radius.
    - Comparison maps: predicted cluster mass maps and maps of other galactic components.
  - **Method:**
    - Model the spatial distribution of globular clusters as an inhomogeneous spatial Poisson point process.
    - Compare GC positions against candidate tracer maps with rank-correlation statistics.
    - Separate bright blue GCs from other GC populations to test which subset best matches the mass field.
    - Release the statistical method in a public repository for application to other cluster GC catalogues.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Bright GCs roughly trace the three main interacting mass clumps in Abell 2744.
    - GC populations correlate more strongly with predicted mass maps than with other galactic components, with Spearman coefficients `>0.7`.
    - Bright blue GCs are compatible with the mass map derived solely from weak lensing.
    - GC catalogues can provide independent cluster-mass information at a level of detail comparable to weak-lensing maps.
  - **Position:** useful cluster-structure paper because it turns intracluster stellar-population data into a quantitative mass-tracing tool for merging systems.

- [2605.21212](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21212) Aristeidis Amvrosiadis et al. `Detection of a dark matter subhalo in the strongly lensed system PJ011646`
  - **Question:** can high-resolution ALMA continuum lensing identify dark-matter substructure while controlling macromodel systematics?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: strongly lensed dusty galaxy PJ011646, with lens at `z=0.555` and source at `z=2.125`.
    - Imaging: ALMA Band 7 dust-continuum data at about `0.1 arcsec` resolution.
    - Supporting data: HST F160W imaging for lens-light context.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit an elliptical power-law macromodel with external shear.
    - Add third- and fourth-order multipoles to absorb realistic angular structure in the lens potential.
    - Search the image plane with an NFW subhalo grid and evaluate evidence gains relative to the best macromodel.
    - Test whether a generalized NFW profile changes the inferred perturber and use non-detection cells to estimate upper limits.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Multipole terms improve the macromodel with `Delta ln Z=52.1`, so angular structure must be included before interpreting subhalo residuals.
    - A physical subhalo still improves the fit with `Delta ln Z>10` and is preferred at about `5.8 sigma`.
    - The inferred subhalo has `M_200=2.78e10 M_sun` and `c_200~30`, consistent with a stripped NFW subhalo.
    - The projected mass inside `2 kpc` is tightly constrained at `3.57e9 M_sun`.
    - Sensitive arc regions could detect subhalos down to `M_200~8e8 M_sun` at `3 sigma`.
  - **Position:** high-value dark-matter lensing paper because it demonstrates ALMA continuum sensitivity to the subhalo mass scale where CDM and WDM population tests become interesting.

- [2605.20393](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20393) Lucas Gabriel-Silva et al. `The Splashback Mass Function of Galaxy Clusters from Photometric Data`
  - **Question:** can splashback radii and splashback masses be measured for large photometric cluster samples well enough to build an observational mass function?
  - **Data:**
    - Calibration data: SDSS photometry and `499` clusters from the CoMaLit weak-lensing compilation.
    - Application sample: redMaPPer clusters in the SDSS North Galactic Cap.
    - Output catalog: splashback masses for more than `1.5e4` systems.
  - **Method:**
    - Assign probabilistic cluster membership from projected radius and photometric redshift information.
    - Use an adaptive probability cut to build galaxy-density profiles around individual clusters.
    - Estimate splashback radii and recalibrate the `M_sp-R_sp` relation over `0.01<z<0.8`.
    - Convert the resulting photometric splashback masses into an observational splashback mass function.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The median cluster boundary is `R_sp/R_200m~1.1`.
    - The recalibrated `M_sp-R_sp` relation has a slope shallower than the constant-density expectation and shows no significant redshift evolution.
    - The high-mass end of the photometric splashback mass function agrees with simulations.
    - Low-mass deviations are consistent with sample completeness limits rather than a clear cosmological discrepancy.
    - Photometric data alone can support large splashback-mass catalogs when membership and boundary calibration are handled probabilistically.
  - **Position:** useful LSS infrastructure paper because it converts a physically motivated cluster boundary into a scalable photometric mass-function observable.

## JWST Morphology and Size Systematics

- [2605.20887](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20887) Mingen Xin et al. `Difference Between Half-mass Radius and Half-light Radius of Galaxies at 0.2 < z < 2.5 Revealed by JWST/NIRCam Data`
  - **Question:** how different are galaxy half-light and half-mass radii when measured with JWST/NIRCam, and how does that affect size-evolution inferences?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRCam: imaging in CANDELS fields.
    - Sample: `14,333` galaxies with `M_*>10^9 M_sun`.
    - Redshift range: `0.2<z<2.5`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure half-light radii and half-mass radii for quiescent and star-forming galaxies.
    - Compare `r_e,mass/r_e,light` as a function of stellar mass, redshift, rest-frame color, and galaxy type.
    - Refit size-mass relations using both light-based and mass-based radii.
    - Track the redshift evolution of half-mass sizes at fixed stellar mass.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Half-light radii are generally larger than half-mass radii for both quiescent and star-forming galaxies.
    - The difference correlates with stellar mass, `r_e,light`, and rest-frame `U-V` color.
    - For star-forming galaxies, `r_e,mass/r_e,light` increases at `z>1.7`; quiescent galaxies do not show the same clear high-z increase.
    - The size-mass slope measured from half-light radii is about `0.1-0.3 dex` larger than the slope measured from half-mass radii.
    - At fixed mass, quiescent-galaxy half-mass radii grow by a factor of `~3-5` from `z~2.5` to `z~0.2`, while star-forming galaxies grow by about `~2`.
  - **Position:** useful JWST morphology paper because it quantifies how color and stellar-population gradients bias structural evolution when size is measured from light rather than mass.
