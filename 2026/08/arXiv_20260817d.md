## Executive View

The read-first item is the Little Red Dot spectroscopy paper. [2608.14534](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14534) Yuta Kageura uses JWST/NIRSpec and VLT/MUSE spectroscopy to measure Lyalpha profiles in LRDs at `z=3-9`. The sample finds Lyalpha emission in both MUSE and JWST LRDs, but broad Lyalpha is rare rather than ubiquitous.

The second high-priority item is early black-hole seeding. [2608.13656](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13656) Ryan Hazlett uses cosmological zoom simulations to test heavy black-hole seed formation under moderate Lyman-Werner backgrounds. The key result is that halos in the intermediate background regime may be common enough to matter if sustained high accretion extends below the classical direct-collapse threshold.

The methods set is worth keeping. [2608.13633](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13633) Juan Cruz-Martinez frames model misspecification as a central risk for simulation-trained machine learning in astronomy and physics. [2608.14101](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14101) David C. Flynn is lower priority scientifically but useful as a reproducible benchmark around rotation-curve reconstruction.

The galaxy-evolution set is broad and mostly lower redshift. [2608.13718](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13718) Patricia Fofie studies how AGN feedback suppresses late disk formation in FIRE-2 Milky-Way-mass halos. [2608.13996](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13996) Guangquan Zeng and [2608.14012](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14012) T. H. Rutherford give complementary morphology and merger-heating diagnostics from simulations and SAMI Schwarzschild modeling.

The gas and AGN entries are useful background rather than direct high-z structure papers. [2608.14473](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14473) Han Zhao catalogs OH emission and absorption in southern IRAS galaxies. [2608.14415](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14415) Yuusuke Uchida uses XRISM Resolve to measure sloshing-driven gas motions in Abell 2029. [2608.13641](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13641) Katherine L. Gonglewski models hierarchical stellar-mass black-hole mergers in AGN disks.

## Little Red Dots, Lyalpha, and Early Black-Hole Seeds

- [2608.14534](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14534) Yuta Kageura et al. `ATLAS. IV. A JWST+MUSE Demographic Study of Lyalpha Profiles in Little Red Dots`
  - **Question:** how common are narrow and broad Lyalpha components in Little Red Dots at `z=3-9`?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `8` LRDs observed in VLT/MUSE Deep and Wide surveys.
    - Sample: `23` LRDs observed with JWST/NIRSpec grating spectroscopy from JADES, CANUCS, and GO programs including SPURS.
    - Spectral resolving power: `R~1000-4000`.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify Lyalpha emission in the MUSE and JWST LRD samples.
    - Separate broad and narrow Lyalpha components using a `FWHM=1000 km s^-1` threshold.
    - Compare broad-Lyalpha incidence against high-redshift star-forming galaxies.
    - Re-examine stacked LRD spectra after excluding individually broad-Lyalpha emitters.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Lyalpha emission is detected in `5` MUSE LRDs and `9` JWST LRDs.
    - Only `2` LRDs show broad Lyalpha with `FWHM>1000 km s^-1`.
    - The remaining Lyalpha emitters are narrow, mostly with `FWHM=300-600 km s^-1`.
    - The broad-Lyalpha fraction above `L_Lyalpha,broad=10^42 erg s^-1` is `0.10(+0.12/-0.07)`, about five times the `2 sigma` upper limit of `<0.02` for high-redshift star-forming galaxies.
    - Broad Lyalpha is not ubiquitous in LRDs and may mark a rare evolutionary stage with strong outflows or unusual physical conditions.
  - **Position:** read first because it directly tests the LRD-AGN interpretation with Lyalpha kinematics rather than only continuum colors or broad Balmer lines.

- [2608.13656](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13656) Ryan Hazlett et al. `Abundant Heavy Black Hole Seeds from Moderate Lyman-Werner Radiation`
  - **Question:** can moderate Lyman-Werner backgrounds produce abundant heavy black-hole seeds in pristine atomic-cooling halos?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: `65` high-resolution cosmological zoom-ins.
    - Targets: `15` pristine halos spanning different Lyman-Werner backgrounds and assembly histories.
    - Regime: atomic-cooling halos with large gas inflow rates.
  - **Method:**
    - Estimate final Population III stellar mass from radial gas inflow profiles at runaway collapse.
    - Validate the estimator against simulations that explicitly follow protostellar accretion with sink particles.
    - Compare gas inflow rates across Lyman-Werner radiation field strengths and halo assembly timescales.
    - Apply a semi-analytic model to cosmological volumes.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The stellar-mass estimator reproduces protostellar masses to within a factor of `~2`.
    - Halos exposed to `J_21>=10` frequently sustain inflow rates above the supermassive-star threshold.
    - Estimated stellar masses reach up to `10^5 M_sun`.
    - Halo assembly timescale shows no statistically significant correlation with predicted stellar mass across assembly rates of `0.01-7 M_sun yr^-1`.
    - Intermediate Lyman-Werner backgrounds of `1<=J_21<10` are much more common than the high-background tail.
  - **Position:** high-priority seed-formation paper because it moves the heavy-seed question from rare extreme radiation fields toward more common early-universe environments.

## AGN, Compact Objects, and Accretion Environments

- [2608.13641](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13641) Katherine L. Gonglewski et al. `Orbital Migration of Interacting Stellar Mass Black Holes in Disks around Supermassive Black Holes. III. Mass Distribution of Hierarchical Mergers`
  - **Question:** what black-hole mass function is produced by repeated stellar-mass black-hole mergers in AGN disks?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: `360` N-body runs with analytic migration torques and gas forces.
    - Environment: migration-trap regions in disks around supermassive black holes.
    - Observable comparison: LVK black-hole mass functions and merger rates.
  - **Method:**
    - Vary the initial black-hole mass function and binary merger criteria.
    - Track convergence near migration traps.
    - Measure the post-merger mass distribution and intermediate-mass black-hole production.
    - Estimate merger rates for migration-trap-aided binary black-hole mergers.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Frequent mergers deplete black holes with masses `<=10 M_sun`.
    - Hierarchical mergers fill the upper mass gap with a roughly uniform `40-100 M_sun` distribution.
    - The model gives an average merger rate of `~6 Gpc^-3 yr^-1`.
    - About `40%` of mergers have uneven mass ratios, and `16%` have primary masses in `50-100 M_sun`.
    - A separate intermediate-mass black-hole population above `200 M_sun` forms after `~2 Myr`.
  - **Position:** useful AGN-disk channel paper, with relevance to black-hole growth in dense gaseous nuclei rather than to high-z galaxy structure directly.

- [2608.13648](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13648) Lumen Boco et al. `A binary black hole merger rate comparison within the same metallicity - star formation rate framework`
  - **Question:** do binary-population-synthesis models overpredict local BBH merger rates when placed in a common metallicity-SFR framework?
  - **Data:**
    - Inputs: multiple BBH merger-efficiency models from population synthesis.
    - Framework: observationally motivated metallicity distribution tied to star-formation rate density.
    - Constraint: observed local BBH merger-rate density.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine several population-synthesis models with the same metallicity-SFR assumptions.
    - Explore metallicity-distribution variations.
    - Compare predicted local BBH rates against observed credible intervals.
    - Identify which galaxy-metallicity components drive the tension.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - In `14` of `18` metallicity variations, no model predicts a local BBH merger rate within or below the observed `90%` credible interval.
    - Even in the four most favorable variations, only `<=10%` of models are consistent with observational constraints.
    - The discrepancy is driven by the low-metallicity tail from low-mass galaxies and starbursts.
    - Stronger natal kicks and nonstandard mass-transfer or common-envelope treatments are the most promising ways to reduce the tension.
  - **Position:** relevant mainly as a metallicity-distribution caution for linking galaxy populations to compact-object rates.

## Galaxy Morphology, Feedback, and Merger Heating

- [2608.13718](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13718) Patricia Fofie et al. `Old Disks Die Hard: How Does AGN Feedback Suppress Disk Formation in Milky Way Mass Galaxies?`
  - **Question:** when and how does AGN feedback suppress disk formation in Milky-Way-mass galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: two Milky-Way-mass FIRE-2 halos.
    - Variants: no-AGN runs plus up to three AGN-feedback implementations.
    - Endpoint: galaxy morphology and star formation at `z=0`.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare star formation histories and morphology across no-AGN and AGN runs.
    - Measure spheroid fractions and thin-disk mass fractions.
    - Identify when AGN feedback becomes effective relative to disk spin-up.
    - Separate early spheroid assembly from late thin-disk formation.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - No-AGN runs form prominent thin star-forming disks at `z=0`.
    - AGN runs have less late-time star formation, higher spheroid fractions, and lower thin-disk mass fractions.
    - Some AGN runs form quenched lenticular galaxies with no cool gas at `z=0`.
    - AGN feedback becomes effective just after disk formation begins, preferentially suppressing late thin-disk stars.
  - **Position:** useful morphology-feedback test because it isolates timing as the key variable in whether disks survive.

- [2608.13996](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13996) Guangquan Zeng et al. `How mergers shape galaxy morphology in the IllustrisTNG simulation`
  - **Question:** which merger properties determine whether a remnant becomes disk- or dispersion-dominated?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: IllustrisTNG.
    - Sample: galaxy mergers across broad stellar-mass and merger-mass-ratio ranges.
    - Diagnostics: collision angle, cold gas fraction, and pre-merger stellar bulge-to-total ratio.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure post-merger morphology across merger configurations.
    - Compare spiral-in and head-on collisions.
    - Track gas settling and star formation in newly formed disks.
    - Interpret remnant morphology as a function of orbital geometry, gas content, and initial structure.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Post-merger morphology is mainly controlled by collision angle, cold gas fraction, and pre-merger morphology.
    - Spiral-in mergers with large collision angle can increase rotational support.
    - Gas-rich systems can form extended disks and strengthen disk-dominated remnants.
    - Head-on mergers with small collision angle tend to disrupt ordered motion and form dispersion-supported remnants.
  - **Position:** useful simulation paper for interpreting high-z disturbed morphologies without assuming every merger destroys a disk.

- [2608.14012](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14012) T. H. Rutherford et al. `The SAMI Galaxy Survey: Linking Tidal Features and Orbit Populations Using Schwarzschild Modelling`
  - **Question:** how do tidal features map onto stellar orbit populations in massive galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: SAMI Galaxy Survey.
    - Sample: massive galaxies with `log(M/M_sun)>10`.
    - Imaging: deep KiDS photometry.
    - Modeling: DYNAMITE Schwarzschild orbit-superposition models.
  - **Method:**
    - Reproduce galaxy luminosity distributions with orbit-superposition models.
    - Measure hot, cold, warm, and counter-rotating orbit fractions.
    - Control for stellar mass and environment.
    - Compare shell-merger systems against the full sample.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Hot plus counter-rotating orbit fractions correlate most strongly with the spin proxy `lambda_Re`.
    - Hot and cold orbit fractions correlate with stellar age after controlling for stellar mass and environment.
    - Young galaxies with shell features have lower `lambda_Re` because they have excess hot orbits and deficient cold orbits.
    - Merger-driven heating appears to move stars directly from cold to hot orbits.
  - **Position:** useful low-redshift calibration for connecting visible tidal debris to dynamical transformation.

## Gas, Dust, and Cluster-Scale Baryons

- [2608.14473](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14473) Han Zhao et al. `OH Line Detections in Southern Galaxies of the IRAS Revised Bright Galaxy Sample`
  - **Question:** what controls OH main-line emission and absorption in nearby infrared-bright galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `186` southern galaxies from the IRAS Revised Bright Galaxy Sample.
    - Observations: archival MeerKAT snapshot data.
    - Lines: OH main-line emission and absorption.
  - **Method:**
    - Search for OH features in the galaxy sample.
    - Classify OH maser emission, OH absorption, and mixed profiles.
    - Compare detection rates with infrared luminosity, radio continuum, mid-infrared colors, and dense-gas indicators.
    - Stack non-detections to test for weak residual OH features.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - OH features are detected in `38` galaxies.
    - The detections include `8` OH maser emitters, `3` of them new, and `30` OH absorbers.
    - OH absorption detection rates reach `~36%` in LIRGs and `~27%` in non-LIRGs with radio continuum flux density above `20 mJy`.
    - OH absorption galaxies occupy an intermediate `L_HCN/L_CO` regime between OH megamasers and non-detections.
  - **Position:** useful dense-gas reference for obscured star formation and molecular absorption work, but lower priority than the high-z entries.

- [2608.14415](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14415) Yuusuke Uchida et al. `XRISM reveals sloshing-driven gas motions in the core of Abell 2029`
  - **Question:** what velocity structure does XRISM Resolve reveal in the core of Abell 2029?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: relaxed cool-core cluster Abell 2029.
    - Instrument: XRISM Resolve spectroscopy.
    - Comparison: Chandra X-ray spiral structure.
  - **Method:**
    - Divide the central cluster region into subregions.
    - Correct for photon mixing caused by the XRISM point-spread function.
    - Measure line-of-sight bulk velocities and turbulent velocity dispersion.
    - Compare turbulent heating with radiative cooling.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The core shows an ordered line-of-sight bulk-velocity gradient.
    - The maximum velocity difference is about `280 km s^-1`.
    - Turbulent velocity dispersion is `<=150 km s^-1`.
    - The implied nonthermal pressure fraction is below `~2.5%`.
    - Turbulent dissipation alone is insufficient to offset radiative cooling across the core.
  - **Position:** relevant as a cluster-gas dynamics benchmark and an early XRISM example, not a direct high-z structure paper.

- [2608.14023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14023) C. J. Inman et al. `The galaxies' energy balance problem solved`
  - **Question:** can a clumpy ISM treatment resolve the galaxy SED energy-balance problem?
  - **Data:**
    - Test galaxy: edge-on NGC 891.
    - Extended sample: seven galaxies with different inclinations.
    - Model inputs: optical, near-infrared, and submillimeter constraints.
  - **Method:**
    - Treat quiescent dust clouds as pseudo-dust grains in radiative-transfer models.
    - Derive absorption, scattering, extinction, and emission properties for the clumpy component.
    - Fit galaxy SEDs with diffuse-only and clumpy models.
    - Compare inferred dust opacity and attenuation.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The clumpy model fits both submillimeter and near-infrared data for NGC 891.
    - Clumpy models produce lower dust opacity and attenuation than diffuse-only models.
    - Maximum face-on optical depth in the B band is lower by factors of `1.3-2.8`.
    - Among seven galaxies, two are optically thick in their centers, two are moderately thick, and three are optically thin.
  - **Position:** useful SED-systematics paper for interpreting dusty galaxies, but it is mostly a low-redshift radiative-transfer calibration.

## Reproducibility, Data Methods, and Lower-Priority Entries

- [2608.13633](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13633) Juan Cruz-Martinez et al. `Unknown Unknowns: Model Misspecification in Machine Learning for Physics`
  - **Question:** how should machine-learning analyses in physics handle unknown model misspecification?
  - **Data:**
    - Scope: simulation-trained machine-learning models used for inverse problems in particle physics and astronomy.
    - Emphasis: deployment on real data when simulations may omit relevant effects.
  - **Method:**
    - Classify misspecification as both a discovery signal and an analysis bias.
    - Discuss diagnostics for detecting model failure.
    - Separate robustness strategies from sensitivity to new effects.
    - Frame validation as an iterative detection-and-mitigation loop.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - No single diagnostic can prove that a model is correctly specified.
    - Robust analysis requires complementary diagnostics, model updates, and repeated checks.
    - Machine learning can amplify misspecification but can also provide new tools to diagnose it.
  - **Position:** high-value methods note because profile-relevant survey inference increasingly depends on simulation-trained models.

- [2608.14101](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14101) David C. Flynn `A Reproducible Two-Boundary Kinematic Correction for Baryonic Rotation-Curve Reconstruction in an 84-Galaxy SPARC Benchmark`
  - **Question:** how reproducible is a two-boundary kinematic correction for baryonic rotation-curve reconstruction?
  - **Data:**
    - Benchmark: frozen `84`-galaxy SPARC sample.
    - Inputs: observed rotation curves plus gas, disk, and bulge baryonic components.
    - Comparisons: maximum-disk reconstruction and Keplerian reference.
  - **Method:**
    - Compute one coefficient per galaxy from the innermost and outermost measured rotation-curve points.
    - Apply the correction to the full observed radial profile.
    - Compare corrected profiles with baryonic reconstructions.
    - Publish deterministic workflow checks and formula-level regression tests.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The primary maximum-disk reconstruction reduces the mean observed-baryonic discrepancy from `51.82` to `30.15 km s^-1`.
    - Bounded mass-to-light optimization reduces the descriptive sensitivity-fit value to `25.45 km s^-1`.
    - The optimization gives a resolved benefit in `53` galaxies and no resolved change in `31`.
    - The work is framed as a reproducible data-transformation benchmark, not a new force law.
  - **Position:** lower scientific priority, but useful as a reminder that transparent benchmark definitions matter before physical interpretation.
