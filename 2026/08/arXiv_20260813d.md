## High-Redshift Quasars and Early Accretion

- [2608.11400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11400) V.B. Diaz et al. `Magnetic fields in extreme primordial halos: turbulent collapse and implications for early quasar formation`
  - **Question:** how do magnetic fields behave during the collapse of rare primordial halos that could seed early massive quasars?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: cosmological high-resolution magnetohydrodynamical zoom-in simulations.
    - Initial field range: `10^-14-10^-8 G`.
    - Target environment: a massive primordial halo associated with a rare high-sigma density peak.
  - **Method:**
    - Follow halo collapse at the intersection of convergent cosmic flows.
    - Compare turbulent amplification across different initial magnetic fields.
    - Evaluate turbulent, thermal, and magnetic Jeans masses across resolved scales.
    - Interpret the expected central-object mass relative to more typical atomic-cooling halos.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The halo develops highly supersonic turbulence with Mach numbers of order `10-20`.
    - Large-scale dynamics dominate the collapse for all tested initial magnetic fields.
    - Magnetic Jeans masses become comparable to thermal Jeans masses only on `10^-2-10 pc` scales.
    - Central massive objects of a few `10^4 M_sun` remain expected even with magnetic fields.
  - **Position:** read first because rare-peak collapse physics is directly relevant to early quasar seed formation and high-redshift black-hole growth.

- [2608.11916](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11916) Adrien Helias et al. `Classifying Quasar Types Without a Spectrum`
  - **Question:** can quasar type be classified from irregular photometric variability without spectra?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: Zwicky Transient Facility light curves.
    - Catalog: MILLIQUAS quasars.
    - Training sample: `516` type 1 and `238` type 2 quasars.
  - **Method:**
    - Decompose irregular light-curve variance using Slepian Wavelet Variance.
    - Cluster wavelet-variance curves with agglomerative hierarchical clustering.
    - Compare the unsupervised classes with spectroscopic type labels.
    - Inspect misclassified systems for variability behavior inconsistent with their spectral class.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The method recovers `99%` of type 1 quasars.
    - The method recovers `87%` of type 2 quasars.
    - Misclassified quasars show variability properties closer to the opposite spectral type.
    - The approach provides a model-independent alternative to structure functions and damped-random-walk fits.
  - **Position:** useful survey-method paper because photometric quasar typing matters for large AGN samples where spectroscopy does not scale.

- [2608.12261](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12261) Margaret Shepherd et al. `The Delay Time Distribution of Quasi-Periodic Eruptions`
  - **Question:** how does the QPE rate depend on time since the most recent star-formation burst?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `10` QPE host galaxies with optical spectra.
    - Stellar-population modeling: Bagpipes fits to host spectra.
    - Comparison context: tidal disruption event hosts and control galaxies.
  - **Method:**
    - Compile QPE host spectra.
    - Fit stellar populations and recover recent burst ages.
    - Construct a delay-time distribution relative to a control sample.
    - Compare burst mass fractions with TDE and control-host distributions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The preferred QPE rate rises toward a post-burst age of `~1 Gyr`.
    - A flat burst-age distribution relative to controls is not ruled out.
    - QPE hosts show a larger fraction of high burst mass fractions, `>1%`, than TDE hosts or controls.
    - If QPEs require EMRIs, large `~1 Gyr`-old bursts may help produce them.
  - **Position:** relevant nuclear-transient entry because QPE hosts may trace the same post-starburst channels that affect TDE demographics and black-hole feeding.

## Environment, Star Formation, and Galaxy Transformation

- [2608.11336](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11336) V. Lora et al. `From blue to red spirals: Slow galaxy transformation via ram pressure stripping in TNG-50`
  - **Question:** can ram-pressure stripping alone transform blue spirals into quenched red spirals in group environments?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: high-resolution TNG-50 cosmological simulation.
    - Environment: low-mass group halos with `M_group<10^14.5 M_sun`.
    - Sample design: blue spiral galaxies after infall, excluding systems with significant mergers.
  - **Method:**
    - Select infalling blue spirals in group-scale halos.
    - Remove merger-dominated cases.
    - Track gas stripping, star-formation quenching, and stellar-disk structure after infall.
    - Compare timescales with merger-driven transformation expectations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Ram-pressure stripping can quench star formation while preserving disk structure.
    - The transformation timescale is `>=6 Gyr` after infall.
    - The timescale is longer than the `~4 Gyr` usually associated with merger-driven evolution.
    - The resulting systems are red, anemic spirals rather than fully transformed S0 galaxies.
  - **Position:** useful low-redshift environment analogue because it separates gas removal from morphological transformation.

- [2608.12301](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12301) Mohamed H. Abdullah et al. `From Cluster Cores to the Low-Density Field: Strong Environmental Quenching of Galaxy Star Formation at Low Redshift`
  - **Question:** how does star formation depend on environment from cluster cores to low-density fields?
  - **Data:**
    - Parent sample: `81647` SDSS galaxies at `0.03<=z<=0.075`.
    - Cluster sample: `18426` members from `572` GalWCat19 clusters.
    - Stellar-mass range: `9.7<=log10(M_star/h^-2 M_sun)<=11.0`.
  - **Method:**
    - Characterize environment by nearest-neighbor density.
    - Measure clustercentric radius and host halo mass for GalWCat19 members.
    - Track sSFR distributions and quenched fractions with environment.
    - Measure environmental quenching efficiency at fixed stellar mass.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The sSFR distribution remains bimodal across environments.
    - The star-forming peak shifts lower by `0.42 dex` from cluster outskirts to inner regions.
    - Across local-density bins, the star-forming component decreases by `0.29-0.35 dex`.
    - Cluster environments show excess quenching beyond stellar-mass quenching alone.
  - **Position:** useful benchmark for environmental quenching because it measures both quenched fractions and suppressed SFR among galaxies still classified as star forming.

- [2608.12132](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12132) Quinten W. E. van Zegveld et al. `The effect of galaxy interactions on star formation rates in the COLIBRE simulations`
  - **Question:** how strongly do galaxy interactions enhance star formation in hydrodynamical simulations?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: COLIBRE galaxy-formation simulations.
    - Volumes: up to `200^3` and `400^3 cMpc^3`.
    - Resolution: gas and dark-matter particle masses of `~10^6 M_sun` and `~10^7 M_sun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Select interacting galaxies with mass ratios `>0.1`.
    - Match isolated controls in stellar mass, environment, and redshift.
    - Measure sSFR enhancement as a function of pair separation.
    - Compare simulated enhancement trends with SDSS observations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Mean sSFR enhancement reaches a factor of `~2` at separations of `~10 kpc`.
    - Enhancement remains significant out to `~200 kpc`.
    - Higher mass ratios produce stronger enhancement.
    - Resolved pre-merger interactions contribute only `~2%` of the `z~0` cosmic SFR density.
  - **Position:** useful interaction baseline because merger-triggered SFR enhancement is often invoked when interpreting dense environments.

- [2608.12260](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12260) Pius Privatus et al. `A systematic comparison of green valley selection criteria across multiparameter spaces using a homogeneous ultraviolet-optical dataset`
  - **Question:** do common green-valley definitions select the same transitional galaxy population?
  - **Data:**
    - Dataset: homogeneous ultraviolet-optical catalog from GALEX and SDSS.
    - Selection axes: rest-frame `u-r`, `NUV-r`, sSFR, and `D_n(4000)`.
    - Diagnostic spaces: color-mass, color-magnitude, and SFR-mass diagrams.
  - **Method:**
    - Construct green-valley samples under each diagnostic definition.
    - Compare distributions across stellar mass, color, SFR, and spectral index.
    - Measure overlap and systematic offsets among selections.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Different criteria identify statistically distinct subsets.
    - UV-based selections shift toward optically redder and lower-SFR systems.
    - The `u-r` selection is biased toward higher SFR than other definitions.
    - The small overlap means one-dimensional green-valley definitions are not interchangeable.
  - **Position:** useful classification note because transitional-galaxy samples depend strongly on selection diagnostics.

## Gas, Magnetic Fields, and Diffuse Backgrounds

- [2608.12116](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12116) Richard M. Feder et al. `CIBER x galaxy cross-correlations reveal a bright, low-redshift NIR background`
  - **Question:** which structures contribute to near-IR extragalactic-background-light anisotropies?
  - **Data:**
    - Background maps: CIBER `1.1` and `1.8 um` imager data.
    - Galaxy catalogs: DESI Legacy Survey DR8 and Hyper Suprime-Cam Ultra-Deep.
    - Redshift focus: tomographic structure across `z=0-1`, concentrated at `z<~0.6`.
  - **Method:**
    - Cross-correlate CIBER maps with photometric galaxy catalogs.
    - Compare cross-power with integrated-galaxy-light expectations.
    - Decompose the signal with a parametric halo model.
    - Estimate the contribution of cluster members and correlated large-scale structure.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The measured cross-power exceeds standard integrated-galaxy-light expectations on scales `ell<2000`.
    - Cluster members and associated structure contribute `15-20%` of large-angle cross-power.
    - One-halo and two-halo terms are detected at high significance.
    - Low-redshift LSS accounts for a substantial fraction of previously reported CIBER auto-power.
  - **Position:** relevant foreground and LSS-method entry because diffuse background measurements require careful separation of low-z structure from high-z signals.

- [2608.12275](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12275) N. Pourjafari et al. `CHANG-ES XL: Magnetic Field Structures in the Disk and Halo of NGC 891`
  - **Question:** what magnetic-field structure appears in the disk and halo of the edge-on galaxy NGC 891?
  - **Data:**
    - Radio data: VLA S-band `2-4 GHz` observations plus C-band data.
    - Target: edge-on spiral galaxy NGC 891.
    - Ancillary maps: H-alpha and diffuse X-ray imaging.
  - **Method:**
    - Use rotation-measure synthesis to detect polarized halo emission.
    - Map Faraday depths and rotation measures across the disk and halo.
    - Compare polarized structures with H-alpha and X-ray features.
    - Identify HII regions and outer-disk star-formation sites.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Extended polarized halo emission is mostly confined to Faraday depths within `+/-150 rad m^-2`.
    - A northeastern polarized-intensity enhancement may be associated with a superbubble.
    - Depolarization is not dominated by small-scale Faraday rotation within the disk.
    - A faint isolated HII region at `16.9 kpc` indicates star formation outside the thin disk.
  - **Position:** useful nearby-galaxy gas-field entry because magnetic halo structure and outflows are local anchors for baryon-cycle interpretation.

- [2608.11504](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11504) Sergio Martin et al. `Chemistry here and there: Comparison between the CMZ of the Milky Way and NGC 253`
  - **Question:** how well can Galactic and extragalactic molecular chemistry be compared at resolved scales?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: ALMA ALCHEMI observations of NGC 253.
    - Comparison targets: Galactic hot cores, quiescent molecular clouds, comets, and the Milky Way Central Molecular Zone.
    - Spatial scale: roughly `1 pc` to `0.5 kpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare molecular abundance patterns between NGC 253 and Galactic environments.
    - Interpret resolved extragalactic chemistry relative to known local ISM phases.
    - Use the comparison to motivate broader ALMA wideband chemical surveys.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Molecular chemistry in Galactic GMCs and the NGC 253 central molecular zone shows strong correspondence across the compared scales.
    - ALMA sensitivity and bandwidth now make one-to-one resolved extragalactic ISM chemistry practical.
    - Future ALMA upgrades should extend similar comparisons to more systems.
  - **Position:** supporting ISM entry because resolved chemistry can calibrate molecular diagnostics used in more distant galaxies.

## Survey and Dark-Matter Context

- [2608.11294](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11294) Courtney D. Dressing et al. `Early Exploration of the Scientific Discovery Space for the Habitable Worlds Observatory`
  - **Question:** what discovery space should the Habitable Worlds Observatory preserve beyond exoplanet biosignature work?
  - **Data:**
    - Science case set: `70` community-developed cases.
    - Thematic pillars: growth of galaxies, evolution of the elements, solar systems in context, and living worlds.
    - Observing-program set: `140` proposed programs.
  - **Method:**
    - Organize START working-group input into science pillars.
    - Map science cases to Astro2020 discovery questions.
    - Identify wavelength, spectroscopy, imaging, polarimetry, and operational requirements.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Galaxy-growth science accounts for `15` of the `70` cases.
    - Spectroscopy is needed for `87%` of cases.
    - UV access below `400 nm` is needed for `83%` of cases.
    - Some cases require high contrast, polarimetry, precise astrometry, fast response, and wide field of regard.
  - **Position:** useful facilities paper because UV/NIR capability choices affect future galaxy-growth and CGM science.

- [2608.12254](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12254) Julie Kiel Holm et al. `Evidence for the First Globular Cluster Stellar Stream beyond the Milky Way`
  - **Question:** can globular-cluster streams constrain dark matter halos outside the Milky Way?
  - **Data:**
    - Imaging: deep Hubble Space Telescope observations.
    - Host: ultra-diffuse galaxy UGC9050-Dw1.
    - Feature: a long, thin stream apparently associated with a compact source.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify stream morphology and color.
    - Compare the stream with simulated globular-cluster stellar populations.
    - Fit generative dynamical stream models to the observed morphology.
    - Infer constraints on the progenitor and host halo.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The morphology, color, and compact-source association support a globular-cluster progenitor.
    - Stream models favor a globular-cluster origin.
    - The host appears to sit in a massive dark matter halo.
    - The method extends stream-based halo constraints beyond the Milky Way.
  - **Position:** useful dark-matter and low-surface-brightness entry because external stellar streams can probe halo structure where kinematics are sparse.

- [2608.12015](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12015) Alberto Dominguez et al. `Constraining the gamma-ray efficiency of LINER outflows with Fermi-LAT and MEGARA`
  - **Question:** can LINER ionized outflows power their gamma-ray emission efficiently?
  - **Data:**
    - Gamma rays: `17` years of Fermi-LAT observations over `0.05-500 GeV`.
    - Optical IFU data: MEGARA ionized-gas kinematics.
    - Sample: local LINERs with outflow kinetic-power estimates.
  - **Method:**
    - Derive gamma-ray luminosities or `95%` confidence upper limits.
    - Measure ionized-outflow kinetic powers from optical kinematics.
    - Place sources on optical/gamma-ray diagrams with starbursts and radio galaxies.
    - Estimate gamma-ray radiative efficiency for LINER outflows.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - No LINER is formally detected at `TS>=16`.
    - NGC 1052 gives the strongest efficiency limit, `eta<41%`.
    - Most limits sit above the outflow kinetic powers, leaving weak source-by-source constraints.
    - Extended ionized outflows appear inefficient as high-energy particle accelerators.
  - **Position:** lower-redshift AGN-feedback calibration because compact nuclear jets remain favored for the most gamma-ray-efficient LINERs.
