## Reionization, GN-z11, and Early Baryon Cycling

- [2608.12699](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12699) Zuyi Chen et al. `SPURS: Massive Stars, Dense Gas, and Lyalpha Escape in GN-z11 at z = 10.6`
  - **Question:** what stellar and gas conditions explain GN-z11's nitrogen enhancement and Lyalpha visibility at `z=10.6`?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: GN-z11 at `z=10.6`.
    - Spectroscopy: ultra-deep JWST SPURS rest-UV data.
    - Diagnostics: P-Cygni stellar winds, broad He II and N IV], UV absorption, Lyalpha, and O I* emission.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit very-massive-star stellar population models.
    - Interpret broad N IV] as dense WN/LBV-like stellar-wind gas or possible AGN-driven gas.
    - Measure outflow velocities and neutral-gas covering fraction.
    - Resolve Lyalpha velocity structure to assess damping-wing escape.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Very massive stars with masses `>100 M_sun` and ages `<=3 Myr` reproduce key wind diagnostics.
    - Broad N IV] has `FWHM=1670 km s^-1`.
    - Highly ionized outflowing gas reaches velocity `~500 km s^-1`.
    - Lyalpha has `EW=5.6 A`, `f_esc,Lyalpha=2.7%`, and a red wing with `44%` of the flux above `500 km s^-1`.
  - **Position:** the week's strongest read because it connects ionizing sources, dense gas, and Lyalpha transmission in a canonical `z>10` galaxy.

- [2608.12466](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12466) Minami Nakane et al. `Why is GN-z11 Bright, Compact, and Nitrogen Enhanced? Insights from UV Absorption and Emission Diagnostics`
  - **Question:** is GN-z11 powered mainly by massive stars or an AGN-like ionizing source?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: GN-z11 at `z=10.60`.
    - Spectroscopy: JWST/NIRSpec high-resolution IFU and medium-resolution MSA observations.
    - Diagnostics: N V, Si IV, C IV P-Cygni profiles, broad N IV], and carbon/nitrogen density-sensitive lines.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare IFU and MSA spectra after optimized reduction.
    - Fit stellar and AGN UV spectral models.
    - Use P-Cygni profiles to identify massive-star signatures.
    - Use C III], N III], and N IV] to infer gas densities in distinct emitting regions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Stellar models are preferred over AGN models with `Delta WAIC=-25`.
    - Broad N IV] has `FWHM~1600 km s^-1`.
    - N IV] density diagnostics reach `>=10^6.5 cm^-3`.
    - Strong narrow nitrogen emission may arise from dense locally enriched gas near massive stars.
  - **Position:** critical companion to SPURS because it narrows the interpretation of GN-z11's nitrogen and compact UV continuum.

- [2608.09813](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09813) Shengzhe Wang et al. `An emerging baryon cycle in a galaxy 500 million years after the Big Bang`
  - **Question:** when does stellar feedback begin regulating gas cycling in the first massive galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: Gz9p3 at `z=9.311`.
    - Spectroscopy: deep JWST observations of neutral gas and outflow phases.
    - Environment: merging galaxy with a tidal structure.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify neutral gas along merger-driven tidal features.
    - Separate multiphase outflow signatures from host-galaxy gas.
    - Infer cool-outflow density from fine-structure absorption.
    - Compare outflow escape with CGM recycling expectations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Gz9p3 contains both a substantial neutral reservoir and a multiphase outflow.
    - The cool outflow has electron density `~17 cm^-3`.
    - The outflow mass-loading factor is among the highest measured at comparable stellar mass.
    - The cool outflow is unlikely to escape the halo, so enriched gas may recycle through the CGM.
  - **Position:** the strongest Tuesday result because it shows a baryon cycle emerging during reionization rather than only after cosmic noon.

- [2608.10063](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10063) Raunaq Singh Rai et al. `Unveiling and Characterising Ubiquitous Nitrogen Enhancement in 6 <= z <= 10 Galaxies with JWST Spectroscopy`
  - **Question:** is N/O enhancement common across reionization-era galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Spectroscopy: JWST/NIRSpec `R~1000`.
    - Sample: `135` galaxies at `6<=z<=10`.
    - Diagnostics: stacked UV lines and auroral [O III] `4363`.
  - **Method:**
    - Stack spectra by star-formation state.
    - Derive direct-temperature abundances.
    - Compare N/O, C/O, and oxygen abundance across burst phases.
    - Test standard CCSNe plus delayed AGB enrichment against exotic-source interpretations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The full stack gives `log(N/O)=-0.57(+0.09,-0.10)`.
    - The same stack gives `log(C/O)=-0.80(+0.03,-0.03)` at `12+log(O/H)=7.79(+0.03,-0.03)`.
    - Recent-burst systems are less enriched in N/O and metallicity than relative-lull systems.
    - Standard stellar enrichment can reproduce the trend without invoking supermassive stars.
  - **Position:** important context for GN-z11 because nitrogen-rich spectra may be a broader reionization-era enrichment phenomenon.

## LRDs, Quasars, and Black-Hole Growth

- [2608.10832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10832) Tomokazu Kiyota et al. `ATLAS. III. Dust Around Little Red Dots: Hydrogen Line Ratios beyond Dust-free Non-Case B Models`
  - **Question:** can broad hydrogen-line ratios in LRDs be explained without dust in the broad-line region?
  - **Data:**
    - High-redshift sample: `5` LRDs at `z>2` with JWST/NIRSpec medium/high-resolution spectra.
    - Low-redshift comparison: `15` sources at `z=0.1-0.9`.
    - Requirement: broad Hbeta detected at `>5 sigma`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure broad Balmer and Paschen line ratios.
    - Model absorption and neighboring emission lines.
    - Compare ratios with plane-parallel Cloudy models.
    - Use narrow-line ratios to separate BLR extinction from host-wide extinction.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Broad Halpha/Hbeta ratios span `6-30`.
    - One low-z LRD is reproduced by gas density `log(n_H/cm^-3)~10-11`.
    - Most modeled LRDs require dust extinction `E(B-V)>~0.2-1.0`.
    - Halpha/Hbeta `>13` in many sources implies dust obscuration in at least about half of the sample.
  - **Position:** the week's key LRD interpretation paper because dust geometry directly affects black-hole and star-formation estimates.

- [2608.12487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12487) Madisyn Brooks et al. `Ripples in the OCEANS: Broad Line Variability of Little Red Dots`
  - **Question:** do LRD broad lines show variability consistent with accretion?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `6` OCEANS LRDs.
    - Spectroscopy: OCEANS `R~2700` plus archival CEERS and RUBIES `R~1000`.
    - Comparison: SDSS-RM quasar variability.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare broad-Halpha measurements between epochs.
    - Search for continuum variability.
    - Set variability upper limits for non-detections.
    - Compare the observed variable/nonvariable mix with quasars.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Two LRDs show marginal broad-Halpha variability at `27%` and `50%`.
    - The significances are `2.1 sigma` and `1.5 sigma`.
    - The other four LRDs have `1 sigma` upper limits of `4.8%-30%`.
    - The pattern is a `~2 sigma` departure from typical quasar variability.
  - **Position:** useful Friday follow-up because variability tests the AGN interpretation independent of static line ratios.

- [2608.10074](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10074) Ayumi Takahashi et al. `Discovery of four Narrow-line Type-1 Quasars at z = 2.54 -- 6.06 with Subaru 'Onohi'ula PFS-SSP`
  - **Question:** can high-redshift narrow-line type-1 quasars be identified in Subaru PFS spectra?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: Subaru 'Onohi'ula PFS-SSP.
    - Sample: four NLQ1s, including one candidate.
    - Redshift range: `z=2.54-6.06`.
  - **Method:**
    - Search broad-line AGN for Lyalpha profiles resembling local NLS1s.
    - Measure Lyalpha FWHM, equivalent width, luminosity, black-hole mass, and Eddington ratio.
    - Use C IV and N V absorption where available for classification.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Lyalpha FWHM values are `1410-1510 km s^-1`.
    - Mean rest-frame Lyalpha equivalent width is `~190 A`.
    - Three objects have black-hole masses `~10^6.9-10^7.7 M_sun`.
    - The inferred Eddington ratios are `>0.1`.
  - **Position:** useful quasar-demographics result because PFS can find low-mass, high-accretion AGN at high redshift.

- [2608.11400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11400) V.B. Diaz et al. `Magnetic fields in extreme primordial halos: turbulent collapse and implications for early quasar formation`
  - **Question:** how do magnetic fields affect the collapse of rare primordial halos that could seed early massive quasars?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: high-resolution cosmological MHD zoom-ins.
    - Initial fields: `10^-14-10^-8 G`.
    - Target: a massive primordial halo in a rare high-sigma peak.
  - **Method:**
    - Follow collapse at convergent cosmic flows.
    - Compare turbulent amplification across field strengths.
    - Track turbulent, thermal, and magnetic Jeans masses.
    - Estimate central-object mass relative to typical atomic-cooling halos.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Supersonic turbulence reaches Mach numbers `10-20`.
    - Large-scale dynamics dominate collapse for all tested initial fields.
    - Magnetic Jeans masses become comparable to thermal Jeans masses only at `10^-2-10 pc`.
    - Central massive objects of a few `10^4 M_sun` remain expected.
  - **Position:** important high-redshift black-hole entry because rare-peak collapse physics sets seed conditions for early quasars.

## Protoclusters, CGM, Gas Reservoirs, and Feedback

- [2608.10070](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10070) C. Bertemes et al. `A cosmic collision in the making: JWST/NIRISS reveals a merging and maturing protocluster core at z~3 around a red quasar`
  - **Question:** what is the galaxy and velocity structure around a luminous red quasar at `z~3`?
  - **Data:**
    - Field: SDSSJ165202.64+172852.3.
    - Instrument: JWST/NIRISS wide-field slitless spectroscopy.
    - Structure: at least `18` member galaxies in a protocluster called The Step.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify members in position-velocity space.
    - Separate two substructures from the redshift distribution.
    - Measure projected overdensity and velocity span.
    - Compare star-forming, quiescent, and AGN fractions with field expectations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The densest sightline contains `7` galaxies within `70` projected kpc of the quasar.
    - The galaxy velocity span reaches `700-850 km s^-1`.
    - The overdensity is `>100` in the core and `>15` within `1 Mpc^2`.
    - The inferred halo mass is `13.1<log(M_halo/M_sun)<13.8`.
  - **Position:** the week's strongest environment paper because it combines quasar environment, protocluster assembly, quenching, and AGN activity.

- [2608.10165](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10165) V. Catan et al. `The Extended Mapping Obscuration to Reionization with ALMA (Ex-MORA) Survey: A Molecular Gas Line Search`
  - **Question:** what molecular gas reservoirs are recovered by ALMA line selection across cosmic time?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: ALMA Band 4 Ex-MORA.
    - Area: `577 arcmin^2`.
    - Redshift range: `0.5<z<5`.
    - Detections: `52` galaxies in CO or CI transitions.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine an unbiased bright-line search with targeted searches at known spectroscopic redshifts.
    - Fit physical properties with CIGALE and non-parametric SFHs.
    - Estimate gas masses using metallicity-dependent conversion factors.
    - Compare gas fractions across redshift, activity class, and overdensity.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The median stellar mass is `log(M*/M_sun)=10.83`.
    - Gas reservoirs are typically `>10^10 M_sun`.
    - Gas fractions rise with redshift for active galaxies.
    - A gas-rich protocluster at `z~2.4` affects the high-redshift trend.
  - **Position:** high-value gas census because molecular-line selection recovers obscured and transitional systems missed by stellar-light selection.

- [2608.10306](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10306) Tiffany Liou et al. `Scaling Relations of Galactic Outflows Across Cosmic Time: New Insights from Cosmic Noon`
  - **Question:** how do warm-ionized outflow scaling relations evolve from low redshift to cosmic noon?
  - **Data:**
    - Joint sample: `387` galaxies from `z~0-9`.
    - New subset: `98` KBSS and KLCS galaxies at cosmic noon.
    - Spectroscopy: Keck/MOSFIRE Halpha or [O III] `5007` with `SNR>50`.
  - **Method:**
    - Decompose high-SNR emission lines into narrow and broad components.
    - Measure outflow velocity, mass outflow rate, and mass loading.
    - Compare trends at fixed stellar mass, SFR, and SFR surface density.
    - Correct occurrence rates for orientation and outflow geometry.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Cosmic-noon outflows reach maximum velocities up to `3` times higher than low-z outflows at fixed galaxy properties.
    - Mass outflow rates are higher at fixed stellar mass.
    - Mass loading shows no evolution at fixed stellar mass.
    - Most outflowing gas recycles at radius `~0.03 R_vir`.
  - **Position:** strong feedback entry because it links cosmic-noon spectra to baryon recycling and CGM fueling.

- [2608.09418](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09418) Sumukha R. Bharadwaj et al. `A Partial Lyman Limit Absorber in the Halo of a Galaxy Pair: A Possible Signature of Gas Inflow`
  - **Question:** does a partial Lyman-limit absorber near a galaxy pair trace inflow, interaction debris, or another CGM component?
  - **Data:**
    - Absorber: partial Lyman-limit system at `z=0.87641`.
    - Background source: HST/COS spectrum of quasar LBQS 0107-0235.
    - Galaxy data: VLT/MUSE detections of two galaxies at the absorber redshift.
  - **Method:**
    - Model the HI Lyman series and metal-line absorption.
    - Run photoionization models for density and metallicity.
    - Compare absorber orientation and velocity with galaxy disks.
    - Interpret the geometry against inflow and interaction scenarios.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The gas metallicity is `log10(Z/Z_sun)=-1.05`.
    - The hydrogen density is `n_H~8.5 x 10^-4 cm^-3`.
    - The galaxies lie at `rho/R_vir~0.9` with velocity offsets `18` and `99 km s^-1`.
    - The major-axis geometry is consistent with sub-solar metallicity inflow, though interaction gas remains possible.
  - **Position:** strong CGM entry because it ties absorber metallicity and geometry to a physically testable inflow picture.

- [2608.12462](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12462) Dongyun Kwak et al. `Recycled Gas Dominates the Metal-rich Fuel of Supermassive Black Holes`
  - **Question:** what gas origin dominates SMBH fueling in massive galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: `30` high-resolution cosmological zoom-ins.
    - Galaxy masses: `10^10.9-10^11.9 M_sun`.
    - Black-hole masses: `10^8.5-10^9.7 M_sun` at `z=0`.
  - **Method:**
    - Trace gas-particle histories.
    - Classify fuel as early, external, recycled, or smooth.
    - Track enrichment before SMBH accretion.
    - Compare abundance ratios with quasar broad-line-region metallicities.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Recycled gas dominates the accretion budget.
    - The recycled component is metal rich early.
    - Other components are enriched inside the galactic environment before accretion.
    - Weak abundance-ratio evolution is compatible with high quasar BLR metallicities.
  - **Position:** useful bridge between galaxy evolution and quasar metallicity because normal stellar recycling can supply metal-rich SMBH fuel.

## High-z Selection, Star Formation, and Survey Methods

- [2608.07461](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07461) F.-Y. F. Liu et al. `A clear detection of proper motion confirms that the claimed z~32 galaxy candidate, "Capotauro", is a Y-type brown dwarf`
  - **Question:** is Capotauro an extreme `z~32` galaxy candidate or a Galactic contaminant?
  - **Data:**
    - Imaging: new JWST/NIRCam medium-band observations `~3.5` years after the discovery data.
    - Astrometry: `68` compact reference sources.
    - Templates: empirical brown-dwarf and extragalactic SED alternatives.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit filter-dependent PSFs in both epochs.
    - Measure motion relative to the local compact-source frame.
    - Run injection-recovery tests.
    - Compare the SED with brown-dwarf templates.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Capotauro moves by `132 +/- 20 mas`.
    - The extragalactic interpretation is rejected at `>6 sigma`.
    - The proper motion is `37.6(+5.5/-5.6) mas yr^-1`.
    - The SED matches a `Y1.0+/-0.5` brown dwarf at `730+/-110 pc`.
  - **Position:** essential cautionary paper because the highest-redshift candidate lists are vulnerable to cool moving contaminants.

- [2608.08203](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08203) Nicholas S. Martis et al. `A New Window on the Halpha Luminosity Function and Star Formation Rate Density from 1.2 < z < 6.6 from JWST Medium-Band Photometry`
  - **Question:** how does the Halpha luminosity function evolve from cosmic noon toward reionization?
  - **Data:**
    - Imaging: JWST/NIRCam medium-band data from CANUCS, JWST in Technicolor, and JUMPS.
    - Filters: up to `29` total filters.
    - Redshift range: `1.25<z<6.6`.
  - **Method:**
    - Use dense SED sampling for photometric redshifts and continuum subtraction.
    - Trace Halpha through `11` medium-band filters.
    - Validate line fluxes against spectroscopy.
    - Correct for dust and derive SFR functions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Photometric redshifts reach outlier fraction `1.7%` and `sigma_NMAD=0.039`.
    - Spectroscopic comparison gives `0.23 dex` line-flux scatter with no systematic offset.
    - The Halpha luminosity function spans nearly `3` dex.
    - SFR density peaks near `z~2` and declines to `z=6`, with high normalization relative to UV estimates.
  - **Position:** high-value survey result because Halpha tracks star formation through the range where UV and IR estimates disagree.

- [2608.08015](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08015) Gitanjali Rajulal et al. `Breathing Fire: Hot Dust in the Big Three Dragons at z = 7.15`
  - **Question:** how much obscured star formation is present in a bright merging LBG pair at `z=7.15`?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: Big Three Dragons.
    - Observations: ALMA Band 9 at `0.45 mm` and Band 4 at `2.2 mm`.
    - Prior data: ALMA Bands 6, 7, and 8.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine multi-band ALMA photometry.
    - Fit modified-blackbody dust models.
    - Compare eastern and western components.
    - Place the system against IRX-beta expectations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Band 4 gives a `3.5 sigma` detection; Band 9 detects the two components at `3.7` and `3.5 sigma`.
    - The global dust temperature is `T_d=78(-23,+35) K`.
    - The system reaches `log(L_IR/L_sun)=12.32(-0.41,+0.43)`.
    - The obscured fraction is `f_obs=0.94(-0.09,+0.04)`.
  - **Position:** important reionization-era census paper because UV-bright galaxies can still hide most of their star formation.

- [2608.12471](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12471) Jack C. Turner et al. `FLAGS II: Constraining Galaxy Formation Models with Dimensionality Reduction of Direct Observables`
  - **Question:** can direct JWST/HST observables compare galaxy-formation models without SED-fitting biases?
  - **Data:**
    - Observables: photometric fluxes in GOODS-S.
    - Models: five galaxy-formation model families.
    - Embedding: UMAP two-dimensional latent space.
  - **Method:**
    - Embed high-dimensional flux vectors.
    - Compare model and observed galaxy distributions with a chi-square-like metric.
    - Test hyperparameter sensitivity.
    - Compare embedding speed with Bayesian SED fitting.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The embeddings preserve enough information to distinguish models.
    - JAGUAR reproduces bright GOODS-S galaxies six times as well as SC-SAM and twelve times as well as SAGE.
    - Embedded positions can be computed `>100` times faster than Bayesian SED fitting.
