## Reionization, LRDs, and AGN

- [2608.05281](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05281) Sukanya Mallik et al. `On the Origin of the Lyalpha Damping Wing in Galaxies at 8<=z<=10: Explorations using the NINJA Simulations`
  - **Question:** what physical gas component drives Lyalpha damping-wing absorption in JWST-era galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: large-scale cosmological hydrodynamical NINJA simulations.
    - Redshifts: `z=8` and `z=10`.
    - Models: uniformly ionized IGM, HII regions with radii of `0-400 pkpc`, and added virial-radius gas absorption.
    - Comparison observable: inferred HI column-density distributions from observed damping wings.
  - **Method:**
    - Generate mock Lyalpha absorption spectra around simulated galaxies.
    - Vary IGM neutral fraction with `x_HI=0.1`, `0.8`, and `1.0`.
    - Vary HII-region sizes.
    - Add partially ionized gas within the virial radius to test the CGM/ISM contribution.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Overdense gas extends to `~60-100 pkpc` around galaxies, with larger extents in more massive halos.
    - Median damping-wing columns can be matched by multiple IGM neutral-fraction and HII-region combinations.
    - Uniformly ionized models or HII regions larger than `~50 pkpc` fail to reproduce absorbers with `NHI>10^22 cm^-2`.
    - Partially ionized virial-radius gas increases the incidence of strong absorbers but still underpredicts the observations.
  - **Position:** read first because it turns damping wings into a joint ISM, CGM, and IGM diagnostic rather than a one-parameter neutral-fraction measurement.

- [2608.05267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05267) Lasse Seyberlich et al. `Where did all the Little Red Dots go? The abundance of LRD analogues among objects with broad lines at z < 0.35`
  - **Question:** do local broad-line objects contain a sizeable population of LRD analogues?
  - **Data:**
    - Parent sample: SDSS galaxies and quasars with broad H-alpha or H-beta.
    - Redshift range: `0<z<0.35`.
    - Additional data: archival photometry and spectroscopy.
    - Analogue definition: broad lines, v-shaped SEDs, and modified-blackbody-like SED behavior.
  - **Method:**
    - Select all broad-line objects in the redshift window.
    - Identify v-shaped SEDs.
    - Fit modified blackbody forms to isolate LRD-like continua.
    - Compare analogues against luminosity-matched broad-line objects.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - `19%` of broad-line objects show a v-shaped SED.
    - Nine local LRD analogues are identified, equal to `0.08%` of all broad-line objects.
    - Among luminosity-matched objects, `5%` show a v-shape and `0.37%` show an MBB-like SED.
    - One known analogue is recovered, while six of the other candidates may have alternative explanations.
  - **Position:** useful LRD context because it shows that the JWST LRD phenomenon has few clean low-redshift counterparts.

- [2608.05283](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05283) Elisha Modelevsky et al. `Emission line formation in scattering dominated media: implications for LRDs`
  - **Question:** can static optically thick scattering media produce the broad Balmer-line profiles seen in LRDs?
  - **Data:**
    - Target phenomenon: broad, prominent Balmer emission in JWST Little Red Dots.
    - Model geometry: static optically thick gas envelopes with thermal populations.
    - Comparison: H-alpha line profiles in LRD spectra.
  - **Method:**
    - Use random-walk and diffusion approximations for intrinsic line formation.
    - Derive profiles for thin planar and radially extended photospheres.
    - Compare intrinsic scattering profiles with external-scattering exponential profiles.
    - Fit the broken power-law profile to observed H-alpha lines.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - A thin planar photosphere gives a logarithmic plateau plus a `v^-1` wing.
    - A radially extended photosphere yields a broken power law from `v^-alpha` to `v^-(alpha+1)` with `0<alpha<1`.
    - The broken power-law model can fit LRD H-alpha profiles.
    - LTE models do not explain the high H-alpha-to-continuum contrast, so non-LTE extensions are required.
  - **Position:** important theory support for LRD interpretation because it tests line-broadening physics without assuming standard broad-line-region structure.

- [2608.06000](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06000) Gaocheng Yin et al. `Luminosity function of quasars at 1.0<z<3.5 from SDSS and DESI`
  - **Question:** how do type 1 quasar luminosity functions evolve across the peak epoch of quasar activity?
  - **Data:**
    - Surveys: SDSS and DESI spectroscopy.
    - Redshift range: `1.0<z<3.5`.
    - Areas: a wide `~1700 deg^2` region and a deep `~265 deg^2` field.
    - Sample: `62426` quasars selected from a library of known quasars.
  - **Method:**
    - Build quasar candidates with rest-frame UV/optical color cuts.
    - Use the quasar library to identify candidates and assess completeness.
    - Estimate quasar luminosity functions in the wide and deep regions.
    - Compare evolution against earlier QLF measurements affected by selection differences.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The color-selected sample has `~96%` completeness and `~93%` purity.
    - Simple color cuts can reduce selection-systematic complexity.
    - The combined SDSS/DESI sample supports QLF measurement through the main quasar-activity epoch.
  - **Position:** high-value survey infrastructure because QLF systematics feed directly into black-hole growth and ionizing-background work.

- [2608.05856](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05856) Young-Bin Shin et al. `Cosmological Distance Measurements of High Redshift Blazars: OJ 248 (z=0.939) and 4C +38.41 (z=1.814)`
  - **Question:** can AGN variability methods measure angular-diameter distances in high-redshift blazars?
  - **Data:**
    - Sources: OJ 248 at `z=0.939` and 4C +38.41 at `z=1.814`.
    - Observables: `43 GHz` radio light curves.
    - Assumptions: equipartition brightness temperature and sample-averaged intrinsic brightness temperature.
  - **Method:**
    - Estimate angular-diameter distances from radio variability.
    - Compute Doppler factors through the inverse-Compton method.
    - Generate additional distance estimates with Doppler-factor corrections.
    - Assess the error budget.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Equipartition-based distances are `7592.7 +/- 396.7 Mpc` for OJ 248 and `11069.8 +/- 1216.9 Mpc` for 4C +38.41.
    - Epoch selection is the dominant systematic uncertainty.
    - Systematic control is required before blazar variability distances can become competitive cosmological probes.
  - **Position:** useful but method-limited AGN entry for extending standardizable radio variability work to higher redshift.

- [2608.05852](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05852) T. V. Mufakharov et al. `A broadband outburst of the compact steep-spectrum quasar 3C 138 in 2024-2026`
  - **Question:** how does a young compact steep-spectrum quasar behave during a major broadband flare?
  - **Data:**
    - Source: 3C 138.
    - Event: broadband outburst during `2024-2026`.
    - Wavelengths: radio through high-energy monitoring.
  - **Method:**
    - Track the outburst across available bands.
    - Compare variability with expectations for compact steep-spectrum quasars.
    - Use the flare as a probe of compact-jet activity.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - 3C 138 underwent a large broadband outburst during the monitoring window.
    - The event shows that compact steep-spectrum quasars can host strong high-state activity.
    - The source is a useful case for connecting young radio-source structure with jet variability.
  - **Position:** relevant AGN time-domain note because compact radio quasars are normally treated as young or confined rather than strongly variable.

## Galaxy Structure, Gas, and Survey Infrastructure

- [2608.05835](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05835) Kai Wang et al. `Ruffled Feathers: Merger-driven galaxy size growth and structural transformation in EAGLE`
  - **Question:** do semi-analytic merger prescriptions reproduce galaxy size growth and morphology change in hydrodynamical simulations?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: EAGLE cosmological hydrodynamical simulation.
    - Merger sample: `~4500` mergers.
    - Targets: post-merger half-stellar-mass radii and disc-to-spheroid transformation.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify mergers in EAGLE.
    - Test an energy-conservation estimator for remnant size.
    - Compare gas-rich merger dissipation corrections against simulated descendants.
    - Quantify morphology change as a function of merger mass ratio.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The size estimator matches descendants with `~0.12-0.15 dex` scatter.
    - A commonly used gas-rich dissipation correction underpredicts post-merger size by up to `~0.4 dex`.
    - Per-merger size growth increases with stellar mass ratio.
    - The results calibrate merger prescriptions against a realistic cosmological merger population.
  - **Position:** useful structural-evolution paper because it checks the recipes used to interpret mass-size evolution and morphology.

- [2608.06350](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06350) Pushpak Pandey et al. `Clump Migration in Disk Galaxies: Revisiting Chandrasekhar's Dynamical Friction`
  - **Question:** does the classical Chandrasekhar dynamical-friction formula predict stellar-clump migration in realistic disks?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: isolated disk galaxy simulation.
    - Objects: long-lived massive stellar clumps.
    - Measured quantities: orbital evolution, mass loss, galactocentric radius, and migration times.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify clumps with a position- and mass-based tracking algorithm.
    - Follow clumps over multiple orbital periods.
    - Build a dynamical-friction model from simulated baryonic and dark-matter mass distributions.
    - Compare measured migration with analytic predictions after the initial transient phase.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Nine long-lived clumps are identified.
    - Eight clumps migrate inward while losing `60-90%` of their initial mass.
    - For six clumps, the analytic model overestimates inspiral times by factors of `~2-10`.
    - Clump-clump interactions, non-circular orbits, and a time-dependent disk potential likely regulate migration beyond the classical assumptions.
  - **Position:** useful disk-evolution entry because clump migration is often invoked for bulge growth in gas-rich high-redshift disks.

- [2608.05923](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05923) M. Klein et al. `Optical Counterparts of MeerKLASS L-band and UHF-band surveys`
  - **Question:** how reliably can optical and infrared hosts be assigned to MeerKLASS radio continuum sources?
  - **Data:**
    - Radio: MeerKLASS L-band and UHF-band continuum surveys.
    - Optical/IR: KiDS DR5 and DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys DR10.
    - Products: counterpart probabilities, redshifts, and host-galaxy properties.
  - **Method:**
    - Develop the Stellar-mass Enhanced Density Association method.
    - Compare candidate densities near radio positions with displaced control catalogs.
    - Use positional offset, stellar mass, redshift, and mid-infrared quasar selection.
    - Run a second-pass search for multi-component radio sources with common hosts.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The L-band survey yields `20400` KiDS-based counterparts.
    - The method is designed to handle high optical source density and extended radio morphologies.
    - The catalogs attach redshift and host-property information to wide-area radio continuum detections.
  - **Position:** useful survey product because radio source environments and AGN demographics depend on robust host association.

- [2608.05286](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05286) Hannah B. Koziol et al. `Variations in the 3.3 micron Polycyclic Aromatic Hydrocarbon Feature Across Nearby Galaxies Driven by Metallicity and Radiation Field Spectrum`
  - **Question:** what local ISM properties drive variations in the JWST/NIRCam 3.3 micron PAH feature?
  - **Data:**
    - Facility: JWST/NIRCam imaging.
    - Filters: F335M for the PAH-correlated feature, with F300M and F360M for continuum subtraction.
    - Scale: nearby galaxies at `~0.1 arcsec` resolution.
    - Comparison: optical ionized-gas line ratios and dust models.
  - **Method:**
    - Build an empirical continuum-subtraction method for F335M.
    - Measure F335M/F300M and F360M/F300M color behavior.
    - Compare 3.3/7.7 and 3.3/11.3 micron ratios with metallicity and radiation-field diagnostics.
    - Interpret band-ratio trends with PAH size, charge, and heating changes.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - PAH-correlated color slopes depend on local ISM properties, especially specific star-formation rate.
    - Weaker features in the filters are suppressed relative to 3.3 micron emission in active star-forming regions.
    - 3.3/7.7 and 3.3/11.3 ratios decrease with increasing metallicity.
    - Metallicity and radiation-field spectrum must be considered when using PAH ratios as diagnostics.
  - **Position:** useful local-calibration paper because JWST PAH maps are increasingly used to interpret ISM structure and star formation.

- [2608.05325](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05325) Christina W. Lindberg et al. `Scylla: Observational Evidence for an Order of Magnitude in Dust Mass Opacity Evolution with ISM Density in the Large Magellanic Cloud`
  - **Question:** can density-dependent dust opacity explain discrepancies between FIR-emission and optical-extinction dust masses?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: Large Magellanic Cloud.
    - Observables: extinction and far-infrared emission measurements.
    - Gas scale: total hydrogen surface density `Sigma_H=4-100 M_sun pc^-2`.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare FIR-derived and optically derived dust mass surface densities.
    - Relate the opacity ratio to gas surface density.
    - Interpret the trend as dust mass opacity evolution with intrinsic ISM density.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The FIR dust mass opacity may vary by nearly an order of magnitude.
    - The reported range is `kappa_160=0.3-6 m^2 kg^-2`.
    - The trend supports theoretical models where FIR opacity evolves with density and grain properties.
  - **Position:** useful calibration result for dust-based gas and mass estimates in galaxies.

- [2608.05650](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05650) Michael Anderson et al. `An ALMA study of hub-filament systems II. Quiescent filaments converging towards highly dynamic hubs`
  - **Question:** how do dense-gas kinematics differ between filaments and hub centers in IR-dark hub-filament systems?
  - **Data:**
    - Facility: ALMA 12m+7m+TP.
    - Line: N2H+ `J=1-0`.
    - Sample: six IR-dark hub-filament systems.
    - Resolution: `~3 arcsec`.
  - **Method:**
    - Map dense-gas emission and complex spectra.
    - Fit multiple velocity components with the mwydyn hyperfine line-fitting code.
    - Compare filament outskirts with hub centers.
    - Interpret kinematics in terms of multidirectional inflow.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Quiescent individual filaments converge toward hub centers.
    - Hub centers show higher velocity dispersion and more velocity components.
    - Centroid-velocity distributions are similar between clumps despite a mass range spanning more than an order of magnitude.
    - The results favor gravitationally driven multidirectional inflows feeding dynamic hubs.
  - **Position:** supporting ISM-structure result because filament-hub kinematics are a local analogue for gas transport in dense star-forming environments.
