## Executive View

The read-first item is the JWST/NIRSpec IFU study of Gz9p3. [2608.16996](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16996) Ryota Ikeda resolves an interacting galaxy at `z=9.31` into chemically and dynamically distinct core and tail regions. The key result is a low-metallicity, high-density core plus lower-density, higher-sSFR tail, with outflows below the escape velocity.

The second high-priority item is early-galaxy modeling. [2608.17272](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17272) Kexin Liu uses `50` VariableTNG simulations at `z=6` and `z=8` to isolate which baryonic subgrid parameters regulate low-mass galaxy abundance, metallicity, sizes, and black-hole growth. This is directly useful for interpreting JWST-era high-redshift population tensions.

The CGM methods item is [2608.17013](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17013) Helena Bouchereau on projected velocity structure functions in FOGGIE. It connects simulated CGM turbulence to the observational constraints imposed by projection, resolution, emission sensitivity, and gas temperature.

The baryon-feedback set is broad. [2608.17735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17735) H. Khalil measures eROSITA hot-gas budgets in galaxy groups to `R_200`. [2608.17570](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17570) Stefano Borgani introduces Dianoga cluster and group simulations and shows that the coupling between AGN energy injection and the unresolved ISM model strongly affects BCG masses, stellar fractions, and cool-core structure.

The AGN and environment notes are useful but secondary. [2608.16976](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16976) David Garofalo links X-shaped radio morphology to Eddington ratio, relic-plasma fading, and environment. [2608.16981](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16981) L. C. Debbrecht ties gamma-ray flares in NRAO 150 to downstream VLBI component ejection and inner-jet magnetic structure. [2608.17041](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17041) Christopher W. Morgan uses microlensing of a lensed quasar to separate compact X-ray and larger far-UV continuum regions.

## High-Redshift Galaxies and Early Baryonic Physics

- [2608.16996](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16996) Ryota Ikeda et al. `Diverse ionized gas conditions in a dynamically hot, interacting galaxy at z = 9.31 revealed by JWST/NIRSpec IFU`
  - **Question:** how do ionized-gas conditions vary across an interacting galaxy at `z=9.31`?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: Gz9p3 at `z=9.31`.
    - Instrument: JWST/NIRSpec G395H IFU at spectral resolution `R~2700`.
    - Spatial components: a dense core and an extended tail.
    - Lines: ionized-gas diagnostics including [OIII]5007.
  - **Method:**
    - Resolve the galaxy spatially and spectrally with IFU data.
    - Compare electron density, gas-phase metallicity, and sSFR between the core and tail.
    - Search for ordered rotation in ionized gas.
    - Fit secondary [OIII]5007 components to identify outflowing gas.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The core has high electron density, `n_e>~2900 cm^-3`, and metallicity `0.25 dex` below the mass-metallicity relation.
    - The tail has low electron density, `n_e<70 cm^-3`, metallicity consistent with the mass-metallicity relation, and high sSFR.
    - The ionized gas lacks ordered rotation, so the system is dynamically hot.
    - Outflow velocities remain below the escape velocity, making the system a likely galactic-fountain case.
    - The contrast between core and tail supports efficient pristine-gas inflow after interaction.
  - **Position:** read first because it gives spatially resolved gas physics in a `z>9` galaxy rather than only integrated JWST line ratios.

- [2608.17272](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17272) Kexin Liu et al. `The VariableTNG project: how baryonic mechanisms shape galaxy properties`
  - **Question:** which baryonic mechanisms control early galaxy and black-hole properties at `z=6` and `z=8`?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: `50` Sobol-sampled VariableTNG boxes.
    - Parameters: `8` subgrid parameters varied at fixed cosmology and initial conditions.
    - Observational comparisons: stellar mass function, star-forming main sequence, stellar and gas-phase mass-metallicity relations, stellar mass-size relation, and black-hole host/accretion properties.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare VariableTNG outputs with recent high-redshift observations.
    - Use controlled parameter variation to separate physical responses from cosmic variance.
    - Train random-forest models to rank parameter sensitivity.
    - Trace low-mass galaxy abundance and high-accretion black-hole tails back to feedback parameters.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The simulations reproduce several broad high-redshift trends but retain tensions in gas-phase metallicity, galaxy size, and extreme black-hole populations.
    - Low-mass galaxy abundance is regulated mainly by stellar feedback, especially supernova temperature `T_SN` and thermal wind fraction `tau_w`.
    - The high-accretion black-hole tail depends on black-hole seeding and feedback parameters.
    - `T_SN` also affects rapid black-hole growth indirectly through gas supply.
    - Cosmic variance can hide these responses in independent small volumes.
  - **Position:** high-priority modeling paper because it maps JWST-era population tensions onto specific baryonic-control parameters.

- [2608.17001](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17001) Sarah Hughes et al. `Sr and Ba yields of the First Generation(s) of stars: Constraints from metal-poor stars`
  - **Question:** what Sr and Ba yields are implied by extremely metal-poor stars that preserve first-enrichment signatures?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `10` extremely metal-poor stars.
    - Metallicity range: `-4.05<=[Fe/H]<=-2.33`.
    - Instrument: high-resolution Magellan/MIKE spectra at `R~28,000`.
    - Chemical tracers: Sr and Ba abundances, including stars with `[Sr/H]<-4.5` and `[Ba/H]<-4.0`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure heavy-element abundances for the new stellar sample.
    - Identify Small Accreted Stellar System stars and hyper neutron-capture-element-poor cases.
    - Divide the wider population into four `[Sr/Ba]` groups.
    - Infer site-agnostic Sr yields for the four groups.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Four stars are hyper neutron-capture-element poor with `[Sr/H]<-5.0`.
    - Gaia DR3 5729400267359655680 reaches `[Sr/H]=-6.4`, the lowest detected Sr abundance in the sample context.
    - The `[Sr/Ba]` groups span `-2.0` to `+1.6`, requiring multiple progenitor event types.
    - Most SASS stars formed from gas enriched by only `~1-10` progenitor events.
    - Sr abundance scatter likely reflects intrinsic yield variation by nucleosynthesis site.
  - **Position:** useful fossil-record constraint on first-generation enrichment, adjacent to Pop III and cosmic-dawn chemical-evolution questions.

## CGM, Quenching, and Feedback

- [2608.17013](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17013) Helena Bouchereau et al. `Measuring Simulated Circumgalactic Medium Turbulence with Emission-Weighted Projected Velocity Structure Functions in FOGGIE`
  - **Question:** how reliably can projected emission-line velocity structure functions recover CGM turbulence?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation suite: high-resolution cosmological zoom-in FOGGIE simulations.
    - Observable proxy: spatially resolved emission-line kinematics.
    - Diagnostic: velocity structure functions and their turnover scale.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare 3D velocity structure functions with emission-weighted projected 2D versions.
    - Vary measurement area as a proxy for nebular size at different sensitivities.
    - Test projection, resolution, and gas-temperature effects.
    - Interpret the VSF turnover as a turbulence-driving-scale proxy.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Projection lowers the VSF normalization but does not strongly change the slope.
    - Measurement-area size correlates directly with the inferred turnover location.
    - Instrument sensitivity and nebular extent can bias the inferred turbulent driving scale.
    - Gas temperature and resolution also measurably affect VSF structure.
  - **Position:** high-value CGM methods paper because it turns simulated gas kinematics into observational caveats for emission-based turbulence work.

- [2608.17011](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17011) Ignacio G. Alfaro et al. `The VariableTNG project: Unveiling the physical drivers of galaxy quenching`
  - **Question:** which feedback parameters dominate the quenched fraction across stellar mass, black-hole mass, gas mass, and environment?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: `26` VariableTNG boxes.
    - Parameters: `8` galaxy-formation parameters varied with fixed initial conditions.
    - Galaxy samples: total population, central galaxies, and satellite galaxies.
    - Observables: quenched fraction versus stellar mass, black-hole mass, and gas mass.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure quenched-fraction variation relative to TNG100-1.
    - Train random-forest regressors on the parameter variations.
    - Use SHAP values to quantify magnitude and direction of parameter influence.
    - Compare model responses with observational passive-galaxy measurements.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - A small subset of parameters dominates quenched-fraction variance.
    - Stellar-feedback wind parameters dominate at low stellar mass.
    - AGN-related parameters become more important at higher stellar mass.
    - Supernova temperature matters at both extremes of the stellar-mass range.
    - The transition persists when central and satellite galaxies are separated.
  - **Position:** useful simulation-control paper for interpreting quenching trends before applying environment claims to observations.

- [2608.17735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17735) H. Khalil et al. `eROSITA cosmology with galaxy groups: hot gas budget out to the virial radius`
  - **Question:** how much hot gas do galaxy groups retain out to the virial radius after baryonic feedback?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: eROSITA-DE eRASS1.
    - Sample: `25` galaxy groups selected from the Two Micron Redshift Survey optical group catalogue.
    - Radial range: hot-gas measurements out to `R_200`.
    - Scaling relations: `M_gas-M_tot`, `L_X-M_tot`, and `L_X-M_gas`.
  - **Method:**
    - Extract and fit X-ray surface-brightness profiles.
    - Build hot-gas mass and hot-gas fraction profiles.
    - Use Bayesian scaling-relation fits with aperture covariance.
    - Compare gas fractions at `R_500` and `R_200`.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Surface-brightness profiles are uniformly flat at `R_500`, with mean beta parameter `0.38+/-0.04`.
    - The outer hot-gas mass slope is consistent with the total-mass slope.
    - Gas fractions stay below the universal baryon fraction at both `R_500` and `R_200`.
    - Groups above `10^14 M_sun` can be nearly baryon-complete when stellar mass is included.
    - Lower-mass groups likely require gas beyond `R_200` or stronger non-thermal pressure support.
  - **Position:** relevant for Mpc-scale feedback accounting, especially when comparing group environments with protocluster descendant expectations.

- [2608.17570](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17570) Stefano Borgani et al. `Dianoga simulations of galaxy clusters and groups: Properties of the baryonic components`
  - **Question:** how do AGN feedback and star-formation implementations affect baryons in simulated clusters and groups?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: Dianoga cluster and group simulations using OpenGadget3.
    - Sample: `293` halos with `M_200>1.5 x 10^13 M_sun`.
    - Calibration: AGN feedback minimally calibrated to local SMBH-host stellar mass relation.
    - Comparisons: GSMF, stellar mass fractions, BCG masses, and ICM/IGM thermodynamic profiles.
  - **Method:**
    - Simulate `28` massive-cluster-centered regions plus a cosmological box.
    - Compare the reference model to observed galaxy and gas scaling relations.
    - Vary the reference feedback model in six alternative configurations.
    - Identify which implementation details move baryonic observables toward observations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The reference model broadly matches the GSMF but overestimates the high-mass end.
    - BCG stellar masses and stellar fractions are too high in massive clusters.
    - Simulated core entropy and temperature profiles are less cool-cored than observed.
    - Including thermal evaporation of the sub-resolution ISM can lower BCG masses and stellar fractions.
    - The interface between AGN energy injection and sub-resolution ISM modeling is as important as total feedback efficiency.
  - **Position:** important cluster-feedback comparison set for interpreting baryon partition and cool-core survival in massive environments.

## AGN, Quasars, and Compact Engines

- [2608.16976](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16976) David Garofalo `A Critical Eddington Ratio for X-Shaped Radio Galaxies`
  - **Question:** what accretion-rate condition allows X-shaped radio galaxies to show two visible jet axes?
  - **Data:**
    - Model inputs: black-hole spin evolution and relic radio-plasma fading.
    - Timescale estimates: spin transition time and synchrotron-fading time.
    - Observational context: low observed X-shaped radio-galaxy fraction of `~1-5%`.
  - **Method:**
    - Require the spin-transition timescale to be shorter than the relic-fading timescale.
    - Estimate `t_trans~5 million/lambda yr`, where `lambda` is the Eddington ratio.
    - Estimate `t_fade~5-20 Myr` from synchrotron break-frequency evolution.
    - Map the resulting threshold onto environmental differences in accretion mode.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The model gives a critical Eddington-ratio range `lambda_crit~0.3-1`.
    - Systems above that threshold can show X-shaped morphology.
    - Rich-cluster feedback tends to drive radiatively inefficient states with `lambda<<lambda_crit`.
    - The rarity of X-shaped radio galaxies follows from a short overlap window plus geometry and detectability.
  - **Position:** useful AGN-environment note because it makes radio morphology depend on accretion state and cluster gas conditions.

- [2608.16981](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16981) L. C. Debbrecht et al. `Probing the gamma-ray emission region and the connection to jet ejections in NRAO 150 with VLBI`
  - **Question:** where is the gamma-ray emission region in the blazar NRAO 150 relative to the VLBI core?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: NRAO 150.
    - Facilities: VLBA and EVN.
    - Time span: `2010-2019`.
    - Observables: `43 GHz` total intensity, linear polarization, radio light curves, and gamma-ray light curves.
  - **Method:**
    - Track VLBI jet-component kinematics.
    - Compare radio and gamma-ray flares with component ejections.
    - Inspect polarization changes before, during, and after a gamma-ray flare.
    - Infer magnetic-field geometry in the inner jet.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Gamma-ray flaring is temporally linked to new VLBI component ejections.
    - The high-energy emission is likely produced downstream of the VLBI core.
    - Kinematic and polarimetric evidence points to a toroidal magnetic field in the inner jet.
    - Magnetic fields regulate both jet dynamics and high-energy emission.
  - **Position:** secondary AGN-jet paper, useful for compact-engine physics rather than galaxy-environment mapping.

- [2608.17041](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17041) Christopher W. Morgan et al. `The X-Ray Continuum Emission Region in the Lensed Quasar SDSS J133907.23+131038.6 is Much Smaller than the Accretion Disk`
  - **Question:** how compact are the X-ray and far-UV continuum regions in a lensed quasar?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: doubly imaged lensed quasar SDSS J133907.23+131038.6.
    - Optical monitoring: `15` seasons.
    - X-ray data: `4` new epochs.
    - Black-hole mass scale: `4.0 x 10^8 M_sun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Use Bayesian Monte Carlo microlensing analysis.
    - Fit the far-UV half-light radius from optical light curves.
    - Fit the full-band `0.2-8.0 keV` X-ray half-light radius.
    - Search stacked spectra for microlensed Fe K-alpha signatures.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The far-UV half-light radius is `log(r_1/2,FUV/cm)=15.78+0.26/-0.28`.
    - The far-UV source corresponds to `~100 r_g`.
    - The full-band X-ray half-light radius is `log(r_1/2,Xfull/cm)=14.32+0.23/-0.31`.
    - The X-ray source is consistent with the Schwarzschild ISCO scale.
    - Microlensed Fe K-alpha lines are detected in stacked image-A spectra at `>99%` significance.
  - **Position:** useful quasar-engine constraint, but less connected to large-scale environment than the CGM and group-feedback entries.

## Structure, Stellar Populations, and Calibration Notes

- [2608.17375](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17375) Arsen Levitskiy et al. `Diffuse Dwarf Galaxies in Galaxy Clusters: I. Stellar Populations and Radial Gradients`
  - **Question:** do cluster diffuse dwarfs preserve stellar-population signatures of their environment and assembly history?
  - **Data:**
    - Instrument: Keck/KCWI spectroscopy.
    - Sample: one UDG and five Nearly-UDGs in Perseus plus one UDG in Coma.
    - Measurements: global age, metallicity, `[Mg/Fe]`, mass-metallicity placement, and radial gradients.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure global stellar populations for the cluster diffuse-galaxy sample.
    - Compare positions against present-day and high-redshift mass-metallicity relations.
    - Test correlations with cluster infall parameters.
    - Measure radial gradients for three galaxies.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Typical ages are `~7 Gyr`.
    - Metallicities are low, around `[M/H]~-0.9 dex`, with enhanced `[Mg/Fe]~0.3 dex`.
    - Six galaxies lie within the present-day mass-metallicity relation scatter.
    - Coma UDG DF11 is more consistent with the `z~2` mass-metallicity relation.
    - Rising metallicity profiles may result from preferential tidal disruption of globular clusters in inner regions.
  - **Position:** useful cluster-dwarf benchmark for environmental processing, though the redshift connection is indirect.

- [2608.17680](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17680) Alister W. Graham `$R_{\rm e}$, or not $R_{\rm e}$: Developing $R_5\equiv R_{-2}$ as a scale radius for galaxy sizes, masses, and mass-to-light ratios`
  - **Question:** can a gradient-defined radius provide a more physically useful galaxy scale than the half-light radius?
  - **Data:**
    - Galaxy-profile framework: projected intensity profiles and Sersic models.
    - Proposed scale: `R_5=R_-2`, where the logarithmic intensity slope is `-2`.
    - Companion tooling: R5Tools.
  - **Method:**
    - Define `R_5` as the radius where luminosity per logarithmic radial interval is maximal.
    - Derive the exact Sersic relation between `R_5` and `R_e`.
    - Reparameterize scaling relations around the gradient-defined radius.
    - Apply the framework to sizes, masses, and mass-to-light ratios.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - `R_e` depends on an arbitrary `50%` light boundary.
    - `R_5` can be measured non-parametrically or with profile fitting.
    - For Sersic profiles, `R_5=(2n/b_n)^n R_e`.
    - As `n` approaches infinity, `R_5/R_e` approaches `1.181`.
    - The proposed radius may make some structural scaling relations less boundary-dependent.
  - **Position:** methods note for galaxy structure work, relevant when morphology and size enter environmental comparisons.

- [2608.16989](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16989) Alena Rottensteiner et al. `A comprehensive cluster census of Orion. An application of the Significance Mode Analysis (SigMA) algorithm`
  - **Question:** how completely can Gaia-era clustering recover the substructure and age map of Orion?
  - **Data:**
    - Region: Orion star-forming complex.
    - Algorithm: updated Significance Mode Analysis.
    - Sample: `11,996` reliable members in `47` co-spatial and co-moving groups.
    - Ages: `1.5-25 Myr`.
  - **Method:**
    - Apply SigMA to Orion with extensions for distant regions beyond `300 pc`.
    - Estimate persistence values and membership probabilities from `10` clustering repetitions.
    - Compare group memberships with prior literature.
    - Identify known clusters, known substructures, and new candidates.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - SigMA finds `~2-3` times more members than previous algorithms.
    - It recovers `28` known clusters and three previously classified substructures.
    - It identifies `16` new coeval substructure candidates.
    - The resulting time-resolved map connects stellar populations to surrounding ISM structure.
  - **Position:** lower priority for this profile, but useful as a clustering-method example for resolved nearby structure.
