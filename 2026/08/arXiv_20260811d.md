## High-Redshift Galaxies and Reionization

- [2608.09813](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09813) Shengzhe Wang et al. `An emerging baryon cycle in a galaxy 500 million years after the Big Bang`
  - **Question:** when does stellar feedback begin regulating gas cycling in the first massive galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: Gz9p3, a merging galaxy at `z=9.311`, about `500 Myr` after the Big Bang.
    - Spectroscopy: deep JWST observations tracing neutral gas and outflow phases.
    - Gas diagnostic: fine-structure absorption measuring the cool outflow electron density.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify neutral gas along the merger-driven tidal structure.
    - Separate multiphase outflow signatures from the host-galaxy gas reservoir.
    - Use fine-structure absorption to infer the cool outflow density.
    - Compare the implied feedback state with feedback-free starburst expectations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Gz9p3 contains a substantial neutral-gas reservoir and a multiphase outflow.
    - The cool outflow has electron density `~17 cm^-3`, reported as the first direct high-redshift measurement of this kind.
    - The inferred mass-loading factor is among the highest measured for comparable stellar mass.
    - The cool outflow is unlikely to escape the halo, so enriched gas may recycle through the CGM.
  - **Position:** read first because it connects merger-driven gas redistribution, bursty star formation, and feedback-regulated recycling during reionization.

- [2608.08203](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08203) Nicholas S. Martis et al. `A New Window on the Halpha Luminosity Function and Star Formation Rate Density from 1.2 < z < 6.6 from JWST Medium-Band Photometry`
  - **Question:** how does the Halpha luminosity function evolve from cosmic noon into the epoch of reionization?
  - **Data:**
    - Imaging: JWST/NIRCam medium-band data from CANUCS, JWST in Technicolor, and JUMPS.
    - Filter set: up to `29` total filters, including up to `20` JWST filters plus HST data.
    - Depth: `29.5-30 AB` at `3 sigma`.
    - Redshift range: `1.25<z<6.6`.
  - **Method:**
    - Use dense SED sampling for photometric redshifts and continuum subtraction.
    - Trace Halpha through `11` medium-band filters.
    - Verify line fluxes against spectroscopy.
    - Correct for dust attenuation and derive star-formation-rate functions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Photometric redshifts reach outlier fraction `1.7%` and `sigma_NMAD=0.039`.
    - Spectroscopic comparison gives no systematic line-flux offset and `0.23 dex` scatter.
    - The Halpha luminosity function spans nearly `3` dex in luminosity.
    - The star-formation-rate density peaks near `z~2` and declines to `z=6`, with a high normalization relative to UV estimates.
  - **Position:** high-value survey result because Halpha provides a direct star-formation tracer across the redshift range where UV and IR estimates differ.

- [2608.08015](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08015) Gitanjali Rajulal et al. `Breathing Fire: Hot Dust in the Big Three Dragons at z = 7.15`
  - **Question:** how much obscured star formation is present in a bright merging LBG system at `z>7`?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: the Big Three Dragons, a pair of merging LBGs at `z=7.15`.
    - New observations: ALMA Band 9 at `0.45 mm` and Band 4 at `2.2 mm`.
    - Prior data: ALMA Bands 6, 7, and 8 at `0.73-1.32 mm`.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine multi-band ALMA photometry.
    - Fit modified blackbody dust models.
    - Use resolved ALMA imaging to compare the eastern and western components.
    - Place the components relative to IRX-beta expectations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Band 4 gives a `3.5 sigma` detection, and Band 9 detects the two LBGs at `3.7` and `3.5 sigma`.
    - The global dust temperature is `T_d=78(-23,+35) K`.
    - The system reaches `log(L_IR/L_sun)=12.32(-0.41,+0.43)` and `SFR_UV+IR=267(-153,+418) M_sun yr^-1`.
    - The obscured fraction is `f_obs=0.94(-0.09,+0.04)`.
  - **Position:** directly relevant to reionization-era census work because bright UV-selected galaxies can hide most of their star formation in hot dust.

- [2608.07668](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07668) Jack C. Turner et al. `First Light and Assembly of GalaxieS (FLAGS) I: The JWST/NIRCam Number Counts and IGL as Constraints on Galaxy Formation Models`
  - **Question:** can JWST/NIRCam number counts constrain galaxy-formation models more robustly than SED-inferred physical parameters?
  - **Data:**
    - Imaging: NIRCam data over `>1 deg^2`.
    - Fields: `30` independent JWST fields.
    - Wavelength range: `0.9-4.4 micron`.
    - Observables: galaxy number counts and integrated galaxy light.
  - **Method:**
    - Process NIRCam imaging consistently across fields.
    - Measure direct number counts and integrated galaxy light.
    - Compare these observables against galaxy evolution models.
    - Assess photometry and forward-modeling uncertainties.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Integrated galaxy light is measured to `~2.5%` at the longest wavelengths.
    - The values are `6.92(+0.17,-0.17)` and `3.46(+0.09,-0.08) nW m^-2 sr^-1` at `2.77` and `4.10 micron`.
    - Integrated galaxy light is an unreliable model-performance metric.
    - Direct number counts favor SC-SAM with `chi^2_nu=18.6`.
  - **Position:** useful JWST methods entry because it shifts model comparison toward direct observables before physical-parameter inference.

## CGM, Gas Flows, and Feedback

- [2608.09418](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09418) Sumukha R. Bharadwaj et al. `A Partial Lyman Limit Absorber in the Halo of a Galaxy Pair: A Possible Signature of Gas Inflow`
  - **Question:** does a partial Lyman-limit absorber near a galaxy pair trace inflow, interaction debris, or another CGM component?
  - **Data:**
    - Absorber: partial Lyman-limit system at `z=0.87641`.
    - Background source: HST/COS spectrum of quasar LBQS 0107-0235.
    - Galaxy data: VLT/MUSE detection of two galaxies at the absorber redshift.
  - **Method:**
    - Model the HI Lyman series and metal-line absorption as a single component.
    - Run photoionization modeling for density and metallicity.
    - Compare absorber orientation and velocity with rotating galaxy disks.
    - Interpret the configuration against cold-mode accretion and CGM-flow simulations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The gas has metallicity `log10(Z/Z_sun)=-1.05`.
    - The hydrogen density is `n_H~8.5 x 10^-4 cm^-3`.
    - The two nearby galaxies lie at `rho/R_vir~0.9` with velocity offsets of `18` and `99 km s^-1`.
    - The absorber lies close to both projected major axes, consistent with a sub-solar metallicity inflowing stream, though interaction gas remains possible.
  - **Position:** strong CGM entry because it maps absorber metallicity, kinematics, and galaxy orientation onto inflow diagnostics.

- [2608.07752](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07752) Mark A. Croom et al. `The Circumgalactic Medium of Present-Epoch Luminous Galaxies as Probed by CIV Absorption`
  - **Question:** what CGM geometry is traced by CIV around isolated luminous galaxies at low redshift?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `88` isolated bright galaxies with `L_B>0.2L*_B`.
    - Redshift: `z<0.03`.
    - Absorption: CIV `1548,1550` detected against background sightlines.
    - Selection: galaxies chosen without prior knowledge of CGM absorption.
  - **Method:**
    - Search for CIV absorption above `5 sigma` and `W_r>0.1 A`.
    - Compare detections with projected separation, virial radius, azimuthal angle, and star formation.
    - Test whether absorber azimuths are randomly distributed.
    - Interpret the absorber geometry with spatial-kinematic wind models.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - CIV absorption is found in `9/88` galaxies within `R_perp<350 kpc`.
    - All nine absorbers lie within `30 deg` of projected minor axes and within `1.2R_vir` of star-forming galaxies.
    - Random azimuthal distribution is rejected at `99.998%`, or `4.3 sigma`.
    - The covering fraction for star-forming minor-axis sightlines is `f_cov~0.5`, implying patchy or transient winds.
  - **Position:** useful low-redshift CGM benchmark because minor-axis CIV provides a clean wind-geometry comparison for higher-redshift absorber work.

- [2608.08275](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08275) M. Bianchin et al. `The Quasar Feedback Survey: Ionised Outflows in type 2 QSOs with MUSE data`
  - **Question:** how do electron-density assumptions affect ionized-outflow rates and energetics in luminous obscured QSOs?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `18` type 2 QSOs with `log L_bol>=45 erg s^-1`.
    - Redshift range: `0.085<z<0.2`.
    - IFU data: VLT/MUSE from the Quasar Feedback Survey.
    - Radio data: VLA low- and high-resolution imaging.
  - **Method:**
    - Use non-parametric [O III] `5007` kinematics.
    - Define outflow-dominated spaxels using nuclear `W80`.
    - Estimate ionized gas mass from extinction-corrected [O III] flux.
    - Measure electron density with the [Ar IV] `4711,4740` doublet in integrated spectra.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Electron densities span `500<n_e<27700 cm^-3`.
    - Mass outflow rates are `0.03<dot(M)_out<4.17 M_sun yr^-1`.
    - Kinetic powers span `10^39-10^42 erg s^-1`.
    - Electron-density treatment changes outflow rates and kinetic powers by about `1 dex`.
  - **Position:** important feedback-method paper because outflow energetics depend on directly measured density rather than assumed values.

- [2608.08369](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08369) Lulu Zhang et al. `GATOS: Distinct Feedback Modes in AGN Central Regions Revealed by Spatially Resolved JWST Spectroscopy`
  - **Question:** can JWST mid-infrared spectroscopy separate radiative and kinetic AGN feedback modes on nuclear scales?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: five AGN spanning `log L_bol/erg s^-1~39.8-43.8`.
    - Instrument: JWST/MIRI MRS.
    - Spatial scale: central `r~40-240 pc` regions at `~4-24 pc` resolution.
    - Diagnostics: PAHs, molecular hydrogen, and ionized gas.
  - **Method:**
    - Map PAH, H2, and ionized-gas diagnostics.
    - Associate irradiation with radiative feedback.
    - Associate shocks, jets, or outflows with kinetic feedback.
    - Compare nuclear PAH suppression and PAH band ratios with feedback mode.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Radiative and kinetic feedback jointly suppress PAH emission in the nuclear regions.
    - Shock processing from jets or outflows explains bimodal PAH band ratios.
    - The result provides a practical JWST-era framework for diagnosing AGN feedback and PAH-based SFR calibration.
  - **Position:** useful AGN-ISM paper because JWST/MIRI resolves the physical channels behind central star-formation tracer suppression.

- [2608.07665](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07665) Kristen B. W. McQuinn et al. `GLOW II: A Census of Oxygen in Low-Mass Galaxies`
  - **Question:** how much oxygen produced by low-mass galaxies remains in the ISM, CGM, or outside the baryon budget?
  - **Data:**
    - Program: Galaxies Losing Oxygen via Winds.
    - Sample: `37` low-mass, low-metallicity, gas-rich galaxies within `D<6 Mpc`.
    - Stellar-mass range: `10^6.5<Mstar<10^9.5`, extended with literature to `10^5<Mstar<10^11.5`.
    - Measurements: resolved-star star-formation histories, ISM metallicities, gas masses, environment metrics, and CGM constraints.
  - **Method:**
    - Couple star-formation histories to oxygen production.
    - Compare retained oxygen with ISM gas and empirical CGM oxygen.
    - Test trends with environment and mass-metallicity residuals.
    - Compare observed retention against hydrodynamical simulations and simple wind models.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Low-mass galaxies retain `4-25%` of their produced oxygen, with mean `11%`.
    - Nearly all retained oxygen is in the gas phase.
    - Retention scatter increases in denser environments with `Theta_5>0.5` and within `1 Mpc` of a `Mstar>10^9 M_sun` galaxy.
    - Simulations that reproduce the mass-metallicity relation overpredict oxygen retention.
  - **Position:** supporting baryon-cycle benchmark because local low-mass galaxies constrain feedback, enrichment, and missing-metals accounting.

## Environments, Surveys, and Methods

- [2608.08393](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08393) Junho Song et al. `Constructing a Hydrogen Line Library for Lyalpha Emitters at Low Redshifts (z < 0.4): Estimating Dust Extinction and Assessing Paschen line Detectability with SPHEREx`
  - **Question:** how can low-redshift hydrogen-line data calibrate dust, Lyalpha escape, and future Paschen-line surveys?
  - **Data:**
    - Compilation: `260` galaxies mostly at `z<0.4`.
    - Lines: Halpha, Hbeta, Hgamma, and Lyalpha.
    - Extinction subsample: `85` galaxies with at least two Balmer-line detections.
    - Curve-optimization subsample: `14` galaxies with all three Balmer lines.
  - **Method:**
    - Compile published hydrogen recombination-line measurements.
    - Infer dust extinction with Case B recombination and the Calzetti curve.
    - Estimate Lyalpha escape fractions.
    - Model Paschen-line detectability for SPHEREx-like near-infrared surveys.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The line library provides a low-redshift calibration set for hydrogen-line-selected galaxies.
    - Balmer-line ratios support dust-extinction estimates for `85` galaxies.
    - Three-Balmer-line systems show varied dust-attenuation curve slopes.
    - Paschen-line modeling informs future SPHEREx line-selection work.
  - **Position:** useful calibration paper because Lyalpha and Balmer diagnostics anchor high-redshift star-formation and ionizing-photon studies.

- [2608.09192](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09192) Vignesh V.V. Rao et al. `Statistical inference of fast radio burst environments using galaxy number density`
  - **Question:** do repeating and non-repeating FRBs occupy different large-scale galaxy environments?
  - **Data:**
    - FRB sample: `19` repeaters and `253` non-repeaters from CHIME Catalog 1.
    - Galaxy catalog: WISE x PS1.
    - Environment metric: galaxy number density around FRB positions.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare repeater and non-repeater environment distributions.
    - Use Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests for population differences.
    - Compare FRB apertures with random galaxy fields.
    - Interpret environment density as a statistical host-population clue.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Repeaters and non-repeaters show no significant density-distribution difference, with `p_KS=0.673`.
    - The result remains limited by localized-sample size.
    - FRBs may preferentially occupy underdense galaxy environments relative to random apertures, with median `p_KS=2.84 x 10^-2`.
  - **Position:** relevant environment-method paper because galaxy density can become a host-proxy observable for large transient samples.

- [2608.09566](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09566) Gunter K. Heemann et al. `Searching for low-surface-brightness galaxies with compact neural networks A parameter-efficient approach to first-pass selection of diffuse galaxy in HSC-SSP imaging`
  - **Question:** can compact neural networks provide scalable first-pass selection of low-surface-brightness galaxies in HSC-SSP imaging?
  - **Data:**
    - Imaging: HSC-SSP.
    - Initial detections: about `77 x 10^6` objects.
    - Final product: Gold catalog of `5156` LSBG candidates.
    - Network size: about `10^4` trainable parameters.
  - **Method:**
    - Run permissive low-threshold source detection with SEP.
    - Apply compact-CNN morphological filtering.
    - Add physically motivated consistency checks.
    - Fit parametric surface-brightness models with galfitm.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The compact CNN reduces the initial object set by more than `4` orders of magnitude.
    - The pipeline recovers essentially all literature LSBGs that pass the detection stage.
    - SDSS and DESI spectroscopic cross-matches support a nearby diffuse-galaxy interpretation.
    - The current bottleneck is source extraction rather than morphological selection.
  - **Position:** useful HSC methods paper because diffuse-galaxy selection affects environment and low-mass structure studies.

- [2608.09086](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09086) Youcai Zhang et al. `Multi-tracer mass bias in matched cosmic voids from SDSS DR7 and the ELUCID constrained simulation`
  - **Question:** how do galaxy and subhalo tracers bias mass measurements inside matched cosmic voids?
  - **Data:**
    - Observations: SDSS DR7 galaxy catalog.
    - Simulation: ELUCID constrained simulation.
    - Void sample: `102` matched void pairs.
    - Tracers: galaxies, subhaloes, and dark matter.
  - **Method:**
    - Match observed and simulated voids in a constrained local-Universe realization.
    - Compare galaxy-to-dark-matter, subhalo-to-dark-matter, and galaxy-to-subhalo mass ratios.
    - Test independent and common coordinate frameworks.
    - Quantify statistical limits in inner void regions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Galaxy-to-dark-matter and subhalo-to-dark-matter mass ratios decline toward void centers.
    - Luminous and halo tracers are depleted relative to matter in deep underdensities.
    - Galaxy-to-subhalo ratios become highly uncertain at `r/R_v<=0.5`.
    - Scarcity of massive subhaloes with `log10(M_sub/h^-1 M_sun)>=11.8` limits inner-void multi-tracer measurements.
  - **Position:** useful large-scale-structure entry because tracer bias in voids is an edge case for environment reconstruction.
