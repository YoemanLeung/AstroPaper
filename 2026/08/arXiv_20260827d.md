## Executive View

The read-first item is [2608.25021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25021) Sara Mascia on compact blue broad-line emitters at `4<=z<=7`. The sample starts from `4145` PRISM-selected galaxies and isolates `20` compact blue broad-line sources with hot ionizing continua, high-ionization UV lines, and high Lya emitter fractions. It directly links LRD-like engines, escape fractions, and local reionization impact.

The strongest high-redshift ISM paper is [2608.25982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25982) Lucie E. Rowland on REBELS-25 at `z=7.31`. ALMA [OIII]88um, [CII], dust continuum, and JWST/NIRSpec IFU data show a dusty, chemically enriched, dynamically cold system with large obscured star formation and shared warm-ionized/cold-neutral rotation.

The most useful methods entry is [2608.25364](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25364) J. A. Hernandez-Guajardo on effective impact parameters in IFU absorption-line tomography. It gives a practical correction for gravitational-arc CGM studies where PSF smearing and source extent make geometric spaxel centers biased.

The best large-scale-structure entry is [2608.25060](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25060) Constanza A. Soto-Suarez on galaxy bias through IllustrisTNG cosmic-web filaments. It links enhanced large-scale bias to node proximity, filament length, and longitudinal position along filament spines.

AGN and black-hole readers should also check [2608.25341](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25341) Yuxuan Zou on long-timescale AGN gas depletion, [2608.25571](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25571) Ross J. ## Reionization-Era Galaxies and High-Redshift Systems

- [2608.25021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25021) Sara Mascia et al. `The ionizing properties of JWST's compact broad-line emitters`
  - **Question:** do compact blue broad-line emitters at `4<=z<=7` contribute meaningful ionizing radiation beyond normal galaxies and LRDs?
  - **Data:**
    - Parent sample: `4145` galaxies with NIRSpec/PRISM spectroscopy and NIRCam imaging from the DJA.
    - Final sample: `20` compact blue broad-line emitters.
    - Selection: broad Halpha with `FWHM>2000 km s^-1`, `S/N>=5`, and completeness at `L_Halpha,broad>=10^42.7 erg s^-1`.
    - Diagnostics: UV continuum slope, optical slope, morphology, high-ionization UV lines, Lya emitter fraction, and X-ray non-detections.
  - **Method:**
    - Select compact systems where UV continuum and broad-line emission come from the same small physical region.
    - Compare compact blue broad-line emitters against LRDs and star-forming galaxies.
    - Model spectra with Sirocco to infer physical configurations and ionizing output.
    - Estimate ionizing photon production efficiency and escape fractions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Compact blue broad-line emitters are `~20%` of the PRISM-selected broad-line sample.
    - Their Lya emitter fraction is `50-67%`, elevated relative to star-forming galaxies and LRDs.
    - The bluest sources reach `log xi_ion~25.4 Hz erg^-1`.
    - The weighted escape fraction is `f_esc~0.2` for the bluest sources and `~0.01` for LRDs.
    - Broad-line sources do not dominate global reionization, but their local contribution can dominate on scales of a few Mpc.
  - **Position:** read first because it links LRD-like broad-line engines to reionization, escape fractions, and source environments at exactly the redshifts where JWST demographics are being revised.

- [2608.25982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25982) Lucie E. Rowland et al. `REBELS-25: multi-phase morphology and kinematics at z = 7.31`
  - **Question:** what are the resolved multiphase ISM morphology and kinematics of a massive star-forming galaxy at `z=7.31`?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: REBELS-25 at `z=7.31`.
    - ALMA: high-resolution [OIII]88um, matched [CII], and dust continuum.
    - JWST: NIRSpec IFU spectroscopy.
    - Resolution: `<=1 kpc` view of morphology, ISM conditions, and gas kinematics.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare rest-frame UV, optical, far-infrared, [CII], [OIII], and dust morphologies.
    - Estimate obscured and unobscured star-formation contributions across the galaxy.
    - Use resolved ionization-line diagnostics to test ISM variation.
    - Model warm-ionized and cold-neutral gas kinematics.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - UV and optical emission are clumpy and irregular, while FIR emission is close to an exponential disk.
    - Obscured star formation contributes `~55-98%` of the total SFR across the system.
    - [OIII]88um and [CII] share the same large-scale rotating structure.
    - The gas is dynamically cold, with `V/sigma~11` for [OIII] and `~4.5` for [CII].
    - Non-circular motions remain and are not captured by a simple rotating-disk model.
  - **Position:** high-priority high-z ISM paper because it gives resolved evidence that dusty, chemically enriched, ordered gas disks already exist deep in the reionization era.

- [2608.25671](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25671) M. P. Koprowski et al. `Stellar mass growth in COSMOS-Web: a mass-complete main sequence to z ~ 8 and its consistency with GSMF evolution`
  - **Question:** does a mass-complete star-forming main sequence to `z=8` reproduce the evolution of the galaxy stellar mass function?
  - **Data:**
    - Field: COSMOS-Web.
    - Sample: `251,462` star-forming galaxies.
    - Stacks: Herschel and JCMT maps with blending corrections from forward-modeled maps.
    - Growth constraint: observed GSMF evolution and quiescent fractions.
  - **Method:**
    - Stack far-infrared and submillimeter maps to estimate obscured SFRs.
    - Combine infrared luminosity with unobscured UV emission.
    - Fit a redshift-dependent main sequence with a high-mass turnover.
    - Evolve the `z=8` GSMF forward with stellar-mass return, quiescent suppression, and mergers.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The low-mass main-sequence slope is `gamma=1.215+/-0.028`.
    - The main sequence turns over toward high masses.
    - The inferred relation better reproduces the progressive flattening of the low-mass GSMF than tested literature prescriptions.
    - Mergers alter low-mass number densities by `<=0.2 dex` by `z~0.65`.
    - Small normalization differences accumulate into large GSMF differences over several Gyr.
  - **Position:** useful for high-z galaxy-growth work because it ties instantaneous SFR scaling directly to the integral mass buildup constraint.

- [2608.25044](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25044) Aurora Mata-Sanchez et al. `To be lensed or not to be lensed: on the nature of the alleged high-z lensed quasars J0109-5424 and P170+20`
  - **Question:** are the alleged `z>6` lensed quasars J0109-5424 and P170+20 actually gravitationally lensed high-redshift quasars?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRSpec: near-infrared Aether survey data for J0109-5424.
    - HST/ACS: WFC F555W imaging of P170+20 below the quasar Lyman limit.
    - Gemini: Flamingos 2 near-infrared observations plus existing GMOS optical spectra.
    - Targets: two proposed high-redshift lensed quasar systems.
  - **Method:**
    - Analyze multi-wavelength spectra and imaging for lensing signatures.
    - Model and subtract the HST point-spread function around P170+20.
    - Use Pabeta emission for black-hole mass and bolometric-luminosity estimates in J0109-5424.
    - Test whether P170+20 is better described by a quasar plus ultracool-dwarf composite.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - J0109-5424 is a FeLoBAL quasar at `z=2.07`, not a lensed `z>6` quasar.
    - Its inferred black-hole mass is `3.9 x 10^8 Msun`.
    - Its bolometric luminosity is `7.7 x 10^45 erg s^-1`.
    - P170+20 has a compact source `0.09+/-0.04 arcsec` from the quasar position, but no convincing foreground-lens galaxy.
    - P170+20 may be a quasar plus M-dwarf composite, though that interpretation is not secure.
  - **Position:** important high-z quasar vetting paper because it shows how FeLoBAL features and unresolved contaminants can distort the census of bright early quasars.

## CGM, Cosmic Web, and Survey Methods

- [2608.25364](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25364) J. A. Hernandez-Guajardo et al. `Recovering the effective impact parameters in integral-field absorption-line tomography`
  - **Question:** how should IFU absorption studies assign impact parameters when extended background arcs and PSF smearing move the absorbed flux away from the geometric spaxel center?
  - **Data:**
    - Configuration: extended gravitational arcs used as dense background sightline grids.
    - Foreground target: CGM absorption around galaxies.
    - Inputs: high-resolution imaging, IFU spaxels, PSF information, and simulated absorption profiles.
    - Test case: data-driven simulation based on a real gravitational-arc configuration.
  - **Method:**
    - Reconstruct the spatial origin of the flux entering each spaxel.
    - Define flux-weighted coordinates and effective impact parameters.
    - Compare geometric spaxel-center coordinates with flux-weighted positions.
    - Test recovery of radial column-density profiles under PSF uncertainty.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Spaxel centers are displaced from flux-weighted positions by `|Delta x| proportional to theta_PSF(1-f)`.
    - The impact-parameter bias scales approximately as `mu^-1/2` with lensing magnification.
    - Off-arc spaxels suffer the largest offsets and can flatten radial column-density profiles.
    - Geometric coordinates leave residuals around `chi^2_nu~4`; flux-weighted coordinates recover profiles near `chi^2_nu~1`.
    - The correction remains robust to PSF misspecification of `+/-10%`.
  - **Position:** high-priority methods paper for CGM tomography because it turns a geometric nuisance into a measurable correction for arc-based absorption maps.

- [2608.25060](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25060) Constanza A. Soto-Suarez et al. `Tracing Galaxy Bias Through the Cosmic Web: The Role of Filaments`
  - **Question:** how does large-scale galaxy bias vary with position inside cosmic-web filaments?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: IllustrisTNG TNG300.
    - Web finder: DisPerSE.
    - Environments: nodes, filaments, filament outskirts, and saddle-to-node positions.
    - Tracer properties: halo mass, galaxy color, local density, filament length, and filament spine location.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify the cosmic web in TNG300.
    - Measure galaxy bias with an object-by-object estimator.
    - Test dependence on filament properties and distance to nodes.
    - Compare trends at fixed halo mass and galaxy color.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Galaxies in node outskirts reach large-scale bias values up to `~4` times halo-mass-only expectations.
    - Low-mass red galaxies in filaments and filament outskirts show enhanced bias that weakens after excluding galaxies close to nodes.
    - Mean bias decreases with filament length, from `1.4` in short filaments to `-0.5` in longer filaments.
    - The filament-length trend persists at fixed halo mass and color.
    - Long filaments show normalized bias rising from `~0.85` near saddle points to `~1.06` close to nodes.
  - **Position:** useful LSS paper because it connects cosmic-web geometry to environmental secondary bias beyond standard halo-mass ranking.

- [2608.24991](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24991) Donghyeon J. Khim et al. `SMUDGes Below the Surface: Clusters Confirm the Diversity of Low-Surface-Brightness Galaxies`
  - **Question:** do cluster populations and internal kinematics reveal multiple formation pathways for low-surface-brightness galaxies and UDGs?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `44` low-surface-brightness galaxies from SMUDGes.
    - Subsample: `30` ultradiffuse galaxies.
    - Instrument: Keck Cosmic Web Imager spatially resolved spectroscopy.
    - Color range: red sequence, green valley, and blue cloud.
  - **Method:**
    - Detect compact stellar and nebular cluster candidates in spatially resolved spectra.
    - Identify clusters with nebular emission lines.
    - Measure rotation velocities from clusters and diffuse stellar light.
    - Compare dynamical mass and stellar mass inside `0.5` effective radii.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At least one cluster candidate is detected in every galaxy.
    - Clusters with nebular emission lines occur in `24` galaxies.
    - Three nuclear star cluster candidates are identified.
    - Rotation velocities are measured for `17` galaxies using emission-line clusters and `12` using diffuse stellar light.
    - Inferred dynamical mass within `0.5 R_e` usually exceeds stellar mass, with dark-matter contribution varying by an order of magnitude.
  - **Position:** lower-redshift than the main profile, but useful because it links UDG diversity to resolved kinematics and cluster-age diagnostics rather than only imaging morphology.

- [2608.25673](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25673) Mengkui Zhou et al. `Evidence for the transformation from lenticular to spiral galaxies`
  - **Question:** can spiral galaxies form by rebuilding disks around pre-existing quenched lenticular-like cores?
  - **Data:**
    - Observations: integral-field spectroscopy.
    - Population: spiral galaxies hosting quenched central cores.
    - Diagnostics: stellar populations, dynamics, radial gradients, mass-size relations, and velocity-dispersion scaling.
    - Comparison set: quiescent early-type and lenticular galaxies.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify spiral galaxies with quenched central cores.
    - Compare inner and outer stellar-population and dynamical distributions.
    - Locate sharp radial changes near the core boundary.
    - Test whether the cores resemble lenticular or early-type systems.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Quenched central cores and outer disks have distinct population and dynamical properties.
    - The cores resemble quiescent early-type and lenticular galaxies in mass-size and velocity-dispersion scaling.
    - The results support disk reassembly around older quenched systems.
    - Reverse morphological transformation appears common in the selected population.
    - Quenching is not necessarily terminal when fresh disk growth resumes.
  - **Position:** relevant morphology paper because it treats quenching and disk growth as reversible phases, a useful caution for interpreting compact cores and late-time morphology.

## AGN, Black Holes, and Gas Diagnostics

- [2608.25341](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25341) Yuxuan Zou et al. `A systematic study of AGN feedback in a disk galaxy using MACER. III. High Gas Fractions in AGN Hosts`
  - **Question:** why can low-redshift quasar hosts remain gas rich while AGN feedback still quenches star formation over long timescales?
  - **Data:**
    - Framework: high-resolution hydrodynamic MACER simulations.
    - Host type: disk galaxy with cosmological gas inflow.
    - Physics: multiphase gas, star formation, stellar feedback, radiative AGN feedback, and mechanical AGN feedback.
    - Observational target: gas-rich PG quasar host behavior.
  - **Method:**
    - Simulate AGN-host coevolution across broad Eddington-ratio states.
    - Compare gas fraction and gas-to-stellar-mass ratio with instantaneous AGN luminosity.
    - Track cold and hot gas depletion through quenching.
    - Separate short AGN variability from cumulative reservoir evolution.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Host-galaxy gas content has weak dependence on instantaneous AGN luminosity across `L_AGN/L_Edd~10^-5-10`.
    - Cold gas declines by nearly `3` orders of magnitude during quenching.
    - AGN feedback progressively removes both cold and hot gas.
    - SFR tracks cold-gas mass more closely than AGN luminosity.
    - The key mismatch is between `10^5-10^6 yr` AGN variability and `~1 Gyr` gas-reservoir depletion.
  - **Position:** high-priority AGN-feedback paper because it reconciles gas-rich active hosts with effective long-term ejective feedback.

- [2608.25571](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25571) Ross J. Turner et al. `Energetics of AGN Feedback`
  - **Question:** where does jet feedback energy from lobed AGN get deposited in realistic cluster environments?
  - **Data:**
    - Model basis: Radio AGN in Semi-analytic Environments dynamical model.
    - Environments: `10` representative cluster atmospheres.
    - Energy channels: buoyant bubbles and collapsing shocked shells.
    - Constraints: radial and polar-angle energy deposition, duty cycle, outburst power, and outburst duration.
  - **Method:**
    - Build an analytic prescription for spatially resolved feedback energy deposition.
    - Track post-AGN jet-phase evolution through buoyant bubble ablation.
    - Model gravitational collapse of swept-up shocked gas.
    - Compare heating rates with radiative cooling under different duty cycles.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Buoyant bubbles deposit energy preferentially near steep density gradients around the core radius.
    - Collapsing shocked shells heat flatter cluster cores.
    - Weak, short outbursts with `Q<10^37 W` and `t_on<10 Myr` confine energy to the inner `10 kpc`.
    - Longer events with `t_on>=100 Myr` deposit less than `1%` of injected energy within `30 kpc`.
    - Time-averaged heating offsets cooling within `100 kpc` except in the highest-density cores for duty cycles `0.08<delta<=1`.
  - **Position:** useful feedback-modeling paper because it provides a compact prescription that simulations can use without resolving jet coupling over full cluster timescales.

- [2608.25919](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25919) E. Perez-Montero et al. `HII-CHI-mistry for active galactic nuclei: optical sulphur abundances without empirical ionisation correction factors`
  - **Question:** can total sulphur abundance in AGN be derived from optical lines without HII-region empirical ionization correction factors?
  - **Data:**
    - Method basis: HII-CHI-mistry extended to AGN.
    - Calibration tests: Seyfert galaxies with direct-method ionic abundances.
    - Survey application: MaNGA Seyfert and LINER-like regions.
    - Diagnostics: optical emission lines, S/H, O/H, S/O, and AGN ionizing SED parameters.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare optical lines with photoionization-model grids.
    - Test AGN models with different ionizing SED assumptions.
    - Adopt models favored by direct-method Seyfert comparisons.
    - Compare sulphur-based abundance behavior among Seyfert, LINER-like, and star-forming regions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Tests favor models with `alpha_ox=-1.2` and a `98%` free-electron stopping criterion.
    - O/H remains consistent with previous determinations.
    - HCm returns larger S/H than direct-method analyses using classical ICFs.
    - MaNGA Seyfert and LINER-like regions share a positive S/O-metallicity relation.
    - Seyferts have slightly larger median S/O than star-forming regions at high O/H, though differences weaken when S/H is the metallicity tracer.
  - **Position:** useful metallicity-method paper because AGN abundance calibrations affect gas-cycle interpretation in active and composite galaxies.

- [2608.25552](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25552) Luan Luan et al. `Energy Partition in AGN-driven Bubbles of NGC 4438: From Nuclear Bubbles to a Galaxy-scale Outflow`
  - **Question:** how is AGN jet energy partitioned among kinetic, thermal, magnetic, cosmic-ray, and radiative channels from nuclear to galaxy scales?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: NGC 4438.
    - Structures: two `200 pc` nuclear bubbles and a lopsided `~10 kpc` galaxy-scale outflow.
    - Observations: multi-wavelength radio and X-ray data.
    - Model comparison: empirical radio-luminosity to jet-power scaling.
  - **Method:**
    - Analyze bubble and outflow morphology.
    - Model non-thermal radio-X-ray emission.
    - Build a spatially resolved energy inventory.
    - Compare nuclear bubble energetics with galaxy-scale jet-power relations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Nuclear-bubble non-thermal emission may require two cosmic-ray electron populations.
    - Bulk kinetic energy dominates the present nuclear-bubble energy budget.
    - Roughly half of the injected energy has become thermal, cosmic-ray, magnetic, or radiative energy.
    - Thermal and magnetic pressures agree within uncertainties across examined bubble sizes.
    - The kiloparsec-scale radio-luminosity jet-power relation overpredicts the power of the `200 pc` nuclear bubbles.
  - **Position:** useful feedback benchmark because it makes the scale dependence of jet energy dissipation explicit.

- [2608.26039](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26039) V. Motta et al. `Accretion Disk Sizes and Temperature Profiles in Lensed Quasars: NIR Microlensing Challenges Thin Disk Theory`
  - **Question:** do homogeneous near-infrared microlensing data recover accretion-disk sizes and temperature profiles consistent with thin-disk theory?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `21` image pairs from `7` lensed quasar systems.
    - Wavelength regime: homogeneous near-infrared observations.
    - Baseline: narrow emission lines used as a microlensing-free reference.
    - Radial reach: accretion-disk regions out to `14` light days.
  - **Method:**
    - Estimate microlensing magnifications from near-infrared quasar image pairs.
    - Use narrow emission lines instead of broad-line cores as the baseline.
    - Infer disk sizes with Bayesian modeling and magnification maps.
    - Fit disk temperature-profile slopes across an extended wavelength baseline.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - NEL-based sizes give logarithmic slope `p=0.68+/-0.23`.
    - The NIR dataset extends disk measurements beyond the usual `<=5` light-day inner regions to `14` light days.
    - Accretion disks remain larger and have steeper temperature gradients than thin-disk predictions.
    - The theoretical slope `p=4/3` is rejected at `98%` confidence.
    - BLR-contamination explanations must reproduce a single broad power law from X-ray to `~5000 A`.
  - **Position:** relevant AGN-structure paper because microlensing disk sizes enter quasar continuum modeling and black-hole accretion physics.

- [2608.25856](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25856) Sebastian Darr et al. `Formation of Accretion Disks around Intermediate-Mass Black Holes in Ultra Compact Dwarf Galaxies and Nuclear Stellar Clusters`
  - **Question:** can gas from AGB stars form accretion disks that grow IMBHs into the massive black holes observed in UCDs?
  - **Data:**
    - Systems: ultra-compact dwarf galaxies and nuclear stellar clusters.
    - Black-hole regime: IMBHs and MBHs with observed mass fractions of `10-15%`, reaching up to `40%`.
    - Gas source: AGB stellar ejecta.
    - Simulations: smooth-particle hydrodynamics.
  - **Method:**
    - Model creation and evolution of gas disks around IMBHs.
    - Vary UCD mass and IMBH mass.
    - Quantify gas trapping and accretion-disk formation.
    - Estimate total black-hole growth and identify observed UCD candidates.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Disk mass increases more strongly with UCD mass than with IMBH mass.
    - For IMBHs of `100 Msun`, a majority of available gas can be trapped in a disk.
    - Star formation in the disk is the main process limiting accretion-disk growth.
    - Gravitational disruption by new stars also removes gas from the accretion channel.
    - The mechanism may help explain a subset of UCDs with large central black holes.
  - **Position:** secondary but relevant black-hole growth paper because it gives an environmental channel for building overmassive compact-system black holes.

- [2608.25763](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25763) Raoul Kinadeter et al. `Dynamics and Spectra-Polarimetric Signatures of GRMHD Simulations with Multiple Magnetic Loops`
  - **Question:** how do alternating magnetic loops in an accretion torus affect transient jet formation and quiescent black-hole emission?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: three-dimensional GRMHD accretion onto a black hole.
    - Initial condition: torus threaded by poloidal multi-loop magnetic fields with alternating polarity.
    - Synthetic observables: total and polarized emission with Compton scattering.
    - Comparison target: quiet-state Sgr A* constraints from radio to gamma rays.
  - **Method:**
    - Follow accretion rate, magnetic flux, jet power, and angular-momentum transport.
    - Track how initial polarity loops accrete and reconnect.
    - Run radiative-transfer calculations for spectra and polarization.
    - Compare disk and jet components with quiescent Sgr A* limits.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Jet power is strongly variable while initial magnetic-polarity loops accrete.
    - Later evolution approaches a semi-MAD state with weaker activity.
    - Disk emission is relatively stable, weakly polarized, and consistent with quiet Sgr A* values.
    - Jet emission is strongly suppressed and depolarized by Faraday rotation and conversion in cold plasma.
    - Near-infrared and X-ray light curves remain within quiescent observational constraints.
  - **Position:** useful black-hole plasma paper for interpreting weak-jet states and horizon-scale variability in low-luminosity accretion systems.

## Local ISM and Lower-Priority Benchmarks

- [2608.25611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25611) Shunya Takekawa et al. `The Nobeyama 45 m Survey of Shocked Molecular Gas in the Central Molecular Zone. I. Survey Data, Cloud Catalog, and SiO Line-Ratio Trends`
  - **Question:** what do large-area SiO and dense-gas line maps reveal about shocked molecular gas across the Central Molecular Zone?
  - **Data:**
    - Facility: Nobeyama 45 m telescope.
    - Region: `3.5 deg x 0.5 deg` in the Galactic Central Molecular Zone.
    - Resolution: `20 arcsec`.

- [2608.26098](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26098) Aishani Das-Ghosh et al. `Probing the Properties of Neutral Clouds in the Vicinity of the Local Bubble: Linking three-dimensional Dust with Neutral Hydrogen Absorption`
  - **Question:** can 3D dust maps locate HI absorption structures and tiny-scale atomic structure near the Local Bubble?
  - **Data:**
    - Sightlines: four selected pulsars.
    - Absorption tracer: time-variable atomic HI absorption.
    - Dust tracer: three-dimensional dust maps and column-density images.
    - Environment: internal and wall structures of the Local Bubble.
  - **Method:**
    - Extract hydrogen volume-density profiles along each line of sight from 3D dust.
    - Compare density components with Gaussian HI absorption components.
    - Integrate column density over full and sub-volume line-of-sight intervals.
    - Estimate ionization-fraction and thermal-pressure limits.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The TSAS toward B1929+10 likely lies outside the Local Bubble.
    - 3D dust maps reveal diffuse and clumpy neutral structure along the same lines of sight.
    - Mean and maximum neutral-hydrogen densities constrain ionization fraction and thermal pressure.
    - The dust-plus-HI method may generalize to validating Local-Bubble and nearby-ISM structures.
  - **Position:** low priority for high-z work, but a useful local-ISM methodology note because it combines absorption-line constraints with 3D dust geometry.
