## Executive View

The read-first paper is [2608.10070](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10070) C. Bertemes, which uses JWST/NIRISS WFSS to identify a dense `z=3` protocluster core around a red quasar. It is the strongest direct environment result in the listing because it combines quasar environment, velocity structure, overdensity, quenching, and AGN fraction in one system.

The high-redshift galaxy set is also strong. [2608.10063](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10063) Raunaq Singh Rai stacks `135` JWST/NIRSpec galaxies at `6<=z<=10` and finds supersolar N/O enhancement without requiring exotic short-lived sources. [2608.10832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10832) Tomokazu Kiyota uses JWST/NIRSpec broad-line ratios to argue that dust in little red dots is often associated with the broad-line region. [2608.10165](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10165) V. Catan uses ALMA Ex-MORA line searches to map molecular gas across `0.5<z<5`.

Feedback and gas-flow entries are useful for comparison samples. [2608.10306](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10306) Tiffany Liou measures cosmic-noon warm-ionized outflows in KBSS and KLCS galaxies and finds faster outflows than at low redshift. [2608.10088](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10088) Kaile Wang uses XMM-Newton absorption-line sightlines to test the hot CGM of M31. [2608.10180](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10180) Emerson Gehr builds X-ray binary spectral models relevant to dwarf AGN searches and the early X-ray heating background.

Quasar and black-hole papers round out the shortlist. [2608.10074](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10074) Ayumi Takahashi reports four narrow-line type-1 quasars at `z=2.54-6.06` from Subaru PFS-SSP. [2608.10719](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10719) Xin Li presents an off-nuclear `35000 M_sun` black-hole candidate fueled by a gravitational wake.

## High-Redshift Galaxies, Protoclusters, and Quasars

- [2608.10070](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10070) C. Bertemes et al. `A cosmic collision in the making: JWST/NIRISS reveals a merging and maturing protocluster core at z~3 around a red quasar`
  - **Question:** what is the galaxy and velocity structure around a luminous red quasar at `z~3`?
  - **Data:**
    - Target field: SDSSJ165202.64+172852.3, a luminous quasar and starburst environment.
    - Instrument: JWST/NIRISS wide-field slitless spectroscopy.
    - Structure: at least `18` member galaxies in a protocluster called The Step.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify galaxies in position-velocity space around the quasar.
    - Separate two substructures using the step-like redshift distribution.
    - Measure projected overdensity and velocity span in the core.
    - Compare star-forming, quiescent, and AGN populations with field expectations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The densest sightline contains `7` galaxies within `70` projected kpc of the quasar.
    - The galaxy velocity span reaches `700-850 km s^-1`, consistent with a possible halo merger.
    - The overdensity is `>100` in the core and `>15` within `1 Mpc^2`.
    - The inferred halo mass is `13.1<log(M_halo/M_sun)<13.8`, and the system could evolve into a Coma-like cluster.
  - **Position:** read first because it is a direct JWST protocluster-core result connecting quasar environment, assembly state, quenching, and AGN activity at cosmic noon.

- [2608.10063](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10063) Raunaq Singh Rai et al. `Unveiling and Characterising Ubiquitous Nitrogen Enhancement in 6 <= z <= 10 Galaxies with JWST Spectroscopy`
  - **Question:** is enhanced N/O restricted to extreme high-redshift galaxies, or is it common across `6<=z<=10` systems?
  - **Data:**
    - Spectroscopy: JWST/NIRSpec `R~1000`.
    - Sample: `135` galaxies at `6<=z<=10`.
    - Diagnostics: stacked UV lines including C III], C IV, N IV], He II, and auroral [O III] `4363`.
  - **Method:**
    - Build full-sample and star-formation-activity stacks.
    - Derive direct-`T_e` abundances with multi-ionization-zone modeling.
    - Compare N/O, C/O, and metallicity across recent burst timescales.
    - Test interpretations against cosmological simulations with CCSNe and AGB yields.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The full stack gives supersolar `log(N/O)=-0.57(+0.09,-0.10)`.
    - The same stack gives subsolar `log(C/O)=-0.80(+0.03,-0.03)` at `12+log(O/H)=7.79(+0.03,-0.03)`.
    - Galaxies caught in recent bursts are less enriched in N/O and metallicity than galaxies in relative lulls.
    - Standard CCSNe plus delayed AGB enrichment can reproduce the trend without invoking supermassive stars.
  - **Position:** high-priority reionization entry because abundance-ratio systematics affect JWST interpretations of early star formation and enrichment.

- [2608.10832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10832) Tomokazu Kiyota et al. `ATLAS. III. Dust Around Little Red Dots: Hydrogen Line Ratios beyond Dust-free Non-Case B Models`
  - **Question:** can broad hydrogen-line ratios in little red dots be explained without dust in the broad-line region?
  - **Data:**
    - High-redshift sample: `5` LRDs at `z>2` with JWST/NIRSpec medium/high-resolution spectra.
    - Low-redshift comparison: `15` sources at `z=0.1-0.9`.
    - Requirement: broad Hbeta detected at `>5 sigma`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure broad Balmer and Paschen lines while modeling absorption and neighboring emission lines.
    - Compare broad Halpha/Hbeta and Paschen ratios with plane-parallel Cloudy models.
    - Test non-Case-B emission from both sides of the slab.
    - Use narrow-line ratios to separate broad-line-region extinction from host-wide extinction.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Broad Halpha/Hbeta ratios range from `6` to `30`, above the Case B value.
    - One low-z LRD is reproduced by high density, `log(n_H/cm^-3)~10-11`.
    - The other modeled LRDs require dust extinction of `E(B-V)>~0.2-1.0`.
    - Halpha/Hbeta `>13` appears in two high-z and eight low-z LRDs, implying dust obscuration in at least about half of the sample.
  - **Position:** important JWST-era AGN entry because LRD line ratios and dust geometry affect black-hole and star-formation interpretations.

- [2608.10074](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10074) Ayumi Takahashi et al. `Discovery of four Narrow-line Type-1 Quasars at z = 2.54 -- 6.06 with Subaru 'Onohi'ula PFS-SSP`
  - **Question:** can narrow-line type-1 quasars be identified in the distant universe using Lyalpha-line profiles?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: Subaru 'Onohi'ula PFS-SSP.
    - Sample: four NLQ1s, including one candidate, at `z=2.54-6.06`.
    - Spectral features: Lyalpha, C IV where available, and broad absorption lines in one object.
  - **Method:**
    - Search for broad-line AGN with rest-UV spectral shapes resembling local NLS1 galaxies.
    - Select using Lyalpha-line width information.
    - Measure Lyalpha FWHM, equivalent widths, luminosities, black-hole masses, and Eddington ratios.
    - Classify the absorption-line source using N V and C IV broad absorption.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Lyalpha FWHM values span `1410-1510 km s^-1`, with mean `~1430 km s^-1`.
    - Mean rest-frame Lyalpha equivalent width is `~190 A`.
    - Two quasars have Lyalpha luminosity equal to `~3-4%` of bolometric luminosity.
    - Three objects have black-hole masses of `~10^6.9-10^7.7 M_sun` and Eddington ratios `>0.1`.
  - **Position:** useful quasar-demographics paper because PFS can find low-black-hole-mass, high-accretion AGN in high-redshift samples.

## Gas Reservoirs, CGM, and Feedback

- [2608.10165](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10165) V. Catan et al. `The Extended Mapping Obscuration to Reionization with ALMA (Ex-MORA) Survey: A Molecular Gas Line Search`
  - **Question:** how much molecular gas is recovered when galaxies are selected by line emission rather than stellar light?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: ALMA Band 4 Ex-MORA.
    - Area: `577 arcmin^2`.
    - Redshift range: `0.5<z<5`.
    - Detections: `52` galaxies in CO or CI transitions, with two galaxies showing two lines.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine an unbiased bright-line search with targeted searches at known spectroscopic redshifts.
    - Fit physical properties with CIGALE and non-parametric star-formation histories.
    - Estimate gas masses using metallicity-dependent conversion factors.
    - Compare gas fraction trends across redshift, activity class, and overdensity.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The sample is dominated by massive main-sequence galaxies with median `log(M*/M_sun)=10.83`.
    - Gas reservoirs are typically `>10^10 M_sun`.
    - Gas fraction increases with redshift for active galaxies, with a gas-rich protocluster at `z~2.4` affecting the high-z trend.
    - Five green-valley galaxies show a cosmic-noon gas-fraction peak, suggesting feedback can heat gas without rapid depletion.
  - **Position:** high-value gas-census entry because ALMA line selection can recover obscured and transitional systems missed by optical/NIR surveys.

- [2608.10306](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10306) Tiffany Liou et al. `Scaling Relations of Galactic Outflows Across Cosmic Time: New Insights from Cosmic Noon`
  - **Question:** how do warm-ionized outflow scaling relations evolve from low redshift to cosmic noon and beyond?
  - **Data:**
    - Joint sample: `387` galaxies from `z~0-9`.
    - New cosmic-noon subset: `98` galaxies from KBSS and KLCS.
    - Spectroscopy: Keck/MOSFIRE Halpha or [O III] `5007` with `SNR>50`.
  - **Method:**
    - Decompose high-SNR emission lines into narrow and broad components.
    - Measure outflow velocities, mass outflow rates, and mass-loading factors.
    - Compare trends at fixed stellar mass, SFR, and SFR surface density.
    - Correct occurrence-rate estimates for galaxy orientation and outflow geometry.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Cosmic-noon outflows have maximum velocities up to `3` times higher than low-z outflows at fixed galaxy properties.
    - Mass outflow rates are higher at fixed stellar mass.
    - Mass loading shows no evolution at fixed stellar mass.
    - Most outflowing gas is recycled at radius `~0.03 R_vir`, consistent with galactic fountains.
  - **Position:** strong feedback entry because it links cosmic-noon spectra to baryon cycling and recycling efficiencies.

- [2608.10088](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10088) Kaile Wang et al. `An X-ray Absorption-Line Survey of the Circumgalactic Medium of M31 with XMM-Newton`
  - **Question:** can X-ray absorption lines detect the hot CGM of M31 in background AGN sightlines?
  - **Data:**
    - Instrument: XMM-Newton.
    - Sightlines: `15` background AGN for O VII and `18` for O VIII.
    - Impact parameters: O VII at `R_imp~300-730 kpc`; O VIII at `R_imp~190-730 kpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure O VII and O VIII absorption toward M31 halo sightlines.
    - Compare inner and outer sightlines relative to `R_imp=300-350 kpc`.
    - Estimate excess absorption above the Milky Way foreground.
    - Convert excess absorption into hot-CGM mass under different halo-boundary assumptions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Inner sightlines show marginal excess absorption consistent with M31 hot-CGM gas.
    - The inner O VII excess is `~4-9 mA` near `R_imp~310 kpc`.
    - The inner O VIII excess is `~17 mA` near `R_imp~200 kpc`.
    - The combined significance is only `2.2-2.3 sigma`, and the inferred CGM mass is strongly model dependent.
  - **Position:** useful low-redshift CGM benchmark because it exposes the sensitivity limits and boundary assumptions behind hot-halo baryon censuses.

- [2608.10180](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10180) Emerson Gehr et al. `Constructing X-ray Spectral Models of Galaxies: Varying Contributions from X-ray Binary Populations with Host Galaxy Properties`
  - **Question:** how should X-ray binary spectral contributions be modeled across galaxy mass, SFR, and metallicity?
  - **Data:**
    - Calibration: galaxies spanning high to low stellar mass, SFR, and metallicity.
    - Application space: dwarf AGN searches and X-ray heating in early galaxies at `z>=8`.
    - Products: main-sequence XRB spectral model library.
  - **Method:**
    - Statistically sample X-ray luminosity functions.
    - Build total X-ray spectra and stochastic uncertainties for XRB populations.
    - Validate models against Chandra observations.
    - Use main-sequence mass-SFR and mass-metallicity relations to generate typical spectra as a function of stellar mass.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Shallow XRB luminosity functions cause large stochastic luminosity variation at low SFR and low metallicity.
    - The procedure reproduces Chandra-observed galaxy spectra across broad host properties.
    - The model library provides mass-dependent spectral shapes, normalizations, and uncertainties for main-sequence galaxies.
  - **Position:** supporting methods entry because XRB backgrounds are a contaminant for low-mass AGN and an input to early heating models.

## Black Holes, Environments, and Survey Methods

- [2608.10719](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10719) Xin Li et al. `A Wandering 35,000-Solar-Mass Black Hole Fed by a Gravitational Wake`
  - **Question:** can an off-nuclear intermediate-mass black hole be fueled by gas captured from its gravitational wake?
  - **Data:**
    - Candidate: a wandering black hole of `35000 M_sun`.
    - Evidence: broad-line emission, compact continuum counterpart, long-term optical variability, and a power-law-like SED.
    - Spectroscopy: multi-epoch spectra resolving gas components around the candidate.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify off-nuclear accretion signatures.
    - Separate upstream, downstream, and inner absorber gas components.
    - Compare the gas geometry with gravitational focusing expectations.
    - Use changing-look variability to constrain optically thick absorbers within the capture radius.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The downstream component is redshifted and dense, consistent with a wake.
    - The upstream component is blueshifted and low density.
    - Optically thick absorbers inside the capture radius drive rapid changing-look broad-line variability.
    - The object provides a candidate fueling channel for displaced intermediate-mass black holes.
  - **Position:** useful black-hole-growth entry because wandering seed remnants are expected in hierarchical assembly but their fueling channels are poorly constrained.

- [2608.10098](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10098) Mohamed H. Abdullah et al. `From Cluster Core to Splashback: Linking Dynamical Structure to Multidimensional Galaxy Evolution in the Coma Cluster`
  - **Question:** how does galaxy evolution change across the Coma cluster dynamical boundary?
  - **Data:**
    - Cluster: Coma.
    - Catalogs: GalWCat19 spectroscopic cluster catalog plus SDSS value-added galaxy properties.
    - Diagnostics: sSFR, color offset from the red sequence, and bulge-to-total ratio.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure the splashback radius.
    - Classify galaxies in joint `log sSFR`, color, and morphology space.
    - Track population fractions from the core to outer regions.
    - Compare demographic transitions with the dynamical boundary.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The measured splashback radius is `R_sp=2.94+/-0.16 h^-1 Mpc`.
    - Red, quiescent, bulge-dominated galaxies make up `51%` of the joint-analysis sample.
    - Blue, star-forming, disk-dominated galaxies make up `29%`.
    - Population fractions change strongly near splashback, marking a coupled dynamical and evolutionary transition.
  - **Position:** lower-redshift environment benchmark because splashback provides a clean physical boundary for interpreting cluster transformation.

- [2608.10163](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10163) Krzysztof Lisiecki et al. `CORN -- Chronometers of Relic Nature I: The first estimate of the expansion rate of the Universe using compact relic galaxies`
  - **Question:** can ultra-compact relic galaxies reduce cosmic-chronometer systematics?
  - **Data:**
    - Tracers: ultra-compact massive galaxies with old stellar populations.
    - Method class: differential-age cosmic chronometers.
    - Measurement: a first relic-based estimate at low redshift.
  - **Method:**
    - Select compact relic galaxies to minimize merger and star-formation-history uncertainty.
    - Apply the cosmic-chronometer approach to infer differential age evolution.
    - Convert the age gradient into `H(z)` without assuming an underlying cosmological model.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The first reported compact-relic chronometer measurement is `H(z=0.15)=85+/-53 km s^-1 Mpc^-1`.
    - The uncertainty is large, but the tracer choice is designed to reduce stellar-population systematics.
    - Relic chronometers could become a useful independent check on standard quiescent-galaxy chronometers.
  - **Position:** useful methods note because stellar-population selection remains a limiting systematic for galaxy-based cosmological clocks.

- [2608.10244](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10244) Benjamin R. Roulston et al. `The Carbon Giant Population Revealed by Gaia DR3`
  - **Question:** what can a high-confidence Gaia DR3 carbon-giant sample reveal about evolved populations in the Milky Way and nearby galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Catalog: all-sky Gaia DR3 carbon stars with `G<16.5`.
    - Samples: `6937` carbon giants in the LMC, `2148` in the SMC, and `219` in Sagittarius.
    - Galactic subset: `4276` carbon giants with `M_G<0` and `|b|>5 deg`.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify uniform high-confidence carbon giant samples.
    - Separate C-AGB stars from lower-luminosity carbon giants using luminosity and Gaia quality indicators.
    - Measure Local Group memberships and Galactic density structure.
    - Cross-match candidates with X-ray counterparts to identify candidate symbiotic stars.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The Sagittarius carbon-giant sample is nearly quadrupled.
    - The C-AGB absolute magnitudes are `M_J^0=-6.30` in the LMC and `-6.15` in the SMC.
    - The local Galactic disk space density is `n_0=11+/-0.5 x 10^-8 pc^-3`.
    - Lower-luminosity carbon giants have large RUWE values, supporting an extrinsic post-mass-transfer origin.
  - **Position:** supporting survey entry because evolved-star maps are useful for Galactic structure and foreground modeling, but it is less central to the high-z profile.
