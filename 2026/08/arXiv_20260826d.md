## Executive View

The read-first item is [2608.23671](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23671) Fabio Pacucci on close blue companions around the brightest Little Red Dots. The sample compares `253` LRDs with more than `7000` matched controls across JWST deep fields and finds a `3.3 sigma` excess of blue companions at `0.5-2 kpc` only for the brightest LRD quartile. This is the strongest early-black-hole/environment entry in the listing.

The best galaxy-gas paper is [2608.23687](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23687) Simon Xinlin Wu on MgII absorption around `716` galaxies at `0.07<z<2.7`. It introduces a star-formation offset metric that separates star-forming, green-valley, and quiescent systems across cosmic time, then shows a gradual decline in cool CGM absorption through the green valley.

The highest-priority high-redshift ISM paper is [2608.23846](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23846) Mabel G. Stephenson on low-ionization electron densities from JWST NIRCam/grism and NIRSpec/MSA spectroscopy. The sample includes `703` galaxies at `3.7<z<7.8` plus lower-redshift literature data and shows that median density rises to `z~4` but does not continue as a simple monotonic function beyond that.

The strongest massive-galaxy evolution entry is [2608.24124](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24124) Novan Saputra Haryana on compaction, quenching, and black-hole growth since `z~5`. JWST imaging plus Chandra stacking show rapid central compaction at `z~4`, inside-out quenching by `z~3`, and X-ray AGN preferentially hosted during the compaction phase.

Other useful entries include [2608.24678](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24678) Bin Jia on ALMA dense-gas conditions in the 3C 84 circumnuclear disk, [2608.24826](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24826) Laura Carnevale on the mass dependence of the fundamental metallicity relation, [2608.24645](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24645) Shengxiu Sun on Paschen-line black-hole mass calibration, [2608.23673](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23673) Juri Wladimir Seib on BLR radius-luminosity quality control, and [2608.24164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24164) Bhavesh Mukheja on deep-learning triage of candidate dual AGN.

## Early Galaxies, CGM, and Quenching

- [2608.23671](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23671) Fabio Pacucci et al. `More Numerous and Bluer: Companions of the Brightest Little Red Dots Differ from Control Galaxies at >3 sigma, Hinting at Synchronized Black Hole Seed Formation`
  - **Question:** do the brightest Little Red Dots have close companions that differ from matched control-galaxy environments?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `253` uniformly selected LRDs across several JWST deep fields.
    - Control set: more than `7000` matched galaxies in the same mosaics.
    - Separation window: close companions at `0.5-2 kpc`.
    - Color diagnostic: UV slope threshold `beta<-1`.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare LRD close-pair fractions against redshift-matched controls.
    - Split the LRD sample by luminosity quartile.
    - Measure companion color distributions and separation dependence.
    - Test whether the signal is specific to close, blue companions around the brightest LRDs.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The brightest LRD quartile has a blue-companion fraction of `27.7%`, compared with `11.6%` for controls.
    - The excess is a factor of `2.39` and has `3.3 sigma` significance.
    - The full LRD sample has a pair rate indistinguishable from controls.
    - The excess is confined to `<2 kpc`; larger-scale environments are ordinary.
    - `92%` of companions around the brightest LRDs are blue, with median `beta~-2.32` versus `beta~-1.92` for controls.
  - **Position:** read first because it ties LRD luminosity, local companions, and black-hole seed scenarios to a directly testable environment signal.

- [2608.23687](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23687) Simon Xinlin Wu et al. `Cool CGM MgII Absorption Across the Star-Forming, Green Valley, and Quiescent Transition`
  - **Question:** how does cool MgII-bearing CGM gas change as galaxies move from star formation through the green valley to quiescence?
  - **Data:**
    - Absorber-galaxy sample: `716` galaxies.
    - Redshift span: `0.07<z<2.7`.
    - Composition: `169` new galaxies plus `547` archival galaxies.
    - Observable: MgII absorption strength and kinematics as a function of host properties.
  - **Method:**
    - Define a star-formation offset metric relative to the main sequence at each epoch.
    - Fit a virial-radius-normalized radial MgII absorption profile.
    - Measure residuals around that profile versus star-formation state.
    - Compare covering fractions in the inner CGM and test azimuthal trends.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - MgII absorption declines with increasing projected distance from the host.
    - Residual absorption correlates positively with specific SFR and star-formation offset.
    - Inner-CGM covering fraction declines monotonically from star-forming to green-valley to quiescent systems.
    - MgII kinematics are consistent with a mostly bound cool CGM.
    - Star-forming galaxies show enhanced MgII along both polar and disk directions, consistent with outflows plus co-planar accretion.
  - **Position:** high-priority CGM paper because it connects cool gas reservoirs to quenching in a way that remains comparable over `10 Gyr` of cosmic time.

- [2608.23846](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23846) Mabel G. Stephenson et al. `The Evolution of Low-Ionization Electron Densities in Galaxies Across Cosmic Epochs`
  - **Question:** how do low-ionization electron densities evolve from the local universe to `z~8`?
  - **Data:**
    - Diagnostics: [OII] and [SII] density-sensitive doublets.
    - JWST sample: `703` galaxies at `3.7<z<7.8`.
    - Grism programs: CONGRESS and FRESCO, with `691` galaxies at mean redshifts near `4.3` and `5.3`.
    - High-resolution sample: `12` NIRSpec/MSA galaxies from GO 1871 and GLASS.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine JWST NIRCam/grism and NIRSpec/MSA measurements with lower-redshift literature samples.
    - Derive low-ionization electron densities from [OII] and [SII].
    - Compare median density evolution with redshift.
    - Test correlations between density, O32, and redshift.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Median [SII] density is `463 cm^-3` for CONGRESS and `301 cm^-3` for FRESCO.
    - The NIRSpec/MSA [OII] median is `202 cm^-3`.
    - Median density increases from `z=0` to `z=4`.
    - Beyond `z~4`, the median density deviates from simple monotonic growth.
    - A shallow positive O32-density correlation suggests evolving gas density and ionization structure.
  - **Position:** important high-redshift ISM reference because density assumptions propagate into nebular modeling, metallicity, ionization, and reionization-era interpretation.

- [2608.24124](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24124) Novan Saputra Haryana et al. `Interplay of Compaction, Quenching, and Black Hole Growth in the Most Massive Galaxies since z~5: Insights from JWST and Chandra Data`
  - **Question:** when do dense stellar cores, inside-out quenching, and black-hole growth emerge in the most massive galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Imaging: JWST near-infrared data.
    - X-ray constraint: Chandra detections and stacking.
    - Selection: `50` most massive galaxies per redshift bin.
    - Number density: about `4.4 x 10^-5 cMpc^-3`.
  - **Method:**
    - Use spatially resolved SED fitting to trace central stellar-mass buildup.
    - Separate AGN-affected photometry with multi-band morphological decomposition and SED analysis.
    - Stack Chandra data to constrain average black-hole accretion.
    - Follow central and galaxy-wide sSFR through time.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The constant-number-density sample undergoes rapid central compaction at `z~4`.
    - Median central `1 kpc` stellar mass increases by `0.60 dex` over `~400 Myr`.
    - `63%+-12%` of X-ray AGN are hosted by galaxies undergoing compaction.
    - No individual or stacked X-ray signal is found at `z>4`.
    - Central sSFR declines by `1.24 dex` over `~700 Myr` at `z~3` while galaxy-wide sSFR remains elevated.
  - **Position:** high-priority massive-galaxy paper because it places AGN growth after, or during, compaction rather than before dense-core assembly.

- [2608.24826](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24826) Laura Carnevale et al. `The Mass Dependence of the Fundamental Metallicity Relation in Observations and Simulations`
  - **Question:** is the fundamental metallicity relation independent of stellar mass and redshift?
  - **Data:**
    - Observations: SDSS galaxy metallicities and star-formation rates.
    - Simulations: EAGLE, SIMBA, Illustris, and IllustrisTNG.
    - Redshift scope: simulations tested to about `z~1`, with broader FMR context to `z<4`.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare metallicity, stellar mass, and sSFR in observations and simulations.
    - Test whether the canonical metallicity-sSFR anticorrelation changes with stellar mass.
    - Examine lower star-forming galaxies separately.
    - Check whether the inversion depends on metallicity diagnostics.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The canonical metallicity-sSFR anticorrelation inverts above `M_star~10^10.5 Msun` in EAGLE, IllustrisTNG, and SDSS.
    - Including lower star-forming galaxies produces a positive correlation in all four simulations and SDSS.
    - The inversion appears in multiple observed metallicity diagnostics, though details vary.
    - The simulated inversion persists to `z~1`.
    - Strong nuclear outflows may quench star formation while preferentially removing enriched central gas.
  - **Position:** useful gas-cycle paper because it turns the FMR from a universal correction into a mass-dependent feedback diagnostic.

## AGN, Black Holes, and Dense Gas

- [2608.24678](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24678) Bin Jia et al. `Spatially resolved molecular gas conditions in the circumnuclear disc of 3C 84`
  - **Question:** what are the physical and chemical conditions in the circumnuclear molecular disk of the radio AGN 3C 84?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: NGC 1275 / 3C 84 at the center of the Perseus cluster.
    - Facility: ALMA.
    - Lines: CO(2-1), HCN(3-2), and HCO+(3-2).
    - Resolution: `72 pc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Partition the circumnuclear disk into beam-sized hexagonal regions.
    - Model line ratios with a Bayesian framework.
    - Couple a neural-network emulator of UCLCHEM time-dependent chemistry to non-LTE radiative transfer.
    - Infer gas density, kinetic temperature, and cosmic-ray ionization rate by region.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Line ratios peak in the inner disk and decline with radius.
    - Density declines from `log10 n(H2)~6.3` to `~5`.
    - Kinetic temperature declines from `~200 K` to `~160 K`.
    - Cosmic-ray ionization declines from `log10(zeta/zeta_0)~4.9` to `~3`.
    - HCN optical-depth saturation means HCN/HCO+ intensity below unity does not imply weak AGN chemistry.
    - A western HCO+/CO enhancement coincides with the filament-disk accretion interface.
  - **Position:** strong dense-gas/AGN paper because it links circumnuclear chemistry to filamentary accretion rather than treating the disk as isolated fuel.

- [2608.24645](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24645) Shengxiu Sun et al. `Broad Paschen lines as black hole mass tracers: A reverberation mapping calibration`
  - **Question:** can broad Paschen lines provide calibrated single-epoch black-hole mass estimators?
  - **Data:**
    - New spectra: LBT/LUCI near-infrared observations of `27` SDSS-RM quasars.
    - Calibration baseline: archival Paschen measurements for AGN with reverberation-mapping masses.
    - Lines: broad Paschen P-alpha and P-beta.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure line luminosities and FWHM after continuum subtraction.
    - Fit mass estimators against reverberation-mapping masses.
    - Test multi-component, single-Gaussian, continuum-luminosity, and fixed-virial-slope variants.
    - Include intrinsic scatter in the calibration.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The P-alpha fiducial relation has intrinsic scatter `0.28 dex`.
    - The P-beta fiducial relation has intrinsic scatter `0.32 dex`.
    - Alternative line-width and continuum choices remain consistent within uncertainties.
    - Paschen relations are useful when rest-UV or optical broad lines are attenuated or unavailable.
    - The calibration remains limited by its sample range and the reverberation-mapping virial factor.
  - **Position:** useful AGN-method paper for dusty and high-redshift regimes where near-infrared broad lines can replace standard UV/optical mass tracers.

- [2608.23673](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23673) Juri Wladimir Seib et al. `A simulation-based quality-control framework for the broad-line region radius-luminosity relation`
  - **Question:** how much of the H-beta BLR radius-luminosity scatter comes from lag-recovery quality rather than intrinsic AGN diversity?
  - **Data:**
    - Database: reverberation-mapping measurements for about `1200` AGN from `32` campaigns.
    - H-beta subset: `248` sources used in radius-luminosity refits.
    - Metadata: lags, luminosities, line widths, black-hole masses, cadence, baseline, and observational quality.
  - **Method:**
    - Simulate damped-random-walk light curves sampled according to each campaign.
    - Recover lags with ICCF.
    - Compare expected, reported, and simulation-retrieved lags.
    - Build a four-tier flagging scheme and refit the relation with cleaner samples.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - About `40%` of H-beta sources show discrepancies between reported and simulation-retrieved lags.
    - About `5%` show direct inconsistencies between expected and retrieved lags.
    - Removing flagged sources and correcting model bias reduces intrinsic scatter from `0.26 dex` to `0.11 dex`.
    - The corrected slope is `0.48+-0.02`.
    - A large part of observed scatter traces observational limitations and lag-recovery bias.
  - **Position:** important calibration paper because black-hole mass scaling relations enter AGN demographics and high-redshift growth arguments.

- [2608.24164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24164) Bhavesh Mukheja et al. `Decoupling candidate dual AGN from chance superpositions in the GOTHIC survey via a deep-learning framework`
  - **Question:** can candidate dual AGN be separated from foreground stars and chance superpositions in large imaging surveys?
  - **Data:**
    - Parent set: `46,061` galaxies flagged but rejected as dual-AGN candidates by GOTHIC.
    - Imaging: SDSS data.
    - Training labels: annotated dual-nucleus and contaminant examples.
    - Spectroscopic check: compact systems at separations below `1 kpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Train a YOLOv11 oriented-bounding-box model.
    - Separate genuine dual nuclei from stars, blends, and spurious alignments.
    - Cross-calibrate YOLO separations against deterministic GOTHIC centroid measurements.
    - Visually inspect structured subsets and define conservative candidates.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Validation precision is `0.919`, recall is `0.905`, and dual-nucleus `F1` is `0.912`.
    - The pipeline returns `29,605` dual-nucleus candidates after removing star-dominated and blended detections.
    - Visual inspection suggests `54.5-62%` are consistent with genuine dual nuclei.
    - A conservative compact subset contains about `13,672` candidates.
    - Spectra of the most compact systems are mostly passive absorption-line galaxies, so higher-resolution follow-up is still required.
  - **Position:** useful merger/AGN survey paper because it expands the plausible candidate census while keeping confirmation status explicit.

- [2608.23677](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23677) Matteo Cantiello et al. `Signatures of Compact Object Mergers Inside Stars in AGN Disks`
  - **Question:** what observable signatures arise when compact objects merge inside massive stars embedded in AGN disks?
  - **Data:**
    - Environment: dense gas disks around supermassive black holes.
    - Stellar channel: captured or in-situ stars altered by rapid accretion.
    - Compact objects: remnants spiraling into AGN-disk stars and binary black holes merging inside them.
  - **Method:**
    - Estimate encounter rates and inspiral timescales for compact remnants inside AGN stars.
    - Model outcomes under different feedback-regulated accretion assumptions.
    - Calculate gas-drag hardening for BBH mergers inside the stellar envelope.
    - Identify gravitational-wave bands where dense-environment dephasing is measurable.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Rapid accretion can keep AGN-disk stars in a long-lived quasi-steady state.
    - A compact remnant can consume the star in a collapsar-like transient or form a quasi-star-like object.
    - Gas drag can harden embedded BBHs to merger far faster than gravitational radiation alone.
    - The dense-environment imprint is strongest in the deci-Hz band rather than the LISA milli-Hz band.
    - The channel could contribute up to `~8 Gpc^-3 yr^-1` in favorable scenarios.
  - **Position:** relevant AGN-environment theory because it treats the AGN disk as a stellar and compact-object processing site, not only a black-hole accretion flow.

- [2608.24110](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24110) Shan-Shan Zhao et al. `Spatially resolved spectral properties of M87* on event horizon scales`
  - **Question:** what do event-horizon-scale spectral-index maps reveal about the emission region around M87*?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: M87*.
    - Facilities: GMVA with ALMA and the Greenland Telescope at `86 GHz`, plus EHT at `230 GHz`.
    - Epoch: nearly simultaneous 2018 observations.
    - Scale: compact region within `<=100 microarcsec`.
  - **Method:**
    - Construct high-resolution images at `3.5 mm` and `1.3 mm`.
    - Derive a spatially resolved spectral-index map.
    - Measure radial spectral-index gradients.
    - Interpret the transition against synchrotron opacity in the innermost accretion flow.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The analysis obtains the first spatially resolved spectral-index map of M87* within `<=100 microarcsec`.
    - The spectral index rises modestly inside `~20 microarcsec`.
    - The index declines systematically at larger radii.
    - The sign transition occurs near `~30 microarcsec`, close to the `3.5 mm` ring radius.
    - The pattern is consistent with frequency-dependent synchrotron opacity near the black hole.
  - **Position:** useful black-hole/jet benchmark because M87* remains the anchor system for horizon-scale plasma and jet-launching models.

- [2608.24432](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24432) Saeed Fakhry et al. `Dark-to-black super-accretion as a spin-imprinting mechanism for supermassive Kerr black holes`
  - **Question:** can ultralight scalar dark-matter clouds help form high-redshift supermassive black holes with characteristic spins?
  - **Data:**
    - System: Kerr black-hole seed surrounded by two ultralight scalar dark-matter clouds.
    - Target regime: black holes with `M>10^9 Msun` and spin `chi~0.9-0.99` at high redshift.
    - Boson masses: `mu~10^-18-10^-17 eV`.
  - **Method:**
    - Model adiabatic co-evolution of the black hole and two scalar-cloud bound states.
    - Deplete a spherical `l=0` cloud through runaway dark-to-black accretion.
    - Follow a second `l=m=1` cloud as it transitions from superradiance to accretion.
    - Track mass growth and spin saturation.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The spherical cloud can be depleted over hundreds of Myr.
    - The first stage drives black-hole spin toward `chi~0` because the accreted material has no angular momentum.
    - The second cloud later transfers mass and angular momentum on thousand-year e-folding timescales.
    - The final spin saturates near a threshold set by boson mass and final black-hole mass.
    - The mechanism erases primordial spin and then resets it to a characteristic value.
  - **Position:** speculative but relevant to high-redshift SMBH assembly because it addresses both rapid growth and spin, two observables that standard seeding scenarios struggle to connect.

## Neutral Gas, Local Environments, and Survey Diagnostics

- [2608.24225](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24225) Ruilei Zhou et al. `An HI study of a large sample of ultra-diffuse galaxies`
  - **Question:** what do HI detections and stacks reveal about gas content and formation pathways in UDGs and LSB galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Parent catalog: SMUDGes.
    - HI surveys: FASHI and ALFALFA.
    - Detected sample: `112` UDGs and `48` LSB galaxies.
    - New redshifts: HI-based redshifts for `76` galaxies.
    - Stacking sample: `168` HI-non-detected galaxies with DESI DR1 redshifts.
  - **Method:**
    - Cross-match optical low-surface-brightness galaxies with HI surveys.
    - Combine optical, UV, and HI measurements.
    - Analyze stellar mass, SFR, gas fraction, and kinematics.
    - Compare HI mass-size and stellar mass-size relations with normal galaxies.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - UDGs and LSBs follow an HI mass-optical size scaling similar to normal galaxies.
    - Their stellar mass-size relation implies nearly constant stellar surface density at low central surface brightness.
    - Many systems are offset from the baryonic Tully-Fisher relation toward higher baryonic mass at fixed velocity.
    - Both populations show low star-formation efficiencies and long gas depletion times.
    - The results support multiple UDG formation pathways.
  - **Position:** useful neutral-gas benchmark for diffuse galaxy formation, though it is lower priority than high-redshift absorber and CGM papers.

- [2608.23868](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23868) Wasundara Athukoralalage et al. `The Local Origin of a Host Dispersion Measure-Stellar Mass Relation in Fast Radio Bursts`
  - **Question:** why does FRB host dispersion measure depend on host stellar mass?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `37` FRBs with secure host associations.
    - Redshift range: `z<0.25`.
    - Host types: dwarf, elliptical, and edge-on galaxies.
    - Observables: host DM, host stellar mass, and rest-frame Faraday rotation measure.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure the DM-host-stellar-mass relation across the sample.
    - Test whether galaxy-averaged ISM or CGM contributions explain the trend.
    - Compare rotation-measure behavior with host mass.
    - Interpret the residual signal through local environments and progenitor channels.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Host DM anticorrelates with stellar mass, with Pearson `r~-0.5`.
    - The relation spans `log10(M_star/Msun)~8-11.4`.
    - Rest-frame host RM is largest for FRBs in dwarf galaxies.
    - The galaxy-averaged ISM and CGM do not explain the DM-mass trend.
    - Local environments within `~100 pc` likely dominate the relation.
  - **Position:** useful environment diagnostic because it separates local progenitor gas from galaxy-scale ISM/CGM contributions.

- [2608.24123](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24123) Jong Chul Lee et al. `Comparing the Nebular and Stellar Dust Attenuation Curves in Nearby Star-Forming Galaxies`
  - **Question:** are nebular attenuation curves the same as stellar attenuation curves in star-forming galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: SDSS.
    - Sample: `90,958` nearby star-forming galaxies.
    - Diagnostics: Balmer line ratios and well-constrained stellar attenuation curves.
  - **Method:**
    - Use H-alpha/H-beta and H-alpha/H-gamma ratios to infer nebular attenuation behavior.
    - Compare nebular curve slopes with stellar attenuation slopes.
    - Test correlations with galaxy properties.
    - Interpret the differences through source-dust geometry.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Nebular attenuation curves are on average shallower than typical stellar attenuation curves.
    - Source-dust geometry can qualitatively reproduce the difference.
    - Nebular slopes weakly correlate with stellar attenuation slopes.
    - Galaxy-property dependences broadly track those of the stellar attenuation slopes.
    - Nebular and stellar curves are distinct but connected.
  - **Position:** useful calibration paper because dust-curve assumptions affect Balmer-line SFRs, nebular diagnostics, and comparisons to high-redshift galaxies.

- [2608.24201](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24201) Zhuo Cheng et al. `SDSS-IV MaNGA: Star Formation Cessation in Low-redshift Galaxies. III. Dependence on Quenching Criteria`
  - **Question:** how do quenching definitions change inferred drivers of star-formation cessation on kiloparsec scales?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: final SDSS-IV MaNGA release.
    - Spatial unit: galaxy spaxels.
    - Classes: star-forming, reliably quenched, and potentially quenched regions.
  - **Method:**
    - Classify spaxels while accounting for measurement uncertainties.
    - Train random-forest classifiers with parameters matched to prior studies.
    - Compare feature importance under different quenching definitions.
    - Split trends by stellar mass.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Local stellar-mass surface density is the strongest feature for reliably quenched regions.
    - Central velocity dispersion becomes highly important mainly when potentially quenched regions are included.
    - Below `~10^10.2 Msun`, central `1 kpc` density is most important.
    - At high mass, local quantities such as stellar surface density and stellar velocity dispersion become more prominent.
    - Quenching criteria and uncertainty treatment can reconcile discrepant feature-importance studies.
  - **Position:** lower-redshift but useful because it warns against over-interpreting AGN-feedback proxies without definition-controlled quenched samples.

- [2608.24321](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24321) Geoffrey Ong'alo et al. `Synchrotron filaments in radio galaxies: rarity, stability, and environmental influences from LOFAR and ROSAT observations`
  - **Question:** how common are filamentary synchrotron structures in radio galaxies, and what stabilizes them?
  - **Data:**
    - Radio survey: LoTSS at `144 MHz`.
    - X-ray support: archival ROSAT data.
    - Parent set: `548` cluster-associated radio sources.
    - Candidate set: `12` filament candidates, with `3` resolved enough for detailed pressure analysis.
  - **Method:**
    - Visually identify narrow collimated filaments linking radio lobes.
    - Compare synchrotron minimum pressure with ambient thermal pressure.
    - Evaluate formation scenarios including relic outflows, magnetic draping, and plasma instabilities.
    - Interpret stability in terms of external pressure and magnetic field geometry.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Filament candidates occur in about `2.2%` of cluster-associated sources.
    - Minimum filament pressures are below ambient thermal pressures.
    - External confinement likely contributes to filament stability.
    - Toroidal magnetic field configurations remain plausible.
    - Higher-resolution multi-frequency data are needed to distinguish formation channels.
  - **Position:** useful AGN-feedback and intracluster-medium context, especially for magnetic-field topology around radio galaxies.
