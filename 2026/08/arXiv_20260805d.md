## Executive View

The read-first paper is [2608.02739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02739) Zizhao He, which predicts the missing population of galaxy-scale lensed Little Red Dots in current JWST survey footprints. It is the closest match to the compact high-redshift AGN/LRD axis and gives a practical null-detection interpretation for COSMOS-Web, PRIMER, CEERS, and JADES fields.

The environment and gas-regulation axis is the strongest part of the posting. [2608.03693](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03693) Natan de Isidio uses IllustrisTNG histories to argue that slow hot-CGM erosion drives delayed-then-rapid satellite quenching. [2608.02898](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02898) Norma Araceli Nava-Moreno presents GARDENS-Wide, a `100 deg^2` dusty-galaxy mock survey that tracks LIRGs and proto-cluster contraction. [2608.03576](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03576) Andreea A Varasteanu measures bTFR and RAR behavior for `130` resolved HI-selected galaxies from MIGHTEE-HI and LADUMA.

The AGN set is mostly compact-object or time-domain support. [2608.03947](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03947) P. Penil decomposes the `~2.1 yr` multiwavelength cycles of PG 1553+113 and keeps a supermassive-black-hole-binary interpretation viable but unproven. [2608.03797](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03797) V. Crespi tests whether iron-line spectroscopy can distinguish Kerr black holes from compact fermionic dark-matter cores. [2608.03593](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03593) Ilidio Lopes builds a two-component dark-fermion-star framework with multimessenger observables.

The local-galaxy and ISM papers are useful supporting context rather than primary high-z reads. [2608.03140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03140) Takashi Onaka uses JWST/NIRSpec PDRs4All data to separate near-IR continuum components in the Orion Bar. [2608.02730](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02730) Avinash Chaturvedi models the NGC 1399 globular-cluster halo to `150 kpc`. [2608.03288](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03288) Liao Yang uses APOGEE multi-epoch velocities to reject binary mass transfer as the dominant origin of nitrogen-rich field stars.

## Compact High-Redshift Sources and AGN Diagnostics

- [2608.02739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02739) Zizhao He et al. `Galaxy-LRD Strong Lenses: A Missing Population?`
  - **Question:** how many galaxy-scale strongly lensed Little Red Dots should current and near-future surveys detect?
  - **Data:**
    - Source population: literature-based Little Red Dot source models.
    - Lens population: foreground early-type galaxy deflectors.
    - Simulation volume: Monte Carlo survey over `50 deg^2`.
    - Counts: `270713` LRDs and `5460841` deflectors.
    - Survey comparison: COSMOS-Web, PRIMER-UDS, PRIMER-COSMOS, CEERS, JADES GOODS-S, and JADES GOODS-N over a de-duplicated `0.66 deg^2` footprint.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine LRD source models with foreground galaxy-scale deflectors.
    - Predict idealized double and quad lens surface densities.
    - Apply JWST point-spread-function and limiting-magnitude detectability cuts.
    - Estimate the probability of zero detections in current deep survey fields.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Idealized surface densities are `10.70+/-3.76 deg^-2` for doubles and `0.64+/-0.69 deg^-2` for quads.
    - Detectable surface densities fall to `3.70+/-1.89 deg^-2` and `0.52+/-0.58 deg^-2` after JWST detectability cuts.
    - The current `0.66 deg^2` deep-field footprint has a `8.6%` probability of detecting no doubles and a `70.8%` probability of detecting no quads.
  - **Position:** read first because lensed LRDs are a direct path to resolving the structure of compact high-redshift red sources.

- [2608.03947](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03947) P. Penil et al. `Profile Analysis of the Multiwavelength 2.1-year Oscillations of PG 1553+113`
  - **Question:** are the repeated multiwavelength cycles of PG 1553+113 self-similar enough to support a simple periodic driver?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: blazar PG 1553+113.
    - Bands: gamma-ray, X-ray, UV, and optical light curves.
    - Signal: known multiwavelength periodicity near `2.1 yr`.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify individual activity cycles in each band.
    - Fit temporal profiles with single- and multi-peaked analytical models.
    - Compare the repeatability of peak timing, amplitude, and substructure across cycles and wavelengths.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The `~2.1 yr` oscillation is a broad activity envelope with shorter-timescale substructure.
    - X-ray cycles strongly prefer multi-component profiles.
    - Secondary components are not systematically repeated across bands or cycles.
    - New cycles show dominant peaks plus weaker twin-peak-like features, keeping a supermassive-black-hole-binary scenario viable but not confirmed.
  - **Position:** useful time-domain AGN context because it separates geometric jet modulation from stricter SMBH-binary periodicity claims.

- [2608.03797](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03797) V. Crespi et al. `Iron Kalpha signatures from accretion disks around fermionic dark matter cores`
  - **Question:** can relativistic iron-line profiles distinguish black holes from compact fermionic dark-matter cores?
  - **Data:**
    - Model: extended RAR fermionic dark-matter distribution with a compact degenerate core and extended halo.
    - Diagnostic: fluorescent iron Kalpha line profiles from accretion disks.
    - Benchmark galaxy: MCG-06-30-15.
  - **Method:**
    - Compute line profiles with the Skylight numerical ray-tracing code.
    - Test lamp-post and phenomenological power-law disk emissivity profiles.
    - Compare predicted profile morphology with Kerr black-hole expectations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Highly compact fermion cores can produce broadening comparable to rapidly rotating black holes.
    - Emission from radii smaller than one gravitational radius creates features absent in the black-hole case.
    - MCG-06-30-15 can be matched when the fermion-core compactness is close to critical.
  - **Position:** lower-priority for galaxy evolution, but relevant to AGN inner-disk spectroscopy and compact-object degeneracies.

- [2608.03593](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03593) Ilidio Lopes `General-relativistic structure of two-component quantum dark fermion stars`
  - **Question:** what observable compact-object signatures arise from two-component quantum dark fermion stars?
  - **Data:**
    - Model: two degenerate fermion species with gravity, a Yukawa-mediated dark fifth force, and Bohm quantum pressure.
    - Parameter range: dark-fermion particle masses of `10^-11-10^-10 eV`.
    - Predicted observables: mass, radius, compactness, tidal deformability, inspiral frequency, and astrometric microlensing.
  - **Method:**
    - Solve the general-relativistic structure equations with a nonlinear Klein-Gordon field in the Schwarzschild interior.
    - Use the dark-fermion mass and Yukawa-to-gravity coupling ratio as controlling Lagrangian parameters.
    - Map equilibrium configurations onto gravitational-wave and microlensing observables.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Predicted radii span `3-24 km`.
    - Compactness spans `0.14-0.34`.
    - The most compact models cross the photon sphere and can mimic low-mass black holes in the mass-gap regime.
    - Non-zero tidal deformability distinguishes them from genuine black holes.
  - **Position:** compact-object theory support paper for interpreting future multimessenger constraints on dark-sector alternatives.

## Environments, Gas Reservoirs, and Galaxy Dynamics

- [2608.03693](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03693) Natan de Isidio et al. `Circumgalactic medium depletion drives satellite quenching in IllustrisTNG`
  - **Question:** what gas reservoir controls delayed-then-rapid quenching of satellite galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: IllustrisTNG MaNGA-like mock galaxies.
    - Sample: `~7300` galaxies, including `2800` satellites.
    - Time axis: time since infall.
    - Mass regime: special attention to low-mass satellites at `M_star lesssim 10^10 Msun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Reconstruct baryonic, dark-matter, structural, and chemical histories.
    - Track hot and cool gas reservoir depletion after infall.
    - Compare satellite histories with quenched central galaxies.
    - Relate CGM loss, SFR decline, tidal stripping, compactness, and metallicity.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Satellites lose `~90%` of their hot CGM within `4.2+/-0.6 Gyr`.
    - Hot-gas mass correlates strongly with SFR, identifying the CGM as the long-term fuel reservoir.
    - Present-day quenched satellites were accreted earlier than star-forming satellites, `6.5+/-0.3` versus `4.3+/-0.3 Gyr` ago.
    - Satellites continue forming stars for at least `~3 Gyr` after infall before rapid decline.
  - **Position:** read-first environment paper because it ties infall, CGM stripping, and quenching timescales into one causal sequence.

- [2608.02898](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02898) Norma Araceli Nava-Moreno et al. `Tracing luminous infrared galaxy populations through cluster evolution in a cosmological mock redshift survey`
  - **Question:** how do dusty star-forming galaxy populations evolve inside cluster progenitors?
  - **Data:**
    - Mock survey: GARDENS-Wide.
    - Area: `100 deg^2`.
    - Base simulation: MultiDark-Planck dark-matter halo simulation.
    - Wavelength calibration: number counts at `500 micron`, `1.1 mm`, `1.4 mm`, and `2.0 mm`.
  - **Method:**
    - Build a cosmological mock redshift survey for dusty star-forming galaxies.
    - Identify gravitationally bound systems and trace assembly histories.
    - Measure galaxy-content evolution and proto-cluster structural extent.
    - Forecast observables for the TolTEC Large-Scale Structure survey.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Star-forming galaxies contribute `~35%` of members at low redshift, `60-65%` at `z~2`, and `~20%` by `z~5`.
    - LIRGs contribute `~20-40%` and peak near `z~2`.
    - Proto-cluster radii contract from `>20` comoving Mpc at high redshift to a few Mpc by `z~0`.
    - ULIRGs become increasingly centrally concentrated at `z>1.5` in rich proto-clusters.
  - **Position:** useful proto-cluster and dusty-galaxy forecast paper for interpreting mm survey environments.

- [2608.03576](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03576) Andreea A Varasteanu et al. `MIGHTEE-HI / LADUMA: Investigating the link between baryons and dynamics with 130 resolved HI-selected galaxies`
  - **Question:** do resolved HI-selected galaxies show evolution in the baryonic Tully-Fisher relation or radial acceleration relation?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `130` purely HI-selected galaxies.
    - Surveys: MIGHTEE-HI and LADUMA.
    - Redshift reach: `z approx 0.09`.
    - Observables: resolved HI kinematics and baryonic mass profiles.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure the bTFR and RAR from resolved kinematics.
    - Fit the bTFR in the inverse direction to reduce HI-flux selection bias.
    - Fit MOND interpolating functions to the RAR.
    - Compare redshift evolution constraints from bTFR and RAR fits.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The RAR acceleration scale is `a0=(1.50+/-0.05)x10^-10 m s^-2`.
    - The RAR intrinsic scatter is `0.096+/-0.006 dex`.
    - The inverse bTFR slope corresponds to a forward slope of `3.72+/-0.16`.
    - No significant RAR acceleration-scale evolution is detected for the pure HI-selected sample.
    - Apparent bTFR zero-point evolution depends strongly on fit direction and likely selection effects.
  - **Position:** useful dynamics baseline for low-redshift galaxy scaling relations and selection-aware evolution tests.

- [2608.02730](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02730) Avinash Chaturvedi et al. `The Fornax Cluster VLT Spectroscopic Survey - V. Mass modelling of the BCG NGC 1399 out to 150 kpc`
  - **Question:** how does the intra-cluster globular-cluster population affect dynamical mass models of the Fornax BCG NGC 1399?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: NGC 1399, the Fornax bright central galaxy.
    - Tracer: globular clusters extending beyond `200 kpc`.
    - Kinematics: Fornax Cluster VLT Spectroscopic Survey radial velocities plus literature data.
    - Modeling radius: `5 R_e`, approximately `150 kpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Apply spherical Jeans equations.
    - Fit dispersion-kurtosis models for GC kinematics.
    - Compare cusp-like NFW and core-like Burkert halos.
    - Include or exclude intra-cluster GCs selected by spatial segregation and velocity clipping.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Both NFW and Burkert halos can reproduce the observed GC kinematics.
    - The full GC sample gives an NFW virial mass of `log M_vir=13.81+/-0.09`.
    - Intra-cluster GCs show radial anisotropy in the outskirts, especially blue metal-poor GCs.
    - The outer halo requires the additional gravitational component associated with the cluster potential.
  - **Position:** local cluster-dynamics anchor for interpreting BCG halos and intra-cluster stellar tracers.

## Stellar Populations, ISM, and Local Calibration

- [2608.03140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03140) Takashi Onaka et al. `PDRs4All XXII. Near-Infrared continuum in the Orion Bar`
  - **Question:** what produces the near-infrared continuum excess in a resolved photodissociation region?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: Orion Bar.
    - Program: JWST Early Release Science PDRs4All.
    - Instrument: NIRSpec integral-field spectroscopy.
    - Regions: nine physically distinct zones, including ionized gas, atomic PDR, and molecular PDR.
  - **Method:**
    - Map near-infrared continuum emission relative to the `3.3 micron` aromatic infrared band.
    - Estimate and subtract foreground ionized-gas contribution with Cloudy.
    - Derive average spectra for each physical region.
    - Fit the continuum with two blackbody-like components.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - All nine regions retain continuum emission across `1-4.5 micron`.
    - Continuum beyond `2.7 micron` correlates well with the `3.3 micron` aromatic infrared band.
    - The `1.2 micron` continuum has no significant aromatic-band correlation.
    - Water-ice and CO2-ice absorption at `3.0` and `4.27 micron` suggests deeper PDR layers along the line of sight.
  - **Position:** local JWST calibration paper for interpreting dust, ice, and near-IR continuum components in star-forming environments.

- [2608.03288](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03288) Liao Yang et al. `Binary Constraints on the Origin of Nitrogen-rich Field Stars`
  - **Question:** are nitrogen-rich field stars mainly produced by binary mass transfer or by escape from dense stellar systems?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: APOGEE DR17.
    - Sample: `266` nitrogen-rich field stars.
    - Binary subset: multi-epoch radial velocities and elemental abundances.
    - Well-sampled subset: `46` stars with robust binary identification.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify radial-velocity variables with `Delta RV > 1 km/s`.
    - Use the `F_2` statistic to identify spectroscopic binaries.
    - Compare close-binary fractions with chemically normal field stars.
    - Test correlations with [N/Fe], [Ce/Fe], [C/Fe], and [Al/Fe].
  - **Conclusion:**
    - `33` of `266` N-rich field stars show radial-velocity variations.
    - `10` robust spectroscopic binaries appear in the well-sampled subset of `46` stars.
    - The close-binary fraction is `21.7+/-6.1%`, consistent with the chemically normal field value of `18.1+/-0.6%`.
    - Strongly Al-enhanced stars with `[Al/Fe] gtrsim 0.5` have binary fractions below `10%`.
    - Binary mass transfer is not the dominant channel; dense birth environments and globular-cluster escape are favored.
  - **Position:** stellar-population support paper relevant to chemical tagging of dense birth environments.

- [2608.03312](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03312) Baitian Tang et al. `Chemical Abundances and Globular Clusters of Milky Way Dwarf Galaxies`
  - **Question:** how do dwarf-galaxy chemical abundances and globular clusters trace Milky Way assembly?
  - **Data:**
    - Project: GASTRONOMI.
    - Targets: stars in five classical dwarf galaxies, Milky Way in-situ populations, accreted populations, and globular clusters.
    - Diagnostics: [Si/Fe], [Al/Fe], [Fe/H], and nitrogen-rich stellar populations.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure precise chemical abundances in dwarf-galaxy stars.
    - Compare in-situ Milky Way stars with stars originating in dwarf galaxies.
    - Use globular-cluster multiple populations as environmental probes.
    - Develop an [Al/Fe]-based classification for primordial globular-cluster populations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Classical dwarf galaxies show mass-dependent chemical evolution, especially in alpha elements.
    - Metal-rich stars with `[Fe/H]>-1.5` separate in [Al/Fe] between in-situ and dwarf-galaxy origins.
    - Nitrogen-rich stars in Fornax are likely relics of disrupted globular clusters.
    - The [Al/Fe]-[Fe/H] plane can separate in-situ and accreted globular clusters.
  - **Position:** lower-priority but useful chemical-tagging context for dense environments and accretion history.

- [2608.03098](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03098) Yang Shen et al. `Lithium as a Signpost for Compact Object Binary Candidates in the LAMOST Medium Resolution Survey`
  - **Question:** can lithium enhancement efficiently pre-select compact-object binary candidates in spectroscopic surveys?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: LAMOST Medium Resolution Survey.
    - Reference system: Gaia NS1.
    - Parent sample: `4441` metal-poor main-sequence stars selected to resemble Gaia NS1.
    - Candidate sample: `33` stars with strong Li `6707 Angstrom` absorption.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure Li equivalent widths.
    - Use radial-velocity variation and Gaia astrometric-binarity signatures.
    - Follow up the strongest compact-object companion candidates spectroscopically.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The Li-selected sample narrows to `3` compact-object-binary candidates.
    - One candidate is an eclipsing binary.
    - Follow-up spectroscopy disfavors massive companions for the other two candidates.
  - **Position:** compact-binary survey-method note, relevant mainly as a caution about lithium as a pre-selection signal.

- [2608.02945](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02945) Hugh Kramer et al. `Kick Velocities and Mass Function of Free-Floating Planets from Dynamical Ejection in Hierarchical Three-Body Systems`
  - **Question:** how do hierarchical three-body systems set free-floating-planet kick velocities and microlensing timescales?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: direct N-body integrations of Solar-mass host stars, giant perturbers, and lighter planets.
    - Parameters: ejected-planet mass, giant-planet mass, semi-major axis, and eccentricities.
    - Survey context: Roman and Euclid microlensing sensitivity.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure asymptotic ejection velocity and finite-radius kick speed.
    - Interpret trends with Hill-scale scattering, Tisserand parameters, and eccentricity-dependent slingshot envelopes.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Ejected-body mass has little effect across four orders of magnitude.
    - Giant-planet mass sets the ejection scale and time.
    - High light-planet eccentricity extends kicks to `~23 km/s`.
    - Highly eccentric giant perturbers can rarely produce kicks near `80 km/s`.
  - **Position:** mostly exoplanet dynamics, but relevant to Roman microlensing foreground populations.
