It directly addresses how chemically enriched dust phases can survive after quenching and remain visible to JWST/MIRI even when ALMA continuum dust is weak.

The best observational ISM entry is [2608.27560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27560) Wuji Wang on resolved PAH and mid-infrared line maps in eight `z~1` galaxies with JWST/MIRI MRS. It gives spatially resolved PAH charge and size diagnostics near the end of cosmic noon, including AGN and interacting systems.

The strongest compact-AGN and early-black-hole context is split between [2608.27596](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27596) Yanlong Shi and [2608.28507](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28507) Joheen Chakraborty. Chakraborty measures relativistic outflows in quasi-periodic eruptions and quantifies the mass and energy recycled into circumnuclear gas.

## Dust, PAHs, and Gas Diagnostics

- [2608.27571](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27571) D. Donevski et al. `Dust and PAHs in late-stage galaxy evolution: Imprints of TP-AGB dust injection, grain growth and AGN feedback in high-z quiescent galaxies with JWST and ALMA`
  - **Question:** how do dust, cold gas, and PAHs evolve after quenching in galaxies now accessible to JWST and ALMA?
  - **Data:**
    - Model population: quiescent galaxies with flexible star-formation histories.
    - Comparison regime: systems near `z~1` with `M_star~8e10 Msun`, stellar ages near `2 Gyr`, and cold-gas fractions of `1-10%`.
    - Observational frame: ALMA dust-continuum limits and JWST/MIRI PAH sensitivity.
  - **Method:**
    - Build a semi-analytic model tracking cold gas, small and large grains, and PAHs after quenching.
    - Include delayed TP-AGB dust injection, ISM grain growth, and AGN feedback.
    - Compare post-quenching dust and gas ratios across different histories.
    - Predict which PAH-rich phases remain visible when cold dust is ALMA faint.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Similar-mass quiescent galaxies can span `2-3 dex` in `M_dust/M_star` and `M_dust/M_gas`.
    - TP-AGB dust injection and grain growth can sustain dust enrichment for up to `~2.5 Gyr` after quenching.
    - Without those channels, pre-quenching dust drops below `10%` of its initial value within `<=0.5-1 Gyr`.
    - AGN feedback can shorten dust survival by roughly another factor of two.
    - PAH fractions of `~2-3%` can persist even when `M_dust/M_star<=1e-4`.
  - **Position:** read first because it separates residual cold gas from chemically enriched dust and PAH phases in quenched high-redshift galaxies.

- [2608.27560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27560) Wuji Wang et al. `Resolved Dust in z~1 Galaxies with JWST/MIRI MRS: Survey Description and First View on PAHs, Mid-IR Atomic Emission, and Warm H2`
  - **Question:** what can resolved mid-infrared spectroscopy reveal about PAH structure and warm gas in main-sequence galaxies near `z~1`?
  - **Data:**
    - Facility: JWST/MIRI MRS integral-field spectroscopy.
    - Sample: eight star-forming main-sequence galaxies at `z~1`.
    - Stellar masses: `M_star=10^10.6-10^11.2 Msun`.
    - Infrared luminosities: `L_IR=10^11.5-10^11.9 Lsun`.
    - Subsamples: two AGN hosts and two interacting systems.
  - **Method:**
    - Map PAH emission from `3.3 um` to `11.3 um`.
    - Measure mid-infrared atomic fine-structure lines, Bralpha, and H2 rotational lines.
    - Use PAH ratios to infer molecule charge and size trends.
    - Compare AGN, merger, central, and outskirts regions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - JWST/MIRI resolves PAH emission directly at the end of cosmic noon.
    - AGN and mergers show a higher neutral-PAH fraction.
    - Atomic and H2 line ratios indicate stronger radiation fields or shocks in those systems.
    - Central PAHs are larger and less ionized than PAHs in galaxy outskirts.
    - The sample gives a resolved benchmark for PAH evolution at fixed `L_IR`.
  - **Position:** high-value observational ISM paper because it turns PAH diagnostics into spatially resolved measurements for cosmic-noon galaxies.

- [2608.27779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27779) Eliza C. Diggins et al. `High Dispersion Measure Fast Radio Bursts from Young, Dust-Poor H II Regions`
  - **Question:** can young low-metallicity H II regions explain extreme local dispersion measures in repeating FRBs?
  - **Data:**
    - Hosts: repeating FRB environments with large host-frame DM excesses.
    - Models: semi-analytic cluster-cloud calculations and one-dimensional radiation-hydrodynamic simulations.
    - Stellar populations: time-dependent ionizing luminosities from pySTARBURST99.
  - **Method:**
    - Model FRB-emitting neutron stars embedded in cluster-forming molecular clouds.
    - Evolve ionized gas columns around young massive associations.
    - Vary stellar mass, age, dust content, and metallicity.
    - Compare predicted H II region DM with observed FRB excesses.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Young associations with `M_star~10^5-10^6 Msun` can produce `DM_HII>=10^3 pc cm^-3`.
    - The required FRBs form early, within `<=5 Myr`, and remain inside ionized gas.
    - LMC-like or lower metallicity environments favor the highest local DM.
    - Dust absorption suppresses ionized columns in higher-metallicity regions.
    - Metal-rich quiescent hosts may provide cleaner FRB samples for intervening IGM/CGM studies.
  - **Position:** useful IGM-probe context because host-local DM physics controls how cleanly FRBs trace intervening baryons.

## AGN, Quasars, and Compact Black-Hole Growth

- [2608.27596](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27596) Yanlong Shi et al. `Formation of black hole stars via star--black hole collisions`
  - **Question:** when do star-black-hole collisions form quasi-hydrostatic black-hole stars instead of unstable remnants?
  - **Data:**
    - Collision target: `100 Msun` main-sequence stars.
    - Environments: globular clusters, dense stellar systems, and AGN disks.
    - Calculations: semi-analytic models, three-dimensional hydrodynamic simulations, and one-dimensional stellar evolution.
  - **Method:**
    - Simulate collisions between stellar-mass black holes and massive stars.
    - Vary impact velocity and black-hole-to-star mass ratio.
    - Follow envelope retention, shock heating, and thermal relaxation.
    - Test whether the remnant becomes quasi-hydrostatic with MESA calculations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Gas drag retains the black hole unless the impact velocity exceeds `~2 sqrt(G(M_star+M_bh)/R_star)`.
    - Black holes with `M_bh>=30 Msun` leave unstable quasi-spherical or disk-like envelopes.
    - Black holes with `M_bh<=10 Msun` can form black-hole stars.
    - The analytic condition for black-hole-star formation is `M_bh<=0.2 M_star`.
    - Repeated collisions may help grow massive black holes in high-redshift nuclear star clusters and may connect to JWST little red dots.
  - **Position:** high-priority compact-object entry because it offers an explicit physical path from dense stellar environments to early massive black-hole growth.

- [2608.28507](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28507) Joheen Chakraborty et al. `Relativistic outflows power a quasi-periodic eruption: constraints on energetics, mass loss, and emission mechanisms`
  - **Question:** do quasi-periodic eruptions launch relativistic outflows that recycle mass and energy into SMBH environments?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: ZTF19acnskyy, also called Ansky.
    - Facility: deep XMM-Newton observations of individual quasi-periodic eruptions.
    - Spectral diagnostics: Fe XIX-XXIV L-shell absorption and emission.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure time-dependent P Cygni profiles during eruptions.
    - Infer wind columns, velocities, mass-loss rates, and kinetic power.
    - Build an analytic wind-turn-on model for evolving luminosity and ionization.
    - Match the observed light curve and spectral-line behavior.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The spectra show outflows with `N_H~10^22-10^23 cm^-2`.
    - Wind speeds reach `|v_w/c|~0.2`.
    - The model requires `Mdot~10^-9-10^-8 Msun s^-1`.
    - Each eruption ejects `~10^-3 Msun` and `>=10^49 erg`.
    - A `~1 Msun` mass reservoir limits the QPE lifetime to `<=30 yr`.
  - **Position:** relevant AGN-feedback paper because QPEs may inject measurable mass and energy into the circumnuclear medium on short duty cycles.

- [2608.27730](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27730) Maggie C. Huber et al. `Overmassive supermassive black holes in SDSS close galaxy pairs`
  - **Question:** do SMBHs in close galaxy pairs grow out of step with their host bulges?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: SDSS value-added catalogs.
    - Systems: close galaxy pairs with AGN broad-line SMBH mass estimates.
    - Host measurements: bulge-disk decomposed stellar masses and velocity-dispersion scaling relations.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare SMBH masses from broad lines with masses inferred from bulge scaling relations.
    - Split trends by primary versus secondary galaxy and projected separation.
    - Compare black-hole mass ratios predicted by `M_bh-M_bulge` and `M_bh-sigma`.
    - Assess implications for major and minor black-hole merger demographics.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - SMBH growth can outpace bulge growth in AGN hosted by secondary bulges.
    - The overmassive signal strengthens at smaller pair separations.
    - `M_bh-M_bulge` predicts black-hole mass ratios closer to unity than `M_bh-sigma`.
    - Major black-hole merger fractions are higher by `6-20%` under the bulge-mass scaling.
    - The result affects gravitational-wave predictions and high-redshift overmassive SMBH interpretation.
  - **Position:** useful merger and AGN-context paper because close-pair growth can bias the scaling relations used at high redshift.

- [2608.27773](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27773) Amy Cavanaugh et al. `Near-Infrared Spectroscopy of Low-Redshift Palomar-Green Quasars and Composite Spectra`
  - **Question:** what are the near-infrared continuum and emission-line properties of the low-redshift Palomar-Green quasar sample?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: all `87` Palomar-Green quasars with `z<0.5`.
    - Facility: Apache Point Observatory `3.5 m` telescope.
    - Instrument: TripleSpec cross-dispersed near-infrared spectroscopy.
    - Wavelength range: rest-frame `8800-22000 Angstrom`.
  - **Method:**
    - Build a high-S/N NIR composite spectrum.
    - Characterize continuum and emission-line properties.
    - Construct composites split by luminosity and Eddington ratio.
    - Compare red continuum behavior across quasar subsamples.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The H-band composite reaches S/N of several hundred per `4 Angstrom` pixel.
    - More luminous NIR composites become redder at long wavelengths.
    - The redder shape is consistent with stronger hot dust emission.
    - Luminosity and Eddington-ratio splits expose systematic NIR spectral differences.
  - **Position:** useful calibration entry because low-redshift quasar composites anchor AGN spectral templates used for higher-redshift interpretation.

## Lensing, Dark Matter, and Gas Dynamics

- [2608.27566](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27566) D. M. Williams et al. `TDCOSMO. XXVII. JWST-based Lens Models and H0 Measurement of WFI2033, HE0435, and PG1115`
  - **Question:** how much do JWST imaging, NIRSpec kinematics, and environment estimates improve time-delay lens cosmography?
  - **Data:**
    - Lenses: WFI2033-4723, HE0435-1223, and PG1115+080.
    - Imaging: JWST/NIRCam F115W.
    - Kinematics: aperture-integrated stellar velocity dispersions from JWST/NIRSpec.
    - Ancillary constraints: published time delays and external-convergence estimates.
  - **Method:**
    - Reconstruct lens light and quasar images with STARRED PSF modeling.
    - Combine lens models with time delays, kinematics, and environment corrections.
    - Infer `H0` in flat Lambda-CDM under `lambda_int=1`.
    - Compare results with prior HST-based lens models.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Individual lens constraints are `H0=71.8`, `74.2`, and `73.4 km s^-1 Mpc^-1` times `lambda_int`.
    - The combined three-lens result is `H0=73.5_-2.8^+2.7 lambda_int km s^-1 Mpc^-1`.
    - The combined value is consistent with HST-based results but shows reduced inter-lens scatter.
    - A forthcoming set of `16` JWST NIRCam targets should tighten the precision further.
  - **Position:** useful environment-method paper because lens cosmography depends on host-galaxy structure, external convergence, and group-scale mass fields.

- [2608.27557](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27557) Ken Van Tilburg et al. `Discovering Substellar Dark Matter Halos with Astrometric Weak Lensing of Multiply Imaged Quasars`
  - **Question:** can time-domain astrometric weak lensing of lensed quasars detect substellar dark-matter halos?
  - **Data:**
    - Targets: benchmark lensed quasar systems B1422+231 and SDSS J1029+2623.
    - Campaign design: `10 yr` monitoring with `300` epochs.
    - Precision targets: `0.1-1 microarcsec` centroiding.
    - Halo regime: subhalos with sizes of `0.01-1 pc` and masses of `10^-6-1 Msun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Model stochastic centroid motion from many line-of-sight microhalos.
    - Use relative angular acceleration between macro-images as the observable.
    - Forecast sensitivity for galaxy-scale and cluster-scale lensed quasars.
    - Translate detections or null results into dark-matter microhalo constraints.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The method extends lensing sensitivity `12-14` orders of magnitude below current detected dark structures.
    - A galaxy-lens benchmark can reach variance S/N of order ten under optimistic microhalo-survival assumptions.
    - A cluster-lens benchmark reaches order-unity sensitivity.
    - Detection would constrain particle properties such as fermion masses and boson masses.
    - Null results would constrain microhalo survival or small-scale power.
  - **Position:** useful quasar-lensing methods entry because multiply imaged quasars become probes of substellar dark structure.

- [2608.27970](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27970) Jixuan Yang et al. `From Inspiral to Expansion: The Wake-Driven Torque on Binary Black Holes in Gaseous Medium`
  - **Question:** how does wake-wake coupling alter binary black-hole orbital evolution in gaseous media?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: three-dimensional hydrodynamic simulations of circular BBHs in uniform flow.
    - Environment context: AGN disks and other gas-rich black-hole environments.
    - Control parameter: `eta=v_g/v_o`, the ratio of gas-flow speed to binary orbital speed.
  - **Method:**
    - Simulate the coupled gas wakes of the two black holes.
    - Measure the net torque as a function of `eta`.
    - Compare merged-wake and separated-wake regimes.
    - Apply the torque behavior to AGN-disk capture channels.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At small `eta`, the two wakes merge into one overdense envelope.
    - The merged-wake regime gives a negative time-averaged torque.
    - At larger `eta`, separated wakes can produce positive torque.
    - AGN-disk capture-channel binaries naturally enter the positive-torque regime.
    - Gas-driven expansion may suppress capture-channel merger rates by competing with gravitational-wave inspiral.
  - **Position:** relevant compact-object environment paper because AGN gas can reshape black-hole binary demographics before merger.

## Local and Low-Redshift Galaxy Structure

- [2608.28109](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28109) A. Vanzanella et al. `Dissecting ultra-diffuse galaxies in the field`
  - **Question:** what are the spectroscopic properties of blue ultra-diffuse galaxies in low-density environments?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `17` blue low-surface-brightness field candidates.
    - Spectroscopy: optical emission-line spectra for redshifts and star-formation rates.
    - Photometry: forced photometry for stellar masses and attenuation estimates.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure spectroscopic redshifts and structural parameters.
    - Compute effective radii and central surface brightnesses.
    - Derive Halpha and Hbeta line fluxes and aperture-corrected SFRs.
    - Separate confirmed UDGs, LSBGs, and giant LSBG candidates.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Nine candidates are confirmed UDGs.
    - Eight additional systems are LSBGs, including one giant LSBG.
    - Confirmed UDGs span `z_spec=0.015-0.037`.
    - Their effective radii are `1.69-4.99 kpc`.
    - Their central surface brightnesses are `24.05-24.98 mag arcsec^-2`.
    - Blue field UDGs sit on the star-forming main sequence with heterogeneous attenuation.
  - **Position:** lower-priority but useful morphology paper because diffuse-galaxy demographics depend strongly on environment.

- [2608.28388](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28388) V. S. Goradzhanov et al. `Dynamical Modeling of Kinematically Decoupled Galaxies: Structural Components`
  - **Question:** can orbit-superposition modeling identify the stellar components of kinematically decoupled galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Galaxies: PGC 35706 and LEDA 2220522.
    - Spectroscopy: MaNGA integral-field data.
    - Imaging: DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys deep photometry.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit axisymmetric Schwarzschild orbit-superposition models.
    - Classify components by orbital circularity `lambda_z`.
    - Separate spheroidal, co-rotating disk, and counter-rotating disk contributions.
    - Compare stellar and ionized-gas kinematics.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Both galaxies contain a spheroidal component plus co-rotating and counter-rotating disks.
    - The spheroidal component contributes about `60%` of the mass in PGC 35706.
    - The spheroidal component contributes about `51%` in LEDA 2220522.
    - Ionized gas follows the counter-rotating disk in both galaxies.
    - The counter-rotating disks likely formed through externally accreted gas or gas-rich minor mergers.
  - **Position:** useful structural-diagnostics paper because external gas accretion leaves dynamical signatures that can mimic or complicate environment-driven histories.
