## Compact AGN and High-Redshift Accretion

- [2608.01647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01647) Zachary Stone et al. `NEXUS: Spectral Variability of Little Red Dots and Blue Active Galactic Nuclei at 2 < z < 6`
  - **Question:** do Little Red Dot broad lines and continua vary like normal broad-line AGN?
  - **Data:**
    - Spectroscopy: multi-epoch JWST/NIRSpec MSA spectra from the NEXUS program.
    - Sample: `17` Little Red Dots and `14` blue broad-line AGN at `2<z<6`.
    - Comparison sample: `56` low-redshift broad-line AGN matched in Halpha luminosity.
    - Timescales: rest-frame `~1-3` months, with context from yearly to decade-scale LRD variability studies.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure Balmer decrements for LRDs, blue JWST AGN, and matched low-redshift AGN.
    - Measure ensemble rest-optical continuum and Halpha variability.
    - Compare broad-line variability patterns across monthly and longer timescales.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - LRDs have enhanced Balmer decrements compared with blue JWST AGN and matched low-redshift AGN.
    - LRD rest-optical continua show little ensemble variability, with rms `<=3%`.
    - Intrinsic Halpha rms variability is constrained to `<=4%` over the sampled timescales.
    - LRD broad-line variability follows a low-level white-noise pattern rather than the `~6%` monthly red-noise behavior of normal AGN.
  - **Position:** read first because LRD variability directly tests whether compact high-redshift broad-line sources are normal accreting nuclei or a distinct population.

- [2608.01249](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01249) Shuang-Liang Li et al. `The physical mechanism for two rapid changing-look AGNs: SDSS J0225+0030 and SDSS J1723+5504`
  - **Question:** what physical mechanism can produce turn-on changing-look AGN transitions in less than one year?
  - **Data:**
    - Targets: SDSS J0225+0030 and SDSS J1723+5504.
    - Phenomenology: turn-on changing-look transitions with timescales shorter than `1 yr`.
    - Physical regime: black-hole masses of order `10^9 Msun` and Eddington-scaled accretion rates near `0.01`.
  - **Method:**
    - Test whether large-scale magnetic fields can shorten viscous timescales enough.
    - Revise an ADAF-to-thin-disk collapse model for the bright-state inner disk.
    - Estimate the transition radius from optical-flux variation between bright and dim states.
    - Compare model cooling timescales with observed transition timescales.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Magnetic-field-enhanced radial inflow still gives a timescale longer than `1 yr`.
    - Lower inner-disk gas temperatures near `10^4 K` can reduce the transition radius.
    - The revised ADAF-collapse model yields cooling timescales comparable to the observed transitions.
  - **Position:** useful for time-domain AGN interpretation because it tests accretion-state physics against the fastest changing-look systems.

- [2608.01163](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01163) Hugh McDougall et al. `AGN Reverberation Mapping with LITMUS: Fundamental Limits on lag Recovery Rates`
  - **Question:** how reliably can modern AGN reverberation-mapping surveys recover broad-line lags?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey context: high-redshift reverberation-mapping programs such as OzDES and SDSS.
    - Problem cases: poor signal-to-noise, seasonal gaps, multimodal lag posteriors, and aliasing.
    - Lines analyzed in the OzDES context: MgII, CIV, and Hbeta.
  - **Method:**
    - Implement the LITMUS lag-measurement package.
    - Use Bayesian evidence to identify false-positive lag measurements.
    - Marginalize over uncertainty in the underlying AGN variability.
    - Compare recovered lags with random-chance recoveries and distinguish non-detections from no-reverberation cases.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Previous reverberation-mapping work likely overestimated confidence in some recovered lags.
    - MgII has a low recovered reverberation percentage of `3-28%`, depending on assumptions.
    - CIV and especially Hbeta have much higher recovered percentages, with Hbeta consistent with `100%`.
  - **Position:** high-value survey-method entry because reliable lag inference underpins black-hole mass calibration for large AGN samples.

## AGN Gas, Obscuration, and Binary Channels

- [2608.02294](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02294) Peter G. Boorman et al. `XRISM Resolves the Circum-nuclear Environment of NGC 4945`
  - **Question:** what obscuration geometry explains the high-resolution X-ray spectrum of the nearest bright Compton-thick AGN?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: NGC 4945, the closest Compton-thick AGN and a bright hard-X-ray benchmark.
    - Instrument: XRISM/Resolve high-resolution spectroscopy.
    - Spectral range: `4-15 keV`, including Fe Kalpha doublet emission and a weak Compton shoulder.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit the full Resolve spectrum with a de-coupled dual-obscurer model.
    - Separate the line-of-sight primary obscurer from the reprocessed-emission component.
    - Use line broadening under a virial assumption to estimate reprocessor scale.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - A low-covering-factor Compton-thick primary obscurer intersects the line of sight.
    - A high-covering-factor Compton-thin secondary reprocessor dominates the `~4-10 keV` reprocessed emission.
    - The secondary reprocessor can lie at `~0.12 pc`.
    - Simpler coupled spectral models may overestimate Compton-thick covering factors and underestimate intrinsic X-ray luminosities.
  - **Position:** important local benchmark for interpreting the obscured growth phase of more distant AGN.

- [2608.00971](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00971) Xiaoyu Xu et al. `VLT/MUSE Study of Close AGN Pairs and Host Galaxies in the Local Universe. I. Overview of the Ionized Gas`
  - **Question:** how do close AGN pairs shape ionized-gas kinematics, ionization, and fading activity?
  - **Data:**
    - Parent selection: Big Multi-AGN Catalog cross-matched with the VLT/MUSE public archive.
    - Sample: `12` AGN pair candidates at `z<0.1`.
    - Pair scale: projected separation `r_p<=20 kpc`.
    - Nuclei: `24` nuclei with spatially resolved ionized-gas measurements.
  - **Method:**
    - Decompose optical emission lines into two Gaussian components.
    - Separate disk rotation from non-circular motions.
    - Classify dominant ionization mechanisms with spatially resolved BPT diagnostics.
    - Use emission-line ratios and AGN photoionization modeling to test fading activity.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Both nuclei are Seyfert or LINER in `4/12` systems.
    - Regularly rotating ionized-gas disks appear in `16/24` nuclei.
    - Prominent tidal ionized-gas features appear in `9` systems.
    - Ionized-gas outflows are detected in `18` nuclei.
    - Three nuclei show evidence for fading AGN activity over several `10^4 yr`.
  - **Position:** useful low-redshift anchor for merger-triggered AGN activity, gas response, and dual-AGN demographics.

- [2608.00242](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00242) Yanlong Shi et al. `Stellar mergers and chemical element mixing: implications for the metamorphic stellar evolution in AGN disks`
  - **Question:** how do stellar mergers in AGN disks alter remnant evolution and chemical enrichment?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: three-dimensional hydrodynamical stellar-merger calculations evolved for `~100` dynamical times.
    - Physics: subgrid turbulent chemical diffusion and both idealized polytropic and realistic composition-dependent equations of state.
    - AGN-disk context: metamorphic AGN stellar models generated with MESA.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify dominant mixing mechanisms in idealized merger remnants.
    - Vary mass ratio, relative velocity, impact parameter, and stellar structure.
    - Extend the models to stars embedded in AGN disks.
    - Follow post-merger thermal relaxation and later stellar evolution.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Younger metamorphic-star mergers and H-rich accreting AGN-star mergers can rejuvenate old metamorphic stars through efficient core mixing.
    - Merger remnants are nitrogen-enriched, with `N/O~1-3` and `N/C>=5`.
    - Remnants can converge onto the isolated metamorphic AGN-star main sequence after reaching accretion-wind equilibrium.
    - Chemically enriched material can be deposited back into the AGN disk.
  - **Position:** theoretical context for nuclear stellar populations and chemically distinctive transients in AGN disks.

- [2608.00290](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00290) Mu'allim Yakubu `A Review of How Supermassive Black Hole Binaries Can Be Detected and Characterized through Gravitational Lensing of Background Stars`
  - **Question:** can background-star lensing reveal sub-parsec supermassive black-hole binaries before the LISA regime?
  - **Data:**
    - Channels reviewed: Milky Way bulge-star lensing by a possible Sgr A-star binary and Quasi-Periodic Lensing of Starlight by binaries in external host galaxies.
    - Target binary regime: sub-parsec separations between directly imaged kpc pairs and millihertz gravitational-wave sources.
    - Forecast regime: periods below `10 yr` and `z<0.3`.
  - **Method:**
    - Review gravitational-lensing channels for background stars.
    - Compare lensing signals with periodic variability, Doppler boosting, and circumbinary accretion diagnostics.
    - Discuss synergies with pulsar timing arrays, LISA, ELT-class instruments, and LSST.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Sgr A-star binary lensing could produce a secondary image accessible to ELT-class instruments.
    - The QPLS framework predicts `1-50` to `190-5000` binaries per cubic parsec depending on assumptions.
    - Stellar lensing can complement electromagnetic and gravitational-wave binary searches.
  - **Position:** lower-priority review, but useful for massive-black-hole-binary search strategy and multimessenger context.

## Environments, Clusters, and Large-Scale Gas

- [2608.01940](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01940) Duho Kim et al. `Environmental dependence of galaxy properties in the DESI DR1 Bright Galaxy Survey: Star formation, morphology, and AGN activity at z < 0.55`
  - **Question:** how much of the environmental dependence of galaxy properties is intrinsic rather than compositional?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: DESI DR1 BGS_BRIGHT.
    - Sample: `~1.4 million` galaxies in groups and clusters.
    - Redshift range: `0.05<z<0.55`.
    - Halo range: `log(M_h/Msun)>=12.5`, spanning `12.5-15.0`.
    - Stellar-mass range: `log(M_star/Msun)=9.0-12.0`.
  - **Method:**
    - Use a magnitude-limited `r<19.5` sample and an extended halo-based group catalog.
    - Measure sSFR, Sersic index, and AGN fraction versus stellar mass and halo mass.
    - Separate central and satellite behavior.
    - Test BPT Seyfert and LINER fractions among emission-line-detected galaxies.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Median sSFR decreases with halo mass, but the trend is driven largely by the rising satellite fraction.
    - Satellites have lower median sSFR than centrals at fixed stellar mass and halo mass.
    - Mean Sersic index changes only weakly with halo mass, with median `Delta log(n)~0.05`.
    - Seyfert and LINER fractions show no clear environmental trend at fixed stellar mass.
  - **Position:** read-first environment paper because it uses DESI scale to separate halo effects from population-mix effects.

- [2608.00164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00164) Jared Siegel et al. `Evidence for a thermal pressure deficit in galaxy groups from the tSZ effect and weak lensing`
  - **Question:** do hydrodynamical simulations reproduce the thermal pressure of group-scale halos?
  - **Data:**
    - Tracers: DESI LRGs for galaxy-galaxy lensing and ACT single-channel temperature maps for tSZ.
    - Halo regime: `M_500=10^13-10^14 Msun`.
    - Redshift range: `0.4<z<1`.
    - Simulation comparison: FLAMINGO hydrodynamical simulations.
  - **Method:**
    - Directly model dust and radio emission from target galaxies to isolate the tSZ signal.
    - Use galaxy-galaxy lensing for like-with-like halo-mass comparison.
    - Compare observed tSZ profiles with multiple feedback prescriptions.
    - Test whether gas depletion alone can explain the discrepancy.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - FLAMINGO overpredicts observed tSZ signal at `<=3 arcmin`, roughly `<=4 R_500` at `z=0.7`.
    - Even the strongest gas-expulsion model overpredicts the thermal pressure.
    - The observed group gas density can already be matched, so additional gas depletion is not enough.
    - Current simulations appear to overpredict group thermal pressure by a factor of `~2`.
  - **Position:** high-value baryon-physics result for group thermodynamics, feedback models, and non-thermal pressure support.

- [2608.00627](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00627) Qinnan Zhu et al. `Metal sign of a large-scale AGN feedback in cool-core cluster MACS J1931.8-2634`
  - **Question:** does the cool-core cluster MACS J1931.8-2634 show metallicity signatures of large-scale AGN feedback?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: massive cool-core cluster MACS J1931.8-2634.
    - X-ray data: `~170 ks` of new XMM-Newton observations and `~100 ks` of archival Chandra observations.
    - Spectroscopy: CCD spectra for radial and 2D Fe abundance maps plus RGS Ne/Fe constraints.
  - **Method:**
    - Re-test the previously reported central Fe drop.
    - Build radial and two-dimensional Fe abundance maps.
    - Fit spectra with updated SPEX atomic data and single- and multi-temperature plasma models.
    - Use Ne/Fe to test whether dust depletion can explain the metal pattern.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The previously reported radial central Fe drop is not confirmed by XMM-Newton.
    - The 2D Fe distribution is asymmetric and elongated along the AGN-cavity axis.
    - Fe-rich regions extend beyond the immediate cavity scale.
    - The core Ne/Fe ratio is solar, `Ne/Fe=1.03^{+0.25}_{-0.23}`, disfavoring dust depletion.
  - **Position:** useful cluster-feedback paper because metal anisotropy traces cumulative AGN uplift and ICM mixing.

## Star Formation, ISM, and Feedback Calibrations

- [2608.00918](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00918) Daniela Calzetti et al. `The HII Regions' Molecular Law of Star Formation`
  - **Question:** what molecular star-formation law applies at the scale of individual HII regions?
  - **Data:**
    - Galaxies: NGC 628, NGC 5194, and NGC 5236.
    - Wavelengths: HST, JWST, and ground-based millimeter imaging.
    - Spatial scale: `~100 pc` HII-region measurements, with tests on `~500 pc` scales.
    - SFR tracer: JWST `21 micron` maps for dust-absorbed star formation.
  - **Method:**
    - Correlate SFR surface density with molecular-gas surface density.
    - Use high-resolution imaging to separate HII-region emission from diffuse galaxy emission.
    - Compare the observed relation with physical star-formation models.
    - Place local HII regions, high-redshift star-forming clumps, and starbursts on a common sequence.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The molecular star-formation relation has a log-log slope of `~1.85`.
    - The steep relation persists on `~500 pc` scales.
    - Diffuse `21 micron` emission correlates with galaxy stellar mass.
    - Local HII regions, high-redshift star-forming clumps, and low- and high-redshift starbursts form one sequence over `3` decades in gas surface density.
  - **Position:** important calibration paper for connecting resolved nearby-galaxy physics to high-redshift clump and starburst measurements.

- [2608.00719](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00719) Cheryl S. C. Lau et al. `Supernova feedback in porous photoionized Giant Molecular Clouds`
  - **Question:** how does pre-supernova photoionization structure change metal and momentum deposition in molecular clouds?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: Type II supernovae detonating in giant molecular clouds.
    - Cloud states: density structures shaped by photoionization feedback.
    - Diagnostic: a channeling parameter `P_chnl` for ionized-channel morphology, porosity, and boundedness.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare multidimensional blowouts with one-dimensional spherical-blast assumptions.
    - Vary cloud density structure and channel morphology.
    - Measure outflow velocity, kinetic energy, momentum, carried mass, and migration distance.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Ionized cavities and channels guide supernova energy into shock-driven blowouts.
    - Outflow velocities are higher than spherical-blast expectations but weakly dependent on porosity.
    - Total kinetic energy and momentum are similar across runs.
    - `P_chnl` mainly controls how much material is carried outward and how far it migrates.
  - **Position:** relevant for galaxy-scale feedback models because unresolved cloud structure can alter metal injection and turbulence scales.

- [2608.01052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01052) Yu Jiang et al. `Investigations of MWISP Bubbles: Identification and Analysis of Enclosed Molecular Bubbles by Weight Fields`
  - **Question:** can molecular bubbles be identified objectively in PPV spectral-line data cubes?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey data: MWISP `13CO` observations of the G17 region.
    - Target structures: enclosed molecular bubbles with cavity and shell signatures in position-position-velocity space.
    - Output: bubble candidates across complex molecular environments.
  - **Method:**
    - Build Bubble-Weight Fields, `W_l,b,v`, from cavity-interior evidence.
    - Aggregate topology across signal-to-noise tiers and velocity-integration scales.
    - Segment contiguous cavity interiors and associate them with surrounding molecular gas.
    - Measure shell morphology and expansion-like velocity structure with radial profiles, skeletons, and PV diagnostics.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - BWFields identifies molecular-bubble candidates with diverse morphologies and velocity structures.
    - The method links cavity interiors to host-cloud shell emission.
    - The turbulence-normalized expansion significance provides a direct expansion-like velocity diagnostic.
  - **Position:** lower-priority for the profile, but useful as a scalable stellar-feedback method for spectral-line surveys.

## Survey Infrastructure, Simulations, and Data Products

- [2608.01908](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01908) Maarten Baes et al. `The TNG50-SKIRT Atlas: Spatially resolved synthetic galaxies from the ultraviolet to the submillimetre (DR2)`
  - **Question:** what resolved synthetic observables does TNG50-SKIRT DR2 provide for galaxy-evolution comparisons?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: TNG50 cosmological hydrodynamical simulation.
    - Sample: `1154` stellar-mass-complete `z=0` galaxies.
    - Stellar-mass range: `10^9.8<M_star<10^12 Msun`.
    - Products: spatially resolved SEDs, broadband images, physical property maps, and integrated-property catalogs.
  - **Method:**
    - Post-process galaxies with the SKIRT Monte Carlo radiative-transfer code.
    - Extend wavelength coverage from ultraviolet through submillimeter.
    - Add dust emission, updated stellar-population models, and improved dust-enshrouded star-forming-region treatment.
    - Validate products through Monte Carlo-noise and internal-consistency checks.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - DR2 supports direct comparison with resolved and unresolved observations.
    - The atlas is useful for dust attenuation, dust emission, SFR tracers, morphology, and multi-wavelength scaling relations.
    - The public products cover multiple viewing orientations and spatial scales.
  - **Position:** important infrastructure for interpreting observed galaxy morphology and SEDs with simulation-calibrated forward models.

- [2608.02597](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02597) H. J. Ibarra-Medel et al. `SDSS-V Local Volume Mapper (LVM): Dithered Data Cube Reconstruction with 3dcubegen`
  - **Question:** how can SDSS-V LVM row-stacked spectra be combined into science-ready data cubes?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: SDSS-V Local Volume Mapper.
    - Targets: Milky Way, Magellanic Clouds, and nearby galaxies.
    - Inputs: multiple dithered RSS observations, including thousands of dithers and millions of spectra.
  - **Method:**
    - Implement 3DCubeGen for data-cube reconstruction.
    - Combine multiple LVM dithers while preserving flux and propagating uncertainties.
    - Improve spatial sampling, spatial resolution, depth, and sensitivity to faint emission.
    - Extend integral-field reconstruction approaches used in surveys such as CALIFA.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - 3DCubeGen produces homogeneous three-dimensional LVM data cubes.
    - The method improves spatial sampling and spectral depth across extended regions.
    - The resulting products support ionized-gas, stellar-population, and kinematic studies.
  - **Position:** useful methods paper for nearby-galaxy IFU data products that can calibrate resolved-galaxy physics.

- [2608.01054](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01054) Tapan K. Sasmal et al. `Identification and Study of Irregular Radio Sources with SKA Continuum Surveys`
  - **Question:** how will SKA continuum surveys identify and exploit irregular radio galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Source classes: bent-tail radio galaxies and winged radio galaxies.
    - Survey context: SKA continuum surveys with improved sensitivity, angular resolution, frequency coverage, and image quality.
    - Science targets: diffuse tails, weak bridges, remnant lobes, and low-surface-brightness wings.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine machine-learning classification, quantitative morphology, multi-wavelength host matching, and expert inspection.
    - Link radio morphology to host-galaxy properties, AGN activity, jet power, and environment.
    - Use irregular radio morphologies as tracers of intracluster or intragroup media.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - SKA surveys should identify large samples of irregular radio galaxies.
    - Bent-tail sources trace interaction between radio plasma and dense group or cluster gas.
    - Winged sources constrain backflow, jet reorientation, episodic activity, mergers, and environmental asymmetry.
  - **Position:** relevant to cosmic-web and protocluster work because radio morphology can map jet-environment coupling across cosmic time.

- [2608.02601](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02601) William Cerny et al. `Discovery of the Distant, Ultra-Faint Milky Way Satellite Aquarius IV with the Vera C. Rubin Observatory Early Data Preview 2`
  - **Question:** what does Rubin EDP2 already reveal about ultra-faint Milky Way satellites?
  - **Data:**
    - Discovery data: Vera C. Rubin Observatory Early Data Preview 2 photometry.
    - Confirmation data: archival Dark Energy Camera imaging.
    - Candidate: Aquarius IV, also Rubin J2201-0234.
  - **Method:**
    - Detect the stellar overdensity in Rubin photometry and independently confirm it in DECam imaging.
    - Jointly fit morphology and distance.
    - Compare the inferred stellar population with old, metal-poor isochrones.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Aquarius IV is detected at `~8 sigma` in Rubin EDP2 and confirmed at `~6 sigma` in DECam.
    - The system has `M_V=-1.9^{+0.6}_{-1.0}`, `r_1/2=19^{+4}_{-6} pc`, and `D_sun=109^{+6}_{-8} kpc`.
    - The population is consistent with age `13 Gyr` and metallicity `Z=0.0001`.
    - A globular-cluster classification is not yet ruled out.
  - **Position:** lower-priority galaxy-archaeology result, but operationally important as an early Rubin discovery demonstration.
