## Reionization, LRDs, and Early Accretion

- [2608.04101](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04101) Casey Papovich et al. `Implications of Broad [O III] 4364 and UV Line Emission in Two Little Red Dots at z ~ 7 - 8`
  - **Question:** what do broad optical and UV emission lines imply about the gas structure and ionizing sources in two high-redshift LRDs?
  - **Data:**
    - Targets: two Little Red Dots at `z=6.68` and `z=8.35`.
    - Spectroscopy: deep JWST/NIRSpec G140M and G395M.
    - Diagnostics: broad Balmer and [O III] 4364 plus UV semi-forbidden and nitrogen lines.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure broad and narrow line widths.
    - Use density-sensitive ratios to infer broad-line gas densities.
    - Compare line ratios with stellar and accretion-disk ionization scenarios.
    - Interpret nitrogen lines as rapid enrichment diagnostics.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Broad [O III] 4364 has `FWHM~1000 km/s`.
    - Broad-line gas densities reach `log n/cm^-3=6.3-7.9`.
    - If virial, the dense metal-enriched clouds lie at `~1-10 pc` from the engine.
    - The lines likely require accretion-disk ionization plus star formation.
  - **Position:** the week's most direct LRD spectrum paper because it measures the high-density gas that simple SED arguments cannot isolate.

- [2608.05281](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05281) Sukanya Mallik et al. `On the Origin of the Lyalpha Damping Wing in Galaxies at 8<=z<=10: Explorations using the NINJA Simulations`
  - **Question:** which gas phase dominates Lyalpha damping wings around reionization-era galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: NINJA cosmological hydrodynamical simulations.
    - Redshifts: `z=8` and `z=10`.
    - HII-region models: `0-400 pkpc`.
    - IGM neutral fractions: `x_HI=0.1`, `0.8`, and `1.0`.
  - **Method:**
    - Generate mock Lyalpha absorption spectra.
    - Compare observed inferred HI column-density distributions with model predictions.
    - Separate contributions from the IGM, CGM, and virial-radius gas.
    - Test whether HII-region size and neutral fraction alone explain the strongest absorbers.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Overdense gas extends to `~60-100 pkpc`.
    - Median damping-wing columns are degenerate between IGM neutral fraction and HII-region size.
    - HII regions larger than `~50 pkpc` or uniformly ionized IGM models fail for `NHI>10^22 cm^-2`.
    - Virial-radius partially ionized gas helps but does not fully match the strongest absorbers.
  - **Position:** week-ending read-first paper because it warns against treating Lyalpha damping wings as purely intergalactic neutral-fraction probes.

- [2608.01647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01647) Zachary Stone et al. `NEXUS: Spectral Variability of Little Red Dots and Blue Active Galactic Nuclei at 2 < z < 6`
  - **Question:** do LRD broad lines and continua vary like normal broad-line AGN?
  - **Data:**
    - Spectroscopy: multi-epoch JWST/NIRSpec MSA spectra from NEXUS.
    - Sample: `17` Little Red Dots and `14` blue broad-line AGN at `2<z<6`.
    - Comparison: `56` low-redshift broad-line AGN matched in H-alpha luminosity.
    - Timescales: rest-frame `~1-3` months.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure Balmer decrements for LRDs, blue JWST AGN, and matched low-redshift AGN.
    - Measure ensemble rest-optical continuum and H-alpha variability.
    - Compare broad-line variability patterns across monthly and longer timescales.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - LRDs have enhanced Balmer decrements relative to blue JWST AGN and matched low-redshift AGN.
    - LRD rest-optical continua show little ensemble variability, with rms `<=3%`.
    - Intrinsic H-alpha rms variability is constrained to `<=4%` over the sampled timescales.
    - LRD broad-line variability is lower than the `~6%` monthly red-noise behavior of normal AGN.
  - **Position:** important early-week LRD entry because variability directly tests whether LRD broad lines behave like normal accretion-driven broad lines.

- [2608.02739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02739) Zizhao He et al. `Galaxy-LRD Strong Lenses: A Missing Population?`
  - **Question:** how many galaxy-scale strongly lensed Little Red Dots should current and near-future surveys detect?
  - **Data:**
    - Source population: literature-based Little Red Dot models.
    - Lens population: foreground early-type galaxy deflectors.
    - Simulation volume: Monte Carlo survey over `50 deg^2`.
    - Survey comparison: COSMOS-Web, PRIMER, CEERS, and JADES fields over a de-duplicated `0.66 deg^2` footprint.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine LRD source models with galaxy-scale deflectors.
    - Predict idealized double and quad lens surface densities.
    - Apply JWST point-spread-function and limiting-magnitude detectability cuts.
    - Estimate zero-detection probabilities for current fields.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Idealized surface densities are `10.70+/-3.76 deg^-2` for doubles and `0.64+/-0.69 deg^-2` for quads.
    - Detectable surface densities fall to `3.70+/-1.89 deg^-2` and `0.52+/-0.58 deg^-2` after JWST detectability cuts.
    - The current `0.66 deg^2` deep-field footprint has an `8.6%` zero-detection probability for doubles and `70.8%` for quads.
  - **Position:** useful synthesis paper because lensed LRDs would resolve compact-source structure and test whether the current null result is surprising.

- [2608.05267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05267) Lasse Seyberlich et al. `Where did all the Little Red Dots go? The abundance of LRD analogues among objects with broad lines at z < 0.35`
  - **Question:** are LRD-like broad-line systems common in the low-redshift Universe?
  - **Data:**
    - Parent sample: broad H-alpha or H-beta emitters from SDSS.
    - Redshift range: `0<z<0.35`.
    - Added evidence: archival photometry and spectroscopy.
  - **Method:**
    - Select broad-line galaxies and quasars.
    - Identify v-shaped SEDs.
    - Fit modified-blackbody-like SEDs.
    - Compare analogue fractions in the full and luminosity-matched samples.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - `19%` of broad-line objects have v-shaped SEDs.
    - Only nine local LRD analogues survive the stronger SED criteria.
    - The analogue fraction is `0.08%` of all broad-line objects.
    - Among luminosity-matched objects, only `0.37%` show MBB-like SEDs.
  - **Position:** important counterpoint because it shows that LRD physics is not a common low-redshift broad-line phenomenon.

- [2608.05283](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05283) Elisha Modelevsky et al. `Emission line formation in scattering dominated media: implications for LRDs`
  - **Question:** can scattering-dominated gas explain broad LRD Balmer lines without standard external line scattering?
  - **Data:**
    - Phenomenon: broad H-alpha in LRD spectra.
    - Model: static optically thick gas envelopes with thermal populations.
    - Geometry: thin planar and radially extended photospheres.
  - **Method:**
    - Derive intrinsic line profiles with diffusion and random-walk arguments.
    - Compare intrinsic profiles against exponential external-scattering profiles.
    - Fit broken power-law forms to observed H-alpha.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Thin planar photospheres give a logarithmic plateau and `v^-1` wing.
    - Radially extended photospheres give a broken power law with `0<alpha<1`.
    - Broken power-law profiles can fit LRD H-alpha observations.
    - Non-LTE modeling is needed to explain line-to-continuum contrast.
  - **Position:** useful physics paper because it offers a testable line-shape prediction for high-quality LRD spectra.

## Quasar and AGN Demographics

- [2608.04078](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04078) L. Ighina et al. `The most extreme high-z radio quasars in the eRASS:1 X-ray survey`
  - **Question:** can X-ray, optical/NIR, and radio selection efficiently expand the known high-redshift radio-quasar population?
  - **Data:**
    - X-ray: eROSITA eRASS:1.
    - Optical/NIR: DECam Local Volume Exploration.
    - Radio: Rapid ASKAP Continuum Survey.
    - Follow-up: spectroscopy for `46` candidates.
  - **Method:**
    - Select candidate high-z radio quasars from matched multiwavelength data.
    - Confirm redshifts spectroscopically.
    - Identify blazars from X-ray-to-optical relative intensity.
    - Compare source counts with fractional IC/CMB expectations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - `39` sources are confirmed at `z>3.5`.
    - `14` new `z>4` radio quasars increase the known comparable population by `~65%`.
    - High-z X-ray selected blazar counts are broadly consistent with fractional IC/CMB expectations after selection effects.
  - **Position:** key quasar census paper because rare luminous radio quasars anchor early jet and black-hole growth studies.

- [2608.06000](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06000) Gaocheng Yin et al. `Luminosity function of quasars at 1.0<z<3.5 from SDSS and DESI`
  - **Question:** how can SDSS and DESI reduce quasar luminosity-function selection systematics?
  - **Data:**
    - Surveys: SDSS and DESI.
    - Redshift range: `1.0<z<3.5`.
    - Sample: `62426` type 1 quasars.
    - Areas: `~1700 deg^2` wide region plus `~265 deg^2` deep field.
  - **Method:**
    - Use a library of known quasars to define the sample.
    - Apply rest-frame UV/optical color cuts.
    - Quantify completeness and purity.
    - Construct QLFs across the peak epoch of quasar activity.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The selected sample reaches `~96%` completeness and `~93%` purity.
    - The design minimizes some selection biases from heterogeneous quasar targeting.
    - The resulting QLFs support black-hole growth and ionizing-background constraints at cosmic noon.
  - **Position:** useful demographic anchor because DESI-era QLFs set the baseline for interpreting rarer high-redshift quasar samples.

- [2608.05098](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05098) Missagh Mehdipour et al. `XRISM spectroscopy of NGC 5548`
  - **Question:** what does high-resolution X-ray spectroscopy reveal about multiphase AGN outflows?
  - **Data:**
    - Facility: XRISM/Resolve.
    - Target: NGC 5548.
    - Diagnostics: highly ionized absorption and emission lines.
  - **Method:**
    - Resolve X-ray line structure with XRISM.
    - Decompose ionized outflow phases.
    - Compare velocity and ionization structure across components.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - XRISM resolves multiphase highly ionized gas in the AGN environment.
    - The data sharpen constraints on outflow velocity and phase structure.
    - The target becomes a local calibration point for AGN feedback physics.
  - **Position:** lower-redshift but high-value AGN paper because Resolve-quality spectroscopy benchmarks outflow interpretations used at high redshift.

- [2608.05856](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05856) Young-Bin Shin et al. `Cosmological Distance Measurements of High Redshift Blazars: OJ 248 and 4C +38.41`
  - **Question:** can radio variability extend angular-diameter-distance measurements to high-redshift blazars?
  - **Data:**
    - Sources: OJ 248 at `z=0.939` and 4C +38.41 at `z=1.814`.
    - Observations: `43 GHz` radio light curves.
    - Distance assumptions: equipartition and sample-averaged intrinsic brightness temperature.
  - **Method:**
    - Estimate angular-diameter distances from variability.
    - Compute Doppler factors with the inverse-Compton method.
    - Quantify systematic uncertainties.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Equipartition distances are `7592.7 +/- 396.7 Mpc` and `11069.8 +/- 1216.9 Mpc`.
    - Epoch selection dominates the systematic error.
    - The approach is promising but not yet precision-limited by statistics alone.
  - **Position:** useful AGN-method paper because it explores whether compact jets can become independent distance probes.

## Gas, Structure, and Survey Products

- [2608.04087](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04087) Connor Jennings et al. `Predicting the Kinematics of the Cold Circumgalactic Medium from its Morphology using Convolutional Neural Networks`
  - **Question:** can cold-CGM morphology predict plane-of-sky velocity fields?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: TNG50 Milky Way and Andromeda analogs.
    - Sample: `182` galaxies.
    - Inputs: synthetic H-alpha emission maps with observational noise.
    - Targets: line-of-sight averaged two-dimensional velocity maps.
  - **Method:**
    - Train a UNet on simulated emission maps.
    - Add noise to mimic survey depth.
    - Compare inferred and true velocity directions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The network usually recovers the global flow direction qualitatively.
    - At future deep-map depths, typical RMS direction error is `0.3-0.5 v_vir`.
    - Two-dimensional emission maps may recover otherwise inaccessible CGM phase-space information.
  - **Position:** key CGM-method paper because it links morphology from future low-surface-brightness maps to gas dynamics.

- [2608.04656](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04656) Seoyoung Lyla Jung et al. `MIGHTEE-HI: Environmental effects of cosmic filaments on extragalactic HI detections in the COSMOS and XMM-LSS fields`
  - **Question:** does cosmic-web location change HI detection probability after controlling for galaxy properties?
  - **Data:**
    - HI: MIGHTEE in COSMOS and XMM-LSS.
    - Optical parent sample: DESI Survey galaxies.
    - Redshift range: `0.02<z<0.09`.
    - Environment: local density and filament distance.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure HI detection fraction versus stellar mass, morphology, density, and filament distance.
    - Control for covariates correlated with filament proximity.
    - Compare late-type and massive early-type galaxies.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Filament-proximate galaxies are more massive, denser-environment, and more often early type.
    - Filament proximity still affects HI detection after controls.
    - Late types in low-density regions have lower HI detection fractions near filaments.
    - Massive early types have enhanced HI detection in filaments relative to voids.
  - **Position:** important environment paper because it connects cold-gas reservoirs to cosmic-web geometry in legacy fields.

- [2608.04784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04784) Kai Wang et al. `Inflow-driven galaxy evolution - I. Revealing the physics of the fundamental metallicity relation`
  - **Question:** what gas-flow model produces the fundamental metallicity relation?
  - **Data:**
    - Framework: cosmological gas-flow model.
    - Calibration: mass-metallicity relation, star-forming main sequence, and stellar-to-halo mass relation.
    - Redshift span: `z=0-3`.
  - **Method:**
    - Solve continuity equations for gas, stars, SFR, and metallicity.
    - Calibrate to observed scaling relations.
    - Derive simplified limits for constant efficiency and mass loading.
    - Compare stellar and gaseous FMR forms.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The FMR emerges as a redshift-invariant surface in `M_star`, SFR, and `Z_g`.
    - In the constant-parameter limit, `Z_g` scales with `M_star/SFR`.
    - The gaseous FMR is more fundamental because `Z_g` follows `M_star/M_g`.
  - **Position:** strong baryon-cycle paper because it turns metallicity scaling relations into inflow and gas-reservoir constraints.

- [2608.05835](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05835) Kai Wang et al. `Ruffled Feathers: Merger-driven galaxy size growth and structural transformation in EAGLE`
  - **Question:** do merger prescriptions used in semi-analytic models match hydrodynamical simulation remnants?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: EAGLE.
    - Sample: `~4500` mergers.
    - Metrics: remnant size and morphology change.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify mergers and descendant properties.
    - Test an energy-conservation size estimator.
    - Evaluate gas-rich dissipation corrections.
    - Quantify mass-ratio dependence.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The estimator reproduces remnant sizes with `~0.12-0.15 dex` scatter.
    - A common dissipation correction underpredicts sizes by up to `~0.4 dex`.
    - Size growth increases monotonically with merger stellar mass ratio.
  - **Position:** useful structural-evolution calibration because size-growth recipes affect high-redshift mass-size interpretation.

- [2608.05923](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05923) M. Klein et al. `Optical Counterparts of MeerKLASS L-band and UHF-band surveys`
  - **Question:** how can optical and infrared hosts be robustly associated with MeerKLASS radio continuum sources?
  - **Data:**
    - Radio: MeerKLASS L-band and UHF-band surveys.
    - Optical/IR: KiDS DR5 and DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys DR10.
    - Products: counterpart probabilities, redshifts, and host-galaxy properties.
  - **Method:**
    - Use the Stellar-mass Enhanced Density Association method.
    - Compare candidate and displaced-control densities.
    - Include offset, stellar mass, redshift, and mid-infrared quasar selection.
    - Run a second-pass multi-component source association.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The L-band catalog includes `20400` KiDS-based counterparts.
    - The framework addresses dense optical fields and complex radio morphologies.
    - The product enables host-based radio source demographics and environment work.
  - **Position:** useful infrastructure because counterpart reliability limits radio AGN and star-forming galaxy analyses.

- [2608.05286](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05286) Hannah B. Koziol et al. `Variations in the 3.3 micron Polycyclic Aromatic Hydrocarbon Feature Across Nearby Galaxies Driven by Metallicity and Radiation Field Spectrum`
  - **Question:** what drives spatial variations in JWST/NIRCam 3.3 micron PAH emission?
  - **Data:**
    - Facility: JWST/NIRCam.
    - Filters: F335M, F300M, and F360M.
    - Targets: nearby galaxies at `~0.1 arcsec` resolution.
    - Comparisons: optical ionized-gas diagnostics and dust models.
  - **Method:**
    - Build an empirical continuum subtraction for F335M.
    - Measure PAH-correlated colors.
    - Compare 3.3/7.7 and 3.3/11.3 micron ratios with metallicity and radiation-field indicators.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - PAH color behavior depends strongly on local specific star-formation rate.
    - PAH ratios decrease with increasing metallicity.
    - Radiation-field spectrum affects PAH feature ratios.
  - **Position:** useful calibration paper because PAH maps are now a JWST tool for ISM conditions in galaxy-evolution studies.
