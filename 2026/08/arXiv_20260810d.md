## High-Redshift Galaxies and Quenching

- [2608.07461](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07461) F.-Y. F. Liu et al. `A clear detection of proper motion confirms that the claimed z~32 galaxy candidate, "Capotauro", is a Y-type brown dwarf`
  - **Question:** is the extremely red JWST source Capotauro a `z~32` galaxy candidate or a Galactic brown dwarf contaminant?
  - **Data:**
    - Imaging: new JWST/NIRCam medium-band observations taken `~3.5` years after the discovery data.
    - Astrometric reference: `68` compact reference sources defining a local relative frame.
    - Source class comparison: empirical brown-dwarf spectral templates and extragalactic alternatives.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit filter-dependent point-spread functions in each epoch.
    - Measure relative astrometric motion against compact field sources.
    - Run joint four-image injection-recovery tests.
    - Compare the photometric SED with empirical brown-dwarf templates.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Capotauro moves by `132 +/- 20 mas` across the `3.5` yr baseline.
    - The motion rejects an extragalactic source at `>6 sigma`.
    - The total proper motion is `37.6(+5.5/-5.6) mas yr^-1`.
    - The SED is best matched by a `Y1.0+/-0.5` brown dwarf with `T_eff~350 K` at `730+/-110 pc`.
  - **Position:** read first because it is a direct cautionary case for the most extreme JWST photometric-redshift candidates.

- [2608.06463](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06463) Thomas de Lisle et al. `JWST PRIMER: Post-starburst galaxies dominate the low-mass quiescent galaxy population since z~2`
  - **Question:** what population drives the low-mass upturn in the quenched galaxy stellar mass function out to `z~2`?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: JWST PRIMER observations of the Ultra-Deep Survey field.
    - Sample: photometrically selected star-forming, passive, and post-starburst galaxies.
    - Redshift range: `0.5<z<3.0`.
    - Post-starburst sample size: more than `800` galaxies.
  - **Method:**
    - Construct stellar mass functions for star-forming, passive, and post-starburst populations.
    - Isolate the low-mass quenched upturn.
    - Evolve post-starburst galaxies under visibility-time assumptions.
    - Compare predicted passive buildup with observed low-mass passive counts.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The quenched mass function shows a low-mass upturn at `log(M*/M_sun)<10` out to `z~2`.
    - Post-starburst galaxies primarily drive that upturn.
    - A simple one-way post-starburst-to-passive pathway overpredicts later low-mass passive counts.
    - Rejuvenation, tidal disruption, merging, or dormant stochastic star formation may reduce the passive buildup.
  - **Position:** high-value JWST population result because it links environmental and rapid-quenching pathways to low-mass galaxy evolution near cosmic noon.

- [2608.07221](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07221) Asa F. L. Bluck `A Review of Galaxy Quenching -- Part I: Defining the Problem and Observational Results`
  - **Question:** what observational constraints define the empirical problem of galaxy quenching?
  - **Data:**
    - Literature base: observational quenching studies across wide-field spectroscopy, IFU surveys, sub-mm, radio, X-ray, HST, and JWST.
    - Focus: galaxy populations, quiescent selections, spatially resolved quenching, and high-redshift frontier results.
  - **Method:**
    - Organize observational routes to identifying quiescent galaxies.
    - Review population-level constraints on quenching.
    - Separate spatially resolved diagnostics from integrated population trends.
    - Include current JWST high-redshift quenching results.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Quenching constraints now combine demographic, structural, gas, and resolved star-formation information.
    - JWST has moved high-redshift quenching from rare-case discovery toward population-level tests.
    - Observational definitions and selection functions remain central to comparing quiescent populations.
  - **Position:** useful reference entry because it consolidates the observational side of quenching for high-z galaxy interpretation.

- [2608.07247](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07247) Asa F. L. Bluck `A Review of Galaxy Quenching -- Part II: Theoretical Solutions and Direct Observational Tests`
  - **Question:** which physical routes can stop star formation, and how can observations test them?
  - **Data:**
    - Literature base: N-body, semi-analytic, idealized hydrodynamic, cosmological hydrodynamic, and zoom-in simulation studies.
    - Mechanisms: supernova feedback, AGN feedback, dynamical stabilization, mergers, ram pressure, stripping, and cosmic-web environment.
  - **Method:**
    - Organize intrinsic and environmental quenching routes.
    - Compare theoretical predictions against direct observational tests.
    - Separate fundamental causes from triggering channels.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Massive-galaxy quenching can involve ejective and preventative AGN or stellar feedback.
    - Satellite and environment-driven quenching require ram-pressure, stripping, and cosmic-web context.
    - Observational tests remain mechanism-dependent rather than reducible to one universal quenching cause.
  - **Position:** useful companion to the observational review because it maps quenching interpretations onto falsifiable physical channels.

## Baryons, Cosmic Structure, and Cosmology

- [2608.06455](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06455) Isabel Medlock et al. `Beyond Feedback: Disentangling Baryonic Effects with tSZ x FRB Cross-Correlations`
  - **Question:** can tSZ x FRB cross-correlations disentangle feedback efficiency from non-thermal pressure in galaxy groups and clusters?
  - **Data:**
    - Observables: tSZ electron pressure and FRB dispersion-measure electron density.
    - Model: Baryon Pasting framework with separate feedback efficiency and non-thermal pressure parameters.
    - Forecasts: DSA-2000 FRBs with Simons Observatory and CMB-HD.
  - **Method:**
    - Model tSZ, FRB, and tSZ x FRB power spectra.
    - Vary feedback efficiency and non-thermal pressure amplitude independently.
    - Compare degeneracies with and without the cross-power spectrum.
    - Forecast parameter precision for future FRB and CMB survey combinations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Adding the cross-power spectrum reduces the feedback and non-thermal-pressure conditional correlation from `0.96` to `0.08` in the noise-free limit.
    - The joint figure of merit improves by a factor of `19`.
    - Existing detections disfavor weak feedback with `epsilon_f^95% >~ 2.40-3.67 x 10^-6` across datasets.
    - `5 x 10^4` FRBs with SO can reach `3.8%` precision on non-thermal pressure amplitude, improving to `2.3%` with CMB-HD.
  - **Position:** strong baryon-census methods paper because it separates gas thermodynamics from gas density in cluster environments.

- [2608.06927](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06927) Teppei Okumura `Joint Geometric and Dynamical Constraints on Cosmology from Anisotropies in Galaxy Intrinsic-Alignment Correlations`
  - **Question:** can intrinsic-alignment anisotropies add cosmological information beyond galaxy clustering?
  - **Data:**
    - Spectroscopy: BOSS galaxies.
    - Imaging: DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys shape measurements.
    - Redshift range: `0.43<=z<=0.7`.
    - Correlations: galaxy density-intrinsic ellipticity and intrinsic ellipticity-intrinsic ellipticity.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure anisotropic GI and II correlations.
    - Decompose spin-dependent angular structure into associated Legendre multipoles.
    - Jointly model redshift-space and Alcock-Paczynski distortions.
    - Map constraints onto a flat `w0CDM` model.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - GI correlations show the expected BAO structure.
    - Adding intrinsic alignments reduces fractional uncertainties by `32%` for `f sigma_8`, `18%` for `D_A`, and `29%` for `H`.
    - Intrinsic alignments tighten `w0`, `Omega_m`, and `H0`, though `w0` depends on the minimum fitted scale.
  - **Position:** useful large-scale-structure method because galaxy shapes become an extra geometric and dynamical observable rather than only a weak-lensing nuisance.

- [2608.07112](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07112) Maurice H.P.M. van Putten `Parameter-free prediction of the asymptotic acceleration scale confirmed by weak lensing`
  - **Question:** does a cosmology-derived acceleration scale match recent weak-lensing constraints on galaxy dynamics?
  - **Data:**
    - Theory input: de Sitter transition scale from `cH` and the deceleration parameter.
    - Empirical comparison: weak-lensing determination of the deep-asymptotic acceleration scale.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare the predicted acceleration scale with weak-lensing measurements.
    - Frame the comparison against galaxy-dynamics scaling and Lambda-CDM expectations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The updated theoretical value is `1.63(+0.13/-0.14) x 10^-8 cm s^-2`.
    - The weak-lensing value is `1.63(+0.23/-0.20) x 10^-8 cm s^-2`.
    - The claimed agreement connects background cosmology to galaxy-dynamics scaling without fitted rotation-curve parameters.
  - **Position:** lower-priority but notable because modified-dynamics claims affect interpretation of galaxy rotation and lensing constraints.

- [2608.07290](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07290) Federico Lelli et al. `Testing dark matter models and modified gravity theories with spatially resolved HI observations`
  - **Question:** how will SKA HI rotation curves test dark matter and modified gravity?
  - **Data:**
    - Facility: SKA-Mid AA4 HI surveys.
    - Tracer: spatially resolved `21 cm` HI rotation curves.
    - Target regime: samples growing from hundreds to tens of thousands of galaxies and reaching `z~1`.
  - **Method:**
    - Use high-angular-resolution HI data to probe inner rotation curves.
    - Combine radio kinematics with optical and near-infrared ancillary data.
    - Compare dynamical scaling laws across environment and redshift.
    - Develop automated kinematic software for large SKA samples.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - SKA-Mid can reach spatial resolutions of `1-2 arcsec` for detailed inner dynamics.
    - HI rotation-curve tests can move from representative local samples to large statistical samples.
    - Extending rotation-curve studies to `z~1` probes halo evolution across about `8 Gyr`.
  - **Position:** useful survey-planning paper because HI dynamics provide a clean bridge between galaxy formation, dark matter, and modified gravity.

## Quasars, Galaxy Structure, and Gas

- [2608.07470](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07470) William Sheu et al. `HST imaging, pipeline modeling, and time-delay predictions of 2 triply-imaged and 15 quadruply-imaged lensed quasars`
  - **Question:** which recently discovered lensed quasars are most promising for time-delay cosmography?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `17` lensed quasars, including `2` triply imaged and `15` quadruply imaged systems.
    - Imaging: HST/WFC3 IR F160W, PID `17916`.
    - Model products: lens mass and light profiles, Fermat potential differences, and time-delay predictions.
  - **Method:**
    - Build uniform gravitational-lens models with Lenstronomy.
    - Constrain deflector light and mass distributions.
    - Predict time delays under a fiducial cosmology.
    - Classify systems by expected time-delay-distance precision.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - All `17` systems yield pipeline models and time-delay predictions.
    - Six systems are classified as excellent with `<=3%` estimated uncertainty.
    - Five systems are classified as good with `3-7%` estimated uncertainty.
    - The recommended follow-up set contains `11` excellent or good systems.
  - **Position:** useful quasar-infrastructure paper because lens follow-up priorities determine the time-delay cosmography yield.

- [2608.07080](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07080) Truman Tapia et al. `Formation of globular cluster-rich ultra-diffuse galaxies through mergers`
  - **Question:** can gas-rich dwarf-galaxy mergers form GC-rich ultra-diffuse galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: high-resolution idealized hydrodynamic dwarf-galaxy mergers.
    - Merger ratios: `1:1` and `1:2`, with an isolated control.
    - Objects: stellar overdensities identified as globular-cluster candidates.
  - **Method:**
    - Simulate gas-rich dwarf encounters.
    - Track remnant structure, gas removal, and dark-matter profiles.
    - Identify bound massive stellar clusters.
    - Compare cluster properties with observed globular-cluster scaling relations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Merger remnants become dispersion-supported UDG-like systems with `r_3D~1.9-2.6 kpc`.
    - The isolated control remains rotationally supported and forms no globular-cluster candidates.
    - Final remnants host `20` and `39` globular-cluster candidates in the `1:1` and `1:2` mergers.
    - One merger produces a nucleated UDG through cluster inspiral and sustained in-situ star formation.
  - **Position:** useful structure-formation result because it links dwarf interactions to diffuse galaxies and rich cluster systems.

- [2608.07321](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07321) Ruilin Xia et al. `CO Structures with Narrow Lines in Nearby Quiescent Regions`
  - **Question:** what are the physical properties of narrow-line CO structures in nearby quiescent molecular regions?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: MWISP Phase I CO data.
    - Sample: `57` CO structures.
    - Local distance estimate: about `200-300 pc` for a subset with large angular extents.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify CO structures with low velocity dispersion.
    - Separate large, diffuse, sheet-like veil clouds from other structures.
    - Use sky distribution and velocities to infer local origin.
    - Interpret turbulence dissipation and cloud formation conditions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Most structures have low densities and subsonic or transonic turbulence.
    - Several large projected-area structures are classified as veil clouds.
    - Diffuse veil-cloud thicknesses of `0.1-0.3 pc` may allow ion-neutral friction to dissipate turbulence.
    - The structures likely trace a widespread local molecular-gas component.
  - **Position:** supporting ISM entry because diffuse molecular precursors calibrate the local physics behind cloud formation.

- [2608.06978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06978) Orestis A. Karapiperis et al. `Smoothed particle magnetohydrodynamics for simulations of galaxy and cosmic structure formation`
  - **Question:** can smoothed-particle magnetohydrodynamics be made robust for galaxy and cosmic-structure simulations?
  - **Data:**
    - Code: SWIFT.
    - Test suite: standard numerical experiments plus proto-stellar jet launching, massive cluster dynamo amplification, and a Milky Way-like disk.
    - Galaxy-formation model: EAGLE coupled to an MHD solver.
  - **Method:**
    - Reformulate SPMHD equations in a density-energy conservative form.
    - Add discontinuity-capturing terms with adaptive switches.
    - Implement tensile-instability correction and mixed hyperbolic-parabolic divergence cleaning.
    - Test convergence and performance across idealized and astrophysical problems.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The scheme is designed to couple robustly to sub-resolution galaxy-formation recipes.
    - The method shows competitive behavior across standard tests and realistic applications.
    - The Milky Way-like disk case is reported as the first coupling of the EAGLE galaxy-formation model to an MHD solver.
  - **Position:** useful simulation-method paper because magnetic fields increasingly enter CGM, ISM, and galaxy-formation interpretations.
