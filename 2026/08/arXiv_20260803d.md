## Executive View

The read-first paper is [2607.29589](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29589) K. Aditya, which measures star-formation efficiency for a vetted `z=9-10` sample and highlights the spectroscopically confirmed galaxy UNCOVER 3686 at `z=9.31`. Its claimed `20-60%` baryon-conversion efficiency makes it the most direct cosmic-dawn match in the posting.

[2607.28713](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28713) Sahil Hegde is the strongest reionization-era survey-method paper. It forecasts Pop III constraints from line-intensity-mapping cross-correlations with SPHEREx, JWST, and next-generation LIM facilities. [2607.28875](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28875) Maria Emilia De Rossi is the main high-redshift galaxy calibration paper, giving corrections from submm-based modified-blackbody luminosities to total infrared luminosities.

The AGN axis is useful but mostly low redshift. [2607.28715](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28715) Francesco D'Eugenio tests Little Red Dot geometry with VLT/FORS2 spectropolarimetry of the local LRD J1025. [2607.28732](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28732) Shengxiu Sun and [2607.28735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28735) Shengxiu Sun use DESI plus HSC to connect quasars and changing-look AGNs to quiescent or post-starburst hosts. [2607.29075](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29075) Jing-Tong Xing adds a theoretical gas-recycling channel for massive stars in AGN disks.

The environment and methods group has several save-for-context entries. [2607.28739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28739) C. Bellhouse releases a large citizen-science jellyfish-galaxy catalog for ram-pressure stripping. [2607.29414](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29414) Baptiste Sigal constrains turbulence in the Coma intracluster medium from Planck SZ fluctuations. [2607.29437](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29437) Vahid Asadi tests CatBoost galaxy-property inference under missing photometry, and [2607.29426](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29426) Paul Vauterin presents ARGOS for interactive cosmological simulation visual analytics.

## Cosmic Dawn, Reionization, and High-Redshift Galaxy Growth

- [2607.29589](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29589) K. Aditya et al. `A Massive Galaxy at the Edge of Feedback-Free Efficiency`
  - **Question:** can a spectroscopically confirmed cosmic-dawn galaxy approach feedback-free baryon-conversion efficiency?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: photometrically and spectroscopically vetted galaxies at `z=9-10`, including `31` spectroscopically confirmed systems.
    - Anchor object: UNCOVER 3686 at `z=9.31`.
    - Physical properties: `M_star=10^9.55 Msun`, `R_e=0.45 kpc`, stellar age `~160 Myr`, `Z_star/Z_sun~0.2`, and inferred `n_gas~3x10^3 cm^-3`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure star-formation efficiency for the vetted high-redshift sample.
    - Compare UNCOVER 3686 with empirical model predictions.
    - Test whether its compact, young, low-metallicity properties match feedback-free starburst conditions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - UNCOVER 3686 has an inferred SFE of `~20-60%`.
    - The SFE is `2-6` times above empirical model predictions.
    - Its size, age, metallicity, and gas-density estimates are consistent with feedback-free galaxy-formation conditions.
  - **Position:** read first because it directly tests the high-efficiency galaxy formation channel at cosmic dawn.

- [2607.28713](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28713) Sahil Hegde et al. `Searching for Population III stars with line intensity mapping cross-correlations`
  - **Question:** can LIM cross-correlations constrain Pop III star formation when direct searches in individual galaxies remain sparse?
  - **Data:**
    - Model: Zeus21/oLIMpus expanded to include Pop III stars.
    - Lines: Halpha and HeII diagnostics for low-metallicity, top-heavy-IMF star formation.
    - Surveys: mock SPHEREx configurations and potential next-generation LIM instruments.
  - **Method:**
    - Extend an analytical LIM power-spectrum model to include Pop III emission.
    - Forecast measurement uncertainties for different instruments and surveys.
    - Quantify sensitivity to Pop II and Pop III parameters.
    - Run Fisher forecasts for SPHEREx and JWST synergies.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Next-generation LIM instruments are needed to survey the full Pop III theoretical landscape.
    - SPHEREx can contribute first-generation constraints on sites of high-redshift star formation.
    - Joint SPHEREx and JWST constraints may reach Pop III SFE and extensions to standard Pop III models.
  - **Position:** high-value survey-forecast paper for reionization and first-star demographics.

- [2607.28875](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28875) Maria Emilia De Rossi et al. `Determining Total Infrared Luminosities from Submm Measurements of High Redshift Galaxies`
  - **Question:** how should ALMA/submm measurements be corrected to recover total infrared luminosities for high-redshift galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Wavelength regime: mm and submm measurements sampling the long-wavelength SED.
    - Redshift regime: high-redshift and very high-redshift dusty galaxies.
    - Modeling basis: simulations tuned to galactic conditions typical of high-z galaxies.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare modified-blackbody luminosities with full infrared SED behavior.
    - Identify which physical parameter controls the mid-IR correction.
    - Estimate correction factors for different luminosity and redshift regimes.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Infrared SED behavior is dominated by luminosity density.
    - A correction factor of `~1.6-1.7` or `~0.2 dex` is appropriate in most cases.
    - High-redshift galaxies at `z>4` and `>10^12 Lsun` may need factors of `~1.75-1.85`, up to `2` or `0.3 dex`.
  - **Position:** practical calibration paper for ALMA-based star-formation rates in dusty high-redshift galaxies.

## AGN, Quasar Hosts, and Compact Accretion Systems

- [2607.28715](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28715) Francesco D'Eugenio et al. `Misaligned or chaotic? A strong break of axial symmetry in the local LRD J1025 revealed with VLT/FORS2 spectropolarimetry`
  - **Question:** what does the closest known Little Red Dot reveal about the geometry of compact broad-line AGN?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: SDSS J102530.29+140207.3 at `z=0.1`.
    - Instrument: VLT/FORS2 optical linear spectropolarimetry.
    - Observables: continuum and broad Halpha polarization plus extended narrow Halpha emission.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure continuum and broad-line polarization.
    - Test Milky Way dust and line-dilution alternatives.
    - Subtract the Stokes continuum and inspect the broad-Halpha polarization-angle behavior.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The continuum polarization is `p_cont=1.53%` with random and systematic errors.
    - Broad Halpha is less polarized at `p_Halpha=0.58-0.84%`.
    - A `48+/-4 deg` continuum-to-line polarization-angle offset implies a break of axial symmetry.
  - **Position:** useful local analog paper because LRD geometry is a major uncertainty in interpreting JWST compact red AGN.

- [2607.28732](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28732) Shengxiu Sun et al. `Quiescent Host Galaxies of Extended Quasars Revealed by Spectrophotometric Decomposition`
  - **Question:** what host populations are hidden beneath low-redshift extended type 1 quasars?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: more than `1000` type 1 quasars at `0.1<z<1`.
    - Selection: extended objects in DESI ground-based optical imaging.
    - Data: DESI high-resolution spectra and Subaru HSC images.
  - **Method:**
    - Iteratively decompose each source into AGN and host-galaxy components.
    - Use spectra and imaging together to break AGN-host degeneracy.
    - Compare host star-formation rates, stellar populations, and black-hole/stellar-mass relation.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Most extended quasar hosts have low SFRs and low specific SFRs.
    - Many hosts show post-starburst features and old stellar populations.
    - The black-hole mass versus stellar-mass relation is broadly consistent with canonical local relations.
  - **Position:** relevant AGN-host paper because it separates extended quasar host demographics from compact quasar samples.

- [2607.28735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28735) Shengxiu Sun et al. `Changing-Look AGNs from DESI. VI. Host Galaxies`
  - **Question:** are changing-look AGN transitions driven by host properties or central accretion changes?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `105` changing-look AGNs selected from DESI and SDSS comparisons.
    - Imaging: HSC constraints on variable AGN and non-variable stellar components.
    - Subsample: `82` changing-look AGNs with extended host emission in HSC images.
  - **Method:**
    - Apply two-epoch spectrophotometric decomposition to DESI and SDSS spectra.
    - Compare CL AGN hosts with extended quasars matched in redshift and stellar mass.
    - Inspect narrow-line response in OII and OIII.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - `79/105` or `75.2%` of CL AGN hosts are quiescent.
    - `31/105` or `29.5%` show post-starburst signatures.
    - OII and OIII narrow lines show no population-wide response to continuum and broad-line changes.
    - The results favor central SMBH accretion-rate changes as the main origin of CL transitions.
  - **Position:** useful DESI time-domain AGN paper because it constrains host versus nucleus interpretations of changing-look behavior.

- [2607.29075](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29075) Jing-Tong Xing et al. `Reincarnations of massive stars in active galactic nucleus discs`
  - **Question:** can embedded supernova remnants in AGN disks seed new massive-star formation?
  - **Data:**
    - Model system: core-collapse supernova remnants embedded in dense AGN disks.
    - Parameters: SMBH mass and explosion radius.
    - Outputs: seed-cloud growth and massive-star production efficiency.
  - **Method:**
    - Build a semi-analytical model for remnant cooling, shell backflow, and seed-cloud formation.
    - Follow shear-limited Hill capture and collapse criteria.
    - Test gravitational, tidal, shear, photoionization, and magnetic conditions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Dense AGN disks can force remnants rapidly into radiative snowplow evolution.
    - The lowest-SMBH-mass models produce fewer than one massive star per event on average.
    - The most massive SMBH models can produce several to several hundred massive stars.
    - Embedded supernovae can create a localized gas-recycling channel for second-generation massive stars.
  - **Position:** theoretical AGN-disk context paper linking nuclear gas physics, massive stars, and compact-object progenitor environments.

## Environments, Clusters, and Dynamical Structure

- [2607.28739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28739) C. Bellhouse et al. `Fishing for Jellyfish Galaxies: Exploring ram-pressure stripping with crowd science`
  - **Question:** can citizen-science classifications identify large samples of ram-pressure-stripping galaxies in groups and clusters?
  - **Data:**
    - Imaging: Dark Energy Camera Legacy Survey color images.
    - Parent sample: `49703` late-type galaxies within `4xR_500` of clusters and groups.
    - Output catalog: `37599` visually classified galaxies.
  - **Method:**
    - Use Zooniverse volunteers to inspect galaxy images.
    - Debias classifications and optimize vote-fraction thresholds.
    - Calibrate completeness and purity against a labeled ground-truth set.
    - Compare spectroscopic velocities for RPS candidates and the general cluster population.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The final catalog contains `6739` jellyfish candidates, including `6621` new candidates.
    - It also contains `5430` merger candidates and `29729` undisturbed galaxies.
    - The RPS-candidate fraction rises from `~10%` in groups to `~20-30%` in massive clusters.
    - Spectroscopic RPS candidates have median clustercentric velocities `53%` higher than the general cluster population.
  - **Position:** environment catalog paper useful for galaxy-transformation studies in dense halos.

- [2607.29414](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29414) Baptiste Sigal et al. `Planck Constraints on Turbulence in the Coma Cluster`
  - **Question:** what turbulence properties can Planck SZ fluctuations recover for the Coma intracluster medium?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: Coma cluster.
    - Observable: Planck SZ surface-brightness fluctuations.
    - Comparison context: XRISM direct velocity measurements.
  - **Method:**
    - Analyze the 2D SZ fluctuation power spectrum.
    - Model the 3D pressure fluctuation power spectrum with Kolmogorov-type turbulence.
    - Use simulation-based inference with normalizing flows.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The inferred injection scale is `l_inj=540^{+450}_{-200} kpc`.
    - The 3D Mach number is `M_3D=0.60^{+0.13}_{-0.09}`.
    - Turbulent velocities span `357-1095 km/s`.
    - The non-thermal pressure fraction is `P_turb/P_tot=0.17^{+0.06}_{-0.04}`.
  - **Position:** cluster-gas paper relevant to baryon physics, hydrostatic-mass bias, and multi-probe ICM turbulence studies.

- [2607.29454](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29454) Julian S. Sommer et al. `Rates of tidal disruption events from constrained cosmological simulations of the local Universe: population properties and implications for transient surveys`
  - **Question:** how do 3D local-Universe environments reshape predicted tidal-disruption-event rates?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: SLOW constrained cosmological simulation.
    - Environments: Coma, Hercules, Shapley, Virgo, Perseus, and Fornax analog volumes.
    - Volumes: zoom-in boundaries with radial extents of `55-92 Mpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Extract black-hole demographics, spin, and host-galaxy properties.
    - Filter dynamically stable main-sequence black holes.
    - Classify hosts as cuspy or cored from the 3D stellar-density slope within `1 kpc`.
    - Apply the relativistic Kesden efficiency correction.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The average volumetric TDE rate is `~600 Gpc^-3 yr^-1`.
    - The average per-black-hole rate is `~4.5x10^-5 yr^-1`.
    - Cuspy satellite galaxies in extended cluster halos dominate the total TDE budget.
    - TDE yields depend more on black-hole demographics and spatial concentration than total cluster mass.
  - **Position:** useful transient-environment paper for connecting LSST-era TDE yields to black-hole demographics.

- [2607.29314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29314) Christine C. Dantas et al. `The Radial Acceleration Relation in Galaxies and Clusters from a Two-Component, Virial-Motivated Framework`
  - **Question:** can a two-component virial framework describe radial acceleration data from galaxies through clusters?
  - **Data:**
    - Observational sources: early-type galaxies, BCGs, galaxy clusters, and weak-lensing measurements of isolated galaxies.
    - Literature anchors: Lelli et al. 2017, Tian et al. 2020 and 2024, Brouwer et al. 2021, and Mistele et al. 2024.
  - **Method:**
    - Interpret acceleration data with a two-component virial theorem baseline.
    - Add a Constant-Interaction Approximation model for ETGs, BCGs, and clusters.
    - Add a Virial-Motivated Interaction model for weak-lensing-scale accelerations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The CIA model reproduces observed RAR trends for ETGs, BCGs, and galaxy clusters.
    - The CIA model breaks down below accelerations of order `10^-14 m s^-2`.
    - The VIM adds a local radius-dependent baryon-DM interaction term for the weak-lensing regime.
  - **Position:** lower-priority but relevant dynamics paper because it links galaxy and cluster acceleration phenomenology.

## Survey Methods, Inference, and Supporting Calibration

- [2607.29437](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29437) Vahid Asadi et al. `CatBoost versus Spectral Energy Distribution-Fitting: Estimating Galaxy Properties under Controlled Photometric Incompleteness`
  - **Question:** can missing-data-aware machine learning recover galaxy properties from incomplete photometric surveys?
  - **Data:**
    - Mock catalog: Horizon-AGN hydrodynamical simulation with known physical parameters.
    - ML input: `12` photometric bands with `10%`, `20%`, and `30%` injected missingness.
    - Reference baseline: idealized SED fitting with complete `26`-band photometry.
  - **Method:**
    - Train CatBoost under deliberately incomplete photometry.
    - Compare mass, SFR, and redshift errors against complete-data SED fitting.
    - Track degradation with increasing missingness.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At `30%` missingness, mass RMSE increases from `0.08` to `0.18 dex`.
    - SFR RMSE increases from `0.41` to `0.53 dex`.
    - Redshift RMSE increases from `0.20` to `0.28`.
    - CatBoost with `12` bands and `30%` missing values beats the ideal SED baseline for mass and SFR RMSE in this controlled setup.
  - **Position:** survey-methods paper relevant to extracting galaxy properties from imperfect wide-field photometric catalogs.

- [2607.29426](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29426) Paul Vauterin et al. `Visual analytics for cosmological simulation results`
  - **Question:** can a lightweight browser-based tool lower the barrier to exploratory analysis of cosmological simulations?
  - **Data:**
    - Application: ARGOS, an open-source web-based visual analytics environment.
    - Demonstrations: cosmological particle datasets and SKIRT synthetic multi-band imaging products.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine GPU-accelerated browser rendering with template-driven dashboards.
    - Support catalog-level and object-level exploration in the same interface.
    - Package the code and reference deployment for reuse.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - ARGOS enables real-time visual exploration of cosmological simulation outputs.
    - The same framework can handle particle data and synthetic imaging products.
    - The design emphasizes dissemination and low-friction knowledge discovery.
  - **Position:** useful infrastructure paper for simulation analysis and survey-product inspection.

- [2607.29406](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29406) Iliya S. Tikhonenko et al. `Recovering pattern speeds of simulated face-on barred galaxies via Schwarzschild modelling`
  - **Question:** can Schwarzschild modeling recover bar pattern speeds when galaxies are nearly face-on?
  - **Data:**
    - Test case: N-body simulation of a strongly barred galaxy.
    - Geometry: nearly face-on inclination and exact face-on limit.
    - Parameters: pattern speed, mass-to-light ratio, and dark-matter halo mass scaling.
  - **Method:**
    - Build a Schwarzschild orbit-superposition code for triaxial rotating potentials.
    - Fit mock barred-galaxy data.
    - Compare recovery accuracy across inclinations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Pattern speed and mass-to-light ratio are recovered to `10%` accuracy at inclination `20 deg`.
    - Dark-matter halo mass scaling is recovered to `20%` accuracy.
    - Pattern speed can remain recoverable in the exact face-on limit through vertical-velocity distribution changes.
  - **Position:** galaxy-dynamics methods paper with moderate relevance for structural evolution work.

- [2607.28729](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28729) N. C. Sterling et al. `The s-process at Subsolar Metallicity: Insights from High-Resolution Infrared Spectroscopy of Magellanic Cloud Planetary Nebulae`
  - **Question:** how well do AGB nucleosynthesis models reproduce n-capture abundances in subsolar-metallicity planetary nebulae?
  - **Data:**
    - Targets: `12` Magellanic Cloud planetary nebulae.
    - Instrument: IGRINS on Gemini South.
    - Detected species: Se, Kr, and first extragalactic PN detections of Rb, Cd, and Te.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure trans-iron element abundances in high-resolution near-IR spectra.
    - Compare enriched PNe with Monash, FRUITY, and NuGrid AGB models.
    - Use machine learning to find best-fitting nucleosynthesis models.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - `10` targets show multiple n-capture elements.
    - `7` targets show s-process enrichments relative to O and Ar.
    - Te and Kr abundances reach enhancements of `~40-50`.
    - Monash and FRUITY models with `2-4 Msun` and `1/3` solar to near-solar metallicities fit five PNe well.
  - **Position:** chemical-evolution context paper, lower priority for the profile but useful for subsolar-metallicity yield systematics.
