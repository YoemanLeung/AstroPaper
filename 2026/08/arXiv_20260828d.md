## Executive View

The read-first item is [2608.26708](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26708) Ikki Mitsuhashi on SERENADE high-redshift dust and far-infrared line diagnostics. ALMA Band 9/10 observations of nine bright Lyman-break galaxies at `5.8<z_spec<8.3` connect high dust temperatures, low metallicity, high ionization parameter, dense gas, and elevated [OIII]/[CII] ratios.

The strongest environment paper is [2608.26348](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26348) R. C. Arango-Toro on COSMOS-Web galaxy groups. It uses COSMOS2025/COSMOS-Web stellar masses, non-parametric star-formation histories, and AMICO group probabilities to show early mixed behavior but late star-formation suppression in group galaxies, especially below `z=1.5`.

The best large-scale-structure methods entries are [2608.26262](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26262) Nelson Padilla and [2608.26668](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26668) Qingyang Li. Padilla uses DESI BGS and SDSS MGS conformity combinations to recover a more linear clustering shape inside the nonlinear regime. Li reconstructs mass, tidal, and velocity fields from DESI-like galaxy groups over `0<z<0.6`.

## High-Redshift ISM, Dust, and Early Chemical Context

- [2608.26708](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26708) Ikki Mitsuhashi et al. `SERENADE III: Insight into the Origin of the High Dust Temperature and High [O III]/[C II] Ratio at z>~6`
  - **Question:** what drives high dust temperatures and high [OIII]/[CII] ratios in bright galaxies at `z>6`?
  - **Data:**
    - ALMA: high-frequency Band 9 and Band 10 observations.
    - Sample: nine bright Lyman-break galaxies at `5.8<z_spec<8.3`.
    - Line coverage: [OI]63um for five galaxies plus [OIII] and [CII] constraints.
    - Comparison: local and high-redshift samples with metallicity and sSFR measurements.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure dust temperatures from high-frequency continuum constraints.
    - Compare dust temperature with gas-phase metallicity and sSFR.
    - Use [CII]/[OI], [OIII], [OI], and [CII] with Cloudy modeling.
    - Infer ionization parameter and hydrogen density in the high-redshift ISM.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Most star-forming galaxies show `T_dust~40 K`, while three reach `T_dust>=60 K`.
    - Dust temperature anticorrelates with gas-phase metallicity as `T_dust proportional to Z^-0.50+/-0.19`.
    - The [CII]/[OI] ratio is lower than in local samples, consistent with [CII] collisional de-excitation at high density.
    - The inferred conditions are `log U_ion~-2` and `log n_H~2.5`.
    - High `U_ion` and dense gas naturally explain high [OIII]/[CII] at `z>6`.
  - **Position:** read first because it connects ALMA far-infrared diagnostics to metallicity, dust heating, and dense gas in reionization-era galaxies.

- [2608.27220](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27220) Hiroko Okada et al. `Bright Metal-Poor Star Survey - I. Tomo-e Gozen narrow-band photometric survey and medium-resolution spectroscopic follow-up`
  - **Question:** can wide-area narrow-band imaging efficiently select bright metal-poor stars for high-resolution abundance work?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: Tomo-e Gozen camera on the Kiso Schmidt telescope.
    - Filters: Ca II H and K, CH G band, Halpha, and a reference band.
    - Coverage: `>22000 deg^2` in all four bands with `~100 hr` total integration.
    - Follow-up: Nayuta/MALLS medium-resolution spectra of selected candidates.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine narrow-band photometry with broad-band photometry and Gaia distances.
    - Estimate effective temperature, surface gravity, metallicity, and carbon abundance.
    - Calibrate against high-resolution literature abundances.
    - Validate candidate selection with medium-resolution spectroscopy.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Typical calibration scatter is `<0.3 dex` in metallicity and `<0.4 dex` in carbon abundance.
    - The survey estimates metallicities for `~1.7 million` stars.
    - It identifies `~16000` very metal-poor candidates with `[M/H]_NB,fin<-2`.
    - MALLS follow-up confirms `24` of `32` candidates below `[M/H]<-2`.
    - One new candidate reaches `[M/H]~-3.4`.
  - **Position:** useful abundance-context paper because bright metal-poor stars calibrate early enrichment channels that feed Pop III and first-galaxy interpretation.

- [2608.26731](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26731) Junbo Zhang et al. `Carbon in J0815+4729: Casting A Shadow of Doubt on the Interpretation of CH Absorption in the Most Primitive Stars`
  - **Question:** do CH molecular lines give reliable carbon abundances in the hyper iron-poor star J0815+4729?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: J0815+4729 with `[Fe/H]=-5.49`.
    - Spectroscopy: high-resolution data with C I, O I, CH, and C2 diagnostics.
    - Models: new atomic models for carbon and oxygen under NLTE conditions.
  - **Method:**
    - Derive carbon and oxygen abundances from neutral atomic transitions.
    - Compare C I, C2, and CH abundance indicators.
    - Apply NLTE corrections to atomic lines.
    - Reassess the carbon-enhanced classification.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - C I gives `A(C)_NLTE=8.08+/-0.07`.
    - C2 gives `A(C)_LTE=7.96+/-0.08`.
    - CH gives a lower value, `A(C)_LTE=7.47+/-0.17`.
    - J0815+4729 remains one of the most carbon-enhanced stars at `[Fe/H]<=-4.5`.
    - CH absorption can bias quantitative carbon interpretation in primitive stars.
  - **Position:** niche but relevant because primitive-star abundance systematics feed constraints on early nucleosynthesis and Pop III enrichment.

## Environment, Groups, Voids, and Satellite Evolution

- [2608.26348](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26348) R. C. Arango-Toro et al. `COSMOS-Web: From early star-formation enhancement to late suppression in galaxy groups`
  - **Question:** how does group environment change star-formation histories across cosmic time and group-centric radius?
  - **Data:**
    - Field: COSMOS-Web with COSMOS2025/COSMOS-Web stellar masses.
    - Environment catalog: AMICO group detections and probabilistic memberships.
    - Star-formation histories: non-parametric SFHs.
    - Comparison: group galaxies versus matched field galaxies.
  - **Method:**
    - Stack SFHs by stellar mass, redshift, and normalized group-centric distance.
    - Compare rich group galaxies with matched field controls.
    - Test radial trends and redshift evolution.
    - Track suppression and enhancement relative to field SFHs.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The clearest group suppression appears at `z<1.5`.
    - Low-to-intermediate mass galaxies show deficits up to `0.8 dex`.
    - At `z>1.5`, group SFHs show weak suppression or occasional enhancement.
    - Low-redshift profiles are broadly quenching-oriented across radius.
    - A clearer inner-outer contrast appears at `z>=1`, though `z>=2` centering uncertainties remain important.
  - **Position:** high-priority environment paper because it gives COSMOS-Web time-resolved evidence for the transition from mixed early group growth to later group quenching.

- [2608.26256](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26256) Anita M. Schiller et al. `Barren but not Empty: The Impact of Void Environments on Galaxy and Halo Populations`
  - **Question:** how do voids alter halo populations and galaxy scaling relations relative to the field?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: Magneticum Pathfinder hydrodynamical suite.
    - Environment: void-centric shells and void core-density measurements.
    - Diagnostics: void halo mass functions, stellar-mass functions, SHMR, and star-forming main sequence.
  - **Method:**
    - Build void halo mass functions as a function of void-centric distance.
    - Test whether void properties beyond core density change halo populations.
    - Compare void and field galaxy stellar properties.
    - Track evolution as voids become more underdense.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Galactic halos in voids are less massive than halos in the general volume.
    - The void-field halo discrepancy strengthens as voids grow more underdense.
    - Void core density is the main relevant void parameter for the halo population.
    - Void galaxies contain less total stellar mass.
    - Their stellar-mass-halo-mass relation and star-forming main sequence remain broadly conventional.
  - **Position:** useful LSS comparison paper because it isolates underdense-environment effects on halo demographics without claiming radically different galaxy growth.

- [2608.26249](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26249) Barry T. Chiang et al. `Scatter, bias, and chaos of satellite orbits in triaxial dark matter haloes`
  - **Question:** how badly do spherical host-potential assumptions bias satellite pericenter histories?
  - **Data:**
    - Orbit suite: `10^6` satellites drawn from a cosmological infall distribution.
    - Host models: static NFW halos with controlled triaxiality at fixed mass profile.
    - Case study: Milky Way dwarf Triangulum II.
  - **Method:**
    - Integrate satellite orbits through triaxial host potentials.
    - Compare pericenter, apocenter, and period statistics against spherical reconstructions.
    - Separate regular dephasing from chaotic orbit behavior.
    - Quantify uncertainty from unknown halo shape and orientation.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Population medians of pericenter, apocenter, and orbital period are nearly unchanged.
    - Individual successive pericenters scatter by `5-23%`.
    - Minimum pericenter over a Hubble time shrinks by up to `21%`.
    - Backward spherical reconstructions can diverge by `0.1 R_vir` within `2-4 Gyr`.
    - For Triangulum II, halo shape and orientation spread inferred pericenter by `~50%`.
  - **Position:** useful environment-dynamics paper because satellite quenching and stripping inferences depend on pericenter histories.

- [2608.26275](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26275) Bruno M. Celiz et al. `The origin of the stellar mass-size relation of satellite galaxies in the COLIBRE simulations`
  - **Question:** what physical pathways make satellite galaxy sizes differ from central-galaxy mass-size relations?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation suite: COLIBRE cosmological hydrodynamical simulations.
    - Population: satellite and central galaxies across stellar-mass bins.
    - Diagnostics: 3D stellar half-mass radius, gas content, dark-matter cores, tides, quenching, and metallicity.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare satellite and central mass-size relations at `z=0`.
    - Trace satellite evolution after infall.
    - Separate dwarf and bright satellite responses.
    - Link gas removal, tidal stripping, and centrally concentrated star formation to size evolution.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Centrals above `log(M*/Msun)>10.5` follow `r_h,* proportional to M*^0.5`.
    - Lower-mass centrals flatten near `r_h,*~3 kpc`.
    - Dwarf satellites become larger than centrals of similar mass.
    - Bright satellites become smaller than centrals of similar mass.
    - Bright satellites reach higher stellar metallicities by `~0.2 dex`.
  - **Position:** useful satellite-evolution context because it ties environmental quenching, tides, and metallicity to observable structure.

- [2608.26293](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26293) Anna Ivleva et al. `Phase-Space Diagnostics for Dwarf Galaxies in Cluster Environments`
  - **Question:** can projected phase space infer dwarf-galaxy infall times in clusters?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: resolved baryonic halos inside Magneticum galaxy clusters.
    - Population: dwarf galaxies with `M*<10^9 Msun`.
    - Coordinates: line-of-sight velocity and projected clustercentric distance.
  - **Method:**
    - Correlate true infall time with projected phase-space location.
    - Build an infall-time template using a `3 Gyr` split.
    - Test how orbit and observer orientation affect projected position.
    - Use idealized simulations to assess dark-matter-deficient galaxy orbits.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Radial trends broadly recover previous phase-space expectations.
    - At least `30%` of recently accreted dwarfs occupy regions previously predicted to be ancient-infall dominated.
    - Dwarfs have a broader 3D phase-space radial distribution than massive galaxies.
    - Dark-matter-deficient galaxies can have strongly altered orbits.
  - **Position:** useful for environmental interpretation because cluster dwarf histories cannot be read from projected phase space with a single clean mapping.

## Clustering, Reconstruction, and Survey Systematics

- [2608.26262](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26262) Nelson Padilla et al. `Assembly bias from nuisance to probe II: Salvaging linear clustering from DESI and SDSS data`
  - **Question:** can assembly-sensitive conformity statistics recover a more linear clustering signal from color-selected galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Surveys: DESI BGS and SDSS MGS.
    - Samples: red and blue galaxy subsamples, with central primaries by default.
    - Comparisons: MTNG, FLAMINGO, and MDPL2-SAG simulations.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure ordinary correlations, conformity, and compensated conformity combinations.
    - Compare projected and redshift-space monopole statistics.
    - Fit linear matter templates.
    - Diagnose response with effective kernels.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Ordinary high-color clustering develops nonlinear small-scale response.
    - DESI compensated statistics track the linear matter shape farther into the nonlinear regime.
    - The effect is stronger for central-primary and dense samples.
    - Projected kernels are enhanced at low line-of-sight separations.
    - Residual nonlinear contamination still needs simulation calibration.
  - **Position:** useful clustering-method paper because it reframes assembly bias as a filter for nonlinear modes rather than only a nuisance.

- [2608.26668](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26668) Qingyang Li et al. `ELUCID-DESI II. Revealing dark matter mass, tidal, and velocity (MTV) fields using galaxy group phase information`
  - **Question:** can galaxy-group phase information reconstruct mass, tidal, and velocity fields for DESI-like surveys?
  - **Data:**
    - Redshift range: `0<z<0.6`.
    - Survey analogue: DESI Bright Galaxy Survey mock catalog.
    - Tracers: galaxy groups identified with an extended halo-based group finder.
    - Selection: apparent magnitude limit `m_z<19.65`.
  - **Method:**
    - Replace explicit theoretical bias correction with simulation-calibrated statistical mapping.
    - Reconstruct velocity fields from group phase information.
    - Correct galaxy-group positions for the Kaiser effect.
    - Iteratively reconstruct tidal and mass-density fields.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Reconstructed velocities are accurate and unbiased.
    - Residual velocity dispersion is `~120 km/s`.
    - Kaiser-corrected groups recover real-space positions.
    - The reconstruction is stable against grid-resolution changes.
    - The method is directly aimed at DESI BGS data.
  - **Position:** relevant LSS methods paper because group-based reconstruction can recover environmental fields needed for galaxy evolution and cosmic-web analyses.

- [2608.26459](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26459) Utsav Siwakoti et al. `Systematic uncertainties on DESI Tully-Fisher distances constrained with Integral Field Spectroscopy`
  - **Question:** how much do photometric-kinematic position-angle offsets bias DESI Tully-Fisher distances?
  - **Data:**
    - DESI PV context: `~53000` spiral targets.
    - Calibration sample: `215` nearby galaxies from PISCO and AMUSING.
    - Photometry: Siena Galaxy Atlas SGA-2020 tractor data plus HostPhot checks.
  - **Method:**
    - Derive kinematic parameters with PaFit.
    - Measure maximum rotational velocities at `0.4 R_26`.
    - Compare photometric and kinematic position angles.
    - Propagate misalignment into Tully-Fisher distance errors.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - About `28%` of the sample has PA misalignment above `10 deg`.
    - Median distance bias is `+1.98 Mpc`.
    - Overall distance scatter is `19.35 Mpc`.
    - Misaligned galaxies show twice the dispersion of aligned galaxies.
    - DESI-TF distances should carry a global fractional uncertainty of `~3.5%`.
  - **Position:** useful survey-systematics paper because DESI peculiar-velocity products depend on photometric assumptions that are not benign for all disks.

## Local ISM, Nearby Groups, and Lower-Priority Context

- [2608.26461](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26461) K. Rajpurohit et al. `A radio continuum view of Stephan's Quintet: age, dynamics and origin of the shock`
  - **Question:** what powers the radio ridge and diffuse emission in Stephan's Quintet?
  - **Data:**
    - Facilities: MeerKAT, uGMRT, LOFAR, and VLA.
    - Frequency range: `120 MHz-8 GHz`.
    - Target: compact galaxy group Stephan's Quintet.
  - **Method:**
    - Map radio spectral index and curvature across the ridge and diffuse structures.
    - Identify filamentary and bridge emission.
    - Estimate synchrotron spectral ages.
    - Infer collision geometry from age gradients.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The southern ridge contains filamentary substructure and branching.
    - A radio counterpart to the gas bridge toward NGC 7319 is detected.
    - Ridge emission likely arises from strong shocks with Mach number `~40-100`.
    - The collision speed is `~850-1000 km/s`.
    - The collision began `~20 Myr` ago in the north and ended only `~5-6 Myr` ago in the south.
  - **Position:** lower-redshift but useful group-physics case because it cleanly links radio emission, shocks, tidal gas, and collision geometry.

- [2608.26261](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26261) Liam O. Dubay et al. `A Broken Clock Is Right Twice a Day: [Ce/Mg] Is Not a Universal Chemical Clock`
  - **Question:** is [Ce/Mg] a universal age indicator across the Milky Way?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: APOGEE in the SDSS-V Milky Way Mapper.
    - Sample: roughly `100000` red giant stars.
    - Diagnostics: age, [Ce/Mg], metallicity, guiding radius, thin disk, thick disk, and halo populations.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure the age-[Ce/Mg] relation across Galactic disk regions.
    - Split trends by metallicity and guiding radius.
    - Compare thin disk, thick disk, and halo behavior.
    - Confront observed trends with chemical-evolution expectations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The age-[Ce/Mg] slope varies strongly with metallicity and guiding radius.
    - The trend is steepest in the outer disk and below solar metallicity.
    - Metal-rich and inner-disk stars show little or no correlation with age.
    - The thick disk shows more spatially consistent [Ce/Mg] behavior.
    - [Ce/Mg] traces local star-formation history and AGB enrichment, not a universal clock.
  - **Position:** useful abundance-systematics reference because chemical clocks can imprint false evolutionary ordering when applied outside their calibrated environments.

- [2608.27389](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27389) Hannah V. Bish et al. `LightCube: A Parsec-Resolution 3D Model of the Local Far-Ultraviolet Interstellar Radiation Field`
  - **Question:** what is the 3D structure of the local ultraviolet interstellar radiation field?
  - **Data:**
    - Inputs: TD1 UV fluxes and Gaia/Hipparcos stellar distances.
    - Dust: 3D dust map.
    - Model volume: out to `1.25 kpc` from the Sun.
    - Resolution: maximum linear resolution of `1 pc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify stars expected to dominate local UV flux.
    - Propagate photons through dust with DIRTY radiative transfer.
    - Model four TD1 bands and integrated FUV range.
    - Compare UV flux with 3D total-to-selective extinction ratio.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The local FUV energy density at the Sun is `5.65 x 10^-14 erg cm^-3` from `912-2000 A`.
    - Dense regions show more than an order-of-magnitude UV variation.
    - Lower-density regions vary by about half that amplitude.
    - UV flux correlates positively with `R_V` in low-UV-radiation regions.
  - **Position:** lower-priority for high-z work, but useful as a calibrated local ISM radiation-field model.
