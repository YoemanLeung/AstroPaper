## Executive View

The read-first item is the reionization-era protocluster paper. [2608.16343](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16343) Wataru Osone studies `23` members of A2744-z7p9OD at `z=7.88` with JWST/NIRCam and NIRSpec. The key result is that stellar population, size, and ionized-gas properties correlate with the global protocluster coordinate, while Lyalpha non-detection and neutral-gas indicators point to a neutral-rich core with suppressed ionizing-photon escape.

The second high-priority item is extended Lyalpha halo physics. [2608.16665](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16665) Daria Kozlova uses MXDF LAEs at `3<z<4` to separate inner-halo emission tied to star formation from outer-halo emission regulated by environment. This is directly relevant to galaxy-IGM coupling and to interpreting Lyalpha maps around overdensities.

The strongest cosmic-noon structure entry is [2608.15997](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15997) Nicholas Foo on the Koi Pond, a strongly lensed `z=2.67` protocluster core with `11` dusty star-forming galaxies. JWST and ALMA resolve diverse morphologies, dust sizes, and gas-star offsets inside a compact forming cluster environment.

The methods and calibration set is useful. [2608.15572](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15572) Yao Yao derives redshift-dependent metallicity calibrations for `z=2-7` galaxies using ASTRID plus photoionization modeling. [2608.16825](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16825) Mark Armah provides AGN metallicity diagnostics tied to X-ray luminosity and narrow-line-region physics.

The CGM and gas-regulation background is broad but valuable. [2608.15782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15782) Ava Polzin introduces QUEST Dwarfs with stellar, ISM, and CGM measurements for low-mass galaxies. [2608.14888](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14888) Benjamin Vigneron measures electron densities in NGC 1275 filaments. [2608.16802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16802) Mansi Padave uses JWST/MIRI in M51 to separate PAH gas-tracing behavior from dust-continuum star-formation tracing.

## Reionization, Protoclusters, and Lyalpha Halos

- [2608.16343](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16343) Wataru Osone et al. `RIOJA. Environmental Effects on Stellar Populations and Ionized Gas in a Protocluster at z=7.88`
  - **Question:** how does environment shape stellar populations, sizes, and ionized gas in the `z=7.88` protocluster A2744-z7p9OD?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: `23` member galaxies in A2744-z7p9OD.
    - Instruments: JWST/NIRCam and JWST/NIRSpec.
    - Environmental indicators: projected distance to the most massive galaxy and nearest-neighbor distance.
    - Gas context: ALMA [C II] `158 micron` emission, Lyalpha non-detection, and high-column-density neutral-hydrogen evidence.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure stellar population properties, rest-frame UV sizes, and ionized-gas properties for member galaxies.
    - Use distance to the most massive galaxy as a global protocluster coordinate.
    - Use nearest-neighbor distance as a local environment coordinate.
    - Compare R23 and O32 line diagnostics across the protocluster structure.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Stellar mass, `100 Myr` SFR, dust attenuation, and galaxy size correlate with the global coordinate at `p<0.05`.
    - The same properties do not correlate with nearest-neighbor distance.
    - R23 varies strongly from galaxy to galaxy, implying inhomogeneous chemical enrichment.
    - O32 correlates with the global coordinate, indicating lower-ionization gas toward the core.
    - Lyalpha non-detection plus neutral-gas indicators point to a neutral-rich core where ionizing-photon escape may be suppressed.
  - **Position:** read first because it connects EoR protocluster structure to neutral gas, metal enrichment, and environmental suppression of escaping ionizing radiation.

- [2608.16665](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16665) Daria Kozlova et al. `Two domains of extended Lyman-alpha emission around galaxies: from local radiation to environmental regulation`
  - **Question:** what regulates Lyalpha halo emission inside and outside the virial radius of high-redshift LAEs?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: MUSE eXtremely Deep Field.
    - Sample: `21` bright-core and bright-halo LAEs at `3<z<4`.
    - Sensitivity: individual-halo surface brightness down to `~10^-20 erg cm^-2 s^-1 arcsec^-2`.
    - Scale: radial profiles out to `50 kpc`, more than `2 r_vir` for typical `~20 kpc` virial radii.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure Lyalpha surface-brightness profiles around each LAE.
    - Compare surface brightness with host SFR.
    - Compare surface brightness with environmental density.
    - Test whether undetected ultrafaint LAEs can explain the outer-halo signal.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The profile correlations break near or just below `1 r_vir`.
    - Inside the break, Lyalpha surface brightness correlates with SFR and not with environment.
    - Outside `~1 r_vir`, the SFR dependence disappears and an environmental-density correlation emerges.
    - Undetected ultrafaint LAEs are insufficient to drive the outer-halo correlation.
    - Outer Lyalpha halo emission is regulated by surrounding diffuse gas rather than by discrete faint sources alone.
  - **Position:** high-priority galaxy-gas paper because it gives an observational radius where Lyalpha changes from local radiation tracing to environmental regulation.

- [2608.15997](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15997) Nicholas Foo et al. `The Koi Pond: A Strongly Lensed Protocluster Core hosting a Diverse Population of DSFGs`
  - **Question:** how diverse are dusty star-forming galaxies inside a strongly lensed cosmic-noon protocluster core?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: PJ0846+15, the Koi Pond.
    - Sample: `11` DSFGs at `z=2.67` within `Delta V=800 km s^-1`.
    - Structure: projected extent `>300 kpc`.
    - Instruments: JWST/NIRCam and ALMA Band 6 continuum.
    - Lensing: foreground cluster at `z=0.77`.
  - **Method:**
    - Use JWST rest-frame optical imaging to map stellar structure.
    - Use ALMA continuum to map thermal dust emission.
    - Compare rest-frame `J`-band continuum with `i-J` color.
    - Inspect resolved dust-to-stellar size differences and color maps.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The protocluster contains interacting and non-interacting DSFGs.
    - Morphologies include spiral arms, bars, bulges, clumps, tidal debris, and displaced molecular gas reservoirs.
    - Colors imply a `>1 dex` stellar-mass spread and dust-attenuation reddening of `Delta A_V>1 mag`.
    - Individual galaxies span compact dusty cores to galaxy-wide dust emission.
    - Some of the reddest, dust-emitting members are clumpy disks, so secular growth as well as mergers can drive starbursts in the core.
  - **Position:** high-priority cosmic-noon structure paper because lensing resolves dusty protocluster-core pathways that are normally blended or surface-brightness limited.

## Metallicity, CGM, and Baryon Cycling

- [2608.15572](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15572) Yao Yao et al. `Theoretical emission lines and metallicity calibrations of H II regions in ASTRID simulation`
  - **Question:** how should metallicity diagnostics be calibrated for galaxies at `z=2-7` when ionization conditions evolve?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: ASTRID cosmological simulation.
    - Stellar populations: SPS models coupled to young star clusters.
    - Photoionization: MAPPINGS V.
    - Diagnostics: R23, O3N2, N2, O32, C3O3, and N3O3.
  - **Method:**
    - Evolve young star clusters under an analytic wind-driven bubble model.
    - Predict ionization parameter and gas pressure self-consistently.
    - Validate emission-line predictions against SFR indicators and the [O III] luminosity function.
    - Use Bayesian tests to quantify calibration performance at different signal-to-noise levels.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The SFR-indicator validation agrees within `<0.05 dex`.
    - Metallicity calibrations evolve significantly with redshift.
    - The redshift evolution is driven mainly by changing ionization conditions.
    - R23 performs well at all modeled redshifts with minimal error.
    - Nitrogen- and carbon-based calibrations are sensitive to abundance-enrichment assumptions and require caution.
  - **Position:** high-value calibration paper for JWST chemical-evolution work from cosmic noon into the reionization era.

- [2608.15782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15782) Ava Polzin et al. `The Low-Mass Baryon Cycle in QUEST Dwarf Galaxies I: Sample definition and first results`
  - **Question:** how are metals distributed between galaxies and the cool CGM in low-mass halos?
  - **Data:**
    - Program: QUEST Dwarfs.
    - First-release sample: `14` galaxies with `M_star<=10^9 M_sun`.
    - Redshift range: `z~0.001-0.017`.
    - CGM probes: FUV absorption spectra of bright background sources within `d_proj<~100 kpc`.
    - Supporting data: optical spectroscopy and broadband imaging.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine stellar population, ISM, and CGM measurements in a common sample.
    - Compare silicon and H I column-density profiles with projected distance.
    - Correct for unobserved silicon in higher ionization stages.
    - Separate radial trends from stellar-mass normalization.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Silicon declines faster with projected distance than H I.
    - Chemically enriched cool gas is preferentially concentrated in the inner CGM.
    - Cool-CGM metal masses within `0.3 R_vir` are `log M_Z/M_sun~4.8` and `6.5` for median `log M_star/M_sun=7.6` and `8.6`.
    - These reservoirs correspond to `~3%` and `~16%` of the metals produced over the galaxies' lifetimes.
    - Higher-ionization species extend farther than low-ionization species, indicating multiphase structure.
  - **Position:** useful local benchmark for the galaxy-CGM metal budget, especially for interpreting low-mass and high-redshift baryon-cycle analogs.

- [2608.16825](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16825) Mark Armah et al. `Identifying AGNs from X-ray detections-II: Metallicity calibrations for the N2O2 and N2S2 diagnostics`
  - **Question:** can AGN metallicity diagnostics isolate chemical abundance from radiation-field systematics?
  - **Data:**
    - Models: CLOUDY photoionization grid tied to intrinsic `2-10 keV` X-ray luminosity.
    - Benchmark sample: Seyfert 2 nuclei from BASS.
    - Metallicity range: `8.0<~12+log(O/H)<~9.1`, or `0.2<~Z/Z_sun<~2.6`.
    - Diagnostics: N2O2 and N2S2 narrow-line ratios.
  - **Method:**
    - Couple X-ray luminosity to narrow-line-region photoionization conditions.
    - Test whether nearly matched ionization potentials reduce diagnostic degeneracy.
    - Compare sensitivity to nebular structure and electron density.
    - Quantify residual dispersion for each calibration.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - N2O2 and N2S2 trace co-spatial narrow-line-region emitting volumes.
    - N2O2 is nearly independent of nebular structural variation.
    - N2S2 has mild electron-density susceptibility through the [S II] doublet.
    - Residual dispersions are `~0.081 dex` for N2O2 and `~0.121 dex` for N2S2.
    - The calibrations reduce X-ray-luminosity-driven biases in standard AGN diagnostics.
  - **Position:** useful for AGN host metallicity work, though it is more directly a nearby-AGN calibration than a high-z environment paper.

## AGN, LRD Analogs, and Nuclear Gas

- [2608.16200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16200) Luis F. Rodriguez et al. `A Radio-Bright Local Little Red Dot Analog`
  - **Question:** can local Little Red Dot analogs reveal whether high-redshift LRDs are radio quiet or simply too distant for current detections?
  - **Data:**
    - Object: J204837.26-002437.2 at `z=0.4332`.
    - Class: local LRD analog.
    - Measurement: radio continuum detection at `3.0 GHz`.
  - **Method:**
    - Search for radio emission in a local analog of high-redshift LRDs.
    - Compare the detected radio power with earlier local analog detections.
    - Compare the luminosity to radio-loud giant elliptical galaxies and quasars.
    - Estimate whether an analogous high-redshift LRD would be detectable.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - J2048 has a flux density of `3.0+/-0.2 mJy` at `3.0 GHz`.
    - It is two orders of magnitude more radio luminous than previous local LRD analog detections.
    - The spectral index `alpha=-0.39+/-0.04` is consistent with moderately optically thick synchrotron emission.
    - The radio luminosity is `1.2 x 10^41 erg s^-1`.
    - A similar `z>4` LRD would be detectable with the VLA in moderately long integrations.
  - **Position:** high-priority LRD analog note because it challenges a simple radio-quiet interpretation of high-z LRD samples.

- [2608.14900](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14900) Douglas Carlos et al. `VAPOLA - A multiyear, multiband polarization survey of AGNs and Sgr A* at millimeter wavelengths with ALMA II`
  - **Question:** how variable are compact AGN-core polarization properties at millimeter wavelengths?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `39` AGN plus Sgr A*.
    - Facility: ALMA.
    - Time span: five VLBI campaigns from `2017` to `2023`.
    - Sources discussed include M87, Sgr A*, 3C273, and 3C279.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure total intensity, linear polarization fraction, EVPA, and Faraday rotation measure.
    - Compare weekly and day-to-day variability.
    - Separate individual-source behavior from ensemble trends across source classes.
    - Test how rotation measure changes with observing frequency.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Total intensity and spectral index are generally stable on weekly timescales.
    - Linear polarization fraction, EVPA, and rotation measure often vary strongly from day to day.
    - Several sources show large EVPA rotations coincident with polarization and rotation-measure changes.
    - Rotation measure increases with observing frequency in reliable multiband cases.
    - The trend favors a magnetized sheath around relativistic jets, although an accretion-flow origin remains possible for Sgr A*.
  - **Position:** useful AGN-jet diagnostic paper for interpreting compact-core variability and Faraday environments.

- [2608.16787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16787) Karan Akbari et al. `A Cross-Band X-ray x Optical Periodicity Search for Supermassive Black Hole Binaries`
  - **Question:** how common are AGN with coherent quasi-periodicity in both X-ray and optical bands?
  - **Data:**
    - Stage 1: `1194` Swift-BAT hard-X-ray AGN.
    - Stage 2: `175` 4XMM-DR14 AGN.
    - Period search range: `P=100-900 d`.
    - Auxiliary monitoring: MAXI and RXTE/ASM for the brightest AGN.
  - **Method:**
    - Model each light curve as a damped random walk.
    - Search with Lomb-Scargle periodograms and look-elsewhere-corrected Monte Carlo significance.
    - Require X-ray and optical periods to match within `5%`.
    - Gate candidates with a model-independent null-signal-template test.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - No source is a co-periodic candidate.
    - The completeness-corrected `95%` upper limit is `<~3%` for hard-X-ray fractional modulation `>~0.3`.
    - The upper limit is `~15%` at modulation amplitude `0.2`.
    - The sensitivity is set by hard-X-ray monitoring rather than optical photometry.
    - Additional MAXI and RXTE/ASM monitoring raises X-ray completeness by `5-8` times and remains null.
  - **Position:** useful AGN-binary demographic constraint because it supplies a sample-level null result with explicit completeness.

- [2608.16304](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16304) J. S. Elford et al. `Coronal gas excitation as a tracer of supermassive black hole mass: on the mid-IR coronal [Ne V] lines`
  - **Question:** can mid-infrared [Ne V] coronal lines trace SMBH mass in AGN?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `27` AGN spanning three orders of magnitude in SMBH mass.
    - Spectra: Spitzer mid-infrared observations.
    - Lines: [Ne V] `14 micron`, [Ne V] `24 micron`, and broad Br-gamma.
    - Comparison: JWST density estimates for a subsample.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit Gaussian line profiles to coronal-line detections.
    - Normalize [Ne V] fluxes to broad Br-gamma.
    - Compare normalized coronal-line ratios with SMBH mass.
    - Estimate coronal-gas electron density.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - [Ne V]/Br-gamma ratios correlate strongly with SMBH mass.
    - The scatter is `0.54 dex` for [Ne V] `14 micron` and `0.53 dex` for [Ne V] `24 micron`.
    - Coronal-gas electron densities are of order `1000 cm^-3`.
    - JWST density measurements for a subsample support a nuclear coronal-gas origin.
  - **Position:** useful AGN diagnostic paper, especially for interpreting JWST/MIRI coronal-line observations.

## Multiphase Gas, Dust, and Lower-Redshift Calibration

- [2608.14888](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14888) Benjamin Vigneron et al. `High-spectral-resolution Observations of the [S II] Emission-line Doublet in the Filamentary Nebula Surrounding NGC 1275`
  - **Question:** what electron densities characterize optical filaments around NGC 1275, and how do they relate to the surrounding ICM?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: filamentary nebula around NGC 1275 in the Perseus cluster.
    - Instrument: SITELLE at `R=7000`.
    - Lines: [S II] `6716` and `6731`.
    - Comparison data: deep Chandra X-ray observations and cold molecular CO observations.
  - **Method:**
    - Use the [S II] doublet ratio to infer electron density.
    - Compare optical-filament densities with ICM densities.
    - Build radial profiles for inner and outer filaments.
    - Combine optical and CO constraints to discuss multiphase temperature-density-pressure behavior.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Outer filaments show lower [S II] line ratios of `~1.1`.
    - Inner filaments show higher ratios of `~1.3`.
    - Most filaments sit near the low-density measurement threshold of `~10^2 cm^-3`.
    - Inner-filament density is roughly constant while ICM density decreases with radius.
    - Local connections between ICM and optical-filament density appear in the outer filaments, but no clear global correlation is seen.
  - **Position:** relevant as a detailed multiphase-gas benchmark in a cool-core environment, lower priority than the high-z structure papers.

- [2608.15633](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15633) Uri Malamud et al. `Multiphase turbulence as the origin of OH+, H2O+ and H3+ column density scatter in the local ISM`
  - **Question:** does column-density scatter in reactive molecular ions require large cosmic-ray ionization-rate variations?
  - **Data:**
    - Tracers: OH+, H2O+, and H3+ in the local Galactic ISM.
    - Simulations: high-resolution three-dimensional MHD models with time-dependent chemistry.
    - Baseline: single cosmic-ray ionization rate of `~2 x 10^-16 s^-1`.
  - **Method:**
    - Model multiphase turbulent gas with time-dependent H, H2, H+, and electron chemistry.
    - Preserve the turbulent, time-dependent H2 field during post-processing.
    - Compare simulated column-density distributions with observed medians and percentile widths.
    - Contrast time-dependent chemistry with equilibrium assumptions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - A single cosmic-ray ionization rate can reproduce broad column-density distributions for all three tracers.
    - Time-dependent H2 enhancements in unstable neutral gas are required for OH+ and H2O+.
    - Equilibrium chemistry underpredicts OH+ and H2O+ columns.
    - H3+ is less affected because most of its column arises in denser gas closer to equilibrium.
    - Sight-line scatter should not be read directly as cosmic-ray ionization-rate scatter.
  - **Position:** useful gas-physics caution for absorption-line interpretation, even though the target regime is local ISM rather than high-z CGM.

- [2608.16802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16802) Mansi Padave et al. `JWST Whirlpool Galaxy Treasury: Mid-Infrared Emission in M51 and its Relation to Gas Column and Star Formation`
  - **Question:** how do JWST/MIRI mid-infrared bands trace gas column and star formation in nearby galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: M51.
    - Instrument: JWST/MIRI imaging in eight broadband filters.
    - Gas tracers: CO(1-0), H I, and Pa-alpha.
    - Spatial scales: `40 pc` molecular-gas analysis and `440 pc` total-gas comparison.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare PAH-dominated and dust-continuum-dominated filters with molecular, atomic, and ionized gas.
    - Model mid-infrared emission as gas-associated plus star-formation-associated components.
    - Test how filter behavior changes with wavelength.
    - Compare total gas surface density with PAH-dominated filters.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - PAH-dominated bands correlate nearly linearly with CO(1-0) at `40 pc`.
    - Dust-continuum bands have shallower CO slopes and stronger star-formation contributions.
    - F2100W is `~75%` associated with the Pa-alpha template in the empirical decomposition.
    - PAH-dominated filters correlate tightly and linearly with total `H I+H2` at `440 pc`.
    - Mid-infrared emission traces both gas and star formation, with a wavelength-dependent shift in dominant driver.
  - **Position:** useful local JWST calibration for gas-column and star-formation mapping, especially where PAH emission is used as a gas proxy.

- [2608.14984](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14984) Junhao Peng et al. report JWST/NIRSpec and MIRI detections of aliphatic C-H and C-D features in 30 Doradus, including a `4.65 micron` aliphatic C-D feature and structured `6.85 micron` emission; useful for dust-chemistry interpretation in star-forming regions.
- [2608.14989](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14989) Junhao Peng et al. analyze `102` local star-forming galaxies with AKARI spectra and find PAH aromatic and aliphatic emission tied to metallicity, SFR, and AGN activity; useful as an IR-diagnostic calibration.
- [2608.16456](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16456) M.A.T. Groenewegen et al. use ALMA CO observations of very red carbon stars in the Magellanic Clouds to constrain gas and dust mass loss; mostly relevant as dust lifecycle background.
- [2608.16149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16149) Aryav Das et al. model H2 formation on carbonaceous dust grains and find regimes where dust-catalyzed H2 chemistry can keep pace with collapse under interstellar conditions.
- [2608.16754](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16754) Felix J. Lockman et al. map high-velocity H I clouds in the Fermi Bubbles with Green Bank Telescope data, giving a resolved neutral-gas view of a Galactic nuclear wind.
