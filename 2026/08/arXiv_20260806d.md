## High-Redshift Galaxies, LRDs, and Quasars

- [2608.04101](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04101) Casey Papovich et al. `Implications of Broad [O III] 4364 and UV Line Emission in Two Little Red Dots at z ~ 7 - 8`
  - **Question:** what do broad optical and UV emission lines imply about the gas structure and ionizing sources in two Little Red Dots?
  - **Data:**
    - Targets: two Little Red Dots at `z=6.68` and `z=8.35`.
    - Spectroscopy: deep JWST/NIRSpec G140M and G395M.
    - Lines: broad Balmer and [O III] 4364 plus narrow C III], O III], N III], and N IV].
  - **Method:**
    - Measure broad and narrow line widths, density-sensitive ratios, and equivalent widths.
    - Infer broad-line gas densities from [O III] 4364/[O III] 5008.
    - Compare line ratios with expectations for stellar populations and accretion-disk ionization.
    - Use nitrogen detections to assess rapid enrichment.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Broad [O III] 4364 has `FWHM~1000 km/s`, about one-third of the H-beta line width.
    - Broad-line gas densities are `log n/cm^-3=6.3-7.9`, or `3-10` times higher than low-redshift quasar broad-line regions.
    - If the broad lines trace virial motions, the metal-enhanced clouds lie at `~1-10 pc` from the LRD engine.
    - The line equivalent widths and [O III] 4364/H-gamma ratios likely require accretion-disk ionization combined with star formation.
  - **Position:** read first because the spectra directly test whether LRDs are stratified compact AGN, dense starbursts, or composite systems.

- [2608.04078](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04078) L. Ighina et al. `The most extreme high-z radio quasars in the eRASS:1 X-ray survey`
  - **Question:** can X-ray, optical/NIR, and radio selection efficiently expand the known high-redshift radio-quasar population?
  - **Data:**
    - X-ray: eROSITA All-Sky Survey eRASS:1.
    - Optical/NIR: DECam Local Volume Exploration.
    - Radio: Rapid ASKAP Continuum Survey.
    - Follow-up: spectroscopy for `46` candidates from an initial list of `68`.
  - **Method:**
    - Select high-z radio-quasar candidates from matched X-ray, optical/NIR, and radio data.
    - Confirm redshifts through follow-up spectroscopy.
    - Identify blazars using X-ray-to-optical relative intensity.
    - Compare `z>4` X-ray selected blazar counts with fractional IC/CMB predictions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The follow-up confirms `39` sources at `z>3.5`.
    - The `14` new `z>4` radio quasars increase the known population above the same flux limits and area by `~65%`.
    - The observed high-z X-ray selected blazar counts broadly agree with fractional IC/CMB expectations after incompleteness and classification biases are considered.
  - **Position:** high-priority quasar census paper because it adds rare luminous radio-loud systems for early black-hole and jet-evolution work.

- [2608.04518](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04518) Bibhuprasad Mishra et al. `Detection and Properties of Lyman Break Galaxies at 1.6 <= z <= 3.0 in GOODS-North`
  - **Question:** what compact Lyman-break galaxy population is recovered in GOODS-North with 3D-HST dropout selections?
  - **Data:**
    - Field: GOODS-North.
    - Imaging and photometry: 3D-HST data.
    - Selection windows: F275W dropouts at `1.6<=z<=2.3` and F336W dropouts at `2.3<z<=3.0`.
  - **Method:**
    - Apply color-selection dropout criteria.
    - Fit spectral energy distributions for stellar ages, stellar masses, and SFRs.
    - Measure morphologies for the selected candidates.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The search identifies `192` candidates at `1.6<=z<=2.3`.
    - The search identifies `135` candidates at `2.3<z<=3.0`.
    - The candidates are mostly low- to intermediate-mass compact galaxies.
    - Typical effective half-light radii are `~3.5 kpc`.
  - **Position:** useful supporting catalog paper for cosmic-noon star-forming galaxy samples in a legacy deep field.

## CGM, Cosmic Web, and Baryon-Cycle Physics

- [2608.04087](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04087) Connor Jennings et al. `Predicting the Kinematics of the Cold Circumgalactic Medium from its Morphology using Convolutional Neural Networks`
  - **Question:** can cold-CGM morphology in emission maps predict otherwise inaccessible plane-of-sky velocity fields?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: TNG50 cosmological galaxies.
    - Sample: `182` Milky Way and Andromeda analogs.
    - Synthetic observables: H-alpha emission maps generated with Cloudy and forward-modeled observational noise.
    - Target labels: line-of-sight averaged two-dimensional velocity maps.
  - **Method:**
    - Train a UNet to map CGM emission morphology to plane-of-sky velocity maps.
    - Inject Gaussian noise to assess survey-depth requirements.
    - Compare inferred and true flow directions as a function of expected observing depth.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The network usually recovers the global flow direction qualitatively.
    - At depths expected for forthcoming facilities such as MOTHRA, the typical RMS velocity-direction error is `0.3-0.5 v_vir`.
    - Deep two-dimensional emission maps could estimate two additional CGM phase-space dimensions.
  - **Position:** method-forward CGM paper because it links future low-surface-brightness imaging to dynamical information beyond line-of-sight velocities.

- [2608.04656](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04656) Seoyoung Lyla Jung et al. `MIGHTEE-HI: Environmental effects of cosmic filaments on extragalactic HI detections in the COSMOS and XMM-LSS fields`
  - **Question:** do cosmic filaments measurably affect neutral-hydrogen detection fractions after controlling for galaxy properties and local density?
  - **Data:**
    - HI survey: MIGHTEE in the COSMOS and XMM-LSS fields.
    - Optical parent sample: DESI Survey galaxies.
    - Redshift range: `0.02<z<0.09`.
    - Environment metrics: local density and distance to the nearest filament.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure HI detection fraction as a function of stellar mass, morphology, local density, and filament distance.
    - Control for covariates that correlate with filament proximity.
    - Compare late-type and massive early-type behavior in filament and void environments.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Galaxies closer to filaments have higher stellar masses, higher local densities, and larger early-type fractions.
    - After those dependencies are controlled, filament proximity still affects HI detection.
    - Late-type galaxies in low-density environments show reduced HI detection fractions near filaments.
    - Massive early-type galaxies show enhanced HI detection in filaments relative to voids.
  - **Position:** read-first environment paper because it connects cold-gas reservoirs to cosmic-web location in two well-used extragalactic fields.

- [2608.04784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04784) Kai Wang et al. `Inflow-driven galaxy evolution - I. Revealing the physics of the fundamental metallicity relation`
  - **Question:** what physical continuity-equation model produces the fundamental metallicity relation?
  - **Data:**
    - Framework: minimal cosmological gas-flow model.
    - Calibration targets: mass-metallicity relation, star-forming main sequence, and stellar-to-halo mass relation.
    - Redshift span: `z=0-3`.
  - **Method:**
    - Solve mass-continuity equations for gas, stars, SFR, and gas metallicity.
    - Calibrate the model to observed scaling relations.
    - Run controlled simplifications with constant star-formation efficiency and mass loading.
    - Derive analytic relations for the gaseous FMR.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The FMR emerges as a redshift-invariant surface in `M_star`, SFR, and `Z_g`.
    - With constant efficiency and mass loading, the FMR reduces to a scaling between `Z_g` and `M_star/SFR`.
    - The gaseous FMR in `M_star`, `M_g`, and `Z_g` is more fundamental because `Z_g` follows `M_star/M_g` in the inflow-driven limit.
    - The observed FMR parameterization depends on how star-formation efficiency and mass loading vary with stellar mass and redshift.
  - **Position:** high-value baryon-cycle theory paper for turning metallicity scaling relations into gas-flow constraints.

- [2608.04088](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04088) Avshalom Badash et al. `Column-Density Estimation from the Equivalent Widths of Absorption Doublets`
  - **Question:** how can unresolved absorption-doublet equivalent widths yield robust column-density constraints in saturated systems?
  - **Data:**
    - Tracer: Na I D absorption-line doublets.
    - Validation set: published Type Ia supernova profile-fitting column densities.
    - Required measurement: separately measured equivalent widths for the two doublet members.
  - **Method:**
    - Derive a Gaussian curve-of-growth inversion using the doublet ratio.
    - Define the exact inversion, a second-order approximation, and a velocity-structure-independent lower bound.
    - Compare integrated-equivalent-width estimates with profile-fitting results.
    - Provide public implementation with uncertainty propagation.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - A resolving power of only `lambda/Delta lambda>=10^3` is sufficient for Na I D doublet use.
    - The recommended `N_min` is an assumption-free lower bound.
    - `N_min` is within a few percent of the exact Gaussian inversion for `R>=1.4`.
    - At `R=1.1`, `N_min` is at most a factor `3.5` below the exact Gaussian inversion.
  - **Position:** useful absorption-method paper for dust and gas-column estimates when velocity structure is unresolved.

## Low-Redshift Dynamics, Dark Matter, and Cluster Gas

- [2608.04371](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04371) Hongwei Xi et al. `FAST Ultra-Deep Survey: the baryonic Tully-Fisher relation in FUDS0 field`
  - **Question:** does the baryonic Tully-Fisher relation evolve in an HI-selected sample reaching beyond the local Universe?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: FAST Ultra-Deep Survey pilot FUDS0 field.
    - Tracer: HI-detected galaxies.
    - Redshift reach: `z=0.42`.
    - Measured relation: baryonic mass versus rotational velocity.
  - **Method:**
    - Separate tight BTFR and dispersed outlier components with a Gaussian mixture model.
    - Fit the BTFR slope, zero point, and intrinsic scatter.
    - Use a random forest classifier to diagnose outlier origins.
    - Compare relation parameters in three redshift bins.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The recovered BTFR slope is `k=3.32_-0.11^+0.12`.
    - The zero point is `b=10.07_-0.03^+0.03`.
    - The intrinsic scatter is `0.036_-0.009^+0.010`.
    - Low signal significance and inaccurate inclinations dominate the outlier component.
    - No significant BTFR evolution is detected within current uncertainties.
  - **Position:** useful HI-dynamics baseline because it extends BTFR tests with FAST-selected galaxies to intermediate redshift.

- [2608.04362](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04362) Morgan Ohana et al. `Cold Dark Matter and Self-Interacting Dark Matter Interpretations of Cloud-9`
  - **Question:** can the gas-rich Cloud-9 object distinguish CDM and SIDM halo interpretations?
  - **Data:**
    - Object: Cloud-9, a FAST-detected HI cloud near M94 without an optical counterpart.
    - Interpretation: Reionization Limited HI Cloud.
    - Observable: hydrogen column-density profile.
    - Simulation comparison: Concerto cosmological zoom-in simulations with velocity-dependent SIDM.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit Cloud-9 with gas embedded in CDM and SIDM halos.
    - Compare required halo concentrations with cosmological expectations.
    - Search for Cloud-9 analogs in SIDM simulations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Cloud-9 can be fit by either a cuspy CDM halo or a cored SIDM halo.
    - Both models require an unusually diffuse central density.
    - The best-fitting CDM halo is `~7 sigma` below the concentration-mass relation.
    - SIDM core formation reduces the concentration tension to `~3 sigma`.
  - **Position:** useful dark-matter test because RELHICs reduce baryonic feedback degeneracies.

- [2608.04757](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04757) Erwin T. Lau et al. `Non-Thermal Pressure due to Gas Motions in the Intracluster Medium: Confronting XRISM/Resolve with TNG-Cluster Simulations`
  - **Question:** why are XRISM cluster gas-motion measurements lower than many simulation predictions?
  - **Data:**
    - Observations: XRISM/Resolve velocity-dispersion and non-thermal-pressure measurements.
    - Simulations: TNG-Cluster.
    - Extreme comparison object: Abell 2029.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure simulated non-thermal pressure profiles by cool-core state and formation history.
    - Build a two-scale fitting function for inner suppression and outer radial rise.
    - Forward-model mock XRISM observations.
    - Compare projected and intrinsic three-dimensional gas-motion quantities.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Non-thermal pressure depends strongly on cool-core state and formation history.
    - Projection effects and azimuthal variations bias observed velocity dispersion and non-thermal pressure low.
    - Those effects cannot explain Abell 2029, which lies below about the `0th-6th` simulated cool-core percentiles at each measured radius.
    - The remaining tension indicates rare dynamical conditions or missing gas-motion physics.
  - **Position:** relevant cluster-gas paper because it tests whether simulations overpredict turbulent support in relaxed systems.

## AGN Outflows, ISM Calibration, and Survey Infrastructure

- [2608.05098](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05098) Missagh Mehdipour et al. `Dissecting the Nuclear Structure of NGC 5548 with XRISM. I. Physical Properties of the Highly Ionized Outflows`
  - **Question:** what ionization and velocity structure does XRISM reveal in the highly ionized outflow of NGC 5548?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: Seyfert 1 galaxy NGC 5548.
    - X-ray spectroscopy: XRISM/Resolve Fe K band.
    - Supporting data: XMM-Newton RGS and HST UV absorption lines.
  - **Method:**
    - Model XRISM and XMM-Newton spectra jointly.
    - Identify ionization components and velocity sub-components.
    - Derive an absorption measure distribution.
    - Compare Fe XXV absorption with UV C IV and Ly-alpha absorption.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - XRISM detects Fe XXV and Fe XXVI absorption in a highly ionized AGN outflow.
    - Four ionization components span `log xi=0.9-3.4`.
    - Three components split into two velocity sub-components.
    - Outflow velocities span `240-2730 km/s`.
    - Column density and velocity generally increase with ionization parameter.
  - **Position:** read as AGN gas-physics context because XRISM directly resolves multiphase high-ionization outflows.

- [2608.04116](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04116) Brandt A. L. Gaches et al. `Resolving dense photo-dissociation regions: the structure of photochemical fronts in three-dimensional gas distributions`
  - **Question:** can three-dimensional density structure explain the observed chemical-front morphology of the Orion Bar?
  - **Data:**
    - Target analogue: Orion Bar photodissociation region.
    - Observational motivation: ALMA and JWST maps of H2 and C+ front morphology.
    - Model: upgraded steady-state 3D-PDR code.
  - **Method:**
    - Build a fully three-dimensional high-resolution Orion Bar analogue.
    - Treat plane irradiation and non-LTE H2 rovibrational levels.
    - Resolve the H2 dissociation and C+ recombination fronts in a structured density field.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The H2 dissociation front forms a complex surface that overlaps the C+ recombination front.
    - The model reproduces arc-like and filament-like H2 emission structures.
    - Shadowing and shielding by dense substructures explain the front overlap and morphology.
  - **Position:** local ISM calibration paper for interpreting JWST and ALMA emission from dense star-forming regions.

- [2608.04282](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04282) Scott C. Chapman et al. `CCAT: The 410 GHz camera module for FYST - design and testing of the MKID focal plane`
  - **Question:** what detector design supports the off-central `410 GHz` Prime-Cam module for FYST?
  - **Data:**
    - Facility: Fred Young Submillimeter Telescope.
    - Instrument: Prime-Cam `410 GHz` imager/polarimeter module.
    - Detector tests: four MKID devices with different inductor volumes.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure optical performance for MKID test devices at `410 GHz` loading.
    - Optimize Qi/Qc and detector responsivity for Cerro Chajnantor atmospheric conditions.
    - Design horn-coupled TiN MKID arrays and RFSoC readout.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The proposed module uses `~6700` MKIDs per array.
    - The planned design has `~20000` MKIDs over three arrays.
    - Readout plans exceed `4000` detectors per board.
  - **Position:** survey-infrastructure note for future submillimeter mapping of dusty galaxies and Galactic/cosmological foregrounds.

- [2608.05121](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05121) Ben Keller et al. `CCAT: Design and Characterization of the 350 GHz Instrument Module`
  - **Question:** what are the design and lab-test results for the `350 GHz` Prime-Cam module?
  - **Data:**
    - Facility: Fred Young Submillimeter Telescope.
    - Instrument: Prime-Cam `350 GHz` broadband camera module.
    - Detector system: more than `10000` KIDs across three arrays.
  - **Method:**
    - Characterize optics, readout, and detector arrays.
    - Test the fully integrated module in the lab.
    - Verify cryogenic performance, detector yield, and passband.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The module reaches stable operation with a `100 mK` focal plane.
    - The lab-tested passband is comparable to design specifications.
    - The module has been shipped to Chile for integration into Prime-Cam.
  - **Position:** infrastructure support for wide submillimeter dust, foreground, and high-redshift galaxy survey programs.
