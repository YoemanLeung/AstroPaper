## Executive View

The read-first item is [2608.19311](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19311) Yongda Zhu on Lyalpha visibility in DIVER galaxies at `4.8<z<11`. JWST/NIRSpec and NIRCam data show that strong Lyalpha emitters avoid nearby H-alpha and [O III] emitters, with the GOODS-N `z~5.2` overdensity showing fewer than `15%` strong Lyalpha emitters. This is directly relevant to environment-dependent reionization topology and local CGM/IGM opacity.

[2608.19439](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19439) Arshi Ali is the second Lyalpha priority. VLT/MUSE plus JWST/NIRSpec IFU data map Lyalpha, H-alpha, H-beta, and UV emission in CRISTAL-02 at `z~5.3`, linking anisotropic Lyalpha escape to outflow direction, dust, and local gas conditions.

The high-redshift morphology and feedback item is [2608.19308](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19308) Zihao Wang. THESAN-ZOOM simulations show that clumpy high-z light distributions can arise from bursty star formation and feedback-driven dense gas reorganization even when stellar-mass maps are smoother.

The strongest early-chemistry warning is [2608.20339](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20339) Tiger Yu-Yang Hsiao. JWST/NIRSpec SPURS data show that high electron densities can make some Abell 2744 galaxies look like extremely metal-poor galaxy candidates when low-density assumptions are imposed.

Useful methods and environment papers include [2608.19429](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19429) Martin de los Rios on `roger v2.0` orbital classification for group and cluster galaxies, [2608.19768](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19768) Pablo Avalos on cluster finding with central-galaxy outer stellar mass, and [2608.19272](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19272) Gioia Rau on cross-survey infrastructure for LSST, Roman, and Euclid-era science.

## Reionization, Lyalpha, and Escape Diagnostics

- [2608.19311](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19311) Yongda Zhu et al. `Low Lyalpha Visibility in Galaxy Overdensities: Reionization Topology and Neutral-Fraction Ceilings from DIVER over 4.8<z<11`
  - **Question:** how does Lyalpha visibility depend on galaxy environment during reionization?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: DIVER JWST/NIRSpec spectroscopy in GOODS-N.
    - Sample: `250` galaxies at `4.8<z<11`.
    - Detections: `84` Lyalpha detections, including `44` strong emitters with `W_Lyalpha>25 A`.
    - Environment map: H-alpha and [O III] emitters from JWST/NIRCam wide-field slitless spectroscopy.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure Lyalpha equivalent widths and effective escape fractions.
    - Map nearby H-alpha and [O III] emitter density around each DIVER source.
    - Compare high- and low-Lyalpha-visibility galaxies as a function of environment.
    - Calibrate an empirical upper envelope in `f_esc,Lyalpha^eff` with reionization simulations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - High-`W_Lyalpha` and high-`f_esc,Lyalpha^eff` galaxies tend to lie farther from nearby H-alpha and [O III] emitters.
    - The GOODS-N overdensity at `z~5.2` has fewer than `15%` strong Lyalpha emitters.
    - Neutral-fraction ceilings are `<x_HI>_max=0.36, 0.76, 0.74, 0.84, and 1.0` at `z~5.2, 5.8, 6.7, 7.7, and 9.8`.
    - The `z~8` ceiling disfavors an almost completely neutral IGM at that epoch.
  - **Position:** read first because it connects Lyalpha visibility, overdensity, local opacity, and reionization topology in one JWST data set.

- [2608.19439](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19439) Arshi Ali et al. `Tracing Lyman alpha escape in the CRISTAL-02 galaxy at z~5.3`
  - **Question:** what regulates spatially resolved Lyalpha escape in CRISTAL-02 at `z~5.3`?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: CRISTAL-02, a star-forming galaxy with five clumps.
    - Spectroscopy: VLT/MUSE Lyalpha and JWST/NIRSpec IFU H-alpha and H-beta.
    - Imaging: JWST/NIRCam UV maps.
    - Context: clumps A and B are candidate AGN sites.
  - **Method:**
    - Construct matched Lyalpha, Balmer-line, and UV maps.
    - Derive line-ratio, extinction, Lyalpha escape-fraction, and ionizing-efficiency maps.
    - Compare Lyalpha morphology with the [C II] outflow direction.
    - Test whether clump-scale Lyalpha differences favor AGN or star-formation feedback.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Lyalpha extends preferentially along the [C II] outflow direction.
    - H-alpha and UV emission trace the disk more closely than Lyalpha does.
    - Clump A has lower dust, higher Lyalpha/H-alpha, and higher Lyalpha escape fraction than clump B.
    - The evidence favors outflow-aided Lyalpha escape but does not uniquely identify AGN or stellar feedback as the driver.
  - **Position:** high-priority companion to DIVER because it resolves the local gas channels that make environment-level Lyalpha trends hard to interpret.

- [2608.19687](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19687) Ravi Jaiswar et al. `OutThere Survey: Addressing xi_ion and f_esc with a population of average galaxies at z~2`
  - **Question:** what do average `z~2` galaxies imply for ionizing photon production and LyC escape calibrations?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: OutThere JWST/NIRISS pure-parallel data.
    - Sample: `230` sources at `1.3<z<2.6`.
    - Ancillary data: JWST/NIRCam, NIRISS, and HST photometry.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure ionizing photon production efficiency `xi_ion`.
    - Estimate LyC escape fraction `f_esc`.
    - Test correlations with UV slope, `M_UV`, stellar mass, sSFR, [O III] equivalent width, and H-alpha equivalent width.
    - Use the low-threshold selected sample as a control for higher-redshift work.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - `xi_ion` has no significant correlation with UV slope, `M_UV`, stellar mass, or sSFR.
    - `xi_ion` correlates with [O III]5007 equivalent width at `rho=0.24` and H-alpha equivalent width at `rho=0.63`.
    - Median `f_esc` is `0.9%` including non-detections and `1.9%` above a `0.01%` threshold.
    - The escape-fraction estimator correlates with redshift, SFR, and `M_UV`.
  - **Position:** useful baseline because it gives a JWST-era control sample for reionization analog calibrations.

## High-Redshift Morphology and Chemical Diagnostics

- [2608.19308](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19308) Zihao Wang et al. `The THESAN-ZOOM project: clumpiness of high-redshift galaxies and its connection to bursty star formation`
  - **Question:** can high-redshift galaxy clumpiness trace bursty star formation rather than only underlying stellar-mass structure?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation suite: THESAN-ZOOM radiation-hydrodynamic simulations.
    - Tracers: stellar mass, H-alpha, FUV, and optical light distributions.
    - Spatial regime: clumpiness across `~10^2-10^3 pc` scales.
  - **Method:**
    - Quantify clumpiness with a power-spectrum method.
    - Compare mass maps with star-formation-sensitive light maps.
    - Vary stellar-feedback prescriptions.
    - Track clumpiness across the bursty star-formation cycle.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Stellar-mass distributions are usually smoother than H-alpha, FUV, and optical light.
    - Recent-star-formation tracers show shallower spectra, `P(k) proportional to k^-1` to `k^-2`, and more small-scale power.
    - Weaker early stellar feedback increases small-scale power in mass and light.
    - Clumpiness varies strongly during burst cycles, so observed samples can be phase biased.
  - **Position:** strong morphology paper because it makes clumpiness a physical and selection-sensitive diagnostic of early feedback.

- [2608.20339](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20339) Tiger Yu-Yang Hsiao et al. `An Optical Illusion: High Electron Densities Create Extremely Metal-Poor Galaxy Impostors`
  - **Question:** can high electron density make JWST galaxies look more metal poor than they are?
  - **Data:**
    - Program: JWST/NIRSpec SPURS in the Abell 2744 lensed field.
    - Sample: four galaxies initially classed as extremely metal-poor under a low-density direct-method assumption.
    - Diagnostics: [O III]5008, [O III]4364, and [O III]1666.
  - **Method:**
    - Recompute electron temperature and density self-consistently in the high-ionization zone.
    - Compare metallicities with and without the common `n_e=10^3 cm^-3` assumption.
    - Test whether [O III]5008 suppression biases direct-method abundance estimates.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Three of four galaxies have `n_e~10^5-10^6 cm^-3`.
    - High density suppresses [O III]5008.
    - Low-density assumptions can underestimate metallicity by up to `~1.1 dex`.
    - The revised oxygen abundances are `12+log(O/H)~7.3-8.2`.
  - **Position:** high-priority JWST diagnostic caution because it affects claims about primordial-like chemical enrichment.

- [2608.19667](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19667) Yuki Takagishi et al. `Diversity of Ionized Gas Structures in Nearby Metal-poor Dwarf Galaxies`
  - **Question:** can integrated optical and far-infrared [O III] diagnostics be represented by one-zone ionized-gas models?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: five Herschel Dwarf Galaxy Survey systems.
    - Instruments: Seimei/KOOLS-IFU optical data plus Herschel/PACS [O III] 88 micron measurements.
    - Diagnostic lines: [O III]4363, [O III]5007, and [O III]88 micron.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare optical and far-infrared [O III] temperature-density diagnostics.
    - Use low-ionization optical diagnostics as independent density checks.
    - Build representative two-zone models where one-zone solutions fail.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - SBS0335-052E and Haro11 sit near or beyond the low-density boundary of the one-zone diagnostic.
    - Conservative density limits still remain below independent density estimates.
    - Two-zone models reproduce the discrepant [O III] lines by combining dense gas with cooler low-density gas.
    - Integrated one-zone diagnostics can return effective, not physically unique, temperatures and densities.
  - **Position:** useful local analog paper for interpreting dense ionized gas in low-metallicity JWST systems.

- [2608.19543](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19543) Yikai Liu et al. `The VariableTNG project: mass-dependent regulation of galaxy morphology by baryonic feedback`
  - **Question:** how do stellar and AGN feedback regulate morphology at fixed initial conditions?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation suite: VariableTNG cosmological MHD runs with AREPO.
    - Variations: eight stellar- and AGN-feedback parameters.
    - Redshift: morphology measured at `z=1`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure morphology with `kappa_co`, `v/sigma`, and axis ratio `c/a`.
    - Compare feedback variations at fixed initial conditions.
    - Separate behavior below and above `M_star~10^11 M_sun`.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Below `M_star~10^11 M_sun`, morphology is mainly controlled by supernova temperature `T_SN`.
    - Larger `T_SN` delays early star formation and favors later rotationally supported disk growth.
    - At higher masses, morphology becomes more sensitive to AGN feedback, especially `epsilon_f,high`.
    - The morphology-mass relation is non-monotonic, with maximum rotational support at intermediate masses.
  - **Position:** useful simulation paper because it separates feedback channels that can mimic or erase environmental morphology trends.

## Environment, Cluster Finding, and Survey Methods

- [2608.19429](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19429) Martin de los Rios et al. `Reconstructing orbits of galaxies in extreme regions (roger v2.0): an extension to intermediate mass systems`
  - **Question:** can projected phase-space orbit classification be extended robustly from clusters to intermediate-mass systems?
  - **Data:**
    - Tool: `roger v2.0`.
    - Inputs: projected phase-space coordinates plus host halo mass.
    - Host range: systems down to `10^13.5 h^-1 M_sun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Add halo mass as a classifier input.
    - Compare classifications with the previous projected phase-space method.
    - Provide a Python implementation for custom training sets.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Including halo mass changes classifications modestly but improves robustness.
    - The usable host-mass range extends into galaxy-group scales.
    - Custom classifier training makes the method adaptable to different surveys.
  - **Position:** practical environment tool for separating infall, backsplash, and virialized populations in group-scale samples.

- [2608.19768](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19768) Pablo Avalos et al. `Cluster finding with outskirt stellar masses and percolation`
  - **Question:** can central-galaxy outer stellar mass define a cleaner optical cluster sample?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: IllustrisTNG300 at `z=0.4`.
    - Observable proxy: outer stellar mass `M_out` in a `50-100 kpc` annulus.
    - Target: cluster samples with reduced projection effects.
  - **Method:**
    - Quantify satellite contamination for `M_out`-selected central galaxies.
    - Apply proximity-based percolation around higher-`M_out` galaxies.
    - Test completeness and purity as functions of `M_out` cut and percolation radius.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Satellite fractions are `<=15%` for `M_out>10^10 M_sun` and `<10%` for `M_out>10^11 M_sun`.
    - Percolation improves contamination control up to `3.0 R_200c`.
    - A cut near `4 x 10^10 M_sun` with `~2.0 R_200c` percolation recovers a complete and pure sample above `~10^14 M_sun`.
  - **Position:** relevant for future environment catalogs because it attacks projection effects without red-sequence richness.

- [2608.19272](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19272) Gioia Rau et al. `The Cross-Survey Decade: A Call to Action`
  - **Question:** what infrastructure is needed for joint analysis across flagship wide-field surveys?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey context: LSST, Roman, and Euclid-era overlapping survey operations by `2027`.
    - Scale: petabyte-class overlapping data over thousands of square degrees.
    - Investment context: more than `$6 billion` in public survey infrastructure.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify infrastructure gaps for joint pixel-level processing.
    - Define requirements for cross-calibration, validation, interoperable access, and AI-ready data products.
    - Map actions to agencies, observatories, universities, philanthropy, and the research community.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Joint survey science needs shared computational infrastructure, not only independent survey products.
    - Four pillars are joint pixel-level processing, AI-ready data substrates, interoperable access, and dedicated personnel.
    - Technical choices need alignment before large survey operations harden incompatible systems.
  - **Position:** survey-strategy note relevant to Roman/Subaru/LSST-style structure mapping and multi-facility sample construction.

- [2608.20239](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20239) Ziming Peng et al. `Re-evaluating the resolved mass-metallicity relation with a self-consistent metallicity calibration`
  - **Question:** does the mass-metallicity relation retain a secondary dependence on SFR under a self-consistent calibration?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: SDSS-IV/MaNGA integral-field spectroscopy.
    - Sample: `~3.5 x 10^6` spaxels in `~4550` galaxies.
    - Calibration: photoionization-model metallicity scale compared with strong-line and direct-method estimates.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit resolved and integrated MZR relations.
    - Compare residuals against local and integrated SFR.
    - Use partial correlation coefficients to quantify secondary dependences.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The preferred calibration is most consistent with direct-method metallicities.
    - Three resolved-MZR equations are provided.
    - Local SFR does not show a significant metallicity correlation.
    - Integrated SFR and sSFR do not correlate with metallicity residuals.
  - **Position:** useful calibration reference for metallicity-environment work because abundance scale choices can change claimed secondary trends.

## Lower-Priority but Useful Background

- [2608.19326](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19326) Minh Ngoc Le et al. `Serendipitous discovery of an almost-dark galaxy in the Virgo Cluster`
  - **Question:** are extreme low-surface-brightness Local Group analogs present in Virgo?
  - **Data:**
    - Object: TTT J1237327+143535 in the Virgo Cluster.
    - Facility: Two-meter Twin Telescope at Teide Observatory.
    - Measured properties: central surface brightness `27.9+/-0.2 mag arcsec^-2`, effective radius `0.9+/-0.1 kpc`, stellar mass `(2.2+/-0.4) x 10^6 M_sun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify the object serendipitously in deep imaging.
    - Compare size, luminosity, and surface brightness with Local Group analogs.
    - Place the discovery in the context of NGVS and Rubin LSST searches.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - TTT J1237327+143535 is a low-mass almost-dark Virgo galaxy.
    - Its radius and magnitude resemble And XXI and And XXIII.
    - Rubin and NGVS should reveal larger samples of similar systems.
  - **Position:** lower priority for the high-z profile, but useful for low-surface-brightness selection and satellite-population context.

- [2608.19402](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19402) Liam M. Wang et al. `Modeling Relativistic Tidal Disruptions of MESA Stars`
  - **Question:** how do relativistic corrections change fast tidal-disruption fallback calculations?
  - **Data:**
    - Model inputs: MESA stellar models.
    - Dynamical setting: equatorial Kerr geodesics and relativistic tidal fields.
    - Output: relativistic fallback timing for TDE debris.
  - **Method:**
    - Add relativistic tidal fields to a perturbative stellar-disruption model.
    - Compare disruption timing and fallback times with Newtonian results.
    - Test spin effects across orbit classes.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Relativistic stars disrupt earlier in the orbit.
    - Earlier disruption leaves less time for deformation before disruption.
    - Increased distance at disruption lengthens fallback time.
    - Black-hole spin has little fallback-time effect except for very close pericenters.
  - **Position:** AGN-transient background paper, mainly useful for interpreting nuclear flare models.
