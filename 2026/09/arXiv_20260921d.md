## Key Findings

- LAE overdensity predicts diffuse-emission incidence, but emitting gas fills only `20-25%` of filament area ([2609.20938](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20938) Davide Tornotti).
- Geometry and ensemble averaging matter: IFS depth estimates depend on turbulence assumptions, while smooth simulated fountains emerge only after stacking ([2609.20929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20929) Mandy C. Chen; [2609.21374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21374) Chengyu Ma).
- ACE extends gas and dust measurements into subsolar metallicities, but CO conversion assumptions remain central to both enrichment and dust-to-gas interpretations ([2609.20921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20921) I. Langan; [2609.20926](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20926) N. N. Geesink).
- Early quenching evidence requires careful classification: protocluster red galaxies remain candidates, and CAPERS finds substantial photometric contamination ([2609.21337](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21337) Scott C. Chapman; [2609.20931](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20931) Lu Shen).
- Neither strong He II nor large nitrogen yields uniquely identifies primordial enrichment; hybrid candidates and yield models retain distinct degeneracies ([2609.20963](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20963) Rosa Roberts; [2609.21939](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21939) Devesh Nandal).

## Cosmic-web emission and CGM geometry

- [2609.20938](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20938) Davide Tornotti: Cosmic web Lyalpha emission in a sample of overdense regions
  - **Question:** How does diffuse cosmic-web Lyalpha emission vary with LAE environment?
  - **Data:**
    - MUSE: MUDF and MXDF ultra-deep spectroscopy; `41` LAE overdensities at `3 <~ z <~ 5`.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify LAE associations with friends-of-friends and extract faint emission with SHINE.
    - Mask galaxy CGM apertures and compare diffuse surface brightness, emitting area, and oriented stacks.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - After excluding six skyline-affected systems and applying CGM masks and a diffuse-area cut, `26/35` usable overdensities show diffuse emission. Search depths reach `3-5 x 10^-20 erg s^-1 cm^-2 arcsec^-2`.
    - Emitting gas covers only `f_s~0.20-0.25` of filament area; its inferred incidence `l(Lyalpha)~1.22` resembles that of Lyman-limit absorbers. Gas density and ionization interpretations require radiative-transfer modeling.
  - **Position:** Extends the filament-emission picture of Bacon+2021 beyond individual spectacular structures; CGM masking and unresolved substructure remain relevant.

- [2609.20929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20929) Mandy C. Chen: From IFS Maps to 3D Structure I: Constraining Geometry in the Circumgalactic Medium
  - **Question:** Can spatially resolved emission kinematics constrain the line-of-sight depth of cool CGM nebulae?
  - **Data:**
    - VLT/MUSE and JWST/NIRSpec: `11` CGM nebulae; direct turbulence simulations and synthetic clumpy emission maps.
  - **Method:**
    - Project statistically isotropic turbulent velocity fields through slabs of different depths.
    - Calibrate the line-width to centroid-dispersion ratio, including finite resolution and discrete emitters.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Observed `sigma_los/sigma_pos~1-2.5` implies `L_los/L_neb~0.1-5` under the adopted calibration.
    - Many clumps per beam make the dispersion ratio insensitive to detailed clump configurations. The geometric inference assumes homogeneous isotropic turbulence and idealized emitting volumes.
  - **Position:** Provides an emission-based complement to absorption-line geometry constraints and a foundation for the companion turbulence-diagnostic study.

- [2609.21374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21374) Chengyu Ma: Fountain pattern of baryon cycle revealed in galaxy ecosystems
  - **Question:** Can ensemble averaging reveal a regular baryon-cycle pattern hidden in individual galaxy ecosystems?
  - **Data:**
    - IllustrisTNG: a fiducial ensemble of `131` central star-forming galaxies at `z=0`, with `M_star~10^10 Msun`; additional mass and redshift ensembles.
  - **Method:**
    - Align galaxies by their disk planes and gas angular momentum, discretize gas fields, and take ensemble medians.
    - Map density, temperature, velocity, angular momentum, and radial mass flux across ISM, CGM, and IGM scales.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Stacks show disk-plane inflow, perpendicular bipolar outflow, and intervening recycling; the regular fountain is an ensemble feature, not a universal individual-galaxy flow.
    - For the fiducial sample, large-scale outward motion beyond roughly `300 kpc` reflects cosmic expansion; it must not all be interpreted as feedback-driven outflow.
  - **Position:** Offers a simulation-to-observation framework for orientation-dependent gas and metal stacks, with population-dependent alignment and interpretation.


## Early environmental quenching and spectroscopic benchmarks

- [2609.21337](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21337) Scott C. Chapman: A large overdensity of LRD-like galaxies discovered by JWST in the SPT2349-56 protocluster at z=4.30
  - **Question:** Does the SPT2349-56 core already contain a suppressed or passive galaxy population alongside its starbursts?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRCam: `11` ultra-red candidates in the `z=4.30` protocluster field; deep ALMA continuum limits.
  - **Method:**
    - Select `F200W-F444W>2.0 mag` sources and inspect resolved morphologies.
    - Fit spectral energy distributions and use ALMA limits to compare star formation with the main sequence.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The projected concentration is at least `~2000` times the comparison field density, but none of these sources is yet spectroscopically confirmed as a member.
    - Most are resolved extended red dots rather than classical compact LRDs. ERD1/Q1 has best-fit `M_star~8 x 10^10 Msun` and is a strong quiescent or recently quenched candidate.
  - **Position:** Extends a dusty-starburst-selected protocluster census toward suppressed systems; environmental transformation remains a conditional interpretation.

- [2609.20931](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20931) Lu Shen: Quiescent Galaxies at z > 2 from CAPERS spectroscopy and their Number densities
  - **Question:** How reliable are photometric quiescent selections, and do corrected abundances still challenge galaxy models?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/CAPERS NIRSpec plus broadband imaging: `19` spectroscopically selected quiescent galaxies at `2<=z<=4`; photometric abundance estimates extend to `z=5`.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit spectra and photometry with BAGPIPES and define quiescence using `sSFR_100<=0.2/t(z)`.
    - Calibrate photometric selection and propagate population-property uncertainties into corrected number densities.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - UVJ selection has `63%` completeness and `63%` purity; synthetic ugi colors give `89%` completeness but only `40%` purity. Strong emission-line galaxies dominate contamination.
    - For `M_star>=10^10.5 Msun` at `2<z<4`, most compared models underpredict abundance by `0.34-0.91 dex`, corresponding to only `~1.0-2.3 sigma`; COLIBRE generally agrees.
  - **Position:** Provides a spectroscopic calibration for JWST quiescent censuses, rather than a blanket failure of all galaxy-formation models.


## ACE: molecular gas, metals, and dust at cosmic noon

- [2609.21604](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21604) Irene Shivaei: ALMA Chemical Evolution (ACE) survey: an overview -- extending dust and gas inference to low metallicities at cosmic noon
  - **Question:** What does a joint low-metallicity gas, dust, and metal census add to cosmic-noon baryon-cycle studies?
  - **Data:**
    - ACE ALMA CO(3-2) and continuum observations, MOSDEF spectroscopy, and multiwavelength imaging at `z=2.0-2.5`; final CO sample `26`, continuum sample `25`.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine line fluxes, continuum measurements, metallicities, and star-formation diagnostics.
    - Fit a censored Bayesian relation between CO luminosity, stellar mass, SFR, and metallicity.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - There are `17` CO and `17` Band 7 detections. CO detectability follows mass and metallicity most strongly; dust detectability more closely follows SFR and IR luminosity.
    - The fitted CO-luminosity relation has intrinsic scatter `~0.1 dex`, potentially underestimated at low luminosity. One extra CO-only object explains the different sample sizes.
  - **Position:** Builds on MOSDEF and Sanders+2023 to underpin the coordinated ACE analyses; the companion papers share data and are not independent replications.

- [2609.21072](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21072) Gergo Popping: ALMA Chemical Evolution (ACE) Survey: Molecular gas properties of low-mass, low-metallicity galaxies at cosmic noon
  - **Question:** Do molecular-gas scaling relations continue into low-mass, subsolar-metallicity galaxies at cosmic noon?
  - **Data:**
    - ALMA Band 3: `26` unlensed galaxies at `z~2-2.5`, with metallicities `8.2<12+log(O/H)<8.6`; stacks and homogenized literature samples.
  - **Method:**
    - Convert CO(3-2) luminosity to molecular mass using metallicity-dependent conversion factors.
    - Compare gas mass, molecular-to-stellar mass ratio, and depletion time against galaxy properties.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Typical depletion times are `~1 Gyr`, with little mass or metallicity dependence; molecular-to-stellar ratios vary strongly with sSFR and main-sequence offset.
    - A molecular-mass prescription based on SFR and sSFR has intrinsic scatter `~0.2 dex`. Reservoir variation dominates the inferred SFR differences, subject to conversion-factor and selection assumptions.
  - **Position:** Extends established gas scaling relations to lower masses and metallicities; it does not establish that CO-dark gas is negligible.

- [2609.20921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20921) I. Langan: ALMA Chemical Evolution (ACE) survey: The gas fundamental metallicity relation at cosmic noon
  - **Question:** Does adding molecular gas improve the metallicity relation for typical cosmic-noon galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - ACE CO(3-2) plus optical-line metallicities: `26` galaxies and `17` CO detections, with median `log(M_star/Msun)=9.96` and `12+log(O/H)=8.44`.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare stellar-mass, SFR, and molecular-gas metallicity relations.
    - Confront the gas-metal budget with gas-regulator models and assess CO conversion choices.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - No statistically significant secondary dependence on CO-traced molecular mass is established: scatters are `~0.10 dex` for MZR, `~0.11 dex` for GFMR, and `~0.13 dex` for FMR.
    - A mean outflow loading `eta~4` is inferred within the regulator model, not directly detected in outflow kinematics. CO-dark gas and uncertain conversion factors limit the interpretation.
  - **Position:** Finds a `~0.3 dex` metallicity overprediction from the local Bothwell+2016 GFMR; resolved CO and independent gas tracers would sharpen the comparison.

- [2609.20926](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20926) N. N. Geesink: ALMA Chemical Evolution (ACE) survey: dust-to-gas ratios in sub-solar metallicity galaxies at cosmic noon
  - **Question:** Does the dust-to-molecular-gas ratio at fixed metallicity evolve between the local Universe and cosmic noon?
  - **Data:**
    - ACE ALMA CO and dust continuum at `z~2-2.5`, extending to `~0.4 Zsun`, plus local and higher-mass comparison samples.
  - **Method:**
    - Infer dust and molecular masses and include metallicity-binned stacks.
    - Compare the dust-to-molecular-gas relation with local calibrations and enrichment models.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The fitted log-space metallicity slope is `1.2+/-0.7`; detected galaxies have mean `log(M_dust/M_mol)=-2.37+/-0.05` at `12+log(O/H)=8.45+/-0.02`.
    - The slope is driven primarily by the adopted metallicity-dependent CO conversion factor. Agreement with local normalization supports, but does not prove, similar grain-growth physics.
  - **Position:** Tests dust-based gas calibration below solar metallicity; molecular-gas ratios are not total-gas ratios without an atomic-gas constraint.

- [2609.21040](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21040) M. Solimano: ALMA Chemical Evolution (ACE) survey: the dust content of subsolar metallicity galaxies at cosmic noon
  - **Question:** What controls the unusually high dust content of subsolar-metallicity galaxies at cosmic noon?
  - **Data:**
    - ACE: `25` galaxies at `z~2.3`, with ALMA `873 micron` or `1.3 mm` continuum and MOSDEF metallicities.
  - **Method:**
    - Infer dust masses with an optically thin, single-temperature modified blackbody.
    - Compare dust-to-stellar ratios and dust mass per SFR with metallicity, sSFR, and molecular-gas content.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The dust-to-stellar ratio is near `10^-2.2`, approximately `4-9` times local comparisons, with no significant metallicity trend over the sampled range.
    - Dust mass per SFR increases with metallicity with slope `1.6+/-0.5`; the inferred grain-growth interpretation depends on dust assumptions. High gas fractions can raise both sSFR and dust-to-stellar ratios.
  - **Position:** Complements the ACE dust-to-gas analysis: elevated dust per stellar mass can coexist with little evolution in dust per molecular-gas mass.


## Primordial populations, reionization, and dense nuclear gas

- [2609.20963](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20963) Rosa Roberts: Sown at Cosmic Dawn: Searching for Pop III Signatures in JWST JADES He II Selected Line Emitters
  - **Question:** Can strong He II emitters reveal surviving Pop III stars within already enriched galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/JADES: `84` strong He II emitters at `z~3-7`, with UV lines, metallicity diagnostics, and resolved imaging.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine UV line-ratio tests with AGN screening.
    - Use resolved SED fits to locate young, blue regions that integrated measurements can dilute.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - No pristine Pop III galaxy is established. Of `12` diagnostic candidates, the Blueberry at `z=5.94` has the strongest complete-line evidence, but its metallicity `log(Z/Zsun)=-1.18+/-0.05` excludes a globally pristine system.
    - Young blue populations also occur in enriched starbursts, and weak or obscured AGN remain possible. The evidence supports a hybrid candidate, not a confirmed metal-free population.
  - **Position:** Applies the Rusta+2025 diagnostic framework while exposing the limits of He II alone and of low-SNR metal-line ratios.

- [2609.21939](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21939) Devesh Nandal: Stellar Yields of Rapidly Rotating Population III Stars for the High Redshift Universe
  - **Question:** Can rapidly rotating ordinary-mass Pop III stars reproduce extreme high-redshift nitrogen enrichment?
  - **Data:**
    - Geneva stellar models: initial masses `5-200 Msun` and rotation `v_ini/v_crit=0.7`, with fate-dependent remnant prescriptions.
  - **Method:**
    - Track hydrostatic CNO production and partition final profiles into adopted ejecta and remnants.
    - Compare retained-core, pair-instability, and IMF-weighted yields, including a direct-collapse alternative.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Retained-core models reach `log(N/O)=-0.49`; pair-instability models eject `1.8-2.5 Msun` of nitrogen but also `57-65 Msun` of oxygen, giving lower N/O.
    - The grid alone does not reproduce the most nitrogen-dominated JWST systems. A more top-heavy IMF adds oxygen rapidly; heavier-element reservoirs are not self-consistent explosion yields.
  - **Position:** Provides a CNO-focused enrichment baseline whose predictions remain sensitive to fallback, explosion success, and remnant boundaries.

- [2609.21333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21333) Juyoung Kim: Patchy Reionization Delays Quenching in Isolated Ultra-faint Dwarf Galaxies
  - **Question:** How much does patchy reionization alter the quenching times of isolated ultra-faint dwarfs?
  - **Data:**
    - Cosmological zoom simulations: `7` halos at gas mass resolution `~63 Msun`, comparing matched-source uniform-UV and on-the-fly radiative-transfer treatments.
  - **Method:**
    - Track gas heating, dense-gas survival, and star formation under both radiation prescriptions.
    - Compare the time when `90%` of final stellar mass has formed and inspect metallicity distributions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Radiative transfer delays the mean quenching proxy by `~440 Myr`; six halos show delays of `300-740 Myr`, while one quenches before reionization.
    - Five halos continue forming stars to `z=5`. Metallicity distributions do not cleanly separate the models, and internal feedback introduces important halo-to-halo variation.
  - **Position:** Connects Local Group fossil star-formation histories to reionization topology; the delay is a controlled model comparison, not a universal measured offset.

- [2609.20913](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20913) Takumi S. Tanaka: Free-Free Radio Emission from Little Red Dots as a Probe of Ionized Gas
  - **Question:** Can radio free-free emission constrain the dense ionized gas proposed around little red dots?
  - **Data:**
    - Analytic spectral models and mock observations for local and high-redshift LRD examples; ALMA and ngVLA detectability forecasts.
  - **Method:**
    - Model free-free emission and self-absorption for different ionized-region sizes and density profiles.
    - Use turnover frequency and luminosity to constrain electron column and size, and low-frequency slope to test radial structure.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Local analogs with `N_e~3 x 10^23 cm^-2` and `R>~1 pc` can be accessible to ALMA in the modeled cases.
    - The diagnostic probes otherwise unresolved gas geometry and tests electron-scattering scenarios; these are predictions, not new radio detections. Other emission components must be separated.
  - **Position:** Adds an independent radio-to-millimeter test of dense-gas explanations for LRD optical and infrared spectra.


## Spectroscopic survey resources

- [2609.20935](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20935) Fengwu Sun: An Archival Calibration of JWST/MIRI Prism Wide-Field Slitless Spectroscopy: Methodology, Performance, and a Mid-Infrared Spectral Atlas of Galaxies at z=0-4 in the GOODS-N/S Fields
  - **Question:** Can archival MIRI prism exposures support calibrated wide-field galaxy spectroscopy?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/MIRI LRS calibration and GOODS-N/S exposures: `5-14 micron`, `R~100`, with an atlas of `180` galaxies at `z=0.08-3.71`.
  - **Method:**
    - Calibrate flats, sky, trace, wavelength, and flux response; validate emission-line recovery through injection and extraction.
    - Combine MIRI and NIRCam spectra to compare PAH and recombination-line luminosities.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Wavelength accuracy is `0.03-0.08` resolution elements over the calibrated `7.9-12.8 micron` interval; PAH `3.3 micron` correlates with Pa-alpha over `~1.5 dex`.
    - Single exposures beyond `~1-2 ks` encounter a systematic noise floor; at the deepest tested total depth (`~180 ks`), ETC predictions overestimate sensitivity by up to `~50%`.
  - **Position:** Releases calibration products and a spectral atlas while separating short-exposure performance from limitations of deep integrations.

- [2609.20947](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20947) Euclid Collaboration (J. Calhau): Euclid Quick Data Release (Q1) Euclid spectroscopy of quasars. 2. Physical properties from spectral fitting
  - **Question:** How reliable are physical quasar measurements from Euclid Q1 slitless spectra?
  - **Data:**
    - Euclid Q1 plus external quasar/redshift catalogs: `5489` input quasars, `5387` successfully fitted spectra, spanning `0.01<=z<=4.8`.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit continua and broad lines with explicit quality statistics.
    - Select reliable spectra and derive single-epoch black-hole masses and accretion estimates.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Only `2410` fitted sources pass the quality cut, about `45%`; fitting success alone is insufficient for a clean science sample.
    - Single-epoch masses are estimated for `1213` sources, with mean `log(M_BH/Msun)~8.7` and mean Eddington ratio `~0.34`; contamination and virial calibration remain relevant.
  - **Position:** Provides a reusable catalog for quasar population work, with explicit separation between fitted, quality-selected, and mass-estimated subsets.
