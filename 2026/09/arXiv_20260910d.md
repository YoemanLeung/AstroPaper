## Key Findings

- Neutral outflows persist after star formation declines: quiescent stacks reach `v_out~310-570 km/s`. Low current SFR inflates mass loading, while geometry and sodium corrections affect inferred rates; stacking does not establish outflows in every galaxy. [2609.09459](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09459) Rion Oh.
- Environmental correlations require population controls. In TNG300, similar AGN fractions at fixed stellar mass coexist with an overall protocluster excess. Virgo III H I deficits extend to non-preselected members, but neither result isolates a universal environmental mechanism. [2609.09297](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09297) A. Traina; [2609.09253](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09253) M. Ramatsoku.
- LRD chemistry and continuum modeling support tests of massive stellar engines and heavy seeds, while a ULX analogy retains much larger black holes. These are conditional interpretations with different assumptions; the abundance measurements are not dynamical black-hole masses. [2609.09271](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09271) V. Kokorev; [2609.09274](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09274) Wendy Q. Sun; [2609.09302](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09302) Andrew King.
- Ionizing production, photon escape, and quenching probe different steps. SALSA has high ionizing efficiency and an apparent Lyalpha escape fraction; no LyC escape fraction is measured. RIGEL dwarfs continue forming stars for hundreds of Myr after reionization cuts off fresh supply. [2609.09281](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09281) Sofia Rojas-Ruiz; [2609.09267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09267) Yunwei Deng.
- Early dust growth and cosmic-noon obscured star formation remain unevenly constrained. The proposed dust transition relies mainly on inferred dust masses, while JELS far-IR/sub-mm stacks provide upper limits too weak to determine gas depletion times. [2609.10290](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10290) Denis Burgarella; [2609.10097](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10097) Malte Brinch.

## Neutral gas, quiescence, and environment

- [2609.09459](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09459) Rion Oh et al. `JWST Spectra Conclusively Show an Excess of Neutral Gas Outflows in Quiescent Galaxies at z=2-5`
  - **Question:** How do neutral outflows differ between quiescent and actively star-forming galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRSpec medium-resolution spectra and NIRCam photometry: 274 DJA/EMBER galaxies at `z=2-5`.
  - **Method:**
    - Stack spectra by redshift and star-formation class; subtract stellar Na I D absorption and fit the interstellar component.
    - Compare outflow energetics, recent star-formation histories, and emission-line diagnostics; test alternative classifications and mass cuts.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Quiescent stacks show `v_out~310-570 km/s` and `Mdot_out~9-30 M_sun/yr`; mass loading exceeds detected non-quiescent stacks by `2-4 dex`.
    - Current residual star formation alone struggles to power the outflows. AGN activity and shocks remain possible; heterogeneous selection, stacked incidence, and assumed radius/geometry/ionization limit interpretation.
  - **Position:** Adds resolved spectral blueshifts to the statistical Na I D approach of Sapori+2026; it does not measure spatially resolved outflow structure.

- [2609.09297](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09297) A. Traina et al. `Exploring the AGN population in protoclusters: results from the TNG300 simulation and comparison with observations`
  - **Question:** Does a protocluster environment increase AGN activity independently of host stellar mass?
  - **Data:**
    - TNG300: 280 protoclusters traced from present-day clusters with `M200,c>10^14 M_sun`, across `z=0-6`.
  - **Method:**
    - Reconstruct descendant-selected membership with merger trees.
    - Apply common AGN cuts and compare stellar-mass distributions, luminosity functions, and black-hole accretion-rate density with the field.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The overall AGN fraction is enhanced by more than a factor of two in relevant selections, but is broadly similar at fixed stellar mass.
    - Protoclusters contribute up to `~50%` of simulated black-hole accretion-rate density at `z~6`. Earlier massive-galaxy assembly largely explains the enhancement; this is a simulation and membership-dependent result.
  - **Position:** Provides a host-population control for interpreting observed protocluster AGN excesses.

- [2609.09253](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09253) M. Ramatsoku et al. `Virgo Filaments VII: MeerKAT HI-imaging of the VirgoIII filament`
  - **Question:** How widespread are H I depletion and morphological disturbance in the Virgo III filament?
  - **Data:**
    - MeerKAT: 15 targeted pointings at a median distance of `~30 Mpc`; 80 H I sources over `500-3000 km/s`.
  - **Method:**
    - Build multi-resolution cubes, identify sources with SoFiA-2, and validate optical counterparts.
    - Compare H I fractions with field scaling relations and quantify image asymmetries.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Typical H I fraction offsets are `~0.45 dex` below field expectations. About `40%` of galaxies not preselected for deficiency lie more than `1 sigma` below the reference relations.
    - Asymmetry shows no strong monotonic dependence on filament-spine distance. Three nearby H I clouds may trace interactions; targeted pointings do not provide a volume-complete filament census.
  - **Position:** Extends Virgo-filament work with a resolved H I atlas and xGASS/Pan+2023 field comparisons.

## Reionization, faint sources, and early dust

- [2609.09281](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09281) Sofia Rojas-Ruiz et al. `Chasing Cosmic Reionization: An Extremely Faint Highly Magnified Source at z=5.66 with high xi_ion`
  - **Question:** How efficiently does an extremely faint lensed source produce ionizing photons?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRSpec IFU: SALSA behind Abell 2744 at `z=5.66`; adopted magnification `mu>100` gives `M_UV>-13.6`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure Halpha, [O III], and Lyalpha; infer Hbeta assuming negligible dust and use strong-line abundance diagnostics.
    - Combine recombination emission and UV continuum to estimate ionizing efficiency.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Find `log10(xi_ion/[Hz erg^-1])=25.49 (+0.09/-0.08)` and `12+log(O/H)=7.43+/-0.09`.
    - The apparent Lyalpha escape fraction is `0.39+/-0.14`, conditional on dust and aperture assumptions. This is not LyC escape; lens-model and differential-magnification uncertainties affect intrinsic properties.
  - **Position:** Extends recombination-line constraints into the very faint galaxy regime without establishing its population-wide reionization contribution.

- [2609.09267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09267) Yunwei Deng et al. `RIGEL: Ultra-faint dwarf galaxy diversity shaped by inhomogeneous cosmic reionization`
  - **Question:** How does patchy reionization shape the surviving diversity of ultra-faint dwarfs?
  - **Data:**
    - Eight RIGEL radiation-magnetohydrodynamic zoom simulations with THESAN radiation fields and present-day halo masses `~10^9 M_sun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Follow ionization-front arrival, self-shielded gas, star formation, enrichment, and gas-loss channels.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Reionization first suppresses diffuse gas supply; retained gas fuels star formation for several hundred Myr. Less than `40%` of the initial gas remains within the halo Lagrangian volume after `500 Myr`.
    - Halo mass at reionization predicts retention and subsequent enrichment; final stellar masses span nearly two dex. The faintest simulated systems remain too metal-poor, with dedicated Pop III enrichment absent.
  - **Position:** Connects reionization timing to fossil dwarf properties through explicit gas evolution, rather than assuming instantaneous quenching.

- [2609.10290](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10290) Denis Burgarella et al. `From stardust to interstellar grain growth in the first galaxies: a cosmological transition in dust evolution near z ~ 8.9`
  - **Question:** Is there a transition from stellar dust production to interstellar grain growth in the first galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST spectrophotometry plus ALMA/NOEMA measurements and limits on early dust masses and attenuation.
  - **Method:**
    - Use censored change-point inference and compare dust/metal evolution models with and without Pop III enrichment.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Dust masses and dust-to-stellar mass ratios favor a break near `z~8.9`, about `570 Myr` after the Big Bang; UV attenuation alone does not require a break.
    - Efficient grain growth above a characteristic metallicity can explain the transition. Much of the dust-mass evidence is SED-derived; Pop III stars are not required, and the mechanism remains model-dependent.
  - **Position:** Frames the early dust deficit as a testable population transition rather than a direct detection of a universal growth threshold.

## Black-hole growth and competing LRD interpretations

- [2609.09266](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09266) Masafusa Onoue et al. `SHELLQs. Black Hole Mass and Eddington Ratio Distributions of Intermediate-Luminosity Quasars at 6<z<7`
  - **Question:** How rapidly do intermediate-luminosity quasars grow their black holes at the end of reionization?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRSpec and Subaru/MOIRCS: 21 SHELLQs quasars at `6.07<z<6.90`; 27 objects after adding earlier mass estimates.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit Balmer or Mg II lines and continua for single-epoch virial masses.
    - Compare with luminosity-matched quasars at `z~1.3` and test an Eddington-dependent calibration.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - New measurements span `log10(M_BH/M_sun)=7.2-9.4`. Relative median offsets are about `-0.4 dex` in mass and `+0.2 dex` in Eddington ratio.
    - The near/super-Eddington fraction changes from `11%` to `22%` under the alternative calibration. These are observed-sample distributions; intrinsic selection-corrected demographics are deferred.
  - **Position:** Bridges luminous early quasars and faint JWST AGN, with explicit single-epoch calibration sensitivity.

- [2609.09271](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09271) V. Kokorev et al. `The Ashes of Supermassive Stars: Globular Cluster-like Aluminum Enhancement in Little Red Dots`
  - **Question:** Do LRD gas abundances require an unusual high-temperature enrichment source?
  - **Data:**
    - SPURS JWST/NIRSpec medium-grating UV spectra of four bright LRDs at `z~7`.
  - **Method:**
    - Infer Mg, Al, Fe, and Si columns from absorption; test saturation, covering, and ionization.
    - Compare corrected abundance patterns with hot hydrogen-burning models and globular-cluster chemistry.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Gas is strongly Mg-poor and Al-rich; the manuscript gives approximately `-1 dex` in [Mg/Fe] and `>~+0.5 dex` in [Al/Fe].
    - Fully convective supermassive-star models reproduce the pattern at temperatures around `75 MK`. This supports recent unusual enrichment; it does not directly identify a surviving star or measure a black-hole mass.
  - **Position:** Adds chemical evidence complementary to LRD continuum and wind models, connecting the problem to globular-cluster abundance anomalies.

- [2609.09274](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09274) Wendy Q. Sun et al. `Overmassive No More: The Case for Little Red Dots Hosting Black Hole Seeds as Massive as Single Supermassive Stars`
  - **Question:** Can a pseudo-photosphere interpretation yield LRD central masses compatible with heavy seeds?
  - **Data:**
    - Host-subtracted stacks of 117 LRDs, atmosphere models, absorption velocities, and variability constraints.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit optical/near-IR continua; combine inferred radius and gravity with super-Eddington, escape-speed, and dynamical-time arguments.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Typical fitted temperatures are `~4200-4800 K` and radii `~700-2000 au`; the methods converge on typical central-engine masses `~10^4-10^5 M_sun`.
    - The agreement is conditional on pseudo-photospheres and associated wind/variability assumptions. It does not establish that classical virial estimators fail for all broad-line AGN.
  - **Position:** Extends the Sun+2026 host-subtraction framework into alternative central-engine mass diagnostics.

- [2609.09265](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09265) Olivia Curtis et al. `A Spectral Framework for Testing the Quasi-Star Hypothesis in Little Red Dots I: Weighing LRDs by Their Super-Eddington Luminosity Ratios---No Signs of Overmassive Black Holes`
  - **Question:** Are absorption-rich LRD spectra consistent with black holes embedded in quasi-star envelopes?
  - **Data:**
    - Four primary absorption-rich LRDs plus 82 archival sources; TLUSTY atmospheres, Prospector host fits, and MESA-QUEST envelopes.
  - **Method:**
    - Infer the electron-scattering Eddington luminosity ratio from fitted temperature and gravity.
    - Map this ratio into quasi-star models and compare predicted escape speeds with absorption-line winds.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Primary objects yield `phi~4-299` and envelope masses `~700-27000 M_sun`; envelope masses must not be relabeled as black-hole masses.
    - The highest ratio lies at the atmosphere-grid boundary and beyond the envelope simulations. Proposed black-hole doubling times of `~0.05-3.5 Myr` depend on the quasi-star framework.
  - **Position:** Provides falsifiable quasi-star consistency tests while leaving dynamic atmospheres and the inferred evolutionary sequence to further work.

- [2609.09302](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09302) Andrew King `Little Dots: the ULX Analogy`
  - **Question:** Can super-Eddington accretion viewed off-axis explain Little Dot ionization cones?
  - **Data:**
    - Published extended ionization-cone observations and a theoretical analogy with ULXs and SS433.
  - **Method:**
    - Relate beaming geometry to mass supply and separate anisotropic hard X-rays from approximately isotropic soft emission.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - A model with black holes around `10^8 M_sun` and strongly super-Eddington mass supply remains viable for the discussed objects.
    - This differs physically from low-mass pseudo-photosphere interpretations; cone geometry tests orientation, not a population-wide mass measurement.
  - **Position:** Offers an alternative to heavy-seed interpretations based on scaled-up supercritical accretion phenomenology.

- [2609.10319](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10319) Krzysztof Lisiecki et al. `Extending the Little Red Dot population at intermediate redshift with VIPERS`
  - **Question:** Can intermediate-redshift LRD-like sources connect local analogs with the high-redshift population?
  - **Data:**
    - VIPERS spectra, multi-band photometry, HSC morphology, and an X-ray-detected source: 13 LRD candidates plus one XRD.
  - **Method:**
    - Select continuum slopes and broad lines, assess compactness and number densities, and model the XRD with CIGALE.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The main demographic interpretation uses `0.5<z<1.75`; the additional `1.75<z<2.6` bin is treated only as an incomplete lower limit in the manuscript.
    - Only two objects have usable environments, both underdense. HSC-unresolved morphology does not confirm the smallest LRD sizes; the XRD admits a radiatively efficient disk interpretation.
  - **Position:** Extends LRD-like selection to intermediate epochs while exposing resolution, line-coverage, and population-comparison limits.

## Star formation and morphology at intermediate redshift

- [2609.10097](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10097) Malte Brinch et al. `The JWST Emission Line Survey (JELS): Multi-wavelength properties of Paschen line-emitters at Cosmic Noon`
  - **Question:** What do Paschen-selected galaxies reveal about burstiness and obscured growth at cosmic noon?
  - **Data:**
    - JELS JWST/NIRCam F466N/F470N: 77 Pa-alpha emitters at `z~1.50` and 31 Pa-beta emitters at `z~2.65`, with multiwavelength COSMOS data.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare line and SED SFRs, infer attenuation from EAZY fits, and stack far-IR/sub-mm data with weighted medians and bootstrap uncertainties.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Most emitters have elevated short-timescale relative to SED-averaged SFRs; line selection favors high equivalent width and high sSFR.
    - No stack beyond MIRI exceeds `3 sigma`. ALMA Band 4 limits are `SFR_IR<53` and `<44 M_sun/yr`; gas limits do not constrain depletion times.
  - **Position:** Provides a Paschen-line view of bursty star formation, with obscured activity still limited by continuum sensitivity.

- [2609.10532](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10532) Manish Kataria et al. `The kinematics of tadpole galaxies at intermediate redshift z ~ 0.4 - 1.5`
  - **Question:** Can strongly asymmetric tadpole galaxies retain coherent gas kinematics?
  - **Data:**
    - VLT/MUSE [O II] spectroscopy and HST/JWST imaging of 20 HUDF tadpoles at `z~0.4-1.5`.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare ionized-gas velocity gradients, kinematic position angles, optical morphology, and spatial centroids.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Ordered-motion evidence appears in 13 of 20 objects. Among 12 with constrained kinematic position angles, median misalignment is `12.2 degrees`.
    - Morphological asymmetry can coexist with a coherent gas axis. Velocity gradients alone do not prove rotational support, and [O II] weights bright star-forming regions.
  - **Position:** Provides an observational caution against equating morphological disturbance with globally disordered dynamics.

## Tools for gas spectroscopy and reconstruction

- [2609.09329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09329) Annalisa De Cia et al. `The future of high-resolution UV spectroscopy: Science with a UV Echelle spectrograph on the Habitable Worlds Observatory, or a dedicated mission`
  - **Question:** What new baryon-cycle measurements would high-resolution UV spectroscopy enable?
  - **Data:**
    - PEGASUS concept science cases for HWO or a dedicated mission, including ISM, CGM, and IGM absorption.
  - **Method:**
    - Translate line diagnostics and source-access requirements into wavelength, resolving-power, and sensitivity goals.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The concept targets `R~100000` over `90-400 nm`, within a broader resolution range and with an optical extension foreseen.
    - Higher sensitivity and resolved absorption components would expand gas/metal diagnostics. These are proposed capabilities, not delivered instrument performance or an approved survey.
  - **Position:** Provides a requirements reference for future UV baryon-cycle work.

- [2609.09307](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09307) Jens Stucker et al. `JZ-FMM: GPU-native differentiable N-body simulations with the Fast Multipole Method`
  - **Question:** Can differentiable small-scale gravity calculations support nonlinear reconstruction?
  - **Data:**
    - GPU-native JZ-FMM benchmarks and a controlled tidally stripped satellite reconstruction experiment.
  - **Method:**
    - Differentiate Fast Multipole Method forces; test gradients and optimize initial satellite conditions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The controlled experiment recovers initial conditions using tidal debris and a strong initial halo prior. Local minima multiply with evolution time.
    - Reliable gradients need more conservative numerical settings than ordinary trajectories. Current isolated boundaries require further integration for cosmological reconstruction.
  - **Position:** Extends differentiable dynamics toward small scales, while retaining prior dependence and optimization limitations.
