## Key Findings

- Galaxy overdensity and strong H I absorption need not coincide: the confirmed structure at `z=3.079` and the strongest absorber at `z~3.01` are distinct. A hidden protocluster is one possible absorber origin, not a confirmed identification. [2609.04349](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04349) Kentaro Koretomo.
- The Spiderweb core already contains gas-poor quiescent systems whose mean histories indicate rapid quenching about `500 Myr` before observation. The gas constraint depends on dust temperature and gas-to-dust conversion. [2609.05285](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05285) C. D'Eugenio.
- Absorption-based gas diagnostics require attention to visibility and sample construction: Na I D flow detections depend on dust, ionization, and geometry, while inferred ionizing escape depends on stacking and the simulated sightline library. [2609.04327](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04327) Hassen M. Yesuf; [2609.04324](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04324) John Trevino.
- In Euclid-like mocks, persistence can distort large-scale clustering even when its most recognizable signature occurs near `100 arcsec`. Stellar-mask mismatches mainly affect smaller angular scales in the tested survey configuration. [2609.05155](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05155) Euclid Collaboration.
- Rapid nuclear growth does not guarantee strong resolved ionized feedback: Ton S180 has a much weaker kiloparsec-scale ionized outflow than its unresolved nuclear outflow. [2609.04317](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04317) Pierpaolo Condo.

## Protoclusters, absorption, and early quenching

- [2609.04349](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04349) Kentaro Koretomo et al. `H I Absorbers as Beacons of Hidden Structure at z~3: Multi-Component, Metal-Rich Absorption System near a Protocluster`
  - **Question:** Does a galaxy-selected protocluster coincide with strong absorption along a background quasar sightline?
  - **Data:**
    - Subaru/HSC selection and FOCAS spectroscopy: 42 LAEs and one LBG confirmed; 12 galaxies define the protocluster at `z=3.079`.
    - Background quasar at `z=3.09`, with FOCAS resolving power `R~1500`.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare galaxy redshifts with H I absorption; decompose the strongest absorber and model its ionization with CLOUDY.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The strongest absorber lies at `z~3.01`, about `60 cMpc` from the protocluster, and spans `~2000 km/s` across five fitted components.
    - One component reaches `[O/H]=+1.19 (+0.91/-0.78)`. A hidden structure and a single-galaxy inflow/outflow complex remain alternatives.
    - The protocluster sightline has `tau_eff=0.214+/-0.005`; scatter in the expected relation prevents a firm conclusion about its overall H I supply.
  - **Position:** Extends the Toshikawa+2024 overdensity selection with direct galaxy-absorber comparison; higher-resolution spectra and obscured-host searches are needed.

- [2609.05285](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05285) C. D'Eugenio et al. `Rapid quenching and early gas depletion in the core of a galaxy protocluster at z=2.2`
  - **Question:** How rapidly did Spiderweb core galaxies quench, and how much cold gas remains?
  - **Data:**
    - HST/F160W and G141, JWST Pa-beta narrowband imaging, and ALMA `1.2 mm` continuum for quiescent candidates at `z=2.16`.
  - **Method:**
    - Revise the quiescent classification and stack spectra and imaging to constrain star-formation histories, residual star formation, and molecular gas.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Mean histories favor roughly steady progenitor star formation followed by rapid quenching about `500 Myr` before observation.
    - Dust-continuum nondetections constrain `M_mol/M_star` to `less than ~7%` for the robust sample, conditional on dust temperature and gas-to-dust ratio.
    - Exclude star-forming ID 443; conservatively omit AGN-hosting ID 588 from the robust sample. No significant age difference separates the remaining AGN and non-AGN subsets.
  - **Position:** Follows Naufal+2024 and connects quiescent systems to the neighboring H-alpha-emitter gas-depletion trend of Perez-Martinez+2025.

## Gas cycling and ionizing-photon escape

- [2609.04324](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04324) John Trevino et al. `Recovering Ionizing Photon Escape and Galaxy Scaling Relations in the LzLCS via Si II and C II Absorption Lines and Mock Spectra from a Radiation-Hydrodynamic Simulation`
  - **Question:** Can low-ionization UV absorption recover ionizing escape without direct Lyman-continuum detections?
  - **Data:**
    - HST/COS stacks of 58 LzLCS+ galaxies; 22,500 mock spectra from one radiation-hydrodynamic galaxy of mass `~10^9 M_sun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare Si II 1260 and C II 1334 across three mass bins and mean, median, and weighted stacks.
    - Select the 30 lowest-chi-squared mock spectra per stack and infer line properties and escape fractions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Best matches achieve `chi^2<1` across the mass bins and largely come from UV-bright starburst phases.
    - Matched sightlines reproduce lower average escape fractions in higher-mass stacks; mock-profile measurements reduce low-S/N line-measurement biases.
    - Large uncertainties, stacking effects, and the single simulated mass limit transfer to individual reionization-era galaxies.
  - **Position:** Calibrates a route from rest-UV absorption to ionizing escape; broader simulation libraries are needed for high-redshift application.

- [2609.04327](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04327) Hassen M. Yesuf et al. `Widespread Inflows Reveal Baryonic Cycling in Star-forming and Quiescent Galaxies`
  - **Question:** Are detected inflows and outflows distinct populations or phases of a common baryon cycle?
  - **Data:**
    - DESI spectroscopy of `~30,000` galaxies, using interstellar Na I D absorption and stellar-population diagnostics.
  - **Method:**
    - Separate stellar and interstellar absorption and classify flow states.
    - Compare age-controlled subsamples in mass, environment, structure, star formation, and metallicity.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Detected inflows reach `~100 km/s` and occur in `20-50%` of the sample, with older/quiescent systems favoring inflow signatures.
    - Star-forming systems more often show bound outflows, consistent with recycling on `~0.5 Gyr` timescales.
    - The absence of clear pristine dilution favors enriched/recycled gas; dust, ionization, and geometry also change which flows are visible.
  - **Position:** Provides a population-level framework for interpreting absorption-selected baryon cycling; inferred flow incidence is not a geometry-independent accretion census.

- [2609.04320](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04320) Vidhi Tailor et al. `Resolved Dust-Gas-Metallicity relations in nearby spiral galaxies`
  - **Question:** How robust are resolved dust-to-gas and dust-to-metal trends to molecular-gas conversion assumptions?
  - **Data:**
    - Dust, H I, CO, and metallicity maps for 10 nearby spirals at `0.6-2.3 kpc` resolution.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare constant and metallicity-dependent CO-to-H2 conversions, then apply a hybrid prescription for CO-bright and CO-dark regimes.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - No single conversion describes the full sample. Under the hybrid prescription, dust-to-gas ratio rises with metallicity.
    - The dust-to-metal ratio is approximately constant at `log(DMR)=-0.53+/-0.13`, corresponding to about `30%` of metals in dust.
  - **Position:** Compares Bolatto+2013, Amorin+2016, and Schruba+2012 prescriptions; useful context for the conversion assumptions behind dust-inferred gas masses.

## AGN environments and massive-seed growth

- [2609.04317](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04317) Pierpaolo Condo et al. `Super-Eddington Accretion and Early-Stage Feedback in Ton S180`
  - **Question:** Does super-Eddington accretion in Ton S180 drive strong host-scale ionized feedback?
  - **Data:**
    - VLT/MUSE integral-field spectroscopy of the nearby narrow-line Seyfert 1 Ton S180.
  - **Method:**
    - Model and subtract the unresolved nucleus, then fit host emission-line and stellar kinematics.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Estimated `log(M_BH/M_sun)=6.5-7.7` implies an accretion-rate ratio of `4.1-980` under the adopted estimators.
    - The resolved `~2 kpc` outflow reaches `~340 km/s` with `Mdot_out~4.2e-4 M_sun/yr`; the nuclear component reaches `~1140 km/s` with `Mdot_out>1.5 M_sun/yr`.
    - Weak coupling or different activity episodes can explain the contrast; this constrains the observed ionized phase.
  - **Position:** A resolved local comparison for rapid black-hole growth; it does not establish the total multiphase feedback budget.

- [2609.04315](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04315) Thomas Bohn et al. `JWST/NIRSpec Reveals Diverse Nuclear Environments in Dwarf Galaxies Hosting AGN`
  - **Question:** How diverse are the central gas phases of dwarf galaxies hosting AGN?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRSpec IFU observations of four dwarfs with `M_star<10^9.5 M_sun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Map emission lines and kinematics; fit near-infrared continua and H2 excitation.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - More than 100 lines are detected across the sample, including coronal species above `300 eV` ionization potential.
    - Three nuclei show both PAH and coronal emission within `100 pc`; nuclear PAH equivalent widths are nevertheless suppressed.
    - Hot H2 masses span `1-20 M_sun` within `300 pc`, with mixed excitation and diverse spatial/velocity structures.
  - **Position:** Resolves the coexistence of AGN and molecular/PAH emission; the hot H2 component is not a measurement of the full cold-gas reservoir.

- [2609.04892](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04892) P. A. Solar et al. `Quantifying collision-driven mass loss in supermassive star formation: the role of stellar structure and accretion`
  - **Question:** How much can stellar collisions reduce the growth of supermassive-star black-hole seeds?
  - **Data:**
    - Post-processed 3D hydrodynamic formation simulations with polytropic, accreting-protostar, and stellar-evolution structures.
  - **Method:**
    - Apply analytic collision mass-loss prescriptions and compare stellar-structure and accretion assumptions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Cumulative loss varies from roughly `10-25%` or less in compact configurations to `30-40%` or more in extended cases.
    - Some models still produce central objects of order `10^3-10^4 M_sun`; alternative prescriptions can strongly suppress growth.
    - Post-processing and uncertain protostellar envelopes prevent a unique seed-formation efficiency.
  - **Position:** Compares Ramirez-Galeano+2025 and Roman-Garza+2026 mass-loss prescriptions; implications for LRD progenitors remain conditional on a collision-built seed pathway.

## Survey catalogs and clustering diagnostics

- [2609.05155](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05155) Euclid Collaboration et al. `Euclid. A two-point correlation approach to diagnosing star-related systematics in the Euclid spectroscopic survey`
  - **Question:** Can star-related contamination be distinguished from genuine galaxy clustering?
  - **Data:**
    - EuclidLargeMocks spectroscopic galaxies over `330 deg^2`, combined with Gaia and 2MASS stars.
  - **Method:**
    - Inject persistence interlopers and mismatched stellar masks.
    - Measure Landy-Szalay angular auto/cross-correlations and the spatial two-point function.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Persistence produces a characteristic feature near `100 arcsec`; contamination of `~10%` is detectable in the tested DR1-like mocks.
    - Persistence can alter large-scale clustering and BAO shape. Mask mismatches produce strong small-scale signatures but little large-scale impact in the tested configuration.
    - The persistence model simplifies decay, saturation, and detector variation; these are simulated forecasts, not measurements of residual Euclid contamination.
  - **Position:** Provides a null-test design for spectroscopic selection systematics; validation with survey data and end-to-end simulations remains necessary.

- [2609.04322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04322) Alessandro Peca et al. `AGN-DB: A Unified Multi-Wavelength Database of Active Galactic Nuclei`
  - **Question:** How can heterogeneous AGN catalogs be combined without losing measurement provenance?
  - **Data:**
    - AGN-DB v1.0: 118 public catalogs, `8,128,739` unique sources, and measurements in 66 bands.
  - **Method:**
    - Use Lyra Bayesian association with positional, density, and photometric information.
    - Preserve input measurements and classifications alongside representative merged values.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The reference sample retains `7,790,749` AGN/candidates; `6,816,620` have at least one AGN-type classification.
    - Spectroscopic redshifts exist for `3,532,035` sources. Recovery reaches `96.5%` for Stripe 82X and `99.0%` for C-COSMOS within `5 arcsec`.
  - **Position:** A useful association and provenance resource; its heterogeneous source surveys require a separately constructed selection function for population or environmental statistics.

- [2609.04351](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04351) Z. Shao et al. `Extending the Stellar-to-Halo Mass Relation to Dwarf Galaxies with DESI DR1`
  - **Question:** Can the stellar-to-halo mass relation be constrained directly into the dwarf regime?
  - **Data:**
    - DESI DR1 Bright Galaxy Survey at `0.01<z<0.2`, divided into 12 stellar-mass samples, with lensing and group information.
  - **Method:**
    - Jointly fit projected clustering, galaxy-galaxy lensing, and satellite occupation with halo occupation models.
    - Combine inferred mean halo masses with the stellar mass function to constrain the stellar-to-halo relation.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The inferred slope steepens from `0.32+/-0.06` above Milky Way mass to `2.08+/-0.21` in the dwarf regime.
    - Scatter increases from `0.17+/-0.02 dex` to `0.68 (+0.21/-0.33) dex` near LMC-like systems.
  - **Position:** Uses satellite occupation to disentangle central and satellite contributions; the broad dwarf scatter provides context for orbital-history effects.

## Environmental history and merger timing

- [2609.04385](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04385) Sophia Um et al. `Linking orbital history to the quenching of isolated dwarf galaxies`
  - **Question:** Does present-day isolation erase the environmental history of quenched dwarfs?
  - **Data:**
    - IllustrisTNG50 central dwarfs with `M_star~10^7-10^9 M_sun` and their orbital histories.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure closest past approach to a neighbor above `10^10 M_sun` and compare quenching, halo mass, circular velocity, and size.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Past pericentric separation predicts dwarf properties better than current environment alone.
    - A transition near `R_peri~0.3 cMpc` separates predominantly backsplash from non-backsplash quenched systems in the simulation.
    - Non-backsplash quenching is consistent with internal feedback or cosmic-web stripping; the mechanisms are not uniquely separated.
  - **Position:** Adds orbital history to empirical dwarf-halo interpretation and predicts extended quenched systems for deeper imaging surveys.

- [2609.04560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04560) Shaoze Geng et al. `Star Formation Evolution in Galaxy Pairs: Constraints from Morphological Disturbances and Recent Star Formation Histories`
  - **Question:** How do tidal disturbance and stellar-age diagnostics locate galaxies along an interaction sequence?
  - **Data:**
    - 5090 star-forming galaxies in SDSS spiral-spiral major-merger pairs at `0.02<z<0.12`; DESI Legacy imaging and Galaxy Zoo DESI classifications.
    - TNG100 histories for 12 recently interacting pairs.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare shape asymmetry and pair separation with SFR, H-alpha, H-delta absorption, and Dn4000.
    - Use mock evolution to interpret the lag between current and intermediate-age star-formation indicators.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Strongly disturbed close pairs show the strongest current star formation; H-delta enhancement peaks at `~50-100 kpc`.
    - The pattern is consistent with a burst near close passage followed by declining SFR while tidal features and `0.1-1 Gyr` stellar signatures persist.
  - **Position:** Links morphology to interaction timing; the small simulated comparison sample supports an evolutionary interpretation without uniquely dating individual observed pairs.
