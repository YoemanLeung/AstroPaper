## Key Findings

- Emission and absorption trace different gas subsets; missing Lyalpha does not mean a missing galaxy ([2609.20938](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20938) Davide Tornotti; [2609.25221](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25221) Grecco A. Oyarzun).
- Cosmic-noon abundances require enrichment-pattern and emitting-phase corrections ([2609.22488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22488) Nathalie A. Korhonen Cuestas; [2609.27081](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27081) Caroline von Raesfeld).
- Reionization scales mix topology, density, and ionization; connected bubbles and sightline distances differ ([2609.28390](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28390) Meredith Neyer; [2609.26483](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26483) Hamish Anderson).
- Early primitive and quenched candidates retain calibration and membership uncertainties ([2609.24589](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24589) Mingyu Li; [2609.21337](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21337) Scott C. Chapman).
- Better decomposition and faster SED fitting enable assumption tests without removing model uncertainty ([2609.30232](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30232) Oscar Veenema; [2609.30145](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30145) Amanda Stoffers).

## The galaxy-gas connection across scales

- [2609.20938](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20938) Davide Tornotti et al. `Cosmic web Lyalpha emission in a sample of overdense regions`
  - **Question:** How does diffuse Lyalpha emission trace ordinary overdense cosmic-web structures?
  - **Data:**
    - Ultra-deep MUSE MUDF/MXDF observations at `z~3-5`, with `41` selected LAE overdensities.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify friends-of-friends overdensities; extract diffuse emission outside masked CGM regions; align and stack filament axes.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Diffuse emission is detected in `26/35` usable systems after skyline exclusions, with emitting covering fraction `~0.20-0.25`. Brighter overdensities reach a surface-brightness plateau near `2e-19 erg/s/cm^2/arcsec^2`.
    - This measures the detectable, denser emitting structures rather than all intergalactic gas; emission/absorption incidence agreement does not establish one-to-one counterparts.
  - **Position:** Extends Bacon+2021 cosmic-web emission work beyond individual extreme nebulae.

- [2609.25221](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25221) Grecco A. Oyarzun et al. `The KAGG survey: KCWI Analysis of Gas around Galaxies at z~3-5`
  - **Question:** How are DLA metallicity and Lyalpha-selected galaxy environments connected?
  - **Data:**
    - KAGG Keck/KCWI: `17` quasar fields with `36` DLAs at `z~3-5`; MAGG comparisons.
  - **Method:**
    - Search emission lines with completeness tests; distinguish candidate counterparts, group associations, and larger-scale neighbors.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - KAGG finds `7` primary DLA LAE candidates. The combined counterpart metallicity-impact-parameter relation is tentative at `1.8 sigma`; broader group trends use a different, overlapping sample.
    - All five known [CII] emitters in the sample lack detected Lyalpha. Absence of a Lyalpha counterpart therefore cannot be read as absence of a galaxy.
  - **Position:** Connects DLA gas properties to emission-selected environments while quantifying tracer incompleteness.

- [2609.26785](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26785) Saloni Deepak et al. `The BRIDGE Survey. I. Metallicities and Physical Properties of Gas Probing the Circumgalactic Medium at Cosmic Noon`
  - **Question:** How did the metallicity of HI-selected diffuse gas evolve through cosmic noon?
  - **Data:**
    - BRIDGE: `105` absorbers at `1<=z<=2.2`, with `16.4<log N_HI<20.3`, from HST and high-resolution optical quasar spectra.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit ionic columns with Bayesian Cloudy models; compare with lower-redshift CCC and higher-redshift KODIAQ-Z samples.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Metallicity evolves strongly into cosmic noon and much less toward lower redshift. A high-column metallicity floor near `[X/H]~-2` coexists with metal-poor lower-column gas.
    - Changing the UV background shifts inferred metallicity by `~0.16 dex` on average. Upper limits and ionization assumptions matter; CGM-like density classification does not establish an individual host association.
  - **Position:** Fills the redshift gap between existing absorber metallicity surveys and separates enrichment by HI column.

- [2609.25455](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25455) Mariko Kubo et al. `PEGASUS (Pfs Emission-line GAlaxy SUrvey with Subaru): I. Three-dimensional large-scale structures at z > 1 probed with emission-line galaxies`
  - **Question:** What does dense emission-line spectroscopy reveal about three-dimensional structure?
  - **Data:**
    - PEGASUS Subaru/PFS observations of `9917` HSC narrowband emitters over `~1.8 deg^2` in COSMOS.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure spectroscopic redshifts and construct density maps and group associations, especially at `z=1.19-1.60`.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Groups lie at filament intersections, and spectroscopy reveals voids hidden by projected imaging. Confirmation fractions span `86-99%` across target subsets.
    - These are success rates among observed emission-line targets, not completeness for all galaxies. Restricted volume, emission-line selection, and preliminary NIR-arm performance limit demographic interpretation.
  - **Position:** Demonstrates the structural gain from following narrowband maps with dense PFS redshifts.

- [2609.20929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20929) Mandy C. Chen et al. `From IFS Maps to 3D Structure I: Constraining Geometry in the Circumgalactic Medium`
  - **Question:** Can resolved line kinematics constrain the line-of-sight thickness of CGM nebulae?
  - **Data:**
    - Eleven MUSE/JWST nebulae and mock maps of isotropic turbulent velocity fields.
  - **Method:**
    - Relate line-of-sight linewidth to sky-plane centroid dispersion; vary emitting depth, clump sampling, spectral slope, and beam smoothing.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Observed dispersion ratios `~1-2.5` imply depths `~0.1-5` times projected extent in the adopted calibration.
    - The relation assumes statistically isotropic, homogeneous turbulence and idealized geometry. Beam averaging hides much of the clump configuration, so it is not a direct three-dimensional reconstruction.
  - **Position:** Provides a geometric interpretation of IFS variance before using projected maps to infer turbulence.

## Chemical diagnostics and the baryon budget

- [2609.20921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20921) I. Langan et al. `ALMA Chemical Evolution (ACE) survey: The gas fundamental metallicity relation at cosmic noon`
  - **Question:** Does molecular gas add predictive power to the cosmic-noon mass-metallicity relation?
  - **Data:**
    - ACE ALMA CO measurements and optical nebular/stellar constraints for subsolar-metallicity star-forming galaxies.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit the gas fundamental metallicity relation and compare with gas-regulator models and alternative CO conversions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - No significant secondary metallicity dependence on molecular mass is found; intrinsic scatter is `~0.11 dex`, comparable with the SFR-based relation.
    - A representative mass-loading factor `eta~4` is inferred from regulator modeling, not measured molecular outflows. The result retains CO-conversion and sample-size limitations.
  - **Position:** Uses a coordinated gas-and-metal dataset to test whether local scaling relations transfer to cosmic noon.

- [2609.22488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22488) Nathalie A. Korhonen Cuestas et al. `CECILIA: Using Flexible Photoionization Models to Accurately Measure Abundances without Auroral Lines`
  - **Question:** When can photoionization fitting recover oxygen abundance without auroral lines?
  - **Data:**
    - CECILIA galaxies at `z~2.3` and local LBT abundance-calibration galaxies.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare Cue-inferred abundances with direct-method values while varying included emission lines.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - High-redshift subsolar S/O and Ar/O make Solar-scaled sulfur/argon constraints bias O/H. Strong-line-only fits retain low/high-metallicity branches; [NeIII] can help but needs broader validation.
    - For local abundance patterns, sulfur lines improve constraints. The useful line set depends on enrichment history, so one universal fitting recipe is unsafe.
  - **Position:** A calibration framework for distinguishing missing abundance physics from apparently precise metallicity inference.

- [2609.27081](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27081) Caroline von Raesfeld et al. `CECILIA: Multi-line Constraints on Excess Nebular Emission from Low-Ionization Gas at Cosmic Noon`
  - **Question:** What fraction of low-ionization emission escapes standard HII-region models?
  - **Data:**
    - CECILIA JWST/NIRSpec spectra of `19` star-forming galaxies at `z~2-3`.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit higher-ionization lines and direct-method abundances with Cue; inspect residual [SII]/[OI] and possible shock or diffuse-gas origins.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Models underproduce `48.0+/-5.1%` of [SII] and `55.0+/-6.1%` of [OI]. These are model-unexplained fractions, not spatially measured DIG fractions.
    - Diffuse gas is plausible but local DIG relations fail. Treating all [SII] as HII-region emission biases sulfur abundance high by typically `~0.06 dex`.
  - **Position:** Complements the CECILIA calibration study by separating abundance-pattern errors from additional emitting phases.

- [2609.22466](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22466) R. Henry Liu et al. `Model-Independent Measurement of Baryon Gas Fractions through Galaxy-Galaxy Lensing and the Kinematic Sunyaev-Zel'dovich Effect`
  - **Question:** Can gas redistribution be measured using matched lensing and kSZ filters?
  - **Data:**
    - DESI galaxies, HSC galaxy-galaxy lensing, and ACT/DESI kSZ measurements.
  - **Method:**
    - Apply a common aperture filter and ratio the gas-sensitive and total-matter observables; correct the CMB beam with simulations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The corrected BGS and lowest-redshift LRG bins show small-scale depletion relative to the cosmic mean, with detection significances `17.1` and `15.8`, respectively.
    - The observable is a filtered projected ionized-gas ratio. Beam compensation, velocity normalization, and neutral/stellar baryons limit a literal total-baryon interpretation; no tested simulation matches every scale.
  - **Position:** Reduces assumed halo-profile dependence while retaining explicit observational and gas-phase corrections.

- [2609.29955](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29955) Irene Shivaei et al. `PAHSPECS: JWST/MIRI Spectroscopy of PAHs at Cosmic Noon`
  - **Question:** How reliably does broad-band photometry recover PAH emission and grain properties at cosmic noon?
  - **Data:**
    - PAHSPECS JWST/MIRI MRS spectroscopy of five ASPECS CO/dust-selected main-sequence galaxies at `z~1.1` in the HUDF.
  - **Method:**
    - Decompose spectra with CAFE; compare individual PAH features with photometric SED fits and resolved continuum maps.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The `6.2`, `7.7`, and `11.3 micron` features occur in all five sources; `3.3 micron` is detected in two. Photometric fits recover the strong `6.2/7.7 micron` luminosities within `~20-25%`, while weaker-band estimates can be high by factors of a few.
    - Enhanced `6.2 micron` and weak `3.3 micron` equivalent widths suggest different neutral and ionized PAH size distributions. Resolved PAH/continuum variations require spectroscopy; the small, CO/dust-selected sample does not establish all-galaxy demographics.
  - **Position:** Extends ASPECS from integrated cold-gas inventories to spectroscopic dust and ISM diagnostics.

## Primitive galaxies, reionization, and early quenching

- [2609.24589](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24589) Mingyu Li et al. `LATED: JWST integral field spectroscopy of a galaxy caught in chemical infancy at z=4.8 behind Abell 2744`
  - **Question:** Can a photometrically selected candidate be confirmed as chemically primitive?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRSpec IFU and earlier MUSE data for LATED-1 at `z=4.80` behind Abell 2744.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure Balmer and marginal oxygen lines; test apertures and jackknifes; exclude the metal-rich branch using line limits.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The adopted strong-line calibration gives `12+log(O/H)=6.45`, or `~0.58%` solar.
    - The estimate extrapolates below the empirical calibration floor. HeII is undetected, so the source is an extremely metal-poor candidate without confirmed Population III stars.
  - **Position:** Demonstrates a practical selection-to-follow-up path and identifies the deeper abundance diagnostics still needed.

- [2609.28390](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28390) Meredith Neyer et al. `The Lumina Project: Morphology of Ionized Bubbles and Neutral Islands`
  - **Question:** How does reionization topology depend on the definition of an ionized bubble?
  - **Data:**
    - Lumina radiation-hydrodynamic simulation with volume `(500 cMpc)^3` and analysis grid `~0.39 cMpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Segment ionized volumes with a watershed; compare effective radii, sightline boundary distances, and shape statistics.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Volume-weighted effective radii grow from `~1 cMpc` at `z>~10` to `~10 cMpc` at `z~7-8`; connected-volume sizes exceed sightline-based sizes by factors of a few.
    - Most bubbles are prolate, while a few late connected structures dominate volume-weighted shape changes. Segmentation and resolution enter counts and morphology; different estimators should not be equated directly.
  - **Position:** Provides topology-aware interpretation for galaxy, Lyalpha, and future 21-cm comparisons.

- [2609.26483](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26483) Hamish Anderson et al. `Exploring the influence of reionisation-era galaxies on their local intergalactic medium using the Sherwood-Relics simulations`
  - **Question:** How do galaxies bias nearby Lyalpha-forest opacity near the end of reionization?
  - **Data:**
    - Sherwood-Relics patchy-reionization mocks at `5.4<z<6.1`, with ASPIRE-like galaxy and sightline geometry.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare opacity near mock [OIII] emitters against random regions while varying influence radius and sample realization.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Small radii probe denser, more opaque gas. Larger radii show a possible ionization bias, but simulated survey realizations overlap and KS differences are not significant.
    - Forecast discrimination requires roughly `4x` the sightline path length on small scales and at least `10x` on larger scales within this model; clustered faint sources remain degenerate with bright-galaxy effects.
  - **Position:** Shows why galaxy-forest comparisons require scale-dependent selection and adequate sightline statistics.

- [2609.28474](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28474) Richard Pan et al. `A sub-100 pc view at z~5 of a Multiply-Imaged Massive Quiescent Galaxy`
  - **Question:** Must early massive galaxies compactify before they stop forming stars?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRCam photometry of three lensed images of VENUS-CLJ0152-QG1 at photometric `z=5.18+/-0.15`.
  - **Method:**
    - Reconstruct source-plane structure and fit spatially binned stellar populations with lens-model uncertainties.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The two highly magnified images imply `M_star~3e10 M_sun`, low recent specific SFR, and half-light radii `~0.9-1.1 kpc`; favorable lensing reaches `~70 pc` resolution.
    - The extended structure permits quenching without contemporaneous compaction. Redshift and quiescence remain photometric inferences; obscured or past AGN activity is not excluded by the absence of an obvious nucleus.
  - **Position:** A resolved early-quenching candidate that tests how unresolved field samples bias structural conclusions.

- [2609.21337](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21337) Scott C. Chapman et al. `A large overdensity of LRD-like galaxies discovered by JWST in the SPT2349-56 protocluster at z=4.30`
  - **Question:** Does an extreme starburst protocluster also host a suppressed red population?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST imaging and deep ALMA constraints in the `z=4.30` SPT2349-56 protocluster field.
  - **Method:**
    - Select extremely red objects; inspect sizes, fit SEDs, and use millimeter nondetections to constrain hidden star formation.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Eleven red candidates concentrate in the core and are generally more extended than classical compact LRDs. ALMA limits favor below-main-sequence activity on average.
    - None yet has spectroscopic membership. Dust, AGN, and population-model degeneracies remain; apparent environmental quenching and any evolutionary link to LRDs require confirmation.
  - **Position:** Adds a potential early passive population to a structure previously dominated by dusty-starburst selections.

## AGN selection, feedback, and inference methods

- [2609.27130](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27130) Shobita Satyapal et al. `Search for High-Ionization Nebular Emission (SHINE). I. A Systematically Selected [Ne V] Sample at z > 3 with JWST/NIRSpec PRISM`
  - **Question:** Which early accretors are recovered by very-high-ionization selection?
  - **Data:**
    - Public JWST/NIRSpec PRISM spectra: `25` vetted [NeV] emitters at `z=3.059-9.444`.
  - **Method:**
    - Validate lines and compare luminosities, broad lines, diagnostic diagrams, imaging, and detection limits.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The luminous [NeV] sample overlaps only partly with conventional AGN selections and includes diverse host morphologies.
    - Current nondetections usually cannot exclude similar [NeV]/[NeIII] ratios in broad-line AGN or LRD samples. Heterogeneous archival targeting and sensitivity prevent intrinsic demographic inference.
  - **Position:** Expands the high-redshift AGN census through a complementary diagnostic rather than a complete selection.

- [2609.28632](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28632) Barbara Balmaverde et al. `The quest for high-redshift radio galaxies II. Discovery of the most powerful known radio galaxy at z=4.946`
  - **Question:** Can Lyman-break selection recover extremely powerful radio galaxies missed by ultra-steep-spectrum cuts?
  - **Data:**
    - Subaru/HSC imaging, VLT/FORS2 spectroscopy, and radio measurements from `74-4800 MHz` for TXS 2354+015.
  - **Method:**
    - Select an optical dropout associated with a radio source; identify Lyalpha and the weak NV doublet component; fit the radio spectral slope.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The redshift is `z=4.946+/-0.001` and rest-frame `P_500MHz=6.2e29 W/Hz`, the highest radio-galaxy power in the comparison assembled here. The spectrum has `alpha=0.94`, outside conventional ultra-steep selection.
    - Lyalpha has observed-frame `EW~900 Angstrom` and a slit-measured extent of `~6 kpc`; the nearly perpendicular slit can underestimate its full extent. NV overlaps a skyline but passes a local extraction test. The body identifies NV near `1240 Angstrom`; the `1640 Angstrom` label elsewhere is inconsistent.
    - Line contamination and stellar-population assumptions prevent a robust host mass. Radio power alone does not establish the surrounding overdensity or feedback efficiency.
  - **Position:** Extends the Lyman-break radio-galaxy searches of Capetti+2025 and motivates resolved gas and environment follow-up.

- [2609.30232](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30232) Oscar Veenema et al. `Breaking the Blend: A multi-tracer kinematic decomposition method for IFS data applied to disentangling the AGN outflow and circumnuclear ring in NGC 5728 with JWST`
  - **Question:** Can shared multi-line kinematics separate star-forming and outflow emission in an AGN host?
  - **Data:**
    - GATOS JWST integral-field data for NGC 5728: `12` mid-IR ionic lines spanning `4.49-25.89 micron`.
  - **Method:**
    - Constrain ring and bicone velocity fields and dispersions; fit component amplitudes in each spaxel and combine flux fractions across tracers.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Two components recover the star-forming ring and AGN bicone without coherent residual evidence for another component in this application.
    - An estimated `~62%` of the field-of-view `21 micron` luminosity is attributed to the AGN only if warm dust follows the inferred ionized-gas fractions. Transfer to other targets requires separable kinematics and suitable tracers.
  - **Position:** A physically constrained alternative to interpreting blended line widths as a single gas component.

- [2609.29782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29782) Rajsekhar Mohapatra et al. `How Magnetic Fields Regulate Cooling and Mixing in Turbulent Radiative Mixing Layers`
  - **Question:** How do magnetic fields change cooling at interfaces between moving hot and cold gas?
  - **Data:**
    - Three-dimensional magnetohydrodynamic mixing-layer simulations up to `1024 x 2048^2`, with aligned, transverse, and reversing fields.
  - **Method:**
    - Vary cooling time, magnetization, field geometry, and resolution; measure interface area, turbulent diffusion, enthalpy flux, and radiative losses.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Even initial hot-phase shear Alfven Mach number `~14` suppresses cooling and mass exchange by up to an order of magnitude compared with hydrodynamics. Magnetic tension reduces gas transport and interface folding.
    - Reversing fields alter morphology without restoring hydrodynamic mixing. MHD cooling continues to decline with increasing resolution, so the measured suppression is a lower limit in this setup, not a converged universal correction.
  - **Position:** Adds magnetic geometry and unresolved transport to radiative-mixing models used for winds, CGM clouds, and cluster filaments.

- [2609.30145](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30145) Amanda Stoffers et al. `CERIDWEN: Fast and Flexible GPU-Accelerated Stellar Population Inference`
  - **Question:** Can flexible Bayesian stellar-population inference become practical for large galaxy samples?
  - **Data:**
    - CERIDWEN JAX/GPU forward model; controlled mocks and matched JWST/JADES fits compared with Prospector.
  - **Method:**
    - Compile differentiable stellar, nebular, and dust models; batch nested-sampling proposals; test flexible star-formation and enrichment histories.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Matched JADES fits reach median sampling time `4.0 min` (`4.3 min` including compilation) on one A100, with posteriors consistent with the tested Prospector configurations.
    - The `~134x` speed-up is per-fit latency for a specific GPU/CPU comparison. Throughput per unit of hardware time improves by `~2x` in that benchmark; `125`-parameter histories instead take hours.
    - Up to `~120` age nodes do not imply that many independently measured epochs. Mock calibration is conditional on the generative model; fixed nebular grids and absent AGN components remain physical limitations.
  - **Position:** Makes sensitivity to SED assumptions more affordable to test while retaining model and prior uncertainty.
