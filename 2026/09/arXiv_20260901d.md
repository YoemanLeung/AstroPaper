## JWST High-Redshift Populations and Reionization

- [2608.28745](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28745) Christopher Cain et al. `Super-Eddington Little Blue Dots May Reionize Helium Too Early`
  - **Question:** can most observed `3<z<7` little blue dots be highly super-Eddington without violating helium-reionization timing?
  - **Data:**
    - Population: JWST-selected faint AGN-like little blue dots at `z>4`.
    - Comparison: helium-reionization constraints favoring completion no earlier than `z~3`.
    - Assumptions: LBDs are `5%` of the `M_UV<-18` galaxy population and have average escape fractions of `15%`.
  - **Method:**
    - Model super-Eddington accretion with high EUV and weak X-ray output.
    - Convert the LBD population into a helium-ionizing emissivity.
    - Compare the implied reionization history with observational timing constraints.
    - Vary the super-Eddington fraction, escape fraction, and source abundance.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The fiducial scenario allows only `<=10%` of LBDs to be highly super-Eddington if LBDs dominate helium reionization.
    - A majority-super-Eddington LBD population would require escape fractions of `<=1.5%` or lower abundance than current observations suggest.
    - Bright quasars would tighten the LBD constraint if they also contribute substantially.
    - EUV spectral-shape uncertainty remains the dominant theoretical caveat.
  - **Position:** read first because it converts the LBD/LRD accretion interpretation into a cosmic reionization consistency test.

- [2608.29011](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29011) Chris Ashall et al. `Can Little Red Dots Contribute To The Early Universe Cosmic Dust Budget?`
  - **Question:** can little red dots form enough dust to affect the early-Universe dust budget?
  - **Data:**
    - Analogs: two low-redshift LRD analogs.
    - Observables: SEDs, Balmer components, and narrow-line metallicity context.
    - Target regime: early systems above `z~5` where rapid dust production is difficult.
  - **Method:**
    - Model SEDs with a thermal pseudo-photosphere plus optically thin dust components.
    - Test dust formation in outer winds and in shielded cold dense shells after central-source fading.
    - Check optical depth, sublimation, and energy-balance constraints.
    - Estimate the population-level LRD contribution relative to CCSNe.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The analog SEDs permit dust reservoirs of order `10^2-10^3 Msun`.
    - Metal-poor narrow-line environments disfavor a dominant diffuse host-ISM origin for the inferred reservoirs.
    - Clumpy or asymmetric dust can produce large dust masses without strong optical attenuation.
    - Efficient LRD dust production can approach or exceed the lower CCSN contribution above `z~5`; less favorable assumptions make the contribution minor.
  - **Position:** important early-dust entry because it ties LRD physical models to the dust budget needed by the first massive galaxies.

- [2608.30459](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30459) Sergio Bonaque-Gonzalez et al. `Broad-band host measurements of little red dots and similar compact nuclei require source-conditioned polychromatic PSFs`
  - **Question:** do field-star PSFs bias host measurements for compact high-redshift nuclei such as LRDs?
  - **Data:**
    - Test case: LRDs analyzed by Zhang et al.
    - Imaging regime: broad filters with diffraction-limited point-source modeling.
    - Measurement targets: extended-light fractions, sizes, luminosities, and masses.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare field-star PSFs with source-conditioned polychromatic PSFs.
    - Track how spectral mismatch shifts light between unresolved and extended components.
    - Quantify the induced host-property bias.
    - Demonstrate a correction applicable to other compact distant sources.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Broad-band PSF mismatch can overestimate an existing extended component or create an apparent one.
    - The error can alter inferred LRD sizes, luminosities, and masses at physically important levels.
    - The bias has a clear mathematical and physical origin and is correctable.
    - Compact-source host inferences need source-conditioned PSFs before population-level interpretation.
  - **Position:** high-priority methods paper because LRD host claims directly affect black-hole, stellar-mass, and early-galaxy interpretations.

- [2608.30011](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30011) Gourav Khullar et al. `Caught Napping by JWST UNCOVER+MegaScience: Constraining bursty star formation histories and number densities of mini-quenched galaxies at redshifts 4-7`
  - **Question:** how common are temporarily quenched low-mass galaxies in the first several hundred Myr after reionization?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: JWST UNCOVER/MegaScience in the Abell 2744 lensing field.
    - Sample: `8` spectroscopically confirmed nappers and `60` photometric candidates.
    - Redshift and mass: `z=4-7`, `log10(M*/Msun)=7.5-10`.
    - Selection: moderate Balmer breaks, weak emission lines with `EW(Halpha)<100 Angstrom`, and blue UV continua.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit flexible non-parametric star-formation histories with Prospector.
    - Use `SFR_10/SFR_100` to identify rapidly declining systems.
    - Compare spectroscopic and medium-band photometric selection.
    - Estimate number densities by redshift and stellar mass.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Medium-band photometry can identify pure napping-galaxy samples when calibrated by high-S/N spectroscopy.
    - Nappers are most common near `log10(M*/Msun)~9`.
    - Number densities may rise from `z~6` to `z~4`, but cosmic variance is likely important.
    - Stochastic feedback is a measurable regulator of low-mass galaxy growth soon after reionization.
  - **Position:** strong JWST galaxy-evolution paper because it connects bursty feedback duty cycles to observable quenching episodes at `z=4-7`.

- [2608.29727](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29727) Saeed Fakhry et al. `Primordial Black Hole Seeds for Little Red Dots in f(R) Gravity`
  - **Question:** can clustered PBH mergers in modified gravity seed the black holes inferred in LRDs by cosmic dawn?
  - **Data:**
    - Target population: JWST compact LRDs and high-redshift SMBHs.
    - Model regime: Hu-Sawicki `f(R)` gravity with chameleon screening.
    - Observational comparison: LRD space densities near `z~5.5-6.5`.
  - **Method:**
    - Model hierarchical PBH mergers within dense clusters.
    - Include modified-gravity enhancement of radiation-capture merger kernels.
    - Add later galactic gas accretion under an AGN duty cycle.
    - Compare active mass functions with JWST and GW constraints.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - A clustered PBH fraction of `f_PBH<=10^-3` with duty cycle `delta~10^-2` can match LRD space densities in the model.
    - Modified gravity drives the high-mass tail and lowers the required time-averaged Eddington ratios.
    - Proposed discriminants include remnant spin states, a stochastic GW background cutoff, and PBH spatial correlations.
    - The model is speculative but gives concrete multi-messenger tests.
  - **Position:** useful as a theory edge case for LRD black-hole seeding, but less immediately actionable than the observational and systematic LRD papers above.

- [2608.28975](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28975) Tyco Mera et al. `Little Red Dots as Shock-Powered High-Energy Neutrino Sources`
  - **Question:** can shock-powered LRD models contribute meaningfully to the IceCube diffuse neutrino background?
  - **Data:**
    - Population: compact high-redshift LRDs.
    - Analogy: Type IIn-like shock interaction with dense material.
    - Comparison: IceCube diffuse high-energy neutrino intensity.
  - **Method:**
    - Model fast central-engine outflows driving shocks through dense surrounding gas.
    - Calculate cosmic-ray acceleration and hadronic neutrino production.
    - Integrate the signal over a cosmological LRD population.
    - Vary luminosity quintile and cosmic-ray efficiency.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Viable shock solutions span luminosities from `2.2e43` to `3.5e44 erg/s`.
    - Fiducial SNe-IIn-like parameters contribute about `0.2%` to `2%` of the IceCube intensity below `2e5 GeV`.
    - A high-efficiency illustrative case reaches about `13%`.
    - Current LRD census incompleteness limits the strength of the constraint.
  - **Position:** relevant LRD-physics paper because it makes high-energy neutrinos a possible diagnostic of dense shock-powered compact sources.

## AGN, Quasars, and Nuclear Gas

- [2608.28775](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28775) V. A. Fawcett et al. `Radio-dust connection in quasars driven by powerful ionised outflows`
  - **Question:** are ionized outflows the physical link between dust-reddened quasar colors and radio emission?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: DESI optical spectra.
    - Sample: `3418` quasars at `0.5<z<0.9`.
    - Radio data: LoTSS DR2.
    - Diagnostics: [OIII] `5007` kinematics, `E(B-V)`, `g-W2`, and `L6um/L5100A`.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit quasar spectra with PyQSOFit.
    - Measure [OIII] outflow velocities.
    - Compare outflow velocity against extinction, optical-MIR color, MIR excess, and radio properties.
    - Control for luminosity when testing color-outflow trends.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The radio-dust connection is driven by sources with high-velocity ionized outflows.
    - [OIII] outflow velocity has no significant trend with `E(B-V)`.
    - `g-W2` correlates positively with outflow velocity after luminosity control.
    - `L6um/L5100A` supports a MIR-excess connection to outflows.
    - Shocks from outflows can heat gas and dust, boosting both MIR and radio emission.
  - **Position:** high-value quasar-feedback paper because it links DESI kinematics to multiwavelength dust and radio observables.

- [2608.29682](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29682) Sayan Basu et al. `The multi-frequency radio light curve of the sub-pc SMBH binary candidate PG 1302-102`
  - **Question:** does the radio behavior of PG 1302-102 support the sub-pc SMBH-binary interpretation of its optical periodicity?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: quasar PG 1302-102 at `z=0.278`.
    - Candidate binary: black holes of `10^8.3-10^9.4 Msun`.
    - Radio data: ATCA observations from `2005` to `2024`.
    - Optical context: `~5.2 yr` periodic signal across roughly `20 yr`.
  - **Method:**
    - Build a multi-frequency radio light curve.
    - Search for periodicity in total radio flux and in-band spectral index.
    - Compare radio periods and period changes against the optical time series.
    - Interpret coupling across emission mechanisms.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The `5.5 GHz` flux is highly variable but does not show strong evidence for the optical periodicity.
    - The in-band spectral index has stronger evidence for a period consistent with the optical period.
    - The reported optical-period change also appears in the radio spectral-index light curve.
    - Radio-optical coupling strengthens the SMBH-binary interpretation before the LSST-SKAO era.
  - **Position:** useful quasar time-domain entry because it tests a rare sub-pc binary candidate with an independent radio baseline.

- [2608.28947](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28947) James Guillochon et al. `A Self-Sustaining Black Hole Engine Powered by the Tidal Disruptions of Stars`
  - **Question:** can gas-rich post-starburst nuclei maintain tidal-disruption rates far above the canonical relaxation rate?
  - **Data:**
    - Host regime: post-starburst or recovering starburst galaxies with molecular gas reservoirs.
    - Target rate: up to `10^-2 gal^-1 yr^-1`.
    - Nuclear constraints: molecular clouds, stellar cusp density, and obscuration.
  - **Method:**
    - Model feedback between TDE debris momentum, cloud compression, cluster compression, and enhanced stellar disruption.
    - Solve for a steady state arrested by stellar collisions.
    - Apply independent bounds from AGN feedback, disk geometry, and observed nuclear cusp densities.
    - Estimate delayed engine turn-on for initially low-mass black holes.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The steady-state rate saturates near `1.4e-2 (M_h/10^6 Msun)^-0.84 yr^-1`.
    - This is roughly two orders of magnitude above the canonical rate at `10^6 Msun`.
    - The allowed stellar-cusp flattening is `0.18<=f_*<=0.25`.
    - Seed black holes below a threshold can switch on after `~1 Gyr` of accretion.
    - Obscuration of `A_V~50` implies many events should be infrared rather than optical discoveries.
  - **Position:** relevant nuclear-gas paper because it connects post-starburst gas reservoirs, black-hole growth, obscured transients, and survey selection.

- [2608.30596](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30596) Nobuyuki Sakai et al. `TeV Gamma Rays and Neutrinos from Winds Driven by Radiatively Inefficient Accretion Flows of Low-Luminosity Active Galactic Nuclei`
  - **Question:** can RIAF-driven winds explain quiet-period TeV gamma rays from low-luminosity AGN without invoking strong jets?
  - **Data:**
    - Primary source: NGC 4278, a nearby LLAGN detected by LHAASO.
    - Scale: ionized gas on `100 pc` scales.
    - Comparison source: Sgr A*.
  - **Method:**
    - Model particle acceleration at wind-driven shocks.
    - Compute gamma-ray and neutrino emission.
    - Tie wind power to Bondi accretion under shallow accretion profiles.
    - Apply the same mechanism to Sgr A*.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Matching the quiet TeV flux requires wind power `<=2e43 erg/s` and cosmic-ray index `2.0`.
    - Bondi accretion of observed `100 pc` ionized gas can supply the required power in the model.
    - Sgr A* TeV emission is reproduced with reasonable parameters.
    - RIAF winds may make LLAGNs a broader gamma-ray and cosmic-ray accelerator population.
  - **Position:** useful low-luminosity AGN entry because it connects unresolved nuclear accretion flows to large-scale wind energetics.

## Galaxy Structure, Feedback, and CGM Accretion

- [2608.29893](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29893) Hassen M. Yesuf et al. `The Structural Abundance Crisis of Massive Galaxies in Current Cosmological Simulations`
  - **Question:** do cosmological simulations match the observed abundance of massive-galaxy structural types?
  - **Data:**
    - Observations: Hyper Suprime-Cam Subaru Strategic Program imaging.
    - Simulations: IllustrisTNG, EAGLE, and SIMBA matched mock images.
    - Galaxy sample: nearby systems with `log(M*/Msun)>10`.
    - Metrics: Sersic index, concentration, size, and ellipticity.
  - **Method:**
    - Build like-for-like observational and mock-image pipelines.
    - Construct structural abundance functions for each morphology metric.
    - Compare population-level abundances rather than only integrated demographics.
    - Quantify systematic offsets and simulation-to-simulation differences.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - All simulations show large structural-abundance discrepancies at `>5 sigma`.
    - RMSE spans `~0.2-1.8 dex`, often implying abundance errors of factors of several.
    - All simulations underproduce highly flattened disks.
    - Agreement with stellar mass functions can hide failures in mass and angular-momentum redistribution.
    - Structural abundance functions provide a stringent benchmark for next-generation galaxy formation models.
  - **Position:** high-value morphology benchmark because it uses HSC-like observables to test physics beyond integrated stellar masses.

- [2608.30698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30698) Ivan Rapoport et al. `Constraining Baryonic Feedback at z~1 with the Halpha Luminosity Function`
  - **Question:** can the `z~1` Halpha luminosity function discriminate baryonic-feedback strength in galaxy formation simulations?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: Illustris and IllustrisTNG.
    - Redshift comparison: observed Halpha luminosity functions at `z~1-1.5`.
    - Emission sources: star-forming HII regions, diffuse ISM recombination and collisional excitation, and AGN-induced photo-excitation.
  - **Method:**
    - Apply direction-dependent dust attenuation.
    - Include Lyman-series photon propagation.
    - Calibrate the SFR-to-HII-region Halpha relation with one free parameter.
    - Compare luminosity-function faint and bright ends across feedback models.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Diffuse gas contributes `~1-10%` of total Halpha luminosity.
    - AGN photo-excitation modestly enhances the bright end and dominates Illustris galaxies with `L_Halpha>=3e42 erg/s`.
    - Low-feedback IllustrisTNG agrees well with observed luminosity functions.
    - High-feedback Illustris fails to reproduce the faint and bright ends simultaneously at `z~1`.
    - Tension with stacked kSZ measurements shows the value of combining emission-line and gas probes.
  - **Position:** important survey-interpretation paper because Halpha luminosity functions will be central for Roman/PFS-like emission-line galaxy work.

- [2608.31144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31144) Jennifer K. S. Friske et al. `The impact of hot mode accretion and angular momentum conservation on metallicity gradients of galactic discs`
  - **Question:** can metallicity gradients constrain the angular momentum and temperature of hot CGM accretion onto disks?
  - **Data:**
    - Model target: Milky Way disk evolution.
    - Gas component: isothermal rotating hot CGM.
    - Observables: structural properties and gas-phase metallicity gradient.
  - **Method:**
    - Build a semi-analytic chemical-evolution model with cosmological growth.
    - Include a time-evolving potential and inside-out formation.
    - Model radial gas flows induced by potential deepening and accretion.
    - Fit structural and metallicity-gradient constraints in a Bayesian framework.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The Milky Way fit mildly prefers a subvirial corona with `T_CGM/T_200=0.82^+0.44_-0.38`.
    - The predicted present-day CGM rotation speed is `~100-160 km/s` and lower at earlier epochs.
    - The model recovers metallicity-gradient evolution over the past `~8 Gyr`.
    - The framework can be applied to external galaxies as a hot-mode accretion diagnostic.
  - **Position:** useful CGM-ISM bridge because it turns metallicity gradients into constraints on accreting halo-gas kinematics.

- [2608.28737](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28737) Raghav Arora et al. `Magnetic destabilisation in disc galaxies: Filament/feather formation`
  - **Question:** how do magnetic fields change gravitational-instability filament and feather formation in disk galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: 3D isolated disk galaxies initialized in equilibrium.
    - Parameter range: magnetization `beta={0.1,0.5,1,10,100,infinity}`.
    - Diagnostics: filament growth rate, spacing, and shear dependence.
  - **Method:**
    - Run a parameter study across magnetic-field strengths.
    - Measure dense-filament and feather formation during gravitational instability.
    - Compare local field strength and shear across disk regions.
    - Interpret trends against the magneto-Jeans mechanism.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Dynamically strong fields with `beta<=10` significantly change filament growth and spacing.
    - Magnetic fields can stabilize or destabilize depending on `beta` and shear.
    - Destabilization is strongest in low-`beta`, low-shear regions.
    - Dwarf galaxies with low-shear rotation curves may be especially affected.
  - **Position:** useful ISM-structure simulation because it links magnetic regulation to filamentary morphology and star-forming disk substructure.

## Survey Methods, Line Modeling, and Calibration

- [2608.30312](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30312) Cody Carr et al. `Testing SALT Approximations with Numerical Radiative Transfer Code. II. Thermal and Microturbulent Line Broadening`
  - **Question:** how much do thermal and microturbulent broadening affect inferred galactic-wind properties in SALT-style line-transfer models?
  - **Data:**
    - Models: mock spectra from Monte Carlo radiative-transfer simulations.
    - Ions: Si+ column-density range `13<=log(N_Si+/cm^-2)<=18`.
    - Wind observables: line profiles, columns, and mass-outflow rates.
  - **Method:**
    - Extend SALT with finite-thickness resonance regions and escape-probability re-emission.
    - Fit mock spectra generated from identical outflow configurations.
    - Test degeneracies among Doppler broadening, density gradients, and velocity profiles.
    - Compare recovered integrated quantities with input values.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Shallower density and velocity gradients can mimic larger velocity dispersion.
    - Recovered ionic columns have `0.26 dex` scatter and `0.22 dex` systematic overestimation.
    - Terminal-radius mass-outflow rates have `0.88 dex` scatter and `0.51 dex` systematic overestimation.
    - The broadened model improves substantially over earlier versions.
  - **Position:** useful outflow-method paper because wind-line systematics propagate directly into feedback and CGM mass-loading inferences.

- [2608.30494](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30494) R. Shirley et al. `Photometric redshifts for active galactic nuclei with LePHARE for the Vera C. Rubin Observatory`
  - **Question:** how should AGN photometric redshifts be configured for Rubin/LSST-like data?
  - **Data:**
    - Field: COSMOS.
    - Photometry: HSC-CLAUDS as an LSST-depth and wavelength analog.
    - Samples: AGN selected by X-ray, radio, infrared, variability, color, and spectroscopy.
    - Reference catalog: `28`-band photometry for COSMOS AGN.
  - **Method:**
    - Test LePHARE under limited-band and incorrect-template assumptions.
    - Implement configurations within RAIL.
    - Compare expected performance as a function of redshift, magnitude, and selection method.
    - Release photometric redshifts and posterior distributions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Point-like optical AGN receive unreliable photo-z values if processed with galaxy templates.
    - Shallow all-sky surveys miss many AGN that can be misidentified as galaxies.
    - Selection-specific AGN configurations are needed for public survey catalogs.
    - The COSMOS release supports panchromatic AGN studies and algorithm comparisons.
  - **Position:** useful survey-calibration paper because hidden AGN contamination can bias galaxy samples, redshift distributions, and environment studies.

- [2608.28740](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28740) Andrew K. Saydjari et al. `Spatially-Resolved Spectra of Diffuse Galactic Light using 10.8 M DESI Sky Fibers`
  - **Question:** what optical diffuse Galactic light spectrum and spatial variation can DESI blank-sky fibers measure?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: DESI Year 3 blank-sky spectra.
    - Sample size: `10.8` million sky fibers.
    - Resolution: `R~4000`.
    - Map scale: `54 deg^2` HEALPix pixels with `NSIDE=8`.
  - **Method:**
    - Correlate blank-sky spectra with far-infrared emission from IRAS.
    - Map spatial variation across the high-Galactic-latitude DESI footprint.
    - Measure nebular emission lines and scattered-light features.
    - Compare molecular-hydrogen and extended-red-emission signatures.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - DESI detects several diffuse-light features beyond prior SDSS-based analyses.
    - Near-infrared ro-vibrational molecular-hydrogen emission has radiance `0.65^+0.13_-0.12e-9 erg cm^-2 s^-1 sr^-1`.
    - The apparent ortho-to-para ratio is lower than expected by a factor of `0.60^+0.28_-0.27`.
