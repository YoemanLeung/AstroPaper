## Key Findings

- Carbon-galaxy correlations trace extended enrichment; the dusty host at `16 kpc` probes an individual inner halo. Neither fixes the total metal budget ([2609.15547](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15547) C. Piscitelli; [2609.17680](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17680) Qinyue Fei).
- Ionizing-source identity remains conditional: forest constraints depend on thermal models, and HETDEX He II candidates still need individual metallicity tests ([2609.16992](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16992) Vid Irsic; [2609.16134](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16134) Mahan Mirza Khanlari).
- Clustering and pair dynamics give complementary halo masses. Cosmic variance limits the former; the latter's efficiency offset is only `1.3 sigma` ([2609.12040](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12040) Seunghwan Lim; [2609.17756](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17756) Zihao Wu).
- Interactions build new stars in dwarf groups; protocluster ICL stars often form in outskirts before stripping in the main halo ([2609.19687](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19687) Yoshihisa Asada; [2609.20560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20560) S. V. Werner).
- Growth maps require dust constraints: FIR data stabilize resolved SED fits, while acceptable ensemble SFRs can hide internal attenuation structure ([2609.12059](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12059) Cheng-Lin Liao; [2609.19298](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19298) Ivana Barisic).

## Multiphase gas, enrichment, and reionization

- [2609.15547](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15547) C. Piscitelli: The Multiphase CGM in the Epoch of Reionization: CII and CIV absorbers around [OIII] Emitters
  - **Question:** How far do low- and high-ionization carbon trace galaxies during reionization?
  - **Data:**
    - EIGER [OIII] emitters at `5.30<z<6.97` and quasar spectra containing `16` C II and `14` C IV absorbers at `5.33<z<6.52`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure covering fractions, equivalent-width profiles, and galaxy-absorber cross-correlations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At fixed slope `gamma=1.8`, correlation lengths are `3.60 +/- 0.73` and `4.09 +/- 0.74 cMpc/h` for C II and C IV, below galaxy autocorrelation.
    - C IV remains enhanced farther out. Carbon likely also traces faint galaxies or the wider environment; the observed ionic mass is a lower limit, not the total carbon budget.
  - **Position:** Extends MAGG/QSAGE-style gas-galaxy comparisons into reionization with two ionization states.

- [2609.17680](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17680) Qinyue Fei: A dense, metal-rich absorber driven by a heavily dust-obscured galaxy: The direct evidence of CGM enrichment at z~7
  - **Question:** Can a dust-obscured galaxy be linked directly to enriched CGM at cosmic dawn?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST and NOEMA observations of ND1 at `z=7.0321` and an absorber toward GNz7q at `16 kpc` projected separation.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine systemic redshifts, absorption curve-of-growth analysis, SED fitting, and a parameterized wind estimate.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The galaxy-absorber association probes the inner halo. The inferred wind rate `~64 Msun/yr` depends on metallicity, geometry, covering, and assumed velocity.
    - Loading near `3` uses the `100 Myr`-averaged SFR. Calibration uncertainty of `~50 km/s` competes with the centroid shift; saturation prevents unique primordial-yield inference.
  - **Position:** Adds a UV-faint dusty counterpart to the early absorber-host census.

- [2609.16992](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16992) Vid Irsic: Lyman-alpha forest 1D flux power spectrum constraints on QSO-assisted reionization models
  - **Question:** How much escaping hard-quasar radiation is compatible with the forest thermal imprint?
  - **Data:**
    - Lyalpha-forest one-dimensional flux power at `z=4.2,4.6,5.0`, modeled with Sherwood-Relics and alternative QSO luminosity functions.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit thermal and quasar-contribution parameters with an emulator; test spectral heating and luminosity-function choices.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - For the more abundant faint-AGN luminosity function and hard spectra, contributions below `MUV=-21.6` are excluded at `2 sigma` within the model.
    - Softer spectra, lower escape fractions, or the less abundant LRD luminosity function relax the restriction. This is not a model-independent bound on all AGN-assisted reionization.
  - **Position:** Translates forest temperature fluctuations into a complementary test of JWST AGN populations.

- [2609.16134](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16134) Mahan Mirza Khanlari: Population III Host Candidates at z~2: Strong He II 1640 and Absent UV Metal Lines in HETDEX Lyalpha Emitters
  - **Question:** Do metal-line-weak He II emitters provide credible targets for primordial-population tests?
  - **Data:**
    - HETDEX: `8` candidates selected from `109,545` high-confidence LAEs at `1.9<z<2.3`.
  - **Method:**
    - Vet spectra, stack in He II, check leave-one-out stability, and compare UV metal-line limits with contaminant diagnostics.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The stack has He II equivalent width `28.7 +/- 6.7 Angstrom` and intrinsic width `449 +/- 105 km/s`, with no detected N V, C IV, or O III].
    - The candidates are not confirmed Pop III systems. Individual He II and rest-optical metallicity measurements remain necessary; the selection yield is not a completeness-corrected occurrence rate.
  - **Position:** Offers a specific follow-up sample while separating unusually hard ionization from a unique stellar origin.

- [2609.13382](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13382) Balpreet Kaur: The GMRT CATz1-COSMOS Survey: HI 21 cm emission from star-forming galaxies at z~1 in the COSMOS field
  - **Question:** How large are atomic-gas reservoirs in star-forming galaxies near redshift one?
  - **Data:**
    - GMRT CATz1-COSMOS: `216 hours` and `8,122` spectroscopic star-forming galaxies at `0.875<z<1.50`.
  - **Method:**
    - Stack HI emission at `90 kpc` resolution; compare mass-matched DEEP2 and local xGASS samples.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Mean HI mass is `(15.5 +/- 3.7)x10^9 Msun`; characteristic depletion time is `1.74 +/- 0.45 Gyr` at mean `z=1.081`.
    - Agreement with independent DEEP2 measurements strengthens the gas-rich, short-depletion-time picture. These are sample averages, not individual gas masses or direct accretion rates.
  - **Position:** Adds an independent field to the small set of HI-emission constraints near cosmic noon.

- [2609.18006](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18006) Jiang-Tao Li: A Possible Hidden Hot Halo Component Revealed by Joint X-ray and Sunyaev--Zel'dovich Observations
  - **Question:** Does X-ray-emitting gas account for all observed halo pressure?
  - **Data:**
    - XMM-Newton observations of NGC 4594 and multiple Planck-based SZ reconstructions.
  - **Method:**
    - Predict Compton-y from X-ray density and temperature profiles, then compare with SZ under alternative gas models.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The inner-halo SZ excess has only `86-94%` probability. A hotter diffuse phase could supply pressure missed by soft X-rays.
    - Very small filling factors follow only from the preferred two-phase equilibrium interpretation. A smaller discrepancy or nonthermal contribution remains possible.
  - **Position:** Illustrates multiphase degeneracy in halo baryon accounting rather than a secure missing-baryon detection.

## Halo inference and environmental selection

- [2609.12040](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12040) Seunghwan Lim: Clustering-based halo mass assignment for high-redshift galaxies: method, validation, and application to JWST
  - **Question:** Can clustering provide useful halo masses for small high-redshift surveys?
  - **Data:**
    - COLIBRE/HOMA mocks and JWST JADES photometric clustering.
  - **Method:**
    - Match projected clustering to halo templates; vary volume, redshift errors, selection, and secondary galaxy properties.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The preferred fitted separation range is `0.5-1 cMpc`. Clustering at fixed halo mass varies by factors near `2-3` in `(50 cMpc)^3` volumes.
    - JADES application gives characteristic `log(Mhalo/Msun)~10.5` at `z~6` and `~9.9` at `z~10` for `MUV<-17`; scale choice and cosmic variance limit interpretation.
  - **Position:** Provides a calibrated clustering route to the galaxy-halo relation, complementary to pair dynamics.

- [2609.17756](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17756) Zihao Wu: JADES: Dynamical Measurements of Dark Matter Halo Masses at z ~ 7 Using Galaxy Pairs
  - **Question:** Can early galaxy pairs directly constrain their dark-matter halo masses?
  - **Data:**
    - JADES [OIII] spectroscopy for `10` pair systems at `z=6-8`, with TNG100 calibration and mock tests.
  - **Method:**
    - Infer masses from velocities and separations while marginalizing projection and observational uncertainties.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At `log(Mstar/Msun)=9`, mean `log(M200/Msun)=11.06 +/- 0.21` implies stellar conversion efficiency `5(+3/-2)%`.
    - The `~0.3 dex` stellar-mass offset from TNG100 is only `1.3 sigma`. The estimator is best calibrated near the virial radius.
  - **Position:** Dynamical evidence complements abundance matching and clustering without yet establishing a significant efficiency anomaly.

- [2609.15542](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15542) Zekang Zhang: Anisotropic redshift distributions in photometric galaxy clustering and their cosmological impact
  - **Question:** Can spatially varying photometric redshift distributions mimic extra clustering?
  - **Data:**
    - Forward survey simulations with parametric observing-condition maps calibrated broadly to KiDS-like conditions.
  - **Method:**
    - Propagate selection and photometry through tomographic binning; fit clustering and lensing while omitting spatial variation in n(z).
  - **Conclusion:**
    - High-redshift bins can gain about `10%` small-angle clustering. In the LSST-like configuration, fitted `sigma8` and `As` shift by `1.7` and `2.7 sigma`.
    - Density-only random-catalog corrections and scale cuts are insufficient in that setup. The numbers are conditional forecasts, not biases measured in actual LSST data.
  - **Position:** Shows why selection variations along the redshift direction must enter environmental and clustering inference.

- [2609.19295](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19295) Kunyao Zhao: Reconstructing the Projected Dark Matter Field across 0.1-100 Mpc Scales from the SDSS Survey
  - **Question:** Can galaxy stellar-mass maps recover the projected dark-matter environment?
  - **Data:**
    - CAMELS training simulations, independent TNG300 validation, and SDSS galaxies with `Mstar>=10^9 Msun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Train a conditional diffusion model; average `100` realizations and test projected-aperture masses against group catalogs.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The released map covers `90x90 (h^-1 Mpc)^2` at `0.097 h^-1 Mpc` pixels. Simulation tests give halo-mass scatter below `0.2 dex` and relevant pixel-density scatter near `0.3 dex`.
    - The `25 h^-1 Mpc` projection slab blends structures, and redshift-space distortions are not explicitly modeled. Group-catalog comparisons show mass-dependent calibration offsets.
  - **Position:** Provides a simulation-calibrated environmental map; it is not an independent lensing measurement or a three-dimensional reconstruction.

- [2609.16333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16333) Navya Saraswat: Disentangling Stellar Mass and Environmental Effects on eROSITA AGN Activity using multi-wavelength data from GAMA, WISE, GALEX, and DESI Legacy Survey
  - **Question:** Does AGN incidence retain an environmental trend after controlling stellar mass?
  - **Data:**
    - GAMA-eFEDS overlap with eROSITA X-ray, WISE infrared, and broad-line AGN selections.
  - **Method:**
    - Rank overdensity within narrow mass bins and compare incidence across selections, colors, and smoothing scales.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - X-ray AGN fractions rise from about `4%` to `6%` across the environmental transition; WISE and broad-line selections lack the same trend.
    - The luminosity-black-hole-mass plane shows little environmental change. Selection dependence and correlation alone do not establish a unique triggering mechanism.
  - **Position:** Provides a practical mass-controlled comparison of how different AGN tracers respond to environment.

- [2609.19993](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19993) Ryusei R. Kano: Topological Signatures of AGN Feedback in the Simba Simulations
  - **Question:** Which galaxy populations carry the topological response to AGN feedback?
  - **Data:**
    - Simba simulations at `z=0` with matched initial conditions and varied AGN feedback channels.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare two-point clustering, Betti curves, persistence diagrams, and Wasserstein distances with same-run sampling references.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Satellite loop-like features show the clearest changes at `0.1-0.4 h^-1 Mpc`; the weaker `1-3 h^-1 Mpc` response is significant only for cumulative full-feedback versus no-AGN changes.
    - Most transport cost reflects shifts in feature birth/death scales, not added or removed loops. Quenched populations in massive halos dominate; topology alone does not identify a unique causal mechanism.
  - **Position:** Adds population-sensitive persistent-homology diagnostics to conventional clustering tests of feedback.

## Assembly from dwarf groups to protocluster outskirts

- [2609.20560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20560) S. V. Werner: Intracluster light in the infall region of simulated protoclusters of galaxies
  - **Question:** Where did the stars making up protocluster intracluster light form and become unbound?
  - **Data:**
    - TNG-Cluster and Manhattan Suite: diffuse stars selected near `z=2` and traced through progenitor galaxies and environments.
  - **Method:**
    - Track stellar birth sites, stripping sites, host masses, and diffuse stellar mass in the main halo and infall region.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Infall regions contain `10^10-3x10^11 Msun` of diffuse stars at `2<z<4`. About `56-58%` of the stars in the `z=2` ICL formed in infall regions, but `69-74%` were stripped inside the main halo.
    - Host galaxies at stripping are `3-4` times more massive than at stellar birth. Stellar formation in the outskirts does not mean formation as unbound stars; detectability requires surface-brightness modeling.
  - **Position:** Connects diffuse-light assembly to protocluster outskirts; both simulation suites support separating stellar birth from later stripping.

- [2609.19687](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19687) Yoshihisa Asada: Early steps in the hierarchical assembly of a Milky Way-mass galaxy 1 Gyr after the Big Bang
  - **Question:** How much can interaction-driven star formation accelerate the assembly of an early dwarf group?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRCam and NIRSpec, plus HST: three strongly lensed galaxies behind MACS J0417.5-1154 at `z_spec=5.196`, with component masses of roughly `10^6-10^7.5 Msun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine lensing-corrected photometry, spectroscopy, burst histories, and abundance matching.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Projected separations of `~2 kpc` and velocity offsets from `-120 +/- 100` to `+160 +/- 110 km/s` support interaction and likely merging.
    - Bursts increase inferred mass growth by `2.6 +/- 0.5` relative to adding the pre-existing masses. A Milky Way-mass descendant is an abundance-matching inference, not a measured future outcome.
  - **Position:** Resolves a low-mass example of hierarchical assembly and interaction-enhanced growth near the end of reionization.

- [2609.19253](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19253) Olivia Curtis: A High-Mass Size Deficit for Void Galaxies in the Complete SDSS DR7
  - **Question:** Do massive void galaxies have different sizes after matching other properties?
  - **Data:**
    - SDSS DR7/NSA galaxies and an identically selected TNG300 comparison, extending to `z<=0.114`.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify voids with VoidFinder; control stellar mass, redshift, color, and underdensity, and compare sizes and close-pair fractions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Void galaxies above `10^11 h^-1 Msun` are `11 +/- 3%` smaller than matched field galaxies. The deficit grows in deeper underdensities and persists for centrals.
    - Reduced close-pair incidence supports suppressed or delayed outer-envelope growth. A void label alone dilutes the signal if density contrast is not controlled.
  - **Position:** Links environmental structure to late merger growth while leaving suppression versus delay unresolved.

- [2609.19265](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19265) Judah Luberto: A model to mini-quench early galaxies by balancing stellar feedback and gas accretion
  - **Question:** Can stellar feedback interrupt both the existing gas reservoir and fresh accretion in early galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Analytic shell model at `z~6`, compared with observed mini-quenched galaxies and plausible galaxy-formation parameters.
  - **Method:**
    - Balance gravity and accretion against supernova, dust-radiation, and Lyalpha forces; vary inflow geometry and follow shell expansion and return.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - For isotropic accretion, stellar feedback can mini-quench systems up to `Mstar~10^10 Msun`, depending strongly on efficiency and the IMF.
    - Some low-mass cases remain quenched for more than `100 Myr`. Filamentary inflow can shorten this, but sufficiently narrow streams cannot be stopped; shell instability is another possible limit.
  - **Position:** Makes the competition between outflows and replenishment explicit; the result is conditional theory, not a measured quenching duty cycle.

## Resolved growth, dust, and quasar interpretation

- [2609.12059](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12059) Cheng-Lin Liao: Resolved SED Modeling with JWST and ALMA: The Role of Stellar Mass Surface Density in Regulating Star Formation in Cosmic Noon Galaxies
  - **Question:** How does stellar surface density relate to molecular gas regulation at cosmic noon?
  - **Data:**
    - ASPECS galaxies at `0.5<z<3.7`, using resolved JWST, HST, and ALMA data plus integrated FIR constraints.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit MAGPHYS SEDs under multiple configurations and compare stellar structure with gas fractions and depletion times.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Dust-sensitive quantities can be overestimated by up to `~1 dex` without suitable FIR constraints; stellar masses are more stable within the tested framework.
    - At matched stellar surface density, cosmic-noon systems have more gas and shorter depletion times than local galaxies. Gas conversion factors affect the normalization.
  - **Position:** Connects resolved stellar structure to gas regulation while quantifying SED-model dependence.

- [2609.19298](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19298) Ivana Barisic: MSA-3D: A Diversity of Dust Attenuation Profiles Across the Epoch of Thin Disk Emergence
  - **Question:** How do radial dust variations affect integrated star-formation estimates?
  - **Data:**
    - MSA-3D JWST/NIRSpec slit-stepping maps: `18` main-sequence galaxies at `z~1`, sampled at roughly `1 kpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure spatial Balmer decrements and compare radially summed, galaxy-wide, and central-aperture attenuation-corrected SFRs.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Flat and rising attenuation profiles are common alongside central peaks. Mean SFR offsets stay within `0.05 dex`, although aperture comparisons have `0.12-0.19 dex` scatter.
    - The median stellar-to-nebular reddening ratio is `0.88` (interquartile range `0.51-0.96`). Balmer lines can still miss regions optically thick to Halpha.
  - **Position:** Shows why acceptable ensemble SFRs do not guarantee reliable internal growth or quenching maps.

- [2609.19459](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19459) Anniek J. Gloudemans: Extreme Enrichment at Cosmic Dawn: Three FeII-Strong Quasars at z > 6 from the JWST Aether survey
  - **Question:** What do extreme optical Fe II emitters reveal about early quasars and their surroundings?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST Aether NIRSpec IFU spectra of three quasars at `z>6`, with complementary ground-based and radio observations.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit Fe II templates and Balmer lines, estimate virial masses, and inspect resolved emission and radio morphology.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Fe II/Hbeta equivalent-width ratios span about `2.5-3.6`, depending on template. J0320-3521 permits `lambda_Edd~0.5-6`, with large virial-calibration uncertainty.
    - Two quasars have nearby emission features consistent with companions or ionized gas. Their origin is not secured, and no alignment with radio jets is established.
  - **Position:** Extends quasar-main-sequence comparisons to cosmic dawn; strong Fe II is not a direct standalone abundance measurement.

- [2609.13444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13444) Emmanuel Durodola: Host Galaxy UV Emission and Dust Reddening in Little Red Dots
  - **Question:** How much of an LRD spectrum can come from dust-reddened host stars?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRSpec: `89` PRISM LRDs, with broad/narrow emission-line decomposition for `13` medium-resolution spectra.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare narrow Balmer decrements with UV slopes and break strength; fit stellar templates and spectral stacks.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Narrow Balmer decrements commonly span `3-13`, supporting substantial host-scale attenuation. Moderate breaks can be consistent with dust-reddened stellar emission.
    - Strong-break systems remain ambiguous; broad UV components may contribute. Host dust does not establish the origin of the entire optical continuum.
  - **Position:** Supplies observational constraints that proposed LRD growth and reprocessing scenarios must reproduce.
