## Executive View

The read-first item is [2609.03608](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03608) Weida Hu on C3PO-8447, a Lyalpha-emitting galaxy at `z=8.99781`. It combines deep JWST/NIRSpec spectroscopy, metal-line diagnostics, Lyalpha escape fraction, velocity offset, and local environment checks to argue for a small ionized bubble powered by one low-mass bursty galaxy.

The strongest early-galaxy companion is [2609.03537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03537) Claudia D.P. Lagos, which shows that the Shark semi-analytic model can reproduce bright `z>10` UV galaxy constraints through violent disk-instability-triggered starbursts without retuning baryon physics. [2609.03166](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03166) M. Dessauges-Zavadsky adds an ALPINE `z~4.5` multi-tracer gas calibration that directly tests [CII]-based molecular-gas masses against CO, [CI], dust, and dynamics.

The most relevant baryon-cycle entry is [2609.03083](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03083) Prachi Khatri, a controlled Auriga simulation experiment showing that similar `z=0` stellar galaxies can have very different CGM metal distributions, cool-gas fractions, and accretion histories. It is a useful caution for interpreting galaxy-CGM correlations from stellar properties alone.

The best structure and morphology papers are [2609.03275](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03275) Joss Bland-Hawthorn on gas-rich early massive bars, [2609.03022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03022) Susnata Chattopadhyay on bar-induced dark gaps to `z~3`, and [2609.03649](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03649) Yizhou Liu on why direct filamentary 21cm morphology is unlikely to constrain dark matter with planned SKA-Low facilities.

The AGN and black-hole set is broad rather than dominated by one high-redshift source. [2609.03019](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03019) Alessandro Peca reports a multi-epoch ultra-fast inflow in a Seyfert 2 galaxy. [2609.03701](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03701) Mengye Wang models disk-induced stellar disruptions as an IMBH growth channel. [2609.04130](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04130) R. Kondapally separates X-ray accretion properties of high- and low-excitation radio galaxies across `0.3<z<=2`.

## Reionization and First-Galaxy Benchmarks

- [2609.03608](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03608) Weida Hu et al. `C3PO: A Lyalpha Emitting Galaxy at z~9`
  - **Question:** can a single low-mass galaxy at `z~9` power its own Lyalpha-transmitting ionized bubble?
  - **Data:**
    - Spectroscopy: more than `10 hr` of JWST/NIRSpec G140M and G395M observations from the C3PO survey.
    - Target: C3PO-8447 at `z=8.99781`.
    - Diagnostics: Lyalpha, C IV, O III], C III], stellar population, oxygen abundance, and local environment.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure Lyalpha escape fraction and velocity offset from the NIRSpec spectrum.
    - Use UV metal-line diagnostics to separate starburst and AGN-like ionization.
    - Infer the ionized-bubble scale implied by the low Lyalpha escape fraction.
    - Check for nearby galaxies and large-scale overdensity contributions to the ionizing budget.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Lyalpha is detected with escape fraction `1.5+/-0.3%` and velocity offset `210+/-80 km/s`.
    - C3PO-8447 has stellar mass `2.2+/-0.1e7 Msun`, age `1.6+/-0.2 Myr`, and oxygen abundance `12+log(O/H)=7.50+/-0.08`.
    - The implied ionized bubble radius is `0.07-0.20 pMpc`.
    - No significant galaxy overdensity or nearby-galaxy ionizing contribution is detected, so the bubble can be powered by C3PO-8447 alone.
  - **Position:** read first because it links Lyalpha visibility, local topology, and a directly measured low-mass galaxy during early reionization.

- [2609.03537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03537) Claudia D.P. Lagos et al. `Bright star-forming galaxies naturally forming at z>10 in the Shark semi-analytic model`
  - **Question:** do bright `z>10` JWST galaxies require modified baryon physics relative to models calibrated at low redshift?
  - **Data:**
    - Model: Shark semi-analytic galaxy formation model tuned to the `z=0` Universe.
    - Comparisons: UV luminosity functions to `z=17`, stellar mass functions to `z=10`, and cosmic SFR density over `0<z<15`.
    - Physical driver: violent disk-instability-triggered starbursts.
  - **Method:**
    - Run high-redshift Shark predictions without retuning the baryon-physics parameters.
    - Compare predicted bright UV galaxy abundance against current JWST constraints.
    - Test model behavior with and without the burst-inducing disk-instability channel.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The same model reproduces current UV luminosity-function constraints up to `z=17`.
    - Stellar mass function evolution and cosmic SFR density remain broadly consistent from `z=0` to high redshift.
    - Violent disk instabilities increase UV luminosity variance with halo mass at `z>10`.
    - Removing that mechanism leaves galaxies insufficiently bursty to match the bright `z>10` observations.
  - **Position:** useful early-galaxy theory benchmark because it reduces the need to invoke ad hoc high-redshift baryon-physics changes.

## High-Redshift Gas, CGM, and Baryon Cycling

- [2609.03166](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03166) M. Dessauges-Zavadsky et al. `Multi-tracer exploration of molecular gas in main sequence galaxies at z~4.5`
  - **Question:** how reliable is [CII] as a molecular-gas tracer for main-sequence galaxies at `z~4.5`?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: three [CII]-luminous ALPINE galaxies at `z~4.5`.
    - Lines: CO(4-3), CO(5-4), [CI](1-0), and [CII].
    - Mass tracers: CO, [CI], [CII], dust continuum, and [CII]-based dynamical mass.
  - **Method:**
    - Search for CO and [CI] lines in the three galaxies.
    - Convert all six tracers into molecular-gas mass estimates with standard calibrations.
    - Compare tracer agreement as a function of metallicity and merger state.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - CO(4-3) and CO(5-4) are detected in the near-solar metallicity galaxy DC873756.
    - DC818760 has a tentative CO(4-3) detection, while VC5110377875 has no CO detection and [CI] is undetected in all three systems.
    - In DC873756, all six molecular-gas tracers agree within uncertainties.
    - In DC818760, [CII]-based gas mass is inconsistent with the other tracers, pointing to [CII] excess from merger-driven shocks or diffuse ionized gas.
  - **Position:** high-priority gas-calibration paper because it tests [CII] molecular-gas use in the regime where ALMA high-redshift samples rely on it.

- [2609.03083](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03083) Prachi Khatri et al. `Same galaxy, different CGM: how the metal loading of galactic winds regulates the baryon cycle in Milky Way-mass galaxies`
  - **Question:** can galaxies with similar stellar content and morphology still have very different CGM properties?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: five cosmological MHD Auriga runs of a Milky Way-mass halo.
    - Controlled parameters: wind metal content and energy loading.
    - Calibration: runs end with similar `z=0` stellar masses and morphologies within about `10%`.
  - **Method:**
    - Vary wind metal and energy loading while holding the final stellar galaxy approximately fixed.
    - Track metal partitioning among stars, gas disk, CGM, and IGM.
    - Connect halo-gas metallicity to cooling efficiency, cool-gas fraction, and accretion onto the galaxy.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Similar stellar galaxies can distribute metals very differently across the halo and baryonic components.
    - Wind metal content regulates halo cooling, CGM cool-gas fraction, and galaxy accretion rates.
    - Extreme models differ by an order of magnitude in median gas metallicity at `|z|>=30 kpc` above the disk.
    - Current Milky Way halo metallicity constraints at `|z|<=15 kpc` are broadly consistent but not directly comparable.
  - **Position:** important CGM interpretation paper because it shows that stellar similarity does not imply CGM similarity.

## Galaxy Structure and Cosmic-Web Methods

- [2609.03275](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03275) Joss Bland-Hawthorn et al. `Turbulent gas-rich discs at high redshift: the origin of early massive stellar bars`
  - **Question:** can massive bars observed at `z=4-5` form quickly in gas-rich baryon-dominated disks?
  - **Data:**
    - Motivation: ALMA and JWST observations of `3-7 kpc`, `3-10e10 Msun` stellar bars at `z=4-5`.
    - Simulations: NEXUS N-body/hydrodynamic models.
    - Disk regime: baryon-dominated disks with typical `75%` gas and `25%` stars within `8-15 kpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Simulate bar formation as a function of disk mass fraction and gas fraction.
    - Test vertical-bending stability in gas-free and gas-rich bars.
    - Track bar formation time, length, mass, and `m=2` Fourier amplitude.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Bars can form in `400-800 Myr` when `f_disc>=70%` and the bar is gas-dominated at formation.
    - Dominant gas suppresses vertical bending modes that destabilize gas-free bars.
    - For `f_gas<=60%`, bars develop X-shaped boxy bulges.
    - For `f_gas>60%`, diffusion suppresses resonant trapping and the bar can collapse within `1 Gyr` into a classical bulge.
  - **Position:** high-value morphology paper because it makes early bars a direct probe of turbulent high-redshift disk physics.

- [2609.03022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03022) Susnata Chattopadhyay et al. `Evolution of bar-induced dark gaps in galaxy discs: evidence of strong bar-driven effects already at z>2`
  - **Question:** how do bar properties evolve across cosmic time when traced by dark gaps along the bar minor axis?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `625` barred galaxies.
    - Surveys: SDSS, HST COSMOS, and JWST CEERS.
    - Redshift range: `0.02<z<3`.
  - **Method:**
    - Use dark-gap strength and extent as proxies for bar strength and length.
    - Track redshift trends in `Delta mu_max`, `R_DG`, and `R_dark`.
    - Compare dark-gap metrics with bar-to-total light ratio and bar ellipticity.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Dark-gap strength evolves weakly, increasing from `z~2.5` and declining slightly below `z<0.05`.
    - Normalized dark-gap extent decreases from `z>=1.4` and then remains roughly constant.
    - The trends imply bar formation and rapid initial growth before `z~3`.
    - `R_dark` is a better bar-length proxy than `R_DG`.
  - **Position:** useful companion to high-redshift bar work because it links JWST morphology to a measurable secular-evolution proxy.

- [2609.03649](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03649) Yizhou Liu et al. `Prospects for probing dark matter with filamentary 21cm emission`
  - **Question:** can direct imaging of filamentary 21cm emission distinguish cold and warm dark matter?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: cosmological hydrodynamical maps of intrinsic 21cm brightness temperature.
    - Redshifts: `z=4` and `z=2.5`.
    - Instruments: SKA1-Low and planned SKA2-Low sensitivity models.
  - **Method:**
    - Generate CDM and WDM filament signals.
    - Convolve maps with Gaussian beams of different angular resolutions.
    - Compare morphology retention against sensitivity gains from larger beams.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Intrinsic 21cm maps preserve CDM-WDM filament morphology differences, especially at `z=4`.
    - Beam sizes larger than `6 arcsec` erase much of the small-scale dark-matter information.
    - SKA1-Low and SKA2-Low cannot resolve individual filaments with enough sensitivity for this test.
    - Direct filament imaging is unlikely to constrain dark matter with current planned SKA-Low facilities.
  - **Position:** relevant cosmic-web methods paper because it clarifies where direct 21cm mapping is limited by resolution-sensitivity tradeoffs.

## AGN Accretion, Variability, and Survey Methods

- [2609.03019](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03019) Alessandro Peca et al. `Multi-epoch Detection of an Ultra-fast Inflow in ESP 39607: Evidence for an Accretion Cascade`
  - **Question:** can multi-epoch X-ray spectra reveal a persistent ultra-fast inflow feeding an AGN?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: Seyfert 2 galaxy ESP 39607 at `z=0.201`.
    - Observations: simultaneous XRISM, XMM-Newton, and NuSTAR plus two earlier NuSTAR epochs.
    - Lines: redshifted Fe XXV He-alpha and Fe XXVI Ly-alpha absorption.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify absorption features near `4.7` and `4.9 keV` in the observed frame.
    - Fit continuum and photoionization models across multiple assumptions.
    - Compare inflow detections across a `2.2 yr` source-frame baseline.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The inflow velocity is `v_in~0.16c`.
    - Single-epoch detections are `3-3.7 sigma`; combined multi-epoch significance is `5.3 sigma`.
    - The inferred radius is `49-77 R_g`, where the dynamical timescale is only a few days.
    - The mass inflow rate is `0.3-1.4 Msun/yr`, or `0.2-1.0` times the accretion rate needed to power the AGN.
  - **Position:** strong AGN gas-flow paper because it directly connects nuclear inflow persistence to accretion supply.

- [2609.03701](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03701) Mengye Wang et al. `Rapid Growth of Intermediate-Mass Black Holes through Disk-induced Stellar Disruptions`
  - **Question:** can disk-induced tidal disruptions grow intermediate-mass black holes in dense gas-rich clusters?
  - **Data:**
    - Systems: IMBHs with accretion disks embedded in dense stellar clusters.
    - Simulations: direct N-body calculations plus analytic star-disk interaction prescriptions.
    - Growth regime: black holes around `10^3-10^5 Msun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Follow stellar cusp formation, disk capture, orbital damping, migration, and consumption.
    - Include collisional relaxation, depletion, replenishment, and physical mergers.
    - Compare stellar mass-supply rates with Eddington-limited gas accretion.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Disk-induced TDEs can sustain stellar mass supply near `1e-3 Msun/yr`.
    - That rate exceeds the Eddington-limited gas accretion rate for IMBHs with `M_bh<1e5 Msun`.
    - The channel can help transform `~1e3 Msun` IMBHs into larger black-hole seeds if compact clusters and disks last for more than `30 Myr`.
  - **Position:** relevant seed-growth theory because it connects dense stellar systems, gas disks, and low-mass black-hole assembly.

- [2609.04130](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04130) R. Kondapally et al. `Fundamental differences in the X-ray accretion properties of low and high-excitation radio galaxies`
  - **Question:** how do X-ray accretion properties differ between radio-AGN excitation classes?
  - **Data:**
    - Field: Bootes.
    - Radio catalog: `2840` LOFAR radio AGN across `0.3<z<=2`.
    - Classes: radio-quiet AGN, HERGs, star-forming LERGs, and quiescent LERGs.
  - **Method:**
    - Build radio-luminosity-complete samples.
    - Stack X-ray data to measure average `L_X,2-10keV` and stellar-mass-scaled specific X-ray luminosity.
    - Compare trends with radio luminosity, stellar mass, and redshift for each class.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - LERGs have lower X-ray luminosity and specific X-ray luminosity than HERGs and radio-quiet AGN at all redshifts.
    - HERGs, radio-quiet AGN, and star-forming LERGs show average X-ray luminosities increasing with redshift.
    - The redshift trend may track higher cold-gas fractions at earlier times.
    - X-ray luminosity has weak or no correlation with radio luminosity at fixed redshift.
  - **Position:** useful AGN demographics paper because it separates accretion state from radio output across cosmic noon.

- [2609.03232](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03232) L. Nakazono et al. `CLAS+: A large catalog of Changing-Look AGN candidates selected through S-PLUS narrow-band photometry`
  - **Question:** can narrow-band photometry efficiently identify changing-look AGN candidates at scale?
  - **Data:**
    - Parent sample: `98,139` quasars.
    - Inputs: synthetic photometry from DESI DR1 and SDSS DR17 spectra plus contemporaneous S-PLUS narrow-band imaging.
    - Candidate catalog: CLAS+.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare spectroscopic synthetic photometry with narrow-band imaging.
    - Select high-confidence candidates with reduced `chi_r^2>15`.
    - Cross-match against known CLAGN compilations and classify temporal states where possible.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - CLAS+ identifies `3,554` strong CLAGN candidates, or `3.6%` of the parent sample under the adopted criterion.
    - `3,481` candidates are previously unreported high-priority systems.
    - Reliable temporal classifications are `40%` photometric turn-on, `41%` intermediate, and `19%` turn-off.
    - Photometric turn-on candidates become more common at higher redshift within the selected sample.
  - **Position:** useful time-domain AGN survey paper because it turns narrow-band imaging into a large spectroscopic follow-up target list.

## Lower-Priority but Useful Technical Anchors

- [2609.03089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03089) Shawn Knabel et al. `TDCOSMO XXIX: JWST/NIRSpec IFU Spatially Resolved Kinematics of Three Time-delay Lenses`
  - **Question:** how much can JWST/NIRSpec IFU stellar kinematics improve time-delay lens mass modeling?
  - **Data:**
    - Systems: HE0435-1223, PG1115+080, and WFI2033-4723.
    - Observations: JWST/NIRSpec integral-field spectroscopy.
    - Goal: spatially resolved deflector-galaxy kinematics for time-delay cosmography.
  - **Method:**
    - Extract two-dimensional stellar kinematic maps.
    - Compare aperture-integrated velocity dispersions with previous TDCOSMO values.
    - Classify rotator state and refine lensed-host source redshifts.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Per-bin total uncertainties are `6-11%`.
    - Average bin-to-bin correlated errors are `~1.2%`.
    - Mean velocity-dispersion uncertainty improves from `3.7%` to `2.4%`.
    - PG1115+080 is a fast rotator, while HE0435-1223 and WFI2033-4723 are slow rotators within the probed radii.
  - **Position:** methods-relevant lensing paper because JWST kinematics directly affect mass-profile degeneracy and cosmological inference.

- [2609.03779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03779) Nicolas Baron Perez et al. `Morphology of Radio Sources in Representation Space`
  - **Question:** can representation learning discover rare radio-source morphologies beyond fixed supervised classes?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: LoTSS-DR3, with more than `13 million` sources.
    - Prior training: DR2-derived labels and classifier ensemble.
    - Target: low-class-probability radio sources in representation space.
  - **Method:**
    - Apply a self-supervised representation and classifier ensemble.
    - Search low-confidence sources for new clusters.
    - Use new cluster centroids to classify rare classes.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - `88%` of sources fall into clearly separated known clusters.
    - Six additional morphological classes are identified, including winged and FR-ambiguous sources.
    - Four dominant classes make up `69%` of the DR3 sample.
    - Rare-class skew makes balanced training sets difficult and favors open-set approaches.
  - **Position:** useful survey-method paper for large radio catalogs and environment studies where rare morphology classes matter.
