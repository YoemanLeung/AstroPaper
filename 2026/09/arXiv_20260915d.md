## Key Findings

- Carbon and bright galaxies trace overlapping but unequal structures. At fixed correlation slope, carbon-galaxy correlation lengths are roughly half the galaxy autocorrelation length; diffuse enrichment and undetected galaxies remain competing explanations. [2609.15547](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15547) C. Piscitelli.
- H I supply and hot-halo cooling require distinct interpretations: a short stacked H I depletion time supports replenishment-limited evolution, while O VI luminosity alone does not establish a steady cooling flow. [2609.13382](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13382) Balpreet Kaur; [2609.13368](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13368) Michael Reefe.
- Environmental comparisons require both mass matching and spatial selection control. Halo proxies select different assembly histories, and varying redshift distributions can alter clustering even after density-based corrections. [2609.14488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14488) Shuo Xu; [2609.15542](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15542) Zekang Zhang.
- Early-galaxy diagnostics are not interchangeable: dusty LRD hosts, chemically ordinary GHZ1, and model-dependent GN-z11 continuum masses each expose different degeneracies in color, lines, and accretion inference. [2609.13444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13444) Emmanuel Durodola; [2609.14574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14574) Borja Perez-Diaz; [2609.15967](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15967) Samik Mitra.
- Wind energetics and resolved shock maps constrain different stages of AGN energy transfer; neither alone demonstrates that star formation has been quenched or triggered. Available wind energy, its spatial deposition, and the host response require separate measurements. [2609.15275](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15275) A. Deconto-Machado; [2609.15913](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15913) Peixin Zhu.

## Gas around galaxies and gas supply

- [2609.15547](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15547) C. Piscitelli et al. `The Multiphase CGM in the Epoch of Reionization: CII and CIV absorbers around [OIII] Emitters`
  - **Question:** How do low- and high-ionization carbon trace galaxies near the end of reionization?
  - **Data:**
    - EIGER JWST/NIRCam [O III] emitters and six quasar sightlines, including VLT/X-Shooter; 16 C II and 14 C IV absorbers at `5.33<z<6.52`, with 136 associated galaxy entries.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure covering fractions and galaxy-absorber correlations within `R_perp<=1000 pkpc` and `|Delta v|<=500 km/s`.
    - Compare randomized backgrounds, galaxy autocorrelation, and lower-redshift absorber surveys.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - C IV remains enhanced to about `1 pMpc`; C II approaches the background beyond about `0.5 pMpc`.
    - For fixed `gamma=1.8`, correlation lengths are `3.60+/-0.73` and `4.09+/-0.74 h^-1 cMpc`, versus `7.79+/-0.64 h^-1 cMpc` for galaxies. Carbon outside bright-galaxy halos or around undetected faint galaxies can explain the difference.
    - The observed C II+C IV reservoir within `300 pkpc` is at least `2.8e6 M_sun`; unobserved ionization stages prevent a total-carbon measurement.
  - **Position:** Extends the QSAGE and MAGG galaxy-absorber comparisons into the reionization era; completeness remains central to assigning metals to hosts.

- [2609.13382](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13382) Balpreet Kaur et al. `The GMRT CATz1-COSMOS Survey: HI 21 cm emission from star-forming galaxies at z~1 in the COSMOS field`
  - **Question:** How much atomic gas do star-forming COSMOS galaxies retain at cosmic-noon exit?
  - **Data:**
    - GMRT: `216 hr`, seven pointings over `2.4 deg^2`; 8,122 spectroscopic star-forming galaxies at `0.875<z<1.50`.
  - **Method:**
    - Stack H I at `90 kpc` resolution and radio continuum to estimate atomic mass and characteristic star formation.
    - Compare stellar-mass-matched DEEP2 and local xGASS populations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The `4.2 sigma` stack gives mean H I mass `(15.5+/-3.7)e9 M_sun` at mean `z=1.081`.
    - The characteristic gas-to-stellar mass ratio is `1.15+/-0.27` and H I depletion time `1.74+/-0.45 Gyr`; the radio-derived weighted-median SFR is `8.90+/-0.92 M_sun/yr`.
    - Agreement with DEEP2 supports a gas-replenishment interpretation of declining star formation; these ratios are population statistics, not individual inflow measurements.
  - **Position:** Provides an independent-field counterpart to the DEEP2 H I stacks by Chowdhury and collaborators.

- [2609.15298](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15298) Yossi Oren et al. `Highly Ionized Oxygen in the Circumgalactic Medium of TNG100 Galaxies: Distributions, Thermal States, and Ionization Mechanisms`
  - **Question:** Which physical phases and ionization mechanisms produce highly ionized CGM oxygen?
  - **Data:**
    - IllustrisTNG100: 785 halos at `z~0`, spanning `10^10.5-10^14.5 M_sun`; Cloudy ionization post-processing.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare collisional-only and combined collisional/photoionization calculations.
    - Separate virial-radius and `2 Mpc` column maps, and hot, intermediate, and cool gas phases.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The balance depends on virial temperature relative to each ion's collisional-equilibrium peak; photoionization can dominate ions in hot virialized gas.
    - In Milky-Way-mass halos, O VI and O VII are mainly collisionally produced, while O VIII is photoionized; all three primarily occupy the hot phase in this model.
  - **Position:** Builds on Oren+2024 and comparisons with Faerman+2017/2020 and Nelson+2018; ionization mechanism should not be equated with gas temperature.

- [2609.13368](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13368) Michael Reefe et al. `Reigniting the FUSE II: O VI Emission in Massive Elliptical Galaxies`
  - **Question:** Does O VI emission measure gas cooling all the way into star formation?
  - **Data:**
    - Archival FUSE far-UV spectra of 29 massive ellipticals, aperture-matched stellar-population constraints, and X-ray cooling comparisons.
  - **Method:**
    - Correct O VI for Galactic and intrinsic extinction.
    - Convert luminosity to cooling rates under steady radiative cooling and compare with SFR and X-ray rates.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Extinction corrections substantially raise inferred O VI cooling rates relative to earlier measurements.
    - Hot-phase cooling is more suppressed at cluster scales by about a factor `4-5`; the inferred transition from `10^5.5 K` gas to the molecular phase is suppressed by about an order of magnitude across masses.
    - Mixing, conduction, recycling, and IMF changes complicate the conversion from O VI luminosity to net cooling.
  - **Position:** Continues the Reigniting the FUSE series by linking UV cooling diagnostics to the multiphase cooling-flow problem.


## Halo selection and clustering systematics

- [2609.14488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14488) Shuo Xu et al. `The Connection between Halo Assembly History and the Stellar Distribution I: The Central Stellar Mass - Richness Plane`
  - **Question:** Can central stellar outskirts distinguish halo assembly histories at fixed mass?
  - **Data:**
    - TNG300 halos at `z=0.4` with `log(M_200c/M_sun)>=13`; central stellar mass, satellite richness, lensing, and accretion histories.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare ranked samples and difference sets selected using stellar mass at `50-100 kpc` and richness.
    - Test halo-mass distributions, projection, and baryonic effects.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At group masses, outskirt-selected halos form earlier and are more concentrated despite halo masses comparable to richness-selected samples.
    - Near `10^14 M_sun`, intrinsic richness is the stronger mass proxy; projection reduces its advantage.
    - Matching richness alone does not guarantee matched halo mass.
  - **Position:** Connects diffuse stellar-halo measurements to assembly selection; transfer to observations requires independent mass checks.

- [2609.15375](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15375) Jitendra Joshi et al. `Assembly Bias in eRASS1 X-ray Galaxy Clusters: Accounting for Non-uniform Survey Exposure`
  - **Question:** Does an exposure-controlled temperature split reveal cluster assembly bias?
  - **Data:**
    - eRASS1 parent sample of 524 clusters; matched high/low-temperature subsets of 213/211 objects; DES Y3 RedMaGiC galaxies and weak lensing.
  - **Method:**
    - Match X-ray luminosity, redshift, and exposure; fit relative cluster-galaxy pair counts jointly with galaxy autocorrelation.
    - Use lensing for an independent mass comparison and random splits as null tests.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - On `4-10 h^-1 Mpc` scales, the fit gives `Delta b=0.8+/-1.1`, with no significant detection.
    - The lensing mass difference is `Delta log M_200m=-0.01+/-0.12`; both samples have central estimates near `10^14.33 h^-1 M_sun`.
    - The uncertainties still permit the assembly bias expected for analogous FLAMINGO halos.
  - **Position:** Offers an observational control framework complementary to simulation-based halo proxy tests; a null result is not exclusion of assembly bias.

- [2609.15542](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15542) Zekang Zhang et al. `Anisotropic redshift distributions in photometric galaxy clustering and their cosmological impact`
  - **Question:** How does spatial variation in the redshift distribution bias photometric clustering?
  - **Data:**
    - Forward-modeled galaxy catalogs with observing-condition variation calibrated broadly to KiDS, evaluated at KiDS-, DES-, and LSST-like statistical power.
  - **Method:**
    - Propagate selection and photometric effects through tomographic binning and angular correlations.
    - Fit clustering and lensing while assuming one global redshift distribution; test internal consistency.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - A global distribution underpredicts small-scale clustering by up to about `10%` in the highest-redshift bins.
    - In the LSST-like setup, `sigma_8` shifts low by `1.7 sigma` and high-redshift galaxy bias can shift by about `4 sigma`.
    - These are scenario forecasts retaining KiDS-like selection and observing variation, not a prediction from actual LSST systematics maps.
  - **Position:** Quantifies the spatial-redshift-distribution problem discussed by Baleato Lizancos & White+2023 and Kong+2026.


## Early galaxies and abundance diagnostics

- [2609.13444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13444) Emmanuel Durodola et al. `Host Galaxy UV Emission and Dust Reddening in Little Red Dots`
  - **Question:** How much of LRD ultraviolet emission comes from dusty host galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRSpec PRISM sample of 89 LRDs, including 13 with G235M/G395M spectroscopy for narrow/broad line decomposition.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit narrow Balmer components separately from broad emission.
    - Compare decrements, UV slopes, break strengths, stellar SED fits, and stacked spectra.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Most narrow Balmer decrements lie around `3-13`, above the Case B value `2.86`, and correlate with UV slope and break strength.
    - Dust-attenuated stellar templates explain moderate-break objects; strong-break systems remain inconclusive.
    - Broad UV features in stacks leave room for AGN contributions even when host starlight dominates.
  - **Position:** Refines the host-versus-accretion decomposition of LRDs without assigning one continuum model to the entire population.

- [2609.14574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14574) Borja Perez-Diaz et al. `PRISMS. GHZ1. A standard tale of galaxy evolution with atypical ionizing conditions at z=9.878`
  - **Question:** Can a chemically ordinary galaxy at z~10 still show unusual ionizing conditions?
  - **Data:**
    - GHZ1 at `z=9.878`, magnification `1.715`, effective radius about `410 pc`; JWST NIRSpec/PRISM and MIRI/LRS.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit spectrophotometric stellar populations and UV/optical abundances.
    - Examine the low Balmer decrement and possible line-transfer, variability, and kinematic explanations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The inferred stellar mass is `log(M_star/M_sun)=9.17`, with `12+log(O/H)=7.81`, `log(C/O)=-0.66`, and `log(N/O)<-1.04`.
    - These abundances fit ordinary massive-star enrichment, while Halpha/Hbeta lies below Case B.
    - Optically thick Balmer emission, hidden accretor variability, or complex line profiles remain alternatives; the current spectra do not select a unique mechanism.
  - **Position:** Provides a useful comparison to compact nitrogen-enhanced early galaxies; normal abundance ratios do not imply simple nebular physics.

- [2609.14460](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14460) Shuo Zhai et al. `Nitrogen-Loud Quasars from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument. II. Broad-Line Region Metallicity and Relative Nitrogen Enrichment`
  - **Question:** Does nitrogen-loud quasar emission trace total metallicity or selective nitrogen enrichment?
  - **Data:**
    - DESI DR1: 121 nitrogen-loud quasars at `2.13<=z<=3.90`, with controls matched in redshift, luminosity, and virial black-hole mass.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit broad UV lines and compare nitrogen-sensitive with nitrogen-independent Cloudy-based diagnostics.
    - Repeat control comparisons with C IV- and Mg II-based masses.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Nitrogen-independent median metallicities are about `5 Z_sun` versus `1.5 Z_sun` in controls; N V/C IV gives about `12` versus `4 Z_sun`.
    - Diagnostic discrepancies grow toward stronger enrichment, supporting nitrogen excess in addition to high overall metallicity.
    - Absolute metallicities remain photoionization-model dependent.
  - **Position:** Demonstrates why nitrogen strength alone is insufficient to infer total metal abundance in broad-line regions.

- [2609.15967](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15967) Samik Mitra et al. `A Standard Ultraviolet Continuum Can Hide Supercritical Accretion in GN-z11`
  - **Question:** Does a thin-disk-like UV slope require globally efficient accretion in GN-z11?
  - **Data:**
    - GN-z11 UV continuum and published broad N IV]-based mass benchmark, interpreted with analytic and composite accretion-disk models.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare the supercritical transition radius with the UV-emitting radius.
    - Replace inner disk regions with a supercritical flow and refit continuum slopes.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The transition can lie inside the UV-emitting region; changing the inner flow alters the slope by only `Delta beta_UV=+0.08 to +0.11`.
    - Within these models, the slope permits masses near `10^6 M_sun`, below the continuum Eddington mass `1.12e7 M_sun`.
    - This relaxes a model-dependent bound; it is not a new direct black-hole mass measurement or proof of supercritical accretion.
  - **Position:** Clarifies which accretion assumptions enter early seed-mass arguments based on UV continua.


## Feedback and spatially resolved quenching

- [2609.15275](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15275) A. Deconto-Machado et al. `The WISSH quasars project XIII. A multi-epoch study of ultra-fast broad absorption line outflows at cosmic noon`
  - **Question:** What do variable ultra-fast absorption troughs constrain about quasar feedback?
  - **Data:**
    - Three WISSH quasars at `z=3.567-4.101`; multi-ion spectra spanning up to `23 yr` in the observed frame.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit C IV troughs and covering factors across epochs.
    - Use multi-ion constraints and ionization-versus-transverse-motion scenarios to bound column density, distance, and kinetic power.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Absorption reaches about `0.2c`, with different troughs favoring different variability mechanisms.
    - Summary mean kinetic powers are about `0.3%`, `1.2%`, and `0.2%` of bolometric luminosity for WISSH53, 56, and 71; the largest individual-trough estimates reach about `25%`.
    - Large column-density and distance uncertainties prevent treating the extreme value as typical or as measured host coupling.
  - **Position:** Extends WISSH feedback studies with temporal constraints on extreme UV winds; the three-source sample remains exploratory.

- [2609.15535](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15535) Roberto P. Infurna et al. `ALMA-JELLY II. Constraining the radial profiles of the quenching timescales in ram-pressure-stripped galaxies`
  - **Question:** How does ram-pressure quenching propagate across galaxy discs?
  - **Data:**
    - 11 stripped galaxies in Coma, Norma, and A1367; 37 resolved disc apertures with MUSE spectroscopy and UV-to-far-IR photometry.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit parametric star-formation histories in a Bayesian spectrophotometric framework.
    - Use mock data to assess degeneracies and compare radial quenching ages.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Massive galaxies generally quench outside-in: inner regions can lag the outskirts by about `500 Myr`.
    - Dwarfs show weaker radial gradients and quenching within a few hundred Myr.
    - These are inferred histories in nearby cluster galaxies; their timing should not be transferred directly to high-redshift environments.
  - **Position:** Adds spatial timing to the ALMA-JELLY environmental-quenching program rather than relying on integrated colors alone.

- [2609.15913](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15913) Peixin Zhu et al. `Common Excitation Patterns of Star Formation, Active Galactic Nuclei, and Shocks in Seyfert Galaxies`
  - **Question:** Can resolved line diagnostics separate shocks from AGN and star formation?
  - **Data:**
    - VLT/MUSE observations of nine nearby Type 2 Seyferts at `z<0.026`, with deep Chandra morphology comparisons.
  - **Method:**
    - Apply a three-dimensional excitation diagnostic to separate star formation, AGN, fast shocks, and pure shocks.
    - Compare optical excitation maps with X-ray photoionized and thermal emission.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Star-forming rings/arcs at `0.8-6 kpc`, AGN bicones, and central shock regions recur across the sample.
    - Fast shocks often extend perpendicular to the bicone; X-ray structure supports the decomposition.
    - Bar-driven resonances and positive feedback remain alternatives for the rings; their presence alone does not prove triggered star formation.
  - **Position:** Provides a route to mapping mechanical energy deposition while avoiding shock contamination in AGN/SF diagnostics.
