## Key Findings

- IGM heating constrains escaping hard photons, with degeneracies in quasar abundance, spectral hardness, and escape fraction. It does not exclude all faint AGN or establish their reionization contribution. [2609.16992](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16992) Vid Irsic.
- Strong He II with weak UV metals identifies unusual ionizing sources; stacked diagnostics do not measure individual metallicities or confirm Pop III stars. [2609.16134](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16134) Mahan Mirza Khanlari.
- Environment changes selected populations differently. X-ray AGN incidence and satellite spin carry distinct information about activity and accumulated cluster exposure. [2609.16333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16333) Navya Saraswat; [2609.16420](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16420) Gustavo F. Goncalves.
- Resolution changes inferred galaxy properties: ALMA removes FIR blending, while HST injection tests expose size and crowding biases in faint morphology. [2609.16951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16951) Evanthia Hatziminaoglou; [2609.16982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16982) Limin Lai.
- Halo gas inference needs both phase coverage and foreground control. X-ray/H I synergy in simulations complements the Milky Way evidence for mixed stellar and diffuse X-ray emission. [2609.16421](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16421) Kalvyn N. Poncelet Adams; [2609.16688](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16688) Kazuki Ampuku.

## Ionizing sources and the IGM

- [2609.16992](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16992) Vid Irsic et al. `Lyman-alpha forest 1D flux power spectrum constraints on QSO-assisted reionization models`
  - **Question:** How much hard quasar radiation is compatible with the high-redshift Lyalpha forest?
  - **Data:**
    - Published 1D forest flux-power measurements at `z=4.2, 4.6, 5.0`; Sherwood-Relics simulated spectra.
  - **Method:**
    - Post-process He III regions with temperature boosts and vary the faint limit of two quasar luminosity functions.
    - Fit an emulator-based likelihood jointly with IGM thermal parameters.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - For the higher-abundance faint-AGN model and `Delta T=2e4 K`, contributions extending fainter than `M_UV=-21.6` are excluded at `2 sigma`.
    - The smaller LRD population remains compatible. Softer spectra or lower faint-source escape fractions can relax the constraint; thermal-history degeneracies remain.
  - **Position:** Extends Boera+2019 forest measurements and Puchwein+2023 Sherwood-Relics modeling to quasar-induced thermal fluctuations.

- [2609.16134](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16134) Mahan Mirza Khanlari et al. `Population III Host Candidates at z~2: Strong He II 1640 and Absent UV Metal Lines in HETDEX Lyalpha Emitters`
  - **Question:** Can metal-poor or Pop III-like ionizing sources be isolated among cosmic-noon LAEs?
  - **Data:**
    - HETDEX: `109,545` high-confidence LAEs at `1.9<z<2.3`; eight vetted candidates.
  - **Method:**
    - Select He II emitters without detected UV metal lines; inspect artifacts, interlopers and AGN diagnostics.
    - Compare aligned stacks and leave-one-out measurements; test contaminating ionization mechanisms.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The stack has He II rest EW `28.7 +/- 6.7 Angstrom`, He II/Lyalpha `0.354 +/- 0.094`, and intrinsic FWHM `449 +/- 105 km/s`.
    - The `3 sigma` C IV/He II limit is `<0.128`. Candidate density `~30 Gpc^-3` is not completeness corrected; individual contaminants and non-Pop-III explanations remain possible.
  - **Position:** A targeted candidate search for surviving poorly enriched gas; individual UV and rest-optical spectra are required for confirmation.

## AGN environments and host measurements

- [2609.16333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16333) Navya Saraswat et al. `Disentangling Stellar Mass and Environmental Effects on eROSITA AGN Activity using multi-wavelength data from GAMA, WISE, GALEX, and DESI Legacy Survey`
  - **Question:** Does AGN incidence vary with environment after controlling for stellar mass?
  - **Data:**
    - GAMA/eFEDS: approximately `36,000` mass-complete galaxies, with WISE, GALEX and optical information.
  - **Method:**
    - Rank local overdensity within narrow stellar-mass bins; compare X-ray, mid-IR and optical AGN selections.
    - Check environmental smoothing scales and trends with color, SFR and Eddington ratio.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - X-ray AGN incidence rises from `~4%` to `~6%`, with a transition near `delta_rank=0.4`; WISE and broad-line AGN lack the same trend.
    - The environmental signal is strongest near `5 h^-1 Mpc`. No detected Eddington-ratio trend supports an incidence interpretation, but does not establish a unique triggering mechanism.
  - **Position:** A practical mass-conditioned environmental statistic whose interpretation still depends on AGN selection and completeness.

- [2609.16951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16951) Evanthia Hatziminaoglou et al. `An ALMA Band 7 survey of SDSS/Herschel quasars in Stripe 82: II. The nature of FIR-bright quasars`
  - **Question:** How does resolving FIR confusion change the inferred co-evolution of quasars and their hosts?
  - **Data:**
    - `142` FIR-bright SDSS/Herschel quasars at `1<z<4`; ALMA Band 7, multiwavelength photometry and optical spectra.
  - **Method:**
    - Refit SEDs with resolved submillimeter counterparts; compare starburst and accretion luminosities.
    - Compare line equivalent widths with matched FIR-faint quasars and inspect FIR-radio behavior.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - SFRs decrease by `~20%` for single counterparts and by a factor `>2` for blended multiple systems; median revised SFR is `~1080 Msun/yr`.
    - Large intrinsic scatter dominates the weak starburst-accretion trend. Suppressed C IV and Lyalpha EWs motivate wind/neutral-gas interpretations; a transitional evolutionary phase remains an interpretation.
  - **Position:** An observational deblending benchmark for cosmic-noon quasar hosts, separating instantaneous luminosity correlations from long-term co-evolution.

- [2609.17383](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17383) Seyda Sen et al. `X-ray to Mid-IR Spectral Energy Distributions: A Catalog of X-ray Selected AGNs in the XMM-COSMOS Survey`
  - **Question:** How does host starlight affect nuclear IR-X-ray luminosity relations?
  - **Data:**
    - `104` spectroscopically confirmed Type 1 XMM-COSMOS AGN; Swift/UVOT and JWST/NIRCam for a subset.
  - **Method:**
    - Use CIGALE to separate host, disk, torus and polar-dust components.
    - Compare wavelength-dependent regressions and JWST-covered versus uncovered subsamples.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At `6 um`, the relation with monochromatic `2 keV` luminosity has slope `0.99 +/- 0.10`.
    - The `1 um` slopes are `1.14 +/- 0.12` with JWST and `0.82 +/- 0.20` without it, consistent with reduced host dilution; their uncertainties limit the contrast. Mid-IR disk components are template extrapolations.
  - **Position:** Builds on the Lusso et al. X-ray-selected sample with explicit component modeling; subsample differences need selection-aware interpretation.

## Halo gas and stellar feedback

- [2609.16421](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16421) Kalvyn N. Poncelet Adams et al. `CNN-Based Inference of Gaseous Halo Properties from Synthetic X-ray and 21-cm HI Observations`
  - **Question:** Which combinations of X-ray and H I observations best constrain gaseous halos?
  - **Data:**
    - IllustrisTNG100/TNG300 mock X-ray CCD or microcalorimeter maps and H I intensity, velocity and dispersion maps.
  - **Method:**
    - Train CNNs to infer halo mass, gas fractions, metallicity and O/Fe.
    - Compare bands and exposure times on simulated data.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Combined X-ray/H I improves gas-fraction inference; group halos above `1e13 Msun` reach mass RMSE `~0.04 dex` for `100 ks` X-ray CCD exposures.
    - Matched-exposure microcalorimeter metallicity inference improves by a factor `1.75` over CCD. These are simulation-based performance estimates, with real-data and foreground robustness still to be demonstrated.
  - **Position:** An information-content forecast for observing strategy, conditional on the simulated halo population and instrument models.

- [2609.16688](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16688) Kazuki Ampuku et al. `Probing the All-sky Distribution of Soft X-ray Emission Associated with the Milky Way`
  - **Question:** What produces the Milky Way soft X-ray sky beyond the local foreground?
  - **Data:**
    - HaloSat plus ROSAT: `330` fields; spatial decomposition on `178` fields after source and structure cuts.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit local-bubble, warm-hot, hot, solar-wind and cosmic-background components.
    - Model emission measures with stellar, disk-like and halo-like spatial templates.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Warm-hot and hot median temperatures are `0.19` and `0.74 keV`; the two-temperature model is favored above `3 sigma` in `60%` of individual fields.
    - Warm-hot emission is mainly disk-like in the adopted model; hot emission combines stellar and halo-like terms. Component masses depend on geometry and abundance, and two temperatures may approximate a broader distribution.
  - **Position:** An empirical foreground and decomposition reference for diffuse-CGM emission studies.

- [2609.16190](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16190) Kaitlyn E. Sheriff et al. `JWST Observations of Starbursts: A Young Bubble in NGC 253's Central Starburst`
  - **Question:** Can an individual nuclear star cluster drive a coherent multiphase bubble?
  - **Data:**
    - NGC 253: JWST/MIRI-MRS lines, archival ALMA CO/continuum and Chandra emission around SSC 6.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare molecular and ionized morphology and expansion kinematics.
    - Use RADEX for molecular mass and stellar-feedback models for energetics.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Radius `11.5 +/- 3.4 pc` and expansion `90 +/- 44 km/s` imply a characteristic age `~0.1 +/- 0.1 Myr`.
    - Kinetic energy `~1e51-1e52 erg` is compatible with stellar feedback. Pre-supernova winds/radiation can reproduce the dynamics; supernovae are not uniquely required, and coupling to the galaxy-scale wind remains tentative.
  - **Position:** A resolved test of cluster-scale feedback in a nuclear starburst, with molecular excitation driving substantial mass uncertainty.

## Galaxy transformation and observing limits

- [2609.16420](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16420) Gustavo F. Goncalves et al. `SAMI and TNG-Cluster: tracing galaxy spin and environmental transformation across cluster phase-space and cosmic time`
  - **Question:** Do satellite quenching and stellar spin loss follow the same cluster-infall timescale?
  - **Data:**
    - SAMI integral-field observations and TNG-Cluster satellite populations, with simulated histories over the last `8 Gyr`.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare spin, color and sSFR across projected phase space.
    - Follow simulated orbital histories to distinguish infall, gas loss and cumulative core exposure.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - TNG reproduces broad environmental trends at `z=0`, rather than the absolute spin normalization.
    - Gas loss and quenching occur earlier than the gradual spin decline; lower spin is clearest in the virialized core and depends on orbital history and stellar mass.
  - **Position:** Connects the weak SAMI spin-environment trends discussed by van de Sande+2017 and Santucci+2023 to simulated histories.

- [2609.16187](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16187) Kai Wang et al. `Forged in Quenching: Morphological Transformation across Star-forming and Quiescent Galaxies in EAGLE`
  - **Question:** Which processes connect central-galaxy structure, black-hole growth and quenching?
  - **Data:**
    - EAGLE central galaxies traced from `z>~4` to `z=0`; sizes, disc fractions, triaxiality and ex-situ stellar mass.
  - **Method:**
    - Control for stellar mass and merger history, then compare structural and black-hole tracks.
    - Separate merger-driven growth from secular transformation and examine rejuvenated systems.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At fixed stellar and black-hole mass, galaxies approaching quenching are `~0.1-0.2 dex` more compact.
    - Near `Mstar~10^10.6 Msun`, roughly half the prolate population has low ex-situ fractions, consistent with secular disc instability. These causal channels are interpretations within EAGLE.
  - **Position:** Tests structural dependence of black-hole growth beyond merger-only prescriptions; secular changes are compared with Efstathiou+1982 instability criteria.

- [2609.16982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16982) Limin Lai et al. `Pushing HST to the limit: Detection completeness and morphology robustness of faint galaxies in the EGS field`
  - **Question:** How reliable are faint-galaxy detections and single-Sersic morphology measurements?
  - **Data:**
    - CANDELS/EGS HST imaging, overlapping JWST/CEERS data, realistic mock sources and galaxy pairs.
  - **Method:**
    - Inject sources across magnitude, size and separation; compare completeness with empirical multi-band validation.
    - Fit single-Sersic models and apply a photometric criterion for likely real sources.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Detection completeness exceeds `95%` brighter than F160W `25 mag`; fits deteriorate at `25<mH<27`, with Sersic-index biases of `~5-70%`.
    - The empirical filter yields `59,646` objects and reports `72%` completeness, `84%` purity. Magnitude-only limits miss crowding and surface-brightness dependence; the faintest true/false fractions are described inconsistently across the manuscript.
  - **Position:** A validation template for faint-galaxy catalogs and future wide surveys; thresholds are specific to the tested imaging and source models.

- [2609.16239](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16239) S. Kendrew et al. `Cosmic Noon Galaxies in the Hubble Ultra Deep Field with MIRI Wide-Field Slitless Spectroscopy`
  - **Question:** Can MIRI slitless spectroscopy efficiently trace obscured star formation at cosmic noon?
  - **Data:**
    - HUDF MIRI WFSS spectra from `3.9 hr` on sky, with ancillary redshifts and SEDs.
  - **Method:**
    - Extract and calibrate spectra while masking contamination; fit PAH emission plus continuum.
    - Compare `3.3 um` PAH luminosities with IR luminosities and SED-derived SFRs.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - PAH emission is detected in `19/31` galaxies at `0.67<z<3.1`; median `L_3.3/L_IR=0.0017`, with substantial scatter.
    - Most fitted line centers agree within `50 nm`, but detector-edge offsets can reach `~0.7 um`, causing much larger calibration errors.
  - **Position:** Extends lower-redshift and targeted PAH comparisons to an efficient cosmic-noon survey mode; calibration is still a limiting factor.

## Dark matter and biased tracers

- [2609.16740](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16740) Yu Lei et al. `Lower central dark matter densities in nearby galaxies than predicted by simulations`
  - **Question:** How do nearby-galaxy dark-matter profiles compare with hydrodynamical simulations?
  - **Data:**
    - PROMISE: `136` galaxies spanning `Mstar~1e9-10^11.5 Msun`, using stellar IFU kinematics and integrated H I spectra.
  - **Method:**
    - Jointly model stellar dynamics and unresolved H I constraints; validate with mock galaxies.
    - Compare enclosed mass and density slopes at fixed physical radii with simulation predictions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Massive galaxies have lower inner dark-matter densities than simulated counterparts; discrepancies extend to `20-50 kpc` in higher-mass systems.
    - For the most massive galaxies, low density to the outermost measured radius may reflect halo mass/concentration rather than a measured core beyond `50 kpc`. H I profile selection and dynamical assumptions matter.
  - **Position:** An extended-radius observational benchmark for baryonic contraction and dark-matter models, rather than a unique identification of new dark-matter physics.

- [2609.16122](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16122) Hua-Peng Gu et al. `A Dark-matter Origin of Little Red Dots: Early Seeding and Super-Bondi Accretion`
  - **Question:** Could self-interacting dark matter both seed and grow black holes associated with LRDs?
  - **Data:**
    - Relativistic SIDM halo calculations combined with halo abundances, concentrations and merger histories.
  - **Method:**
    - Evolve gravothermal collapse through horizon formation and conductive accretion.
    - Estimate surviving SMBH abundance after merger disruption and an assumed active duty cycle.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - A fiducial `10^7.5 Msun` halo formed at `z=15`, with concentration `10`, produces a `~30 Msun` seed after `258 Myr`.
    - Conductive dark-matter accretion then enables rapid growth. Agreement with LRD abundance depends on rare concentrated halos, quiet histories and duty cycle; it does not establish a dark-matter origin observationally.
  - **Position:** Extends earlier SIDM seeding proposals into continuous relativistic growth, with clustering proposed as a future discriminant.

- [2609.16209](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16209) Dorsa Sadat Hosseini et al. `Gravitational Wave Bias in IllustrisTNG300 from Machine-Learned Population-Synthesis Calibrated Merger Rates`
  - **Question:** How do host-galaxy properties affect predicted binary-black-hole merger clustering?
  - **Data:**
    - TNG300 galaxies and GALAXYRATE population-synthesis merger rates linked to EAGLE formation histories.
  - **Method:**
    - Train a merger-rate emulator on mass, SFR, metallicity and redshift; populate simulated galaxies.
    - Compare clustering with a stellar-mass-only host weighting in the same volume.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Both prescriptions produce merger hosts more biased than the overall galaxy population.
    - The richer model enhances small-scale bias beyond mass-only weighting; host-property correlations contribute to scale dependence. These are mock-source predictions, not a detected GW clustering signal.
  - **Position:** Extends Dehghani+2025 and Hosseini+2026 host-selection studies to a simulation and population-synthesis comparison.
