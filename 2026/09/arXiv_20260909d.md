## Key Findings

- Gas supply and compaction coexist in SSA22, but the observed sequence does not directly measure filamentary accretion. Molecular excitation remains broadly field-like despite the dense environment. [2609.06679](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06679) Hideki Umehata.
- Apparent CGM trends can reflect tracer physics and sample composition: rotation changes Lyalpha line splitting, while satellites change environmental covering fractions in a low-redshift simulation. [2609.08396](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08396) Hee-Gyeong Kym; [2609.08654](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08654) Georg Herzog.
- High-redshift feedback evidence has different strengths: AEON-z5 has extended enriched emission and asymmetric kinematics, whereas RUBIES-EGS-9809 has an evolved stellar population but uncertain quenching status. Neither source fixes a unique gas-flow or quenching history. [2609.08264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08264) Xiaoyang Wei; [2609.07825](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07825) Francesco D'Eugenio.
- Early-universe mapping benefits from complementary depth, area, and tracers. RXDF specifies an approved imaging design; TILING tests reconstruction in mocks; LRD BAO remains a forecast requiring future wide-field infrared spectroscopy. [2609.08145](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08145) Haojing Yan; [2609.09102](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09102) Nikolaos Triantafyllou; [2609.06926](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06926) Jessica A. Zebrowski.
- LRD continuum continuity and local spectropolarimetry offer distinct tests of the source population. Template mixtures and orientation models fit useful observables, but neither uniquely establishes the physical identity of all LRDs. [2609.07025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07025) Kristiphong Boonmee; [2609.06239](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06239) Piero Madau.

## Cosmic-web gas supply and CGM interpretation

- [2609.06679](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06679) Hideki Umehata et al. `ADF22-WEB: Massive galaxy formation shaped by cosmic web filaments in the z = 3.1 proto-cluster core`
  - **Question:** How do molecular gas, dust, and compactness vary among galaxies embedded in protocluster filaments?
  - **Data:**
    - ALMA, Herschel/SPIRE, JWST, and JVLA: 18 SSA22 DSFGs at `z=3.09`; eight have approximately kiloparsec imaging.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit far-infrared SEDs, measure CO excitation, and compare gas fractions and stellar/dust surface densities with field relations and local quiescent members.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Median dust temperature is `25 (+6/-3) K`; 12 CO(1-0) and 18 CO(3-2) detections give median `r31=0.66 (+0.05/-0.04)`.
    - Lower-mass objects have enhanced gas fractions and depletion times relative to field scaling relations; stellar and dust surface densities correlate.
    - The gas-rich to compact gas-poor sequence supports coupled depletion and structural growth. Continued filamentary supply is an interpretation; gas-to-dust and CO conversion factors remain uncertain.
  - **Position:** Extends the Umehata+2019 filament picture with the Huang+2025 galaxy census and multi-transition gas measurements.

- [2609.08396](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08396) Hee-Gyeong Kym et al. `The Impact of Circumgalactic Rotation on Lyalpha Radiative Transfer`
  - **Question:** Can rotation mimic the Lyalpha peak separation usually attributed to high H I column density?
  - **Data:**
    - Three-dimensional Monte Carlo radiative-transfer models of a spherical rotating neutral medium, including simple clumpy variants.
  - **Method:**
    - Vary rotation speed, column density, inclination, clumpiness, and intrinsic line width; compare integrated and spatially resolved spectra.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At `N_HI less than ~10^19 cm^-2`, peak separation grows approximately linearly with rotation speed and is largest edge-on.
    - At larger optical depth, frequency diffusion weakens rotational sensitivity. A rotating low-column halo can resemble a static higher-column halo.
    - Opposite halo sides show red/blue asymmetries; resolved maps help, but rotation can also mimic patterns attributed to inflow or outflow.
  - **Position:** Provides a controlled radiative-transfer caution for using Lyalpha splitting as a column-density or gas-flow diagnostic.

- [2609.08654](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08654) Georg Herzog et al. `The environmental dependence of the circumgalactic medium in a high-resolution cosmological simulation`
  - **Question:** Does environment alter the CGM after central and satellite populations are separated?
  - **Data:**
    - An EAGLE-based high-resolution simulation at `z=0`, with `M_star>10^8 M_sun` and gas-particle mass `~4.5e4 M_sun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Define group and isolated samples with Friends-of-Friends, match stellar and halo masses, and compare cool/warm gas and covering fractions with and without satellites.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Matched central galaxies show no significant environmental difference in the tested CGM properties or covering fractions.
    - Including satellites restores an enhanced group covering fraction. Satellite position within a larger halo changes the gas sampled.
    - This simulation result motivates control of satellite admixture; it does not prove that environmental CGM physics is absent, especially at high redshift.
  - **Position:** Separates a sample-composition effect from environmental physics while retaining multiphase-resolution limitations.

- [2609.05618](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05618) G. Mountrichas et al. `Halo mass, star formation, and morphology in galaxies: A morphology-dependent connection between halo environment and star formation activity`
  - **Question:** Does the halo-mass connection to star formation depend on morphology?
  - **Data:**
    - Non-active VIPERS and Stripe 82 galaxies at `0.5<=z<=1.2`, with DESI-based Sersic classifications and homogeneous SED estimates.
  - **Method:**
    - Split disc/bulge populations into sSFR quantiles with modest stellar-mass differences; infer halo masses from projected cross-correlations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Bulge-dominated galaxies occupy lower-mass halos at high main-sequence offset and higher, mutually consistent masses at lower offsets.
    - Disc halo masses vary little over the narrower SFR range sampled. The morphology-averaged relation hides this difference.
    - Correlated central properties and residual stellar-mass differences limit causal attribution to halo mass.
  - **Position:** Connects morphology-conditioned clustering to quenching without identifying a unique quenching mechanism.

## Early feedback, enrichment, and compact galaxy growth

- [2609.08264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08264) Xiaoyang Wei et al. `AEON-z5: A Candidate AGN-driven Outflow Enriching the Circumgalactic Medium at z~5.23`
  - **Question:** Is enriched gas being transported into the surroundings of a galaxy at z>5?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRCam imaging and F444W grism spectroscopy of AEON-z5 in COSMOS at `z~5.23`.
  - **Method:**
    - Map continuum-subtracted line emission, forward-model Halpha+[N II] components, and compare N2 abundances and AGN-inclusive SED solutions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Line emission extends beyond projected `4 kpc`. The dominant component has offset `~+489 km/s`, `FWHM~630 km/s`, and `v84~792 km/s`.
    - Nominal N2 metallicities are `~0.7-1.0 Z_sun`; illustrative excitation/calibration corrections still allow `Z greater than ~0.37 Z_sun`, not a model-independent lower bound.
    - An AGN-associated outflow is favored, but the extended velocity gradient permits rotation or recycled inflow. The detached `~2800 km/s` feature is only `1.5 sigma` in 1D and `2.4 sigma` in 2D.
  - **Position:** A candidate view of metal transport to the outer ISM/inner CGM; IFU data and an independent systemic redshift are needed.

- [2609.07825](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07825) Francesco D'Eugenio et al. `A Dusty Quenching-candidate AGN host at z=5.7: massive quiescent galaxies may quench already during the dust-obscured phase`
  - **Question:** Can a galaxy begin quenching while it remains heavily dust obscured?
  - **Data:**
    - RUBIES-EGS-9809 at `z=5.7`: JWST/NIRCam and NIRSpec, Chandra, Spitzer, radio data, and tentative SCUBA-2 cold-dust emission.
  - **Method:**
    - Model the resolved continuum, stellar-plus-AGN SED, Balmer break, broad emission, and Na I absorption.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The source has `A_V>3 mag`, a Balmer break detected at `4 sigma`, and optical radii `~0.4-0.6 kpc`; stellar mass is approximately `10^10.5-10^11 M_sun`.
    - Broad [O III] supports an ionized-outflow interpretation. Na I absorption has `EW=17+/-2 Angstrom`; its blueshift is tentative, described as `2 sigma` in the PDF conclusions versus `3 sigma` in the listing abstract.
    - Recent SFR is insufficiently constrained to establish quiescence. The PDF retains AGN feedback, stellar feedback, and gas consumption as possible causes of decline.
  - **Position:** Connects dusty starbursts and compact quiescent candidates, following the transition systems discussed by Parlanti+2024 and D'Eugenio+2025; the dusty pathway remains conditional.

- [2609.07850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07850) Nikko J. Cleri et al. `LEGGOS: A Shocking Lack of Evidence for Shocks at sub-kiloparsec Scales at 2 < z < 4`
  - **Question:** Do spatially resolved optical diagnostics reveal shock-powered emission at cosmic noon?
  - **Data:**
    - LEGGOS JWST/NIRSpec IFU spectroscopy of six lensed galaxies at `2.37<z<3.625`.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare shock-sensitive line ratios and velocity dispersions at spaxel, clump, and integrated scales; fit MAPPINGS V shock models.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - No galaxy has more than `8%` of image-plane spaxels in shock diagnostic regions, and flagged pixels lack coherent spatial structure.
    - For galaxy/diagnostic pairs with at least 100 measured spaxels, the fraction is at most `3.3%`; line-ratio/dispersion tests also fail to support shock domination.
    - The null result applies to these six systems and their measured lines; it does not exclude feedback or shocks in other phases.
  - **Position:** Uses spatial and kinematic checks to distinguish shocks from noisy line-ratio outliers, with a shock-dominated MaNGA galaxy as a comparison.

- [2609.07851](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07851) Brian Welch et al. `LEGGOS: Direct abundances of N, O, Ne, S, and Ar in five lensed galaxies at Cosmic Noon`
  - **Question:** Are multiple elemental abundances in cosmic-noon galaxies chemically unusual?
  - **Data:**
    - LEGGOS JWST/NIRSpec spectra of five lensed galaxies at `2.481<=z<=3.625`; multiple temperature zones are constrained in three.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure auroral-line temperatures and direct elemental abundances; compare abundance ratios with local trends and recent star-formation histories.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Oxygen abundances span `12+log(O/H)=8.04-8.79`, approximately `0.22-1.26 Z_sun`.
    - N/O and Ne/O broadly follow local trends. SGAS-J1050 has only mild possible nitrogen enhancement within the local scatter.
    - Low S/O and Ar/O are consistent with core-collapse enrichment and recent star formation within `~100 Myr`; they do not uniquely determine the enrichment history.
  - **Position:** Provides direct-temperature benchmarks for high-redshift strong-line studies; numerical N/O is omitted because the listing abstract contains an apparent sign inconsistency.

- [2609.05611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05611) Alexander de la Vega et al. `Discovery of Very Minor Merger-Induced Star-forming Clumps at z=3.33 with JWST`
  - **Question:** Can a very minor interaction trigger a substantial high-redshift star-forming clump?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST spectroscopy and imaging of JADES 1034862 at `z=3.33` and a companion about 28 times less massive.
  - **Method:**
    - Subtract host light, fit clump and companion SEDs with alternative histories, and test clump redshift solutions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The associated clump and companion show rising star formation over the last `100-300 Myr`; the clump formed `~10^7.2 M_sun` of stars and contributes `~10%` of host SFR.
    - The correlated histories support interaction-triggered formation at mass ratio `~1:28`. One system does not determine the population fraction of such clumps.
  - **Position:** Extends merger-triggered clump formation into a weaker-interaction regime alongside disk-instability models.

## Little red dots and early black holes

- [2609.07025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07025) Kristiphong Boonmee et al. `Between Little Red Dots and Star-Forming Galaxies: A Sequence in the Optical Continuum Slope`
  - **Question:** Is there a clear continuum boundary between LRDs and ordinary star-forming galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - DJA NIRSpec/PRISM spectra of 3,566 selected sources, primarily at `3 less than ~z less than ~8`, plus NIRCam stacks.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit UV and optical slopes; compare dust/quiescent models and linear mixtures of galaxy stacks with a The Cliff-based black-hole-star template.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - A continuous vertical sequence reaches the LRD region. Roughly 50 intermediate sources occupy `-1 less than ~beta_opt less than ~0` and have intermediate stacked compactness.
    - Template-dependent black-hole/stellar mass ratios at the LRD end are `~0.006-0.1`, with strong mass and Eddington-ratio assumptions.
    - The data establish continuity in observables, not a time sequence or unique proof of black-hole-star envelopes.
  - **Position:** Offers a practical transition-candidate window and an explicit test of hard LRD selection boundaries.

- [2609.06239](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06239) Piero Madau et al. `Connecting the little dots in polarized light`
  - **Question:** Can an orientation model explain different continuum and broad-line polarization in a local LRD analog?
  - **Data:**
    - Published spectropolarimetry of SDSS J1025+1402: optical continuum polarization `~1.5%` and broad Halpha `~0.7%`, with different angles.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine intrinsic electron-scattering polarization in a thick super-Eddington flow with outer-disk dilution and a dichroic dust screen.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The model reproduces the polarization contrast and angle offset; broad Halpha receives only the screen contribution under the adopted geometry.
    - High accretion rate and high inclination are favored but strongly degenerate; additional scattering can alter the interpretation.
  - **Position:** Builds on Madau+2026 LRD/LBD unification and contrasts the local analog with ordinary-quasar spectropolarimetry such as Capetti+2021.

- [2609.05628](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05628) Silvia Belladitta et al. `Rapid radio variability in the z~7 blazar VLASS J0410-0139: Indication of a hidden population of weak radio jets at Cosmic Dawn`
  - **Question:** How stable is a z~7 blazar as a background source for future 21 cm absorption?
  - **Data:**
    - uGMRT, LOFAR, and VLA measurements across observed `0.144-11 GHz` for J0410-0139 at `z=6.995`.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare multi-epoch and near-simultaneous radio spectra; infer compactness and beaming from variability.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Two uGMRT epochs show substantial spectral changes over `~16 days` in the source frame. Variability-based bounds favor `theta<3 deg`, `delta>19.3`, and `Gamma>9.7`.
    - Low-frequency flux is only a few mJy versus an earlier extrapolation of `~8 mJy`; monitoring is necessary to select useful 21 cm forest observing states.
    - The short baseline limits the jet interpretation, and unsampled scintillation cannot be excluded.
  - **Position:** Connects early relativistic-jet physics to the practical availability of bright reionization-era radio sightlines.

## Survey reach, reconstruction, and calibration

- [2609.08145](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08145) Haojing Yan et al. `The Roman eXtreme Deep Field (RXDF)`
  - **Question:** What new depth and area will Roman RXDF add for early galaxies and environments?
  - **Data:**
    - The approved RXDF Cycles 1-2 imaging program description: `386.41 hours`, seven bands in the Euclid Ultra Deep Field near the North Ecliptic Pole.
  - **Method:**
    - Specify layered depth and cadence, and forecast galaxy, AGN, environmental, and transient reach with complementary NEXUS data.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The design reaches `5-sigma AB=30` in RZYJH, `29` in F, and `28` in K across a full-depth `678.75 arcmin^2`, within total `1243 arcmin^2`.
    - Three annual epochs each contain three sub-epochs separated by about 10 days. These are observing plans, not achieved data products.
    - A photometric-environment test forecasts `~0.25 dex` scatter for seventh-neighbor density at `1<z<3`; performance depends on redshift errors and selection.
  - **Position:** An approved deep imaging complement to Roman core surveys and JWST; total footprint and full-depth area must remain distinct.

- [2609.09102](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09102) Nikolaos Triantafyllou et al. `TILING I: Field-level Bayesian reconstruction of cosmological initial conditions during the epoch of reionization`
  - **Question:** Can galaxy and 21 cm surveys jointly reconstruct initial conditions during reionization?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulated galaxy and 21 cm maps at `z~8`, with varied magnitude limits, thermal noise, and foreground-wedge removal.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine a neural-network point estimate with conditional score-based diffusion to sample initial-condition posteriors.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Fiducial mock tests give posterior-mean cross-correlation `>0.8` and power errors of a few percent for `k less than ~0.2 cMpc^-1`.
    - Galaxy maps supply most phase information; 21 cm data help recover power. Foreground-excised 21 cm modes reach correlation `>0.5` over much of their tested domain.
    - These are model-trained mock results; observational transfer and astrophysical model dependence require validation.
  - **Position:** Extends field reconstruction to EoR radiation fields, building its point-estimate stage on Savchenko+2025.

- [2609.06926](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06926) Jessica A. Zebrowski et al. `Little Red Dot Cosmology: A Matter-Era Baryon Acoustic Oscillations Probe of LambdaCDM`
  - **Question:** Could LRDs support a high-redshift BAO distance measurement?
  - **Data:**
    - A Fisher forecast adopting LRD density `~1e-4 h^3 Mpc^-3`, bias increasing from `~3` to `~8`, and a `14000 deg^2` spectroscopic footprint.
  - **Method:**
    - Forecast isotropic BAO distances in four bins across `4 less than ~z less than ~9` using assumed tracer abundance and bias.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The fiducial survey reaches percent-level `D_V/r_d` precision, conditional on its population and survey assumptions.
    - The required wide-area infrared spectroscopy exceeds current facility capability. Roman imaging depth does not itself deliver this BAO sample.
  - **Position:** A future-mission science case for a matter-era standard ruler, rather than a present LRD clustering measurement.

- [2609.07040](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07040) Amrita Banerjee et al. `Recovering the characteristic halo mass of rare populations from clustering: a benchmark using the Millennium Simulation`
  - **Question:** How accurately does rare-tracer clustering recover a characteristic halo mass in a controlled test?
  - **Data:**
    - Millennium halos: 25-10,000 rare objects and 5,000-50,000 reference objects at six redshifts spanning `0<=z<=5.29`.
  - **Method:**
    - Cross-correlate halo positions, measure bias at `8 h^-1 Mpc`, invert the Tinker+2010 relation, and compare with true mean logarithmic masses.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Across 192 configurations, median absolute mass offset is `0.103 dex`; uncertainties shrink from `+/-0.71` to `+/-0.08 dex` with rare-sample size.
    - Reference-sample errors propagate coherently, and bias-mass calibration contributes `~0.07 dex`.
    - Three-dimensional halo-only tests omit galaxy occupation, projection, and redshift uncertainty; the quoted accuracy is not yet an observational error budget.
  - **Position:** Isolates clustering-estimator performance before galaxy selection and halo occupation enter.

- [2609.08290](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08290) Vivek Kumar Jha et al. `Low-Redshift Interlopers in the SDSS DR16 z > 5 Quasar Catalogue`
  - **Question:** How many pipeline-selected z>5 DR16 quasars are low-redshift interlopers?
  - **Data:**
    - 655 DR16Q entries selected above `z=5`, with supplementary fitted redshifts and optical spectra.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare estimator families and visually inspect catastrophic disagreements for alternative line identifications.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - 125 entries, `19.1%` of the selected total, are identified as low-redshift AGNs: 114 involve Mg II/C III/C IV confusion and 11 Halpha confusion with Lyalpha.
    - The 480 estimator disagreements are not all confirmed contaminants; `73.2%` is a disagreement fraction, not the established interloper fraction.
  - **Position:** Reinforces the Wu and Shen catalog corrections and source-level verification before high-redshift quasar population analyses.

- [2609.06493](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06493) Shihong Liu et al. `A Direct DESI--SDSS Two-Fibre Test of Aperture Bias in Optical Emission-Line Diagnostics`
  - **Question:** Do different fibre apertures bias line diagnostics even with identical spectral fitting?
  - **Data:**
    - 20,545 matched DESI/SDSS emission-line galaxies; 20,200 pass common-fit quality cuts. Fibre diameters are `1.5` and `3 arcsec`.
  - **Method:**
    - Refit both spectra with the same eMILES continuum/line model and native resolution; test star-forming, concentric, and redshift-controlled subsets.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - DESI minus SDSS gives `Delta log N2=+0.0067+/-0.0002` and `Delta O3N2=-0.0327+/-0.0007`; other diagnostic offsets have different signs and magnitudes.
    - Trends depend on redshift and relative fibre coverage. Full-sample offsets are not universal corrections, especially for weak-line or quiescent galaxies.
  - **Position:** A paired-aperture calibration test for precision cross-survey metallicity and excitation comparisons.
