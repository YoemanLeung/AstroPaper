## Key Findings

- Ionization and thermal structure need not track gas escape: the two low-mass halos contain warm components but no unambiguously escaping material ([2609.30834](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30834) Evan Haze Nunez-Cravin).
- Regular rotation and tidal interaction coexist at early epochs; resolved velocity gradients alone do not establish dynamically undisturbed disks ([2609.30375](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30375) Federico Lelli).
- Post-starburst optical spectra can hide substantial current star formation, while absorption stacks retain signatures of earlier feedback. These complementary measurements do not by themselves close the molecular-gas budget ([2609.31307](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31307) David J. Setton; [2609.31308](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31308) Yuanze Luo).
- Time-domain information changes high-redshift interpretation: it distinguishes a lower-redshift supernova interloper from a galaxy and tests transient fueling in an established z>7 quasar ([2609.30440](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30440) Seiji Fujimoto; [2609.30441](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30441) Seiji Fujimoto).
- Gas-tracer interpretation depends on unresolved structure and averaging: FRB cloudlet constraints require a scattering model, and integrated HCN-IR scaling need not directly measure dense-gas consumption ([2609.31106](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31106) Sharon Lapiner; [2609.31226](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31226) I. I. Grozdanova).

## Inner CGM and cosmic-web gas

- [2609.30834](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30834) Evan Haze Nunez-Cravin et al. `KBSS-InCLOSE II: First Detailed Insights on the Inner CGM of Low-Mass z~2.3 Galaxies`
  - **Question:** What phases and motions characterize the inner CGM of low-mass galaxies at cosmic noon?
  - **Data:**
    - Keck/KCWI and MOSFIRE emission spectroscopy, HST imaging, and Keck/HIRES background-QSO absorption for two galaxies at `z~2.245`, with `M_star<=1e9 M_sun` and projected separations `<=50 kpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Decompose absorption into Voigt components; compare ion species and line widths to separate temperature and turbulent broadening.
    - Compare component velocities with estimated halo escape speeds.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Each halo contains at least seven components, intermediate/high ions, and no detected low ions. Six of ten thermally analyzed components are compatible with `T~1e4 K`; four have `log(T/K)=4.6-5.1` and require heating or replenishment.
    - No gas is unambiguously unbound. The two galaxies share one QSO field, so tentative mass trends are not independent population statistics; line-of-sight velocities do not recover full three-dimensional orbits.
  - **Position:** Extends KBSS absorption-emission comparisons into low-mass halos; a direct bridge between Lyalpha-selected galaxies and multiphase CGM measurements.

- [2609.31106](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31106) Sharon Lapiner et al. `Probing the cosmic web with fast radio bursts: II. Scintillation`
  - **Question:** Can FRB scintillation reveal small cold cloudlets in halos, filaments, and sheets?
  - **Data:**
    - Analytic multiphase cosmic-web and plasma-screen models; this is a prediction study rather than a cloudlet detection.
  - **Method:**
    - Model scattering by pressure-confined `T~1e4 K` cloudlets and suppression of Milky Way scintillation.
    - Combine screen geometry, spectral resolution, and encounter probabilities to predict suppression versus extragalactic dispersion measure.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Halo cloudlets can affect sources at `z~0.5`; filament and sheet tests generally require `z>=2` and `z>=3`, respectively.
    - A fiducial model predicts frequent suppression below `~1 GHz` at `DM_IGM>=500 pc cm^-3` if turbulent cloudlets populate massive halos with volume fraction `>=1e-4`. Cloudlet size, turbulence, pressure support, and rare high-redshift intersections control the forecast.
  - **Position:** Offers a complementary probe of gas substructure below the spatial resolution of absorption tomography; predicted sensitivity is not an observed filling fraction.

## Resolved high-redshift gas and obscured quenching

- [2609.30375](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30375) Federico Lelli et al. `TRICEPS. I. Tracing cold gas, dust, and stars at sub-kpc scales in massive star-forming galaxies at z = 4-5`
  - **Question:** How ordered is cold gas in massive star-forming galaxies at z=4-5?
  - **Data:**
    - TRICEPS: 16 galaxies selected for `[CII] flux>2 Jy km/s`; ALMA [CII]/dust at `~0.1 arcsec` (`~0.8 kpc`), alongside JWST imaging and ALMA/VLA CO data.
  - **Method:**
    - Construct resolved gas/dust maps and velocity fields; inspect pairs for bridges and tidal tails.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Every target shows regular rotation; three of four pairs show clear tidal signatures. On average, 46 resolution elements per galaxy reach `S/N>3`.
    - The sample spans `M_star~1e10.5-1e12 M_sun` and `SFR~100-3000 M_sun/yr`. Bright-line selection and deliberate pair inclusion limit extrapolation to ordinary high-redshift galaxies; detailed dynamical modeling remains future work.
  - **Position:** A resolved baseline for comparing gas, dust, and stellar structure in the massive early galaxy population.

- [2609.31307](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31307) David J. Setton et al. `SQuIGGLE: Spatially resolved NIRSpec Pa-alpha reveals that the most CO-luminous, z~0.7 post-starburst galaxies exhibit falling---but still active---star formation behind optically thick dust`
  - **Question:** Are the most CO-luminous post-starbursts actually quiescent behind their dust?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRSpec IFU and Magellan/FIRE spectroscopy of three CO-luminous SQuIGGLE galaxies at `z~0.7`.
  - **Method:**
    - Map Pa-alpha and Brackett lines to infer nebular attenuation and dust-corrected star formation; use [FeII]/Pa-alpha to assess other ionization.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Inferred rates are `27-80 M_sun/yr`, with possible `30-40%` AGN/shock contamination. Effective `A_Pa-alpha~1 mag` reaches `~2 mag` in individual `~700 pc` elements.
    - Rates remain `0.5-1 dex` below inferred burst peaks. Present activity alone cannot consume the CO-inferred reservoirs on sub-Gyr timescales: mechanical removal, a different CO conversion, or a different evolutionary path remains necessary.
  - **Position:** Near-IR recombination lines resolve an optical-SFR ambiguity, but these three extreme CO-selected systems do not represent every post-starburst.

- [2609.31308](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31308) Yuanze Luo et al. `SQuIGGLE: Ubiquitous Cool Gas Outflows with Large Mass-Loading Factors in z~0.7 Post-starburst Galaxies`
  - **Question:** Do massive post-starbursts retain cool outflows after their starbursts fade?
  - **Data:**
    - More than 1300 SQuIGGLE post-starbursts at `0.5<z<0.9`; stacked FeII and MgII absorption.
  - **Method:**
    - Stack by mass and star-formation-history properties; fit absorption profiles and infer outflow energetics and loading.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Blueshifted absorption appears in the principal stacks except the oldest quenching bin (`>~260 Myr`); outflow speed decreases with elapsed quenching time.
    - Inferred mass loading is `~1-120`. Energetics exceed current-star-formation expectations and favor earlier starbursts or AGN. ISM escape need not mean halo escape; molecular depletion estimates additionally assume a comparable molecular outflow rate.
  - **Position:** Connects intermediate-redshift quenching to high-redshift feedback evidence; a stacked signal does not establish a wind in every individual galaxy.

## Early black holes and time-domain selection

- [2609.30441](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30441) Seiji Fujimoto et al. `A tidal disruption event in a quasar at redshift 7.19`
  - **Question:** Can a tidal disruption explain the long nuclear transient in GNz7q?
  - **Data:**
    - Multi-epoch HST, Spitzer, Subaru, and JWST imaging; NIRSpec spectra and three single-epoch MIRI bands for GNz7q at `z=7.19`.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit thermal light curves with a `t^-5/3` decline; test supernova and stochastic-variability alternatives; check dust reprocessing.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - A `~1.1 mag` UV decline over `~2 rest-frame yr` accompanies inferred peak luminosity `~3e45 erg/s` and radiated energy `~1.5e53 erg`. The tested stochastic model reproduces the behavior with probability `<=1e-5` under its adopted priors.
    - A TDE is favored, not conclusively established. MIRI colors support `~1500 K` dust, but single-epoch data do not measure an infrared lag or uniquely determine dust radius and covering fraction.
  - **Position:** Builds on the GNz7q discovery of Fujimoto+2022; continued monitoring is needed to distinguish transient fueling and dust-echo scenarios.

- [2609.30440](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30440) Seiji Fujimoto et al. `SN Helios: A Multiply Imaged Type II Supernova Opening Time-Delay Cosmography beyond redshift of 3`
  - **Question:** What can a lensed supernova reveal about faint hosts and contamination of extreme-redshift samples?
  - **Data:**
    - VENUS JWST/NIRCam epochs, NIRSpec spectroscopy, cluster lens models, and ALMA host limits for SN Helios at `z=3.34`.
  - **Method:**
    - Classify broad hydrogen features and light-curve phase; compare lens forecasts and galaxy-template photometric redshifts.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Broad H-alpha (`FWHM~7900 km/s`) and P-Cygni absorption identify a Type II SN. The undetected host has de-lensed `M_UV>=-13` under the adopted magnification.
    - Without temporal information the transient resembles a `z~16` dropout. Future-image timing remains model-dependent: the abstract quotes `~2-6 yr`, while discussion gives `1.5-3 yr`; this inconsistency precludes a single firm monitoring window. No time-delay H0 measurement exists yet.
  - **Position:** Extends the lensed-supernova approach exemplified by Refsdal and Requiem, while supplying an empirical interloper template for early-galaxy searches.

- [2609.31083](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31083) Philip Cho et al. `Simulating tidal disruption events in nuclear star clusters`
  - **Question:** How does partial debris accretion change simulated black-hole growth from stellar disruptions?
  - **Data:**
    - Pilot direct N-body nuclear-star-cluster models with initial black holes of `10%, 15%, 20%` of cluster mass.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare full stellar-mass accretion with a bound-debris prescription dependent on orbital properties.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At least `~95%` of disruptions have bound fractions `~0.4-0.6`; partial accretion lowers both cumulative growth and disruption counts relative to the full-accretion model.
    - An enlarged numerical disruption radius and reduced particle count limit interpretation to controlled model comparisons. These runs do not predict absolute observed TDE rates or explain GNz7q quantitatively.
  - **Position:** Improves the accretion bookkeeping in Nbody6++GPU; nuclear dynamics and radiative flare modeling remain distinct problems.

## Gas diagnostics and feedback physics

- [2609.31226](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31226) I. I. Grozdanova et al. `ATLASGAL: The HCN-IR Star Formation Relation for the Milky Way`
  - **Question:** Does the galaxy-scale HCN-IR relation directly encode star formation in dense clumps?
  - **Data:**
    - ATLASGAL HCN observations combined with existing clumps; 343 usable profiles, with Hi-GAL estimates of Galactic luminosity totals.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit HCN-IR scaling across evolutionary stages; compare clump-integrated luminosities to galaxy-wide values.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The full sample has slope `1.80+/-0.08`, while star-forming stage subsets give `~1.2` and quiescent clumps show no correlation.
    - Estimated dense-clump contributions are only `~5-25%` of Galactic HCN and `~2-3%` of IR. Diffuse/subthermal HCN and evolved HII/PDR emission weaken a literal instantaneous dense-gas-law interpretation; the integrated budget relies on scaling assumptions.
  - **Position:** Revisits the Gao-Solomon and Wu dense-gas scaling framework with broader evolutionary coverage.

- [2609.30376](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30376) Mohamad Shalaby et al. `Saturation Mechanism of Cosmic Ray Streaming Instabilities with a Shell Distribution`
  - **Question:** What saturates the simulated cosmic-ray streaming instability?
  - **Data:**
    - Kinetic and fluid particle-in-cell calculations with a shell distribution, `v_A=0.01c`, and `n_CR/n_i=0.01`.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare ideal and Landau closures; inspect particle phase space and repeat with a tenfold larger domain.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - All runs saturate near `delta B/B_0~0.25` through gyro-phase bunching, including ideal-closure runs without nonlinear Landau damping.
    - The mechanism is robust within this setup. Lower cosmic-ray densities and Alfven speeds characteristic of the ISM are untested, so a universal replacement for galaxy-scale transport prescriptions is premature.
  - **Position:** A microphysical test of closure assumptions used in cosmic-ray feedback models.

- [2609.30649](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30649) Aurora Wilde et al. `A VLA Study of the Disturbed Massive Cluster PLCK G165.7 + 67.0 and Its Two Narrow Angle Tail Galaxies`
  - **Question:** Can bent radio jets constrain transverse galaxy motion in a disturbed cluster?
  - **Data:**
    - VLA `6 and 15 GHz` imaging of seven radio galaxies in PLCK G165.7+67.0, including two narrow-angle-tail systems.
  - **Method:**
    - Match map resolution, measure spectral aging gradients, and fit Mach-cone and hydrodynamic bending models.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The manuscript conclusion identifies model-inferred transverse speeds of `~2000 km/s`; combining the BCG radial speed (`~-3300 km/s`) gives a total near `4000 km/s`.
    - The abstract calls the `~2000 km/s` estimate a space velocity, so the transverse/total distinction matters. Ram-pressure geometry and flow assumptions limit the dynamical inference; core passage is an interpretation.
  - **Position:** Uses radio morphology to supplement line-of-sight cluster kinematics, with X-ray and polarization follow-up needed to constrain the surrounding flow.
