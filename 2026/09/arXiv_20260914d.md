## Key Findings

- Survey size matters more than photometric-redshift precision for the tested halo-mass inference. Small fields fluctuate in both density and clustering; splitting by secondary properties addresses assembly bias but does not remove cosmic variance. [2609.12040](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12040) Seunghwan Lim.
- Gas regulation and early quenching can change substantially through density-dependent physics. The observed stellar-surface-density correlations and the modeled cold-gas-density prescription probe different quantities and do not by themselves establish a common causal law. [2609.12059](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12059) Cheng-Lin Liao; [2609.12060](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12060) Akash Vani.
- Quasar demographics favor intermittent growth in BAQARO, while disk obscuration and line-emitting gas structure can reshape observed AGN spectra. These are complementary explanations at different scales, not a unique identification of the LRD engine. [2609.12061](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12061) Elia Pizzati; [2609.12049](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12049) Daniel J. Whalen; [2609.12062](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12062) Adele Plat.
- A UV bump does not directly measure a galaxy's total dust reservoir. The z=7.108 continuum limit is temperature-dependent; grain processing and viewing geometry remain competing explanations. [2609.12203](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12203) Katherine Ormerod.
- SED assumptions destabilize transition samples; stellar mass and assembly history explain much of the bar-environment correlation. [2609.12465](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12465) Sabine Bellstedt; [2609.12339](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12339) Karol Chim-Ramirez.

## Halo clustering and quasar growth

- [2609.12040](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12040) Seunghwan Lim et al. `Clustering-based halo mass assignment for high-redshift galaxies: method, validation, and application to JWST`
  - **Question:** How reliably can high-redshift galaxy clustering recover host halo masses?
  - **Data:**
    - COLIBRE/HOMA mocks and JADES photometric clustering at `z=6,10`; Roman Deep Tier forecasts.
  - **Method:**
    - Match projected clustering to halo templates over `0.5<r_p/cMpc<1.0`; vary volume, redshift errors, and galaxy-property splits.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Mock inference has scatter below `0.3 dex` down to volumes of `(50 cMpc)^3` in the tested configurations; field-to-field clustering fluctuations remain substantial.
    - JADES galaxies with `M_UV<-17` yield `log10(M_h/M_sun)=10.52` and `9.91` at `z=6,10`. Roman model differences near `0.5 dex` face only `~20` expected bright-galaxy pairs in the forecast.
  - **Position:** Applies a simulation-calibrated alternative to the Dalmasso+2026 HOD analysis; central and satellite halo assignments are not separated.

- [2609.12061](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12061) Elia Pizzati et al. `BAQARO: Tracing Stochastic Black Hole Growth Histories and Quasar Lightcurves in a Cosmological Context`
  - **Question:** Can one stochastic growth model explain quasar abundances, environments, and accretion rates?
  - **Data:**
    - FLAMINGO-10k subhalo merger trees; bolometric luminosity functions, quasar clustering, and conditional Eddington-ratio distributions.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit six parameters with Gaussian-process emulation and MCMC; link cold halo accretion to stochastic black-hole fueling with slim-disk radiative efficiency.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The joint fit favors radiatively inefficient super-Eddington episodes lasting `~1 Myr`; mergers remain a subdominant mass-growth channel.
    - The model broadly matches constraints over `0<z<7`, but underpredicts the strong `z~4` clustering measurement and retains faint-end and Eddington-ratio tensions.
  - **Position:** Connects Shen+2020 luminosity functions to Eilers+2024 clustering; empirical seeds stand in for growth below the resolved halo scale.


## Gas, dust, and compact galaxy formation

- [2609.12059](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12059) Cheng-Lin Liao et al. `Resolved SED Modeling with JWST and ALMA: The Role of Stellar Mass Surface Density in Regulating Star Formation in Cosmic Noon Galaxies`
  - **Question:** How does wavelength coverage affect resolved gas-regulation inferences?
  - **Data:**
    - ASPECS: `35` main-sequence galaxies at `z~0.5-3.7`; HST, JWST/NIRCam and MIRI, and ALMA maps at `0.2-0.5 arcsec`.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare resolved and integrated MAGPHYS fits, vary FIR constraints, and correlate mass-map surface density with molecular gas properties.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - MIRI/ALMA reduce dust-luminosity overestimates reaching `~0.8 dex`; resolved and integrated stellar masses agree within `~0.05 dex` with rest-NIR coverage.
    - Gas-fraction and depletion-time slopes versus stellar surface density are consistent with local trends, but normalizations evolve and depend on gas conversion factors.
  - **Position:** Extends the rest-NIR outshining test of Song+2023 to gas-regulation measurements; the correlations do not isolate gravitational causation.

- [2609.12203](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12203) Katherine Ormerod et al. `NOEMA probes the [CII] and dust content in a 2175 Angstrom UV Bump Galaxy at z=7.1`
  - **Question:** Does a strong reionization-era UV bump imply a large dust reservoir?
  - **Data:**
    - NOEMA [CII]/continuum observations of GNWY-7379420231; JWST optical-line and SED constraints.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure line significance and luminosity; compare SFR tracers and convert the continuum limit using an assumed dust temperature.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - [CII] is detected at `5.1 sigma`, with `z=7.1078+/-0.0005` and `L_[CII]=1.32(+0.46/-0.44)x10^8 L_sun`.
    - For `T_dust=40 K`, the continuum non-detection gives `SFR_IR<32 M_sun/yr`; strong grain shattering or line-of-sight effects can explain the bump.
  - **Position:** Combines an early UV-bump measurement with the De Looze+2014 [CII]-SFR comparison; FIR limits do not uniquely determine grain formation.

- [2609.12060](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12060) Akash Vani et al. `Galaxies in the first two billion years: Earlier formation and quenching with surface-density modulated star formation and feedback`
  - **Question:** Can density-dependent baryonic physics reproduce early massive and quenched galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - L-Galaxies on MillenniumTNG dark-matter merger histories; stellar-mass, quenched-fraction, and UV-luminosity constraints.
  - **Method:**
    - Calibrate surface-density-dependent star formation and stellar feedback together with disk instabilities and dissipative merger sizes.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Massive/UV-bright abundance increases by up to `~100x` over the legacy model; quenched systems at `z~3-8` also become much more abundant.
    - The AGN feedback prescription is unchanged: greater bulge growth and fueling drive earlier quenching. Some low-mass morphology discrepancies remain.
  - **Position:** Extends Henriques+2015/2020 and connects to Somerville+2025 density regulation; fitted UV luminosity functions are calibration data, not independent validation.


## Early black-hole interpretations and line diagnostics

- [2609.12062](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12062) Adele Plat et al. `Unified modelling of broad and narrow optical-UV emission lines from massive black holes: interpreting high-redshift JWST observations`
  - **Question:** Which emission lines reveal low-mass AGN without confusing gas conditions with black-hole mass?
  - **Data:**
    - relqso/agnslim ionizing spectra and Cloudy BLR/NLR grids; local AGN and high-redshift JWST comparisons.
  - **Method:**
    - Vary black-hole mass, accretion rate, and cloud conditions; compare multi-cloud spectra, detection thresholds, and stellar/AGN diagnostic separation.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At `z=6`, broad Balmer detection reaches `M_BH~10^5.7 M_sun` at Eddington under optimistic `20 h` NIRSpec and BLR covering-factor `0.4` assumptions.
    - Narrow-line ratios depend chiefly on metallicity and ionization parameter. Improved AGN classification still cannot uniquely identify a seed mass; broad/narrow decomposition and dust worsen practical limits.
  - **Position:** Extends Feltre+2016 NLR grids with mass-dependent continua and broad lines; local line-to-accretion conversions need conditional recalibration.

- [2609.12049](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12049) Daniel J. Whalen et al. `Little Red Dots are Direct-Collapse Black Hole-Forming Galaxies`
  - **Question:** Can a newly formed direct-collapse black hole and its host reproduce LRD spectra?
  - **Data:**
    - Cosmological direct-collapse simulations and synthetic spectra compared with RUBIES-EGS-42046, MoM-BH*-1, and CAPERS-LRD-z9.
  - **Method:**
    - Model radiation emerging through the natal disk alongside the compact stellar component.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Dense disks trap X-rays and generate Balmer absorption while an adjacent `~10^8 M_sun`, `~150 pc` stellar cluster supplies blue light.
    - The simulated black hole/stars form around `z~17/13`; applying their morphology and spectra to later LRD epochs requires extrapolation. Spectral agreement does not establish a unique origin.
  - **Position:** Provides a formation-based alternative for LRD spectra; uses the Habouzit et al. formation-rate comparison to motivate later direct-collapse populations.

- [2609.12619](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12619) Zijian Wang et al. `Heavy Seed Black Hole Growth in Metal-Enriched Halos through Disk-Induced Stellar Disruptions: A Semi-Analytical Modelling`
  - **Question:** Can disk-induced stellar disruptions materially accelerate heavy-seed growth?
  - **Data:**
    - Semi-analytic nuclear-cluster/disk models within cosmological merger trees; host metallicity `Z<~10^-3 Z_sun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Follow orbital damping, disk capture, migration, and tidal disruption; compare growth with gas accretion and a no-TDE baseline.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Median seeds grow from `~10^4` to several `10^5 M_sun` within `~0.2 Gyr`; the massive black-hole abundance at `z~9-10` rises by up to `~10x`.
    - Extreme candidates remain difficult to reproduce. Disk lifetime, debris retention, and omitted external metal enrichment affect the predicted contribution.
  - **Position:** Tests a stellar-fueling addition to heavy-seed scenarios, rather than a complete explanation of the most massive early black holes.

- [2609.13041](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13041) Sambo Sarkar et al. `High-redshift supermassive black hole population from core-collapse in self-interacting dark matter halos`
  - **Question:** Can self-interacting dark-matter core collapse seed the observed early black-hole population?
  - **Data:**
    - SatGen halo assembly histories to `z~20`, halo mass functions, and observed high-redshift black-hole masses.
  - **Method:**
    - Calculate gravothermal collapse and seed occupation; evolve accretion/mergers and compare predicted mass-redshift distributions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - For unity Eddington accretion, favored velocity-dependent parameters are `sigma_0~56 cm^2/g` and `omega~101 km/s`.
    - Only favorable halo histories collapse early enough; inferred interaction parameters depend on assumed subsequent accretion. This is a conditional model fit, not a dark-matter detection.
  - **Position:** Links a nonstandard seed mechanism to population statistics; occupation probabilities matter alongside individual growth tracks.


## Selection robustness, environment, and diffuse imaging

- [2609.12465](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12465) Sabine Bellstedt et al. `The constrainability of galaxy positions in the M_* -- SFR plane from SED fitting`
  - **Question:** Which SED-based star-formation categories remain stable across modeling choices?
  - **Data:**
    - GAMA: `6,029` galaxies at `z<0.06`; `24` ProGeny/ProSpect configurations.
  - **Method:**
    - Vary population libraries, star-formation histories, and metallicity treatment; compare positions and class membership in mass-SFR space.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Below `log10(sSFR/yr^-1)~-11`, inferred positions become highly unstable; green-valley selections repeat for only `~25%` on average.
    - An sSFR cut separates passive and star-forming samples more consistently than distance from the fitted main sequence. The boundary reflects inference sensitivity, not a physical quenching threshold.
  - **Position:** A direct reproducibility test for galaxy classification; the numerical boundary is established for this local sample.

- [2609.12339](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12339) Karol Chim-Ramirez et al. `Stellar bar occurrence in TNG50 and TNG100: disentangling environmental effects and assembly history at z = 0`
  - **Question:** Does satellite bar incidence primarily reflect environment or assembly history?
  - **Data:**
    - TNG50/TNG100 at `z=0`: `859/1,719` satellites within `2 R_200`, above respective stellar-mass cuts of `10^9/10^10 M_sun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify bars by Fourier decomposition and components with MORDOR; compare halo mass, cluster radius, and stellar assembly.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Bars become mildly more common toward cluster centers and in larger halos, but stellar mass and earlier assembly explain much of this trend.
  - **Position:** Uses the Zana+2022 component framework to separate demographic correlations from a direct environmental interpretation.

- [2609.12934](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12934) Elham Saremi et al. `The Infrared Glow of Galactic Outskirts: A New Window with NASIM`
  - **Question:** How much diffuse near-IR light can a reduction designed for extended sources recover?
  - **Data:**
    - VISTA/VIRCAM KEDFS K_s imaging and VIDEO comparison fields.
  - **Method:**
    - Use NASIM/Gnuastro exposure-specific flats, conservative sky modeling, NoiseChisel masks, and weighted stacking.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The reported surface-brightness limit is `mu_Ks~27.7 mag/arcsec^2` at `3 sigma` over `100 arcsec^2`; it is not a point-source depth.
    - Comparisons recover extended galaxy light suppressed in standard products while retaining compact-source sensitivity.
  - **Position:** Builds on Akhlaghi and Ichikawa+2015 masking for reproducible outskirts and intracluster-light imaging.
