## Lyalpha, Reionization, and Early Galaxies

- [2609.03608](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03608) Weida Hu et al. `C3PO: A Lyalpha Emitting Galaxy at z~9`
  - **Question:** can a single low-mass galaxy at `z~9` power its own Lyalpha-transmitting ionized bubble?
  - **Data:**
    - Spectroscopy: more than `10 hr` of JWST/NIRSpec G140M and G395M observations.
    - Target: C3PO-8447 at `z=8.99781`.
    - Diagnostics: Lyalpha, C IV, O III], C III], stellar population, oxygen abundance, and local environment.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure Lyalpha escape fraction and velocity offset.
    - Use UV line diagnostics to identify the dominant ionization source.
    - Infer the ionized-bubble size and search for environmental contributors.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Lyalpha is detected with `f_esc,Lyalpha=1.5+/-0.3%` and velocity offset `210+/-80 km/s`.
    - The galaxy has `Mstar=2.2+/-0.1e7 Msun`, age `1.6+/-0.2 Myr`, and `12+log(O/H)=7.50+/-0.08`.
    - The inferred ionized bubble radius is `0.07-0.20 pMpc`.
    - No large-scale overdensity or nearby-galaxy ionizing contribution is detected.
  - **Position:** the week's read-first reionization paper because it joins Lyalpha transmission, galaxy properties, and topology at `z~9`.

- [2609.02558](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02558) A. L. Patrick et al. `Constraining reionization-era Lyalpha escape with JELS-MUSE: a highly complete Halpha-selected sample at z~6.1`
  - **Question:** what is the intrinsic Lyalpha escape distribution near the end of reionization before IGM attenuation dominates?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `24` Halpha-flux-limited star-forming galaxies at `z~6.1`.
    - Surveys: JWST Emission Line Survey and VLT/MUSE JELS-MUSE Large Area Survey.
    - Diagnostics: Lyalpha detections, non-detections, dust attenuation, UV slope, stellar mass, and reionization-simulation IGM transmission.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure Lyalpha escape fractions for an Halpha-selected sample.
    - Include non-detections with reverse Kaplan-Meier survival analysis.
    - Compare stacked flux and individual detections.
    - Use matched reionization simulations to separate IGM transmission scatter from intrinsic ISM scatter.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Lyalpha is detected in `12/24` sources.
    - The Lyalpha emitter fraction is `33+/-12%` for rest-frame `EW(Lyalpha)>25 Angstrom`.
    - Mean Lyalpha escape fraction is `0.07(+0.04,-0.03)`, consistent with the stacked value `0.08+/-0.02`.
    - Similar expected IGM transmission across the sample implies that large Lyalpha escape scatter is mainly ISM-driven.
  - **Position:** key control point for interpreting `z>7` Lyalpha suppression because it fixes an Halpha-selected baseline at `z~6.1`.

- [2609.00199](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00199) L. Napolitano et al. `The gradual decline of Lyalpha visibility in the CANDELS fields: evidence for the combined effects of galaxy evolution and reionization`
  - **Question:** how much of the high-redshift Lyalpha visibility decline is IGM attenuation rather than changing galaxy properties?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: public JWST/NIRSpec PRISM spectroscopy across the five CANDELS fields.
    - Catalog: `3446` spectroscopically confirmed sources at `4<=z<14.2`.
    - LAE subset: `539` robust Lyalpha emitters with `S/N>3`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure the fraction of galaxies with rest-frame Lyalpha equivalent width above `25 Angstrom`.
    - Split visibility by UV luminosity.
    - Compare LAE physical properties with the full star-forming population.
    - Fit a combined galaxy-evolution and IGM-attenuation framework.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - In the fainter UV bin, Lyalpha visibility rises from `z=5` to `z=6` and then declines.
    - LAE stellar mass, UV slope, reddening, SFR, metallicity, sSFR, and burstiness are approximately stable with redshift.
    - The full star-forming population evolves toward LAE-like properties at earlier times.
    - Gradual reionization histories are favored over rapid neutral-fraction jumps.
  - **Position:** important weekly complement because it links the JELS and C3PO cases to field-scale Lyalpha visibility evolution.

- [2609.03537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03537) Claudia D.P. Lagos et al. `Bright star-forming galaxies naturally forming at z>10 in the Shark semi-analytic model`
  - **Question:** do bright `z>10` JWST galaxies require high-redshift-specific baryon physics?
  - **Data:**
    - Model: Shark semi-analytic galaxy formation model tuned at `z=0`.
    - Observables: UV luminosity functions to `z=17`, stellar mass functions to `z=10`, and cosmic SFR density over `0<z<15`.
    - Mechanism: violent disk-instability-triggered starbursts.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare unchanged Shark predictions with high-redshift JWST constraints.
    - Test model behavior with and without burst-triggering disk instabilities.
    - Track the variance of UV luminosity with halo mass.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The unchanged model reproduces current UV luminosity-function constraints up to `z=17`.
    - The model also matches broad stellar-mass-function and SFR-density evolution.
    - Disk instabilities increase high-redshift burstiness enough to populate the bright UV tail.
    - Without that channel, the model underproduces bright `z>10` galaxies.
  - **Position:** useful early-galaxy theory anchor because it emphasizes stochastic burst physics rather than parameter retuning.

## LRDs, Early Black Holes, and AGN Growth

- [2609.00112](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00112) Dale D. Kocevski et al. `Skyfire: A Spectroscopic Census of Little Red Dots and Broad-Line AGN in the CEERS Field`
  - **Question:** how common are broad emission lines among photometrically selected LRDs, LBD-like sources, and compact high-redshift AGN candidates in CEERS?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: `21 hr` of JWST/NIRSpec G395M observations across five CEERS pointings.
    - Sample: spectroscopic redshifts for `178` Skyfire sources.
    - Broad-line catalog: `34` broad-line sources at `2.7<z<6.5`, including `18` LRDs.
  - **Method:**
    - Target LRDs, blue EELGs, and X-ray AGN candidates.
    - Measure spectroscopic redshifts and broad-line incidence.
    - Compare broad-line detection with rest-optical continuum slope and observed color.
    - Revise LRD color selection toward bluer sources.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - LRD spectroscopic completeness for `beta_opt>-0.02` reaches `73%`.
    - Broad-line incidence remains high for bluer rest-optical colors, connecting LRDs and LBDs.
    - Only `18%` of high-equivalent-width compact EELGs show broad lines.
    - Extending selection to `beta_opt=-0.52` yields an `80.9%` broad-line fraction for F444W-bright LRD candidates.
  - **Position:** read first for LRD demographics because it calibrates the CEERS broad-line selection function.

- [2609.00113](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00113) Vinh Tran et al. `Weighing Little Red Dots with Transient Events`
  - **Question:** can stellar transient rates distinguish overmassive LRD black holes from lower-mass alternatives?
  - **Data:**
    - Targets: compact red JWST sources at `z>4`.
    - Diagnostics: TDE and QPE rates from stars interacting with gas envelopes around black holes.
    - Surveys: Euclid, Roman, and LSST as future wide-field transient monitors.
  - **Method:**
    - Compute TDE and QPE rates for three black-hole mass scenarios.
    - Include steep stellar cusps.
    - Compare rates per square degree across scenarios.
    - Treat light curves as mass and gas-structure diagnostics.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - TDE rates span `1.96e-3` to `3.37e-2 yr^-1 deg^-2`.
    - QPE rates span `1.64e-2` to `4.96e-1 yr^-1 deg^-2`.
    - Transient statistics can separate truly overmassive LRD black holes from lower-mass hidden populations.
    - Wide-field time-domain surveys provide an independent alternative to broad-line virial estimates.
  - **Position:** high-value follow-up because it gives an observable path around the LRD mass degeneracy.

- [2609.01711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01711) Jed McKinney et al. `JWST Reveals a Candidate Supermassive Black Hole Binary at z=4.3 in the Brightest Sub-millimeter Galaxy in COSMOS-Web`
  - **Question:** does AzTEC-1 host a candidate supermassive black-hole binary rather than a single AGN plus outflow?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: AzTEC-1, a massive submillimeter galaxy at `z=4.34` in COSMOS-Web.
    - Spectroscopy: JWST/NIRSpec PRISM and G395M.
    - Supporting data: NIRCam imaging plus ALMA dust continuum, CO, [CII], and [NII] kinematics.
  - **Method:**
    - Decompose narrow and broad emission-line components.
    - Use BPT line ratios to identify AGN activity.
    - Check 2D spectra and resolved imaging/kinematics for extended outflow evidence.
    - Interpret broad-line velocity offsets in a candidate binary SMBH scenario.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The PRISM spectrum shows strong Halpha, a Balmer break, no Hbeta detection, and high dust attenuation.
    - Broad Halpha is blueshifted by `1245 km/s` and has `FWHM~2500 km/s`.
    - No extended outflow is detected in spectra, NIRCam morphology, or ALMA kinematics.
    - A dense unstable gas disk could stall binary infall over `~10-100 Myr`.
  - **Position:** important high-redshift black-hole paper because it ties COSMOS-Web dusty galaxy assembly to future low-frequency gravitational-wave source physics.

- [2609.02179](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02179) Hengxiao Guo et al. `The Intermediate-Mass Black Hole Reverberation Mapping Project: Scientific Overview and Sample Characteristics`
  - **Question:** can a homogeneous local IMBH reverberation-mapping sample constrain black-hole seed formation?
  - **Data:**
    - Catalog: uniformly reanalyzed SDSS broad-line IMBH candidates.
    - Reliable sample: `192` candidates at `z<=0.3` with `log(M_BH/Msun)<6`.
    - Low-mass subset: `4` compelling sources below `log(M_BH/Msun)<5`.
  - **Method:**
    - Refit literature candidates with consistent spectral decomposition and mass estimation.
    - Select a carefully controlled reverberation-mapping subsample.
    - Target broad-line region and accretion-disk size measurements.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The sample provides a statistically meaningful local anchor over `10^4-10^6 Msun`.
    - Direct size and lag measurements can calibrate single-epoch black-hole masses in the IMBH regime.
    - The local IMBH mass distribution can constrain seed-formation scenarios that cannot yet be observed directly.
  - **Position:** lower-redshift but relevant because it anchors the seed-mass problem behind high-redshift SMBH and LRD interpretation.

- [2609.03019](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03019) Alessandro Peca et al. `Multi-epoch Detection of an Ultra-fast Inflow in ESP 39607: Evidence for an Accretion Cascade`
  - **Question:** can persistent ultra-fast inflow supply a substantial fraction of AGN accretion?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: Seyfert 2 ESP 39607 at `z=0.201`.
    - Observations: XRISM, XMM-Newton, and NuSTAR plus two earlier NuSTAR epochs.
    - Features: redshifted Fe XXV and Fe XXVI absorption.
  - **Method:**
    - Model multi-instrument X-ray spectra across continuum assumptions.
    - Fit photoionized inflow velocity, ionization parameter, and column density.
    - Compare detections across a `2.2 yr` source-frame baseline.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The inflow velocity is `v_in~0.16c`.
    - Combined evidence reaches `5.3 sigma`.
    - The inferred radius is `49-77 R_g`, implying a dynamical timescale of days.
    - The mass inflow rate is `0.3-1.4 Msun/yr`, or `0.2-1.0` of the AGN accretion requirement.
  - **Position:** strong baryon-cycle-at-nucleus entry because it measures inward gas supply rather than only outflow feedback.

## High-Redshift Gas and the CGM

- [2609.03166](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03166) M. Dessauges-Zavadsky et al. `Multi-tracer exploration of molecular gas in main sequence galaxies at z~4.5`
  - **Question:** how reliable is [CII] as a molecular-gas tracer in `z~4.5` main-sequence galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: three [CII]-luminous ALPINE galaxies at `z~4.5`.
    - Lines: CO(4-3), CO(5-4), [CI](1-0), and [CII].
    - Tracers: CO, [CI], [CII], dust continuum, and [CII]-based dynamical mass.
  - **Method:**
    - Search for CO and [CI] in the three systems.
    - Convert six tracer measurements into molecular-gas mass estimates.
    - Compare agreement across metallicity and merger state.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - DC873756 has significant CO(4-3) and CO(5-4) detections.
    - DC818760 has a tentative CO(4-3) detection and VC5110377875 has no CO detection.
    - [CI] is undetected in all three galaxies.
    - [CII] is consistent with other tracers in DC873756 but excessive in the merging system DC818760.
  - **Position:** the week's most directly useful high-redshift gas calibration.

- [2609.03083](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03083) Prachi Khatri et al. `Same galaxy, different CGM: how the metal loading of galactic winds regulates the baryon cycle in Milky Way-mass galaxies`
  - **Question:** can wind metal loading change CGM observables while keeping the final galaxy similar?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: five Auriga Milky Way-mass cosmological MHD runs.
    - Controlled outcome: similar `z=0` stellar mass and morphology within about `10%`.
    - Variable physics: metal content and energy loading of stellar-feedback winds.
  - **Method:**
    - Vary wind prescriptions in a controlled halo experiment.
    - Track metal locations in stars, gas disk, CGM, and IGM.
    - Link halo metallicity to cooling, cool-gas fraction, and accretion.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Similar stellar galaxies can have substantially different CGM and IGM metal distributions.
    - Metal loading regulates CGM cooling and galaxy accretion.
    - Extreme models differ by an order of magnitude in median gas metallicity at `|z|>=30 kpc`.
    - Milky Way halo gas measurements at `|z|<=15 kpc` are broadly consistent but not a direct discriminator.
  - **Position:** high-priority CGM modeling paper because it warns against reading halo gas physics from stellar mass and morphology alone.

- [2609.01707](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01707) S. Weng et al. `Linking neutral gas inflows and outflows to offsets in the star-forming main sequence and mass-metallicity relation`
  - **Question:** do neutral gas inflows and outflows explain scatter around the SFMS and MZR?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: about `6000` star-forming galaxies with down-the-barrel Na I D absorption from DESI DR2.
    - Flow classes: outflows with `v_flow<=-50 km/s`, slow inflows with `0<v_flow<100 km/s`, and fast inflows with `v_flow>=100 km/s`.
    - Controls: stellar-mass- and redshift-matched galaxies without detected gas flows.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare flow hosts with matched controls and scaling-relation fits.
    - Measure offsets from the star-forming main sequence and mass-metallicity relation.
    - Track narrow `4000 Angstrom` break offsets at fixed redshift, mass, and SFR.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Outflow hosts have sSFR enhancements of `0.25-0.40 dex` and central metallicity enhancements of `0.04-0.06 dex`.
    - Slow inflow hosts have sSFR enhancements of `0.20-0.30 dex` with no significant metallicity offset.
    - Fast inflow hosts show weaker SFR enhancement and modestly lower metallicity.
    - Neutral gas flows contribute to population-level scaling-relation scatter.
  - **Position:** useful low-redshift DESI baryon-cycle analog for interpreting gas-flow signatures in galaxy evolution.

## Environment, Morphology, and Survey Structure

- [2609.01903](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01903) Aurelien Henry et al. `The GOGREEN Survey: AI Powered Deconvolution Lifts The Veil on Outside-in Environmental Quenching at z>1`
  - **Question:** where within cluster galaxies is star formation suppressed at `1<z<1.4`?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: galaxies in nine GOGREEN clusters plus field comparison.
    - Imaging: deep-learning deconvolution trained on HST and JWST imaging.
    - Classification: resolved rest-frame UVJ states for inner and outer regions.
  - **Method:**
    - Recover spatially resolved optical and near-infrared photometry from ground-based imaging.
    - Classify galaxies as star-forming, quiescent, inside-quenched, or outside-quenched.
    - Compare cluster and field fractions by mass and radial star-formation state.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - `24%` of integrated-color quiescent galaxies retain significant star formation.
    - The cluster predominantly quenched excess is about `20%` when using resolved classifications.
    - Outside-quenched galaxies are much more common than inside-quenched galaxies among systems retaining star formation.
    - Low-mass cluster galaxies show an outside-quenched excess of `22.8+/-5.8%` relative to the field.
  - **Position:** high-priority environment paper because it recovers spatial quenching information in the redshift range where integrated colors can mislead.

- [2609.03275](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03275) Joss Bland-Hawthorn et al. `Turbulent gas-rich discs at high redshift: the origin of early massive stellar bars`
  - **Question:** can observed `z=4-5` massive bars form rapidly in gas-rich disks?
  - **Data:**
    - Observational target: `3-7 kpc`, `3-10e10 Msun` stellar bars at `z=4-5`.
    - Simulations: NEXUS N-body/hydrodynamic models.
    - Disk conditions: baryon-dominated disks with typical `75%` gas fraction.
  - **Method:**
    - Simulate bar formation across disk mass fraction and gas fraction.
    - Test how gas suppresses vertical bending and changes orbit trapping.
    - Measure bar onset time, length, mass, and `m=2` amplitude.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Bars can form in `400-800 Myr` for `f_disc>=70%`.
    - Gas-rich bars remain sites of vigorous star formation.
    - `f_gas<=60%` favors X-shaped boxy bulges.
    - `f_gas>60%` can suppress resonant trapping and collapse bars into classical bulges within `1 Gyr`.
  - **Position:** strong high-redshift morphology paper because it ties early bars to baryon fraction and turbulence.

- [2609.03022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03022) Susnata Chattopadhyay et al. `Evolution of bar-induced dark gaps in galaxy discs: evidence of strong bar-driven effects already at z>2`
  - **Question:** how do bar strength and length proxies evolve from `z~3` to the local Universe?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `625` barred galaxies from SDSS, HST COSMOS, and JWST CEERS.
    - Redshift range: `0.02<z<3`.
    - Metrics: dark-gap strength, `R_DG`, and `R_dark`.
  - **Method:**
    - Use preferential light deficits along the bar minor axis as bar-property proxies.
    - Compare dark-gap metrics with bar-to-total light ratio and bar ellipticity.
    - Track redshift evolution in normalized dark-gap extent.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Dark-gap strength evolves weakly from `z~2.5` to the local Universe.
    - Normalized dark-gap extent decreases from `z>=1.4` and then stabilizes.
    - The trends favor bar formation and rapid initial growth before `z~3`.
    - `R_dark` is the better proxy for bar length.
  - **Position:** useful observational counterpart to the early massive-bar simulation paper.

- [2609.01739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01739) Kim Conger et al. `Virgo Filaments VIII: Characterizing the Structural Parameters of Virgo Galaxies with Machine Learning to Probe Environmental Quenching`
  - **Question:** does environmental quenching depend on structural class after controlling multidimensional galaxy properties?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `2831` galaxies in the extended Virgo environment.
    - Inputs: size, light distribution, and stellar mass.
    - Classes: dwarfs, spheroids, and large disks from k-means clustering.
  - **Method:**
    - Partition galaxies into structurally similar feature classes.
    - Compare star-forming main-sequence offsets across classes.
    - Test environmental density trends within each class.
    - Check whether Sersic-index differences drive the result.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Structural classes have distinct star-formation offsets at more than `3 sigma`.
    - Denser environments are associated with stronger quenching.
    - Dwarfs and large disks are weakly affected until rich-group and cluster environments.
    - Spheroid star formation decreases smoothly with increasing density.
  - **Position:** lower-redshift environment anchor because it separates structure-dependent quenching response from environment density.

## Survey and Analysis Infrastructure

- [2609.02167](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02167) Wenke Ren et al. `Empirical Near-Infrared Spectral Templates from SPHEREx: Disentangling AGN and Host-Galaxy Emission`
  - **Question:** can SPHEREx near-infrared spectrophotometry separate AGN and host-galaxy emission at population scale?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: SPHEREx North Ecliptic Pole field.
    - Training sample: `2777` normal galaxies and `1064` AGN.
    - Spectral range: `0.75-5.0 micron`.
  - **Method:**
    - Learn rest-frame templates with weighted non-negative matrix factorization.
    - Forward-model empirical galaxy and AGN components.
    - Validate host fractions against SDSS spectra and HSC imaging decompositions.
    - Test redshift recovery and AGN/galaxy classification.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The dictionary has `14` components: seven galaxy templates and seven AGN templates.
    - Near-infrared host fractions correlate with SDSS and HSC decompositions at `r=0.68` and `r=0.59`.
    - NEP deep-field redshifts satisfy `|z_fit-z_spec|<0.01` for `93.0%` of galaxies and `93.5%` of AGN.
    - Classification accuracy is `81.3%` in the deep field and `72.9%` at all-sky depth.
  - **Position:** useful survey-method paper for future AGN-host decomposition and SPHEREx-selected environment studies.

- [2609.03649](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03649) Yizhou Liu et al. `Prospects for probing dark matter with filamentary 21cm emission`
  - **Question:** can direct 21cm imaging of cosmic filaments distinguish CDM from WDM?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: intrinsic 21cm brightness-temperature maps from hydrodynamical runs.
    - Redshifts: `z=4` and `z=2.5`.
    - Facilities: SKA1-Low and SKA2-Low sensitivity expectations.
  - **Method:**
    - Generate CDM and WDM filament maps.
    - Smooth them with beams of different angular resolution.
    - Compare preserved morphology against observational sensitivity.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Intrinsic maps show clear CDM-WDM filament differences, especially at `z=4`.
    - Beam sizes larger than `6 arcsec` erase the small-scale dark-matter signal.
    - Planned SKA-Low facilities cannot directly resolve individual filaments with enough sensitivity.
    - Direct filamentary 21cm morphology is unlikely to constrain dark matter soon.
  - **Position:** relevant to cosmic-web survey design because it quantifies a hard resolution-sensitivity limitation.

- [2609.03779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03779) Nicolas Baron Perez et al. `Morphology of Radio Sources in Representation Space`
  - **Question:** can open-set representation learning identify rare radio morphologies in very large surveys?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: LoTSS-DR3 with more than `13 million` sources.
    - Prior model: self-supervised representation and classifiers trained from LoTSS-DR2.
    - Target subset: low-probability classifications in representation space.
  - **Method:**
    - Apply classifier ensembles and inspect representation-space clusters.
    - Use low-confidence source clusters as centroids for new classes.
    - Quantify the final class distribution and rare-class skew.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - `88%` of sources fall into known, clearly separated clusters.
    - Six additional rare morphological classes are found.
    - Four dominant classes make up `69%` of the DR3 sample.
    - Future radio surveys need open-set approaches because rare classes are severely imbalanced.
  - **Position:** useful for radio-AGN and environment work because rare morphologies may trace special jet or host conditions.
