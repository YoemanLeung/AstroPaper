## Little Red Dots, Early Black Holes, and Reionization

- [2609.00112](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00112) Dale D. Kocevski et al. `Skyfire: A Spectroscopic Census of Little Red Dots and Broad-Line AGN in the CEERS Field`
  - **Question:** how common are broad emission lines among photometrically selected LRDs, LBD-like sources, and compact high-redshift AGN candidates in CEERS?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: `21` hours of JWST/NIRSpec G395M observations across five CEERS pointings.
    - Sample: spectroscopic redshifts for `178` Skyfire sources.
    - Broad-line catalog: `34` broad-line sources at `2.7<z<6.5`, including `18` LRDs.
  - **Method:**
    - Target LRDs, blue EELGs, and X-ray AGN candidates from CEERS.
    - Measure spectroscopic redshifts and broad-line incidence.
    - Compare broad-line detection against rest-optical continuum slope and observed colors.
    - Revise LRD color selection to include bluer sources.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - LRD spectroscopic completeness for `beta_opt>-0.02` in CEERS reaches `73%`.
    - Broad-line incidence remains high into bluer rest-optical colors, supporting continuity between LRDs and LBDs.
    - Only `18%` of high-equivalent-width compact EELGs show broad lines, so EELG-like selection alone is a poor broad-line predictor.
    - Extending the selection to `beta_opt=-0.52` yields a broad-line fraction of `80.9%` for F444W-bright LRD candidates and makes LRDs `54%` of the CEERS broad-line population.
  - **Position:** read first because it directly constrains the spectroscopic selection function behind current LRD/LBD black-hole claims.

- [2609.00113](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00113) Vinh Tran et al. `Weighing Little Red Dots with Transient Events`
  - **Question:** can stellar transient rates distinguish overmassive LRD black holes from lower-mass or scaling-relation alternatives?
  - **Data:**
    - Target population: compact red JWST sources at `z>4`.
    - Diagnostics: TDE and QPE rates produced by stars interacting with gas envelopes around black holes.
    - Observational path: future Euclid, Roman, and LSST wide-field transient surveys.
  - **Method:**
    - Compute TDE and QPE rates for three black-hole mass scenarios.
    - Include the effect of steep stellar cusps.
    - Compare predicted event rates per square degree across scenarios.
    - Treat light curves as additional mass and gas-structure diagnostics.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - TDE rates differ from `1.96e-3` to `3.37e-2 yr^-1 deg^-2` across the tested scenarios.
    - QPE rates differ from `1.64e-2` to `4.96e-1 yr^-1 deg^-2`.
    - Transient statistics can separate genuinely overmassive LRD black holes from lower-mass hidden populations.
    - Wide-field surveys can test LRD mass assumptions without relying only on broad-line virial estimates.
  - **Position:** high-value LRD follow-up because it offers an independent observable for the black-hole mass debate.

- [2609.00115](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00115) Junehyoung Jeon et al. `Early Supermassive Black Holes and Little Red Dots Require Free-Fall Growth`
  - **Question:** can early SMBHs and LRD-like compact AGN grow fast enough without cold free-fall fueling?
  - **Data:**
    - Targets: JWST early SMBHs, AGN, and LRDs with black-hole masses above `10^6 Msun`.
    - Halo comparison: rare halos with number densities near `1 Gpc^-3`.
    - Growth channel: cold-mode accretion through cosmic streams.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare baryon delivery through cold streams against shock-heated gas inflow.
    - Connect halo abundance to observed massive quasars and LRD number densities.
    - Estimate whether enough gas reaches the central potential on a free-fall timescale.
    - Interpret declining LRD abundance as a possible end of efficient cold-mode supply.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Cold-mode accretion is required for observed early SMBH masses in this argument.
    - Shock-heated inflow would need implausibly rare host halos.
    - Rare cold-flow halos can match massive quasar abundances.
    - Halos able to support super-Eddington growth for `~10^7 Msun` black holes can match LRD number densities.
  - **Position:** useful theory framing because it connects LRD abundance to large-scale gas supply rather than only seed mass.

- [2609.00437](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00437) Sunmyon Chon et al. `Formation of Heavy Seed Black Holes and Little Red Dots-like Compact Clusters in Metal-enriched Star-forming Regions`
  - **Question:** can heavy black-hole seeds and LRD-like spectra form in weakly enriched clustered regions rather than pristine direct-collapse sites?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: cosmological radiation-hydrodynamic zoom-in calculation with rapidly accreting supermassive stars.
    - Environments: clustered star-forming regions with internal Lyman-Werner intensity near `J_21~1000`.
    - Cluster sample: `8` massive clusters with stellar masses above `10^6 Msun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Follow metal enrichment, radiation fields, and supermassive-star growth self-consistently.
    - Track heavy-seed formation in halos above `10^9 Msun`.
    - Build cluster spectra with MIST/PHOENIX stellar libraries and bloated-supermassive-star blackbodies.
    - Compare compact-cluster spectra with LRD-like V-shaped continua.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Most heavy seeds form in weakly enriched gas with `[Z/H]~-3` to `-2`.
    - Every halo above `10^9 Msun` in the zoom-in region hosts a black hole above `10^5 Msun`.
    - Five of eight massive clusters show intrinsically V-shaped spectra similar to LRDs.
    - Heavy seeds, compact clusters, and LRD-like continua can be linked outcomes of enriched clustered environments.
  - **Position:** strong LRD-seeding paper because it weakens the need for pristine direct-collapse conditions.

- [2609.00199](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00199) L. Napolitano et al. `The gradual decline of Lyalpha visibility in the CANDELS fields: evidence for the combined effects of galaxy evolution and reionization`
  - **Question:** how much of the high-redshift Lyalpha visibility decline is IGM attenuation rather than changing galaxy properties?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: public JWST/NIRSpec PRISM spectroscopy across the five CANDELS fields.
    - Catalog: `3446` spectroscopically confirmed sources at `4<=z<14.2`.
    - LAE subset: `539` robust Lyalpha emitters with `S/N>3`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure the fraction of galaxies with rest-frame Lyalpha equivalent width above `25 Angstrom`.
    - Split the visibility measurement into two UV luminosity bins.
    - Compare LAE physical properties against the full star-forming population.
    - Fit a framework that combines galaxy evolution with IGM attenuation.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - In the fainter UV bin, Lyalpha visibility rises from `z=5` to `z=6` and then declines at higher redshift.
    - LAE stellar mass, UV slope, reddening, SFR, metallicity, sSFR, and burstiness remain approximately stable with redshift.
    - The full star-forming population evolves toward the LAE-like region of galaxy-property space at earlier times.
    - Gradual reionization histories are favored over rapid neutral-fraction jumps.
  - **Position:** important LAE/reionization entry because it separates intrinsic galaxy evolution from IGM transmission.

- [2609.01205](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01205) Ryan A. Cooper et al. `The Physical Conditions of Low-Mass Galaxies at z=3.5-7.0 from JWST Spectroscopy: The Behaviour of Spectral Line Ratios with Burstiness`
  - **Question:** how does bursty star formation alter emission-line diagnostics in low-mass galaxies from reionization to cosmic noon?
  - **Data:**
    - Spectroscopy: JWST/NIRSpec emission-line diagnostics.
    - Sample: `1889` star-forming galaxies at `z=3.5-7.0`.
    - Low-mass subset: `1552` galaxies below `10^9 Msun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure Halpha/Hbeta, [OIII]/Hbeta, [OII]/Hbeta, and related line ratios.
    - Track median line-ratio evolution with redshift and stellar mass.
    - Compare scatter and extrema between high-burstiness and low-burstiness systems.
    - Interpret the line ratios as enrichment and outflow diagnostics.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Median Halpha/Hbeta and [OIII]/Hbeta remain nearly constant from `z=3.5` to `z=7.0`.
    - Stellar-mass trends follow chemical enrichment and dust build-up expectations.
    - High-burstiness galaxies show larger scatter in [OIII]/Hbeta and [OII]/Hbeta.
    - Metallicity estimates in bursty galaxies can be unstable, so metal-poor searches should favor low-burstiness or very low-mass systems.
  - **Position:** high-priority JWST spectroscopy paper because it calibrates line-ratio interpretation for early low-mass galaxies.

- [2609.01586](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01586) C. A. Pirie et al. `The JWST Emission Line Survey (JELS): A narrow-band determination of the Halpha Luminosity Function and Cosmic Star Formation into the Epoch of Reionization`
  - **Question:** what does a narrow-band Halpha luminosity function imply for star formation at `z~6.1`?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: JWST Emission Line Survey.
    - Sample: `39` Halpha emitters at `z~6.1`.
    - Comparisons: slitless measurements and cosmological simulation predictions.
  - **Method:**
    - Build observed and dust-corrected Halpha luminosity functions.
    - Combine luminosity-function datasets to constrain the faint-end slope.
    - Integrate luminosity functions into Halpha-based star-formation-rate density.
    - Test metallicity dependence in the Halpha SFR calibration.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The high-redshift Halpha faint-end slope is constrained to `-1.79<alpha_Halpha<-1.62`.
    - The dust-corrected SFR density is near `log10(rho_SFR)=-1.93` or `-2.00` depending on dust assumptions.
    - A metallicity-dependent calibration lowers the inferred SFR density by `0.43 dex`.
    - Narrow-band JWST surveys add constraints on faint Halpha populations during the EoR.
  - **Position:** useful reionization-era benchmark because it probes star formation with Halpha rather than only UV luminosity.

## CGM, AGN Feedback, and Nuclear Gas

- [2609.00151](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00151) Yong Zheng et al. `The Halo Gas of Local Spiral Galaxies and the Link to Gaseous Satellites`
  - **Question:** do gaseous satellites measurably contribute to the cool CGM of local spiral galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Host sample: `21` local spirals within `15 Mpc`.
    - Satellite data: existing HI surveys down to `M_HI>~10^7 Msun`.
    - CGM probes: `26` HST/COS QSO sightlines at `0.1-0.9 R_200c`.
  - **Method:**
    - Characterize each host's gaseous satellite population.
    - Measure ion columns and detection rates along CGM sightlines.
    - Split hosts by gaseous-satellite presence.
    - Compare CGM columns with satellite number, satellite HI mass, and sightline proximity.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Most hosts have no more than `3` gaseous satellites above the adopted HI threshold.
    - The cool CGM mass is `2.5e9 Msun` scaled by metallicity, with `1-2 dex` intrinsic scatter.
    - Hosts with gaseous satellites show metal-ion detection-rate enhancements up to `50%`.
    - CGM column density does not correlate strongly with satellite number or total satellite HI mass.
  - **Position:** relevant low-redshift anchor for galaxy-CGM connection work because it separates satellite contribution from large intrinsic CGM scatter.

- [2609.00278](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00278) Francisco Pozo Nunez et al. `Accretion-disk sizes in two quasars with interferometrically resolved broad-line regions at z=2.3 and z=4.0`
  - **Question:** how do accretion-disk sizes compare with interferometric broad-line-region sizes in the most luminous high-redshift quasars?
  - **Data:**
    - Targets: SDSS J092034.17+065718.0 at `z=2.33` and SMSS J052915.80-435152.0 at `z=3.96`.
    - Luminosity regime: `L_bol~10^48 erg s^-1`.
    - Constraints: continuum lags plus GRAVITY/GRAVITY+ BLR sizes and dynamical masses.
  - **Method:**
    - Monitor medium-band continuum variability with the MPG/ESO `2.2 m` telescope.
    - Measure wavelength-dependent continuum reverberation lags.
    - Infer UV disk sizes and compare with thin-disk predictions.
    - Compare disk scales with interferometric Hbeta and Halpha BLR radii.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - UV disk sizes are `4.35` light-days for J0529 and `3.15` light-days for J0920.
    - J0920 remains consistent with standard thin-disk expectations despite high Eddington ratio.
    - J0529 implies disk inflation if the lower GRAVITY+ dynamical mass is adopted.
    - BLR-to-disk size ratios reach `~270` for J0529 and `~115` for J0920.
  - **Position:** strong quasar-structure paper because it joins reverberation and interferometry at high redshift.

- [2609.00182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00182) R. Middei et al. `Local Heavily Obscured Active Galaxies Observed With Chandra (I): Spectral Properties of the Nuclear and Extended X-ray Emission`
  - **Question:** what are the nuclear and extended X-ray spectral properties of nearby heavily obscured AGN?
  - **Data:**
    - Telescope: deep Chandra/ACIS observations above `100 ks`.
    - Sample: nearby obscured AGN with extra-nuclear extended emission.
    - Absorption regime: nuclear columns above `log(N_H/cm^-2)>23`.
  - **Method:**
    - Separate unresolved nuclear emission from spatially extended regions.
    - Fit spectra with photoionized and collisionally ionized plasma components.
    - Compare spectral parameters between nuclear and extra-nuclear zones.
    - Measure hard X-ray excess and Fe Kalpha extension.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Extended X-ray spectra usually require both photoionized and thermal components.
    - Nuclear regions tend to host higher-column and higher-ionization gas.
    - Hard X-ray emission beyond nuclear PSF spillover appears in `19/20` sources.
    - Extended Fe Kalpha emission appears in `12/20` systems.
  - **Position:** useful AGN-feedback reference because it gives a homogeneous local baseline for extended high-energy gas.

- [2609.01407](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01407) A. Cattaneo et al. `Energy deposited by black holes in the hot X-ray gas of elliptical galaxies, groups and clusters`
  - **Question:** how much central black-hole energy is deposited as heat in hot halo gas?
  - **Data:**
    - Systems: elliptical galaxies, galaxy groups, and clusters.
    - Observable basis: X-ray luminosity-halo mass relation and hot-gas entropy.
    - Reference: entropy from cosmological adiabatic simulations at fixed halo mass.
  - **Method:**
    - Model hot gas with core entropy as the single free parameter.
    - Estimate entropy excess relative to adiabatic expectations.
    - Convert entropy excess into deposited heat.
    - Compare required heat with semi-empirical SMBH energy histories.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - SMBHs thermalize roughly `1-3%` of their energy output into surrounding gas.
    - In ellipticals and groups, AGN heating can outpace cooling and produce the observed entropy excess.
    - Quenching occurs when accumulated heat can unbind the gas reservoir.
    - In clusters above `M_200>10^14 Msun`, heating and cooling approach self-regulated equilibrium.
  - **Position:** relevant feedback paper because it expresses AGN quenching efficiency as an energy-coupling constraint.

- [2609.00496](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00496) Jochen Heidt et al. `Detection of a triple nucleus in the Wolf-Rayet composite Sy 2 galaxy NGC 6764`
  - **Question:** does NGC 6764 contain a compact multiple nucleus that could include more than one AGN?
  - **Data:**
    - Imaging: adaptive-optics Ks-band LUCI/SOUL observations at the Large Binocular Telescope.
    - Target: composite Seyfert 2 and Wolf-Rayet galaxy NGC 6764 at `D=32 Mpc`.
    - Resolved structure: three compact sources separated by `11.0-30.9 pc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Resolve the nuclear region in near-infrared adaptive-optics imaging.
    - Compare H, Ks, H2, and Br-gamma colors for the three compact sources.
    - Evaluate star-formation and AGN interpretations.
    - Identify the spectroscopy needed to classify each source.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The nucleus resolves into three compact sources with very small projected separations.
    - Near-infrared colors cannot distinguish accretion from star formation.
    - A Seyfert nucleus plus two star-forming regions is plausible.
    - A dual or triple AGN remains possible and would be unusually compact.
  - **Position:** lower-priority but notable AGN-demographics entry because it flags an extreme compact multiple-nucleus candidate.

## Galaxy Structure, Surveys, and Low-Redshift Anchors

- [2609.01152](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01152) Jurgen J. Popp et al. `The fraction of clumpy star-forming galaxies in nearby galaxies from CLAUDS and HSC-SSP`
  - **Question:** how common are off-center star-forming clumps in low-redshift galaxies when measured with wide-field survey imaging?
  - **Data:**
    - Surveys: CLAUDS plus HSC-SSP in XMM-LSS, E-COSMOS, and DEEP2-3.
    - Sample: `5395` mass-complete star-forming galaxies at `z<=0.32`.
    - Detected clumps: `12790` star-forming clumps.
  - **Method:**
    - Detect clumps with a deep-learning framework using ZOOBOT features.
    - Define clump hosts using a u-band clump-to-galaxy flux ratio of at least `8%`.
    - Measure redshift, stellar-mass, and sSFR trends in the clumpy fraction.
    - Test an alternate clump definition above `10^7 Msun`.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The off-center clumpy fraction decreases from `~31%` at `z~0.3` to `~23%` at `z~0.1`.
    - At fixed redshift, clumpiness decreases with stellar mass and increases with sSFR.
    - A mass-threshold definition raises the clumpy fraction to `~60%`.
    - Low-redshift clumpy analogs can be measured in survey-scale samples.
  - **Position:** useful morphology anchor because it ties HSC/CLAUDS imaging to clump statistics often interpreted at high redshift.

- [2609.00154](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00154) Margherita Grespan et al. `The promise of self-supervised and active learning for Strong Lens discovery: Astronomaly applied to KiDS`
  - **Question:** can human-in-the-loop active learning find strong lenses without simulated supervised training sets?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: KiDS DR4.
    - Parent sample: `3.7` million bright galaxies.
    - Human inspection: `3000` objects across active-learning rounds.
  - **Method:**
    - Extract image features with an ImageNet-pretrained CNN fine-tuned on KiDS with BYOL.
    - Use Astronomaly active learning to prioritize informative candidates.
    - Expand discoveries through nearest-neighbor similarity in feature space.
    - Compare discoveries with known KiDS lenses.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Active learning identifies `34` grade A/B lens candidates.
    - Similarity expansion yields `140` grade A/B candidates and more than `1000` lower-confidence systems.
    - `81` high-quality candidates are newly identified.
    - The method reduces dependence on simulated lens training sets for Euclid, Roman, and Rubin-era searches.
  - **Position:** relevant survey-method paper because it improves rare-object discovery in wide-field imaging without simulation-dominated labels.

- [2609.00283](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00283) Jiaxuan Li et al. `ELVES-Dwarf. II. A Systematic Search for Satellite Systems of Dwarf Galaxies in the Local Volume`
  - **Question:** what is the satellite abundance around isolated dwarf hosts in the Local Volume?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: ELVES-Dwarf.
    - Hosts: `39` mostly isolated galaxies with `10^7<Mstar<10^10 Msun`.
    - Imaging: `155 deg^2` of Legacy Surveys data plus Subaru/HSC, Magellan/IMACS, and Gemini/GMOS follow-up.
  - **Method:**
    - Search for candidates within projected virial radii.
    - Confirm membership with surface-brightness-fluctuation distances, TRGB distances, and radial velocities.
    - Compare satellite abundance and mass functions with TNG50 and galaxy-formation models.
    - Measure quenched fractions around dwarf hosts.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The survey confirms `39` satellites from `207` candidates.
    - Above `Mstar~10^5.7 Msun`, `21` hosts have no confirmed satellites and only two have four.
    - Satellite abundance and stellar mass functions broadly match model predictions.
    - Satellite quenched fractions are lower around dwarf hosts than around Milky-Way-mass hosts.
  - **Position:** useful low-mass environment benchmark because it measures environmental quenching in the dwarf-host regime.

- [2609.00526](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00526) Sarah Pearson et al. `Extragalactic Stellar Streams in Time-Dependent Cosmological Halos`
  - **Question:** can extragalactic stellar streams recover host halo properties when halos evolve with time?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: FIRE-2 `m12m` cosmological zoom-in.
    - Mock system: an extragalactic host with three stellar streams.
    - Tool: X-Stream fitting of observed stream morphology and kinematics.
  - **Method:**
    - Mock observe three streams in a cosmological halo.
    - Fit each stream with static-halo models.
    - Compare recovered orbits and halo parameters with simulation ground truth.
    - Test the effect of adding progenitor radial velocities and fainter debris.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Two streams are reproduced reasonably with static-halo stripping near pericenter.
    - The longest stream gives unbiased constraints on progenitor and halo properties.
    - The shortest stream needs fainter extended debris for useful progenitor and halo constraints.
    - The most massive stream yields biased parameters when time-dependence is ignored.
  - **Position:** relevant structure paper because it identifies where stream morphology can and cannot constrain dark halos.

- [2609.00477](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00477) Sam Rose et al. `SPIRITS 19q: Dust Production by a Subsolar-metallicity Carbon-rich Wolf-Rayet Star in NGC 2403`
  - **Question:** how much dust can a single subsolar-metallicity WC binary candidate produce?
  - **Data:**
    - Telescope: JWST/NIRSpec IFU observations from April `2024`.
    - Target: SPIRITS 19q in NGC 2403.
    - Event context: a dusty outburst detected by Spitzer in `2019`.
  - **Method:**
    - Confirm the association between the infrared transient and an early-type WC star.
    - Model the WC star with PoWR LMC grid atmospheres.
    - Estimate dust mass from Spitzer/IRAC peak flux and JWST spectral constraints.
    - Convert the event dust mass into a period-averaged dust-production rate.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The inferred WC mass-loss rate is at least `10^-4 Msun yr^-1`.
    - The outburst formed `6.6e-6 Msun` of dust.
    - For an orbital period above `12 yr`, the average dust-production rate is below `5.5e-7 Msun yr^-1`.
    - WC binaries can contribute carbonaceous dust at subsolar metallicity.
  - **Position:** useful early-dust analog because it gives a JWST-calibrated stellar dust source in low-metallicity conditions.
