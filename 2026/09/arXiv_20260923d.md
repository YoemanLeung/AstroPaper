## Key Findings

- DLA metallicity tracks Lyalpha-selected surroundings only tentatively; missing counterparts matter ([2609.25221](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25221) Grecco A. Oyarzun). CGM enrichment also depends on HI column density ([2609.26785](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26785) Saloni Deepak).
- Spectroscopy reveals voids missed in projection ([2609.25455](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25455) Mariko Kubo), qualifying how to interpret projected LAE size-environment null results ([2609.25886](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25886) Ronaldo Laishram).
- Density raises small-scale IGM opacity; radiation boosts larger-scale ionization, with substantial mock-survey overlap and variance ([2609.26483](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26483) Hamish Anderson).
- LRD calcium absorption permits multiple atmosphere solutions ([2609.25349](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25349) Jenny E. Greene); absent central AGN gas depletion remains conditional on resolution and active-phase selection ([2609.25371](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25371) Scott Wilkinson).
- Rising NaI incidence constrains host abundance times gas cross-section, not total HI mass evolution ([2609.26794](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26794) Abhijeet Anand).

## Absorber-selected gas, enrichment, and environments

- [2609.25221](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25221) Grecco A. Oyarzun et al. `The KAGG survey: KCWI Analysis of Gas around Galaxies at z~3-5`
  - **Question:** How do DLA metallicity and Lyalpha-emitting surroundings trace host galaxies and their environments?
  - **Data:**
    - Keck/KCWI: `36` DLAs in `17` quasar fields at `z~3-5`, selected without a metallicity cut; complementary MAGG/MUSE emitters.
  - **Method:**
    - Subtract quasar/background light, search emission lines, and quantify detection completeness.
    - Separate candidate DLA counterparts from group and wider-velocity associations; compare incidence with survey simulations and halo models.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - KAGG identifies `7` candidate DLA Lyalpha counterparts. The combined `11`-object KAGG+MAGG counterpart sample has a tentative `1.8-sigma` metallicity-impact-parameter anticorrelation.
    - The combined group sample has `35` emitters and a `2.3-sigma` impact-parameter trend; these are distinct from the `17` KAGG large-scale emitters. The classes overlap.
    - None of the `5` known [CII] emitters in the DLA sample is detected in Lyalpha. Faint hosts, resonant scattering, dust, and redshift-dependent sensitivity remain alternatives for non-detections.
  - **Position:** Extends MAGG emission-absorption comparisons; the connection between metallicity and halo mass is model-supported rather than a direct halo-mass measurement.

- [2609.26785](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26785) Saloni Deepak et al. `The BRIDGE Survey. I. Metallicities and Physical Properties of Gas Probing the Circumgalactic Medium at Cosmic Noon`
  - **Question:** When did cool CGM-like gas become enriched, and does that history depend on absorber density?
  - **Data:**
    - HST UV plus Keck/HIRES and VLT/UVES optical spectra: `105` HI-selected absorbers at `1<=z<=2.2`, spanning `16.4<=log N_HI<20.3` with columns in `cm^-2`.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit metal/HI columns with Bayesian Cloudy photoionization grids; compare consistently with CCC and KODIAQ-Z.
    - Test UV-background and ionic-phase systematics; exclude generally distinct OVI/NV absorption from the cool-gas models.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Median `[X/H]=-1.19` is close to the `z<1` value `-1.18`, compared with `-2.02` at `z>2.2`. Diffuse absorbers reach the low-redshift enrichment level earlier than denser systems.
    - Mean HI columns differ by roughly `400` across classes, but total hydrogen columns differ by only `2.5`: neutral fraction is essential to interpreting the sequence.
    - Switching HM05 to HM12 raises inferred metallicities by `0.16+/-0.13 dex` on average. No pristine gas is established in this sample, but metallicity upper limits prevent a universal absence claim.
  - **Position:** Bridges CCC and KODIAQ-Z across cosmic noon; CGM-like is an absorption/physical-condition classification, not a complete set of confirmed galaxy-halo associations.

- [2609.26794](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26794) Abhijeet Anand et al. `The First Blind Survey of Intervening NaI Absorbers: Incidence and Cosmic Evolution with DESI`
  - **Question:** How does the incidence of dense neutral-gas NaI absorbers evolve in the nearby Universe?
  - **Data:**
    - DESI DR1: `3,636` NaI doublets on `214,035` quasar sightlines at `0.01<z_abs<0.27`.
  - **Method:**
    - Model quasar continua, detect doublets, and measure apparent-optical-depth columns.
    - Use injection-recovery completeness and visual purity estimates to correct incidence and NaI mass density; inspect reddening and candidate foreground hosts.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Completeness reaches `50%` at `W_5891=0.95 Angstrom`; catalog-weighted purity is about `77%`.
    - For `0.95<W_5891<3.5 Angstrom`, incidence rises by about `3.4` from `z~0.25` to `z~0.03`; NaI mass density follows similar evolution.
    - The approximately constant mass-density/incidence ratio indicates little change in mean measured NaI column. Incidence constrains `n_com*sigma_eff`, leaving host abundance and cross-section degenerate; NaI is not total HI.
  - **Position:** Adds a selection-calibrated low-redshift absorber census complementary to HI and metal-line surveys; host associations remain exploratory.

## Three-dimensional structure, LAE morphology, and reionization

- [2609.25455](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25455) Mariko Kubo et al. `PEGASUS (Pfs Emission-line GAlaxy SUrvey with Subaru): I. Three-dimensional large-scale structures at z > 1 probed with emission-line galaxies`
  - **Question:** How well can narrowband-selected galaxies reconstruct the three-dimensional cosmic web beyond redshift one?
  - **Data:**
    - Subaru/HSC narrowband targets and PFS spectroscopy over about `1.8 deg^2` in COSMOS, with line fluxes `>=2e-17 erg s^-1 cm^-2`.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit and visually verify emission-line redshifts; identify groups and filamentary structure in spectroscopic density maps.
    - Compare the reconstructed volume distribution with projected narrowband maps.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Of `9,917` targets, `9,079` were observed; redshifts succeed for `92%` of observed targets. This is not completeness of all galaxies in the field.
    - At `z=1.19,1.47,1.60`, samples of roughly `520-920` [OII] emitters per slice above `M_star~10^9.5 Msun` trace groups with inferred halo masses `1e12-6e13 Msun`; dense filament intersections host the most massive groups.
    - Volume maps recover voids hidden in projection. Emission-line selection and limited area remain explicit limits; the manuscript also flags preliminary NIR-arm reduction/sky-subtraction performance.
  - **Position:** Builds on the Hayashi+2020 HSC narrowband catalog and provides a spectroscopic basis for subsequent environment studies rather than a completed environmental-evolution analysis.

- [2609.25886](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25886) Ronaldo Laishram et al. `Rest-Frame UV and Optical Structural Evolution of Narrowband-Selected Lyman-alpha Emitters at z = 2-7 with JWST/NIRCam`
  - **Question:** Do LAE optical sizes track redshift, Lyalpha strength, or large-scale environment?
  - **Data:**
    - SILVERRUSH LAEs with COSMOS-Web NIRCam imaging in F115W/F150W/F277W/F444W: `770` objects at `z=2.18-6.99`, with `766` at `z<=6.58` in quantitative analyses.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit Sersic profiles and rest-frame UV/optical size evolution; compare size-mass and size-EW relations.
    - Test fifth-nearest-neighbor and Voronoi projected LAE densities.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Median optical/UV size ratio is `1.14 (+0.01/-0.02)`; optical size follows `R_e proportional to (1+z)^(-0.94+/-0.09)`.
    - Smaller sizes correlate weakly with higher Lyalpha EW (`rho_s=-0.167`), mainly through marginally resolved compact sources. High inferred SFR surface density partly follows `Sigma_SFR proportional to R_e^-2` by construction.
    - No significant size-density correlation appears (`rho_s=0.05, p=0.18` for nearest neighbors; `rho_s=0.03, p=0.48` for Voronoi). Projection, high-redshift sample size, and extreme-environment coverage limit this null result.
  - **Position:** Links narrowband LAEs to optical structure; the correlations establish neither an escape mechanism nor universal environmental independence.

- [2609.26483](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26483) Hamish Anderson et al. `Exploring the influence of reionisation-era galaxies on their local intergalactic medium using the Sherwood-Relics simulations`
  - **Question:** Does excess Lyalpha-forest transmission near reionization-era galaxies isolate their ionizing influence?
  - **Data:**
    - Sherwood-Relics hybrid radiation-hydrodynamic simulations, mock [OIII] emitters, and ASPIRE-like quasar-forest geometry near `z~5.4-6.2`.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare effective-optical-depth distributions near mock galaxies and random IGM regions across influence radii and redshift.
    - Vary emitter halo thresholds at fixed number density and repeat mock realizations to assess cosmic variance.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At `r<=5 cMpc/h`, overdensity raises opacity; at `15<r<=50 cMpc/h`, enhanced photoionization favors more ionized surroundings. These are comoving scales.
    - Near-galaxy and random opacity distributions overlap substantially; differences are not statistically significant in the mock-survey KS tests. Large-radius samples can contain both low- and high-opacity tails.
    - The modeled discrimination requires about `4x` the ASPIRE forest path for small radii and at least `10x` for larger radii. These are model-dependent survey estimates, not measured detection significances.
  - **Position:** Tests the ASPIRE/Jin+2024 interpretation and retains the source-clustering degeneracy discussed by Garaldi+2025: the selected bright galaxies need not supply all of the local ionization.

## Nuclear gas and the scales of feedback

- [2609.25349](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25349) Jenny E. Greene et al. `Calcium Triplet Absorption is Common around Little Red Dots`
  - **Question:** Can calcium triplet absorption constrain the gas surrounding Little Red Dot central engines?
  - **Data:**
    - Archival JWST/NIRSpec grating spectra of `17` LRDs at `2<z<5`, covering the rest-frame calcium triplet near `8500 Angstrom`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure individual absorption and stack spectra; compare CaT strengths with atmosphere grids.
    - Evaluate stellar-light dilution, metallicity/temperature changes, and departures from hydrostatic equilibrium.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - CaT is detected in `3` individual LRDs; the stack has `EW_CaT~-5 Angstrom`. This does not mean every source has an individually detected absorber.
    - Hotter, metal-poor hydrostatic atmospheres (`T_eff>4500 K`, `[M/H]<-1`) can reproduce weaker absorption, while a low-density alternative (`rho_ph<1e-11 g cm^-3`) requires non-hydrostatic structure.
    - A central-engine photosphere is favored, but external absorbing gas is not excluded. Absorption location, broadening, and dynamics remain obstacles to enclosed-mass estimates.
  - **Position:** Extends the local Egg analogy to a high-redshift sample; simultaneous continuum and absorption modeling is needed to distinguish the atmosphere solutions.

- [2609.25224](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25224) Xihan Ji et al. `Gone with the wind? Potential gas inflow in the broad-line region of a QSO at z=0.287 traced by time-varying Balmer absorption`
  - **Question:** Does variable redshifted Balmer absorption reveal gas feeding a quasar broad-line region?
  - **Data:**
    - DESI spectra of J115724.80-000455.56 at `z=0.287` across `10` observed-frame months; Einstein Probe X-ray observations and host imaging.
  - **Method:**
    - Decompose stationary and dynamic absorption with partial-covering fits.
    - Use Cloudy constraints and variability scales to test BLR-scale gas; compare optical and X-ray obscuring columns.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Both absorbers require total `N_H~1e22 cm^-2` and likely `n_H>=1e8 cm^-3`, implying effective sizes `<=1e-3 pc`. Redshifted velocities support an inflow interpretation.
    - The inferred inflow can be comparable to the single-epoch accretion rate, conditional on geometry. X-ray obscuration is higher, `N_H=4.0 (+1.6/-1.5)e23 cm^-2`, allowing an additional obscuring component.
  - **Position:** A time-domain complement to dense nuclear absorption studies; a merger-fed phase is an interpretation rather than a uniquely established trigger.

- [2609.25371](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25371) Scott Wilkinson et al. `SALVAGE II: no systematic molecular gas depletion in the central 2 kpc of AGN hosts`
  - **Question:** Are molecular reservoirs systematically depleted in the central regions of currently observable AGN?
  - **Data:**
    - SALVAGE SDSS-ALMA measurements of `70` AGN at `z<0.07`, selected with optical, mid-IR, and X-ray diagnostics; central apertures of `0.5-2 kpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Match inactive controls in stellar mass, SFR, and redshift.
    - Compare central gas fraction and surface density separately, then split by AGN diagnostic.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Central molecular surface density is enhanced by `0.12+/-0.05 dex`, while gas fraction is consistent with controls (`-0.02+/-0.04 dex`).
    - Two optical classes have tentative gas-fraction deficits at only `1.5` and `1.8 sigma`, without an accompanying clear surface-density deficit or luminosity explanation.
    - The result does not exclude sub-resolution feedback or depletion after AGN emission fades. It constrains resolved gas reservoirs during the selected active phase.
  - **Position:** Extends earlier small central-gas samples using matched controls; resolution and duty cycle remain central to interpreting an apparent absence of depletion.

## Multiphase gas response and reusable analysis methods

- [2609.26697](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26697) Clara C. de la Casa et al. `The molecular gas of four ram pressure stripped galaxies in the core of the Hydra I cluster`
  - **Question:** How does ram pressure change molecular gas before it becomes visibly stripped?
  - **Data:**
    - ALMA CO(1-0) and MeerKAT HI in four Hydra I core galaxies, at matched resolution near `15 arcsec` or `4 kpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare CO/HI/stellar morphology, molecular-to-atomic ratios, and gas deficiencies.
    - Place the galaxies against xCOLDGASS field and VIVA-VERTICO stripping samples, accounting for the low-mass target falling below reference mass limits.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Molecular disks are disturbed, but no CO is detected in the tails; the dwarf LEDA 753342 also lacks a molecular-gas detection. Non-detection is not proof of gas-free tails.
    - NGC 3312 and NGC 3314a appear earlier in the stripping sequence than NGC 3314b. Molecular rearrangement and possibly enhanced short-term star formation can precede direct molecular removal.
  - **Position:** Adds a matched-resolution multiphase comparison within one cluster substructure; the proposed accelerated consumption/quenching sequence needs future depletion-time tests.

- [2609.26715](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26715) Fergus R. Donnan et al. `JWST Observations of Starbursts: The motion of dust - PAH kinematics of M82 and NGC 253 with JWST/MIRI spectroscopy`
  - **Question:** Do PAHs move with molecular or ionized gas at the base of starburst winds?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/MIRI spectra of M82 and NGC 253: PAH features at `5.2,6.2,11.3 micron`, H2 rotational lines, [NeII], and hydrogen recombination lines.
  - **Method:**
    - Apply PCA tomography to recover velocity maps and separate rotating and outflowing components.
    - Compare PAH-feature kinematics with warm molecular and ionized gas.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - M82 is predominantly rotation-dominated in the observed field. In NGC 253, PAHs trace the wind-launching region while the warm molecular signal remains dominated by rotation.
    - NGC 253 PAH velocities resemble the faster ionized component more than the molecular gas; the `6.2 micron` feature reaches higher velocities than other PAH features. Preferential ionization in faster gas is a proposed explanation.
  - **Position:** Adds kinematic information to dust/gas morphology comparisons; PAHs cannot automatically be assigned the molecular-gas velocity field.

- [2609.26472](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26472) Mathieu Perron-Cormier et al. `Bayesian Galaxy Asymmetry`
  - **Question:** Can noisy HI asymmetry measurements retain nearly symmetric galaxies with calibrated uncertainties?
  - **Data:**
    - WALLABY HI datacubes and simulated Gaussian-noise realizations, including convolved spatial noise.
  - **Method:**
    - Derive a Bayesian posterior for squared-difference asymmetry and test posterior coverage.
    - Account for correlated noise through an effective dimensionality factor; compare with the previous frequentist correction.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Reliable asymmetries increase from `116` to `331`, recovering many low-asymmetry objects previously lost to the noise correction.
    - The derivation assumes uniform Gaussian noise. Its correlation treatment does not correct beam-smearing information loss, center uncertainty, or Poisson-noise optical/UV data.
  - **Position:** Improves the Deg et al. squared-asymmetry correction; extension to optical morphology requires separate noise and coverage validation.

- [2609.25200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25200) Martina Rossi et al. `CRIMSONS: An Online Tool for Modeling Chemical Enrichment with Stochastic IMF Sampling`
  - **Question:** Can stochastic sampling mimic chemically unusual first-star enrichment signatures?
  - **Data:**
    - CRIMSONS web/Python framework: individual-star enrichment for `30` elements from H to Zn, multiple IMFs/yield sets, and optional Pop III pre-enrichment.
  - **Method:**
    - Vary stellar population mass and IMF sampling while comparing yields and primordial enrichment histories.
    - Use a closed-box setup to isolate effects; gas flows and spatial mixing are omitted.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Poorly sampled top-heavy populations can generate abundance scatter above `1 dex` without a changing underlying IMF. Yield choices introduce additional element-dependent shifts.
    - Pop III pre-enrichment changes subsequent Pop II/I patterns and realization scatter; a metallicity floor is not an equivalent replacement. These are controlled enrichment experiments, not complete galaxy-evolution predictions.
  - **Position:** Complements the stochastic-enrichment work of de Bennassuti+2017 and Applebaum+2021; single abundance anomalies are not unique evidence for a particular first-star population.
