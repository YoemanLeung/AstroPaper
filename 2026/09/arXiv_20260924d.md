## Key Findings

- Low-ionization diagnostics need abundance and gas-phase corrections ([2609.27081](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27081) Caroline von Raesfeld).
- Metal-poor galaxies have more asymmetric stellar light, favoring external burst triggering ([2609.26887](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26887) Abby Mintz).
- Reionization bubble sizes and shapes depend on estimator and segmentation ([2609.28390](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28390) Meredith Neyer).
- Lensed photometry supports extended early quenching, with redshift and hidden-AGN uncertainties ([2609.28474](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28474) Richard Pan).
- Mock LRD lifetimes depend on unresolved gas and AGN-to-host contrast ([2609.26999](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26999) Patrick LaChance).

## Gas flows, morphology, and cosmic-noon line diagnostics

- [2609.27081](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27081) Caroline von Raesfeld et al. `CECILIA: Multi-line Constraints on Excess Nebular Emission from Low-Ionization Gas at Cosmic Noon`
  - **Question:** How much low-ionization emission at cosmic noon lies outside conventional HII-region models?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRSpec CECILIA: `19` star-forming galaxies at `z~2-3`, with [OI], [SII], and abundance-sensitive lines.
  - **Method:**
    - Use Cue photoionization models constrained by direct-method metallicities and higher-ionization lines; allow non-solar sulfur abundances.
    - Compare residual low-ionization emission with shock, turbulence, and diffuse-ionized-gas diagnostics.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Conventional HII regions underproduce `48.0+/-5.1%` of [SII] and `55.0+/-6.1%` of [OI]. Ignoring sub-solar S/O can hide this excess on diagnostic diagrams.
    - Diffuse gas is a plausible source, but local DIG relations fail; the estimated density is `~165 cm^-3`. Existing shock models do not establish shocks as the cause. The excess is model-unexplained emission, not a spatially measured DIG fraction; the diffuse density also comes from unresolved line ratios.
    - Assigning all [SII] to HII regions biases sulfur abundances high by typically `~0.06 dex`, reaching `~0.1 dex`; the correction is model-dependent.
  - **Position:** Extends CECILIA abundance work to the emitting gas phases; spatially resolved spectra are needed to identify the excess component.

- [2609.26887](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26887) Abby Mintz et al. `Galaxies Below the Fundamental Metallicity Relation Are Morphologically Disturbed: Merian Halpha Morphologies and DESI Metallicities`
  - **Question:** Does morphology explain metallicity residuals beyond stellar mass and specific star-formation rate?
  - **Data:**
    - Merian medium-band Halpha maps, HSC continuum imaging, and DESI DR1 spectra: about `2600` galaxies at `0.064<z<0.094`, reaching `M_star~10^8 M_sun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure non-parametric morphology and strong-line oxygen abundances; fit morphology against mass, sSFR, and metallicity.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At fixed mass and sSFR, lower-metallicity galaxies have more asymmetric continuum and Halpha emission. Adding metallicity raises explained continuum-asymmetry variance by `0.06-0.07`, versus `~0.01` for Halpha.
    - Disturbed older stars favor external burst triggering over a gas-only feedback response. This remains a statistical interpretation; fiber metallicities emphasize central gas rather than whole-galaxy abundances.
  - **Position:** Adds a resolved structural axis to FMR studies and a way to test burst triggers without directly detecting faint companions.

- [2609.26884](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26884) Alejandro J. Olvera et al. `DIISC-VII: Linking Radial Gas Motions to Anomalously Low Metallicity Star-forming Regions in the XUV Disk of NGC 3344`
  - **Question:** Are metal-poor outer-disk star-forming regions associated with inward-moving neutral gas?
  - **Data:**
    - VLA HI imaging and MMT optical spectroscopy of `76` HII regions in NGC 3344.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit tilted-ring HI kinematics with 3DBarolo; compare radial abundances with closed-box and bathtub chemical models.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The XUV disk beyond `R~6 kpc` has nearly twice the inner-disk metallicity scatter and mean inward radial motion of `~6 km/s`. The abundance gradient is `-0.378 dex/R25`.
    - The spatial association supports dilution and fueling by inflowing gas; the actual mixing is not directly observed. The asymmetry-derived `1.76 M_sun/yr` accretion estimate is an upper limit.
  - **Position:** A nearby resolved test of gas-accretion interpretations of anomalously low-metallicity regions, complementary to statistical FMR trends.


## Reionization geometry and the first galaxies

- [2609.28390](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28390) Meredith Neyer et al. `The Lumina Project: Morphology of Ionized Bubbles and Neutral Islands`
  - **Question:** How do three-dimensional ionized regions and late neutral islands change throughout reionization?
  - **Data:**
    - Lumina radiation-hydrodynamic simulation: a `(500 cMpc)^3` volume, analyzed on a `~0.39 cMpc` grid.
  - **Method:**
    - Segment ionized and neutral volumes with a three-dimensional watershed; measure effective radii, shape tensors, density, and radiation fields.
    - Compare volume-based sizes with line-of-sight mean-free-path estimators and test segmentation thresholds.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Volume-weighted characteristic ionized radii grow from `~1 cMpc` at `z>10` to `~10 cMpc` at `z~7-8` and `>100 cMpc` near the end of reionization. Watershed sizes exceed line-of-sight estimates by a factor of a few because the estimators measure different geometry.
    - Most bubbles remain prolate. A late volume-weighted oblate component comes from a few percolated structures and depends on segmentation; it is not a universal change in individual bubble shape.
    - Residual neutral islands peak near `~10 cMpc` at `z~5-6` and remain elongated. Counts near the grid scale are resolution-sensitive.
  - **Position:** Complements Lin+2016 and Lu+2024 estimator comparisons; useful for connecting LAE environments and 21-cm topology without equating unlike bubble-size definitions.

- [2609.26993](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26993) B. Das et al. `The birth and fate of stellar clumps in super-early galaxies`
  - **Question:** Can compact early stellar clumps form by disk fragmentation without mergers?
  - **Data:**
    - Five RAMSES-RT realizations of one isolated `1.5e10 M_sun` halo, evolved for `100 Myr` from `z=16`, with `3.6 pc` maximum resolution.
  - **Method:**
    - Vary supernova and radiative feedback; follow Toomre instability, clump structure, and intrinsic UV light.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Clumps reach `10^6-2e8 M_sun` and radii `7-50 pc`; unstable gas precedes the first stellar structures. Kinetic feedback lowers cumulative stellar mass by `30-40%` without preventing initial fragmentation.
    - In the fiducial run, the clump stellar-mass fraction falls from `0.41` to `0.13`, and intrinsic UV fraction from `0.40` to `0.10`, between `20` and `100 Myr`.
    - This establishes a viable pathway in one isolated halo, not its cosmological prevalence or long-term survival. The most compact observed systems remain unresolved.
  - **Position:** Connects JWST clump properties to in-situ disk instability while leaving accretion, mergers, and bound-cluster survival for broader simulations.

- [2609.26998](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26998) Boyuan Liu et al. `N/O-enhanced chemical enrichment by massive stars: the interplay of optically-thick winds and rotation`
  - **Question:** Can ordinary massive-star winds explain nitrogen-rich high-redshift galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - MESA single-star grids: `20-100 M_sun`, metallicity `Z=0.001-0.0045`, and initial spin `0-0.7` of critical; three high-redshift galaxies with WR signatures.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare yields with and without optically thick winds; mix ejecta with ambient gas and fit C, N, O, and He abundance patterns.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Rotating-star winds produce an early N-rich phase; optically thick winds drive a C/O-rich transition after `~3-4 Myr`. The joint abundance sequence matches RXCJ2248-ID, Sunburst Arc, and MARTA 4327.
    - For an SMC-like spin distribution, strong N/O enhancement lasts `~2-3 Myr` only with inefficient mixing, `M_ISM/M_star<=0.01`. Wind prescriptions and omission of binaries limit uniqueness.
  - **Position:** Builds on Sabhahit+2023 and Boco+2025 wind treatments; C/O and WR features help distinguish mechanisms that N/O alone cannot separate.

- [2609.28257](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28257) Jiashuo Zhang et al. `JWST evidence for a sharp "Cosmic Daybreak" at z = 15`
  - **Question:** Does the present high-redshift frontier imply a sudden onset of galaxy formation?
  - **Data:**
    - A compilation of JWST `z>10` stellar-age estimates, spectroscopic redshifts, photometric candidates, and published UV luminosity densities.
  - **Method:**
    - Translate fitted stellar ages into formation redshifts; compare a wave-dark-matter Jeans threshold with halo mass functions.
    - Map halos to UV luminosities using constant star formation and a fixed halo-to-stellar mass ratio of `125`.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The compiled ages converge toward a proposed onset near `z~15`; the calibrated wave-dark-matter model gives a boson mass of `~2.2e-22 eV`.
    - This is a conditional interpretation, not a confirmed cosmic cutoff or dark-matter detection. Stellar ages, heterogeneous survey selection, and the assumed star-formation histories control the inference; quoted mass errors do not encompass all those systematics.
  - **Position:** A falsifiable early-structure hypothesis; deeper spectroscopy and consistent age/selection modeling are required before interpreting the redshift frontier as evidence against LCDM.


## Quenching, nuclear structure, and early black-hole selection

- [2609.28474](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28474) Richard Pan et al. `A sub-100 pc view at z~5 of a Multiply-Imaged Massive Quiescent Galaxy`
  - **Question:** Must early massive galaxies become compact before they quench?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/VENUS NIRCam and HST imaging of triply lensed VENUS-CLJ0152-QG1; photometric `z=5.18+/-0.15`, with approximate magnifications `7`, `11`, and `0.6`.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine a lens reconstruction with spatially binned Prospector SED fits and medium-band emission-line maps; adopt a `20%` magnification uncertainty floor.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The two bright images imply `M_star~2.8-3.4e10 M_sun`, recent `SFR~4 M_sun/yr`, and half-light radii `~0.9-1.1 kpc`. Resolved ionized emission is offset from the stellar center.
    - The extended, low-sSFR system is consistent with quenching without a simultaneous compact central starburst. Redshift, star-formation history, and line identification are photometric; spectroscopy is still needed.
    - Absence of an obvious central unobscured AGN does not exclude an obscured or recently faded AGN, or prove that past compaction never occurred.
  - **Position:** Extends lensed quiescent-galaxy work toward z~5 and tests structural biases in unresolved samples; one system does not establish a population-wide density correction.

- [2609.27460](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27460) Jianyuan Luo et al. `Ubiquitous nuclear disks and bars embedded in massive galaxies in the cosmic morning`
  - **Question:** Are compact centers of massive high-redshift galaxies necessarily classical bulges?
  - **Data:**
    - TNG50: a mass-complete sample of `45` galaxies with `M_star>10^10.5 M_sun` at `z=3`.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine double-Sersic fits, isodensity ellipses, intrinsic shapes, stellar kinematics, and evolutionary tracks.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - More than `90%` favor a separate inner component; most centers are flattened or elongated and rotation-supported, with typical radii `<0.5 kpc` and Sersic indices near `1`.
    - Gas-rich compaction builds nuclear disks and bars first; spheroidal, dispersion-supported centers emerge much later, at `z<~1`. This is a TNG50 prediction rather than an observed high-z incidence.
  - **Position:** Refines the gas-rich compaction picture by separating central concentration from classical-bulge formation.

- [2609.27130](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27130) Shobita Satyapal et al. `Search for High-Ionization Nebular Emission (SHINE). I. A Systematically Selected [Ne V] Sample at z > 3 with JWST/NIRSpec PRISM`
  - **Question:** Which early accreting black holes are recovered by very-high-ionization emission?
  - **Data:**
    - SHINE: `25` vetted [NeV]3426 emitters at `z=3.059-9.444` selected from over `9000` archival JWST/NIRSpec PRISM spectra; NIRCam and Chandra comparisons.
  - **Method:**
    - Validate line fits; compare sensitivity-qualified line ratios, continuum properties, broad-line classifications, and X-ray matches.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Luminous [NeV] identifies a heterogeneous population missed in part by conventional diagnostics: `5/25` have adopted broad-line classifications and `6/25` have secure Chandra counterparts.
    - Most broad-line-AGN and LRD nondetections are too shallow to demonstrate a universal [NeV] deficit. Redder continua and stronger Balmer breaks do not establish AGN-driven quenching.
    - No significant evolution is seen in observed [NeV] incidence, but the heterogeneous archive does not deliver completeness-corrected intrinsic AGN demographics.
  - **Position:** A complementary selection of hard ionizing sources; high-ionization lines require sensitivity and gas-geometry controls before comparison with continuum-selected populations.

- [2609.26999](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26999) Patrick LaChance et al. `The lifetimes and properties of Little Red Dots in the AMBRA simulation`
  - **Question:** What determines how long a simulated galaxy appears as a little red dot?
  - **Data:**
    - AMBRA galaxies and black holes at `z=5-8`, with time-series mock photometry extending to `z=10`.
  - **Method:**
    - Generate SYNTHESIZER images with standard and gas-enshrouded AGN SEDs; apply observational LRD cuts and test unresolved AGN variability.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Gas-enshrouded emission is needed to produce faint LRDs with `F444W>26`. Even with a unity enshrouded duty cycle, high-z abundances remain low; `0.5 dex` luminosity scatter reduces but does not remove the discrepancy.
    - Lower-mass objects have typical selection-phase durations of `~30 Myr`, higher-mass compact hosts `~100 Myr`. Low-mass durations are upper limits because the gas-enshrouded phase is unresolved and could be much shorter.
  - **Position:** Connects ASTRID/BRAHMA seeding experiments to LRD observability; a color-selected phase is not the lifetime of a black hole or galaxy.

- [2609.26931](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26931) Anniek J. Gloudemans et al. `Radio Quasars in the Cosmic Dawn: A Spectroscopic Sample from the DESI Survey`
  - **Question:** Do radio-detected quasars at cosmic dawn have distinct UV spectral properties?
  - **Data:**
    - DESI high-redshift quasars and LoTSS-DR3 at `144 MHz`: `83` radio detections among `510` quasars with radio coverage, drawn from a `646`-quasar parent sample.
  - **Method:**
    - Use forced radio fitting and radio-loudness limits; compare UV equivalent widths, CIV shifts, BALs, and luminosity-matched lower-z quasars.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Radio luminosities of `~3e25-3e28 W/Hz` favor jet-dominated emission. The radio-loud fraction is bounded at `5-15%`; restricting to better radio coverage narrows it to `~6-9%`.
    - Radio detections have smaller average Lyalpha/CIV equivalent widths, but the weak-line excess is only `~1.3 sigma`. CIV-shift differences are not significant, and BAL absorption strength does not correlate with radio-loudness.
  - **Position:** Expands uniform early radio-quasar samples; nondetection limits and uncertain systemic redshifts constrain comparisons of winds and jets.

- [2609.28329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28329) C. Daoutis et al. `Investigating the ionization mechanisms powering the extreme emission lines in metal-poor galaxies`
  - **Question:** What powers the hardest optical and infrared lines in extremely metal-poor galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/MIRI and optical spectroscopy of `11` local extreme-emission metal-poor galaxies, with a broader `14`-object set for [NeV] incidence.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare low- and high-ionization diagnostics and photon budgets with stellar, shock, ULX, and composite AGN models.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Mid-IR [NeV] occurs in `6/14` objects in the expanded comparison, not `42%` of the primary 11-object sample.
    - Stellar emission plus a modeled `4-8%` AGN contribution to the ionizing budget can match the line set. Pure shocks overproduce low-ionization lines; ULXs fall short in HeII-ionizing photons by `~1-2 dex`.
    - The favored IMBH contribution is an ionization-model interpretation, not a direct black-hole mass measurement.
  - **Position:** Provides local analog constraints for interpreting high-ionization JWST spectra, with energetics supplementing line-ratio classification.


## Multiphase feedback and reusable inference methods

- [2609.26967](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26967) Prathamesh Tamhane et al. `Cavity-driven Gas Transport in Cool-Core Clusters: Implications for Multiphase Gas Cooling`
  - **Question:** Can cavity uplift supply the cold gas observed in cool-core cluster galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Chandra and multiphase-gas measurements for `24` cool-core clusters; cavities identified in `21` systems.
  - **Method:**
    - Estimate displaced hot-gas masses from cavity geometry and ambient density; compare molecular reservoirs, cooling times, and transport times.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The median molecular-to-displaced mass ratio is `~0.35`. Gas with short cooling times can reach `10-30 kpc` at `150-400 km/s`, allowing condensation near observed filaments.
    - Several reservoirs exceed what can cool during the present cavity generation, favoring accumulated feedback cycles or additional supply. Displaced mass is an upper bound on uplifted mass; cooling-efficiency assumptions and projection matter. These budgets test condensation feasibility rather than directly following hot gas into molecules.
  - **Position:** Tests uplift-assisted condensation through mass and time budgets; infrared luminosity is not a universal instantaneous cooling-rate tracer.

- [2609.28072](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28072) Elena Sofia Mangola et al. `Detection of metal absorption lines in quasar spectra: a neural network approach using U-Net`
  - **Question:** Can neural segmentation find weak metal absorption features in large quasar surveys?
  - **Data:**
    - WEAVE-like mock quasar spectra redward of Lyalpha, covering rest-frame `1230-3095 Angstrom`.
  - **Method:**
    - Train a U-Net on normalized mock spectra; test feature recovery and repeat with autoencoder continuum fits.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Ideal-continuum completeness, purity, and F1 reach about `90%` at line `S/N~4`; strong false positives occur in broad DLA wings.
    - With fitted continua, purity and F1 can differ from ideal values by roughly `10%` and `5%` near that threshold, converging by `S/N~6`. Feature-center scatter is `~0.205 Angstrom`.
    - Validation is on mocks; the network detects features but does not assign ionic identities, and blends can merge into one detection.
  - **Position:** Extends Pistis+2025 continuum tools toward scalable absorber searches; ion identification and transfer to real spectra remain separate acceptance steps.

- [2609.26879](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26879) Pietro Benotto et al. `EPIMETHEUS: Effective PSF inferred from mosaics`
  - **Question:** How can empirical PSFs be recovered from mosaics combining different telescope orientations?
  - **Data:**
    - HST and JWST mosaics in COSMOS, UDS, Abell 2744, and GOODS-S; released empirical PSFs for available filters.
  - **Method:**
    - Select stellar candidates, fit orientations, optionally co-rotate cutouts, inspect stars, and iteratively align and stack across fields.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Pooling suitable stars improves the sample for sparse fields. Co-rotation prevents rejection during stacking from erasing outer PSF structure and inflating the normalized core.
    - The workflow retains manual stellar inspection and requires matching the exposure-orientation context; a released mean PSF is not automatically appropriate at every mosaic position.
  - **Position:** EPIMETHEUS provides transparent PSF construction for photometry, AGN-host separation, and morphology; compare with the multi-field approach in Merlin+2024.

- [2609.27028](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27028) Xiaowei Ou et al. `From starlight to dark matter: a stochastic interpolation approach to map dark matter from stellar density`
  - **Question:** How well can stellar-density maps predict dark-matter structure across galaxy-formation models?
  - **Data:**
    - Milky-Way-mass DREAMS simulated galaxies, with independent TNG50 and FIRE transfer tests; idealized density maps without instrumental systematics.
  - **Method:**
    - Train BaryonBridge stochastic interpolation; assess held-out maps, sampling uncertainties, anisotropy, and cross-simulation residuals.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Held-out DREAMS dark-matter maps reach typical accuracy of `0.09 dex`. FIRE inner profiles are overpredicted by `~0.2-0.4 dex` within `~5 kpc`, despite useful broad-structure recovery.
    - Present-day stellar density does not uniquely encode feedback history. In-domain uncertainty calibration therefore cannot be assumed to cover cross-model bias or real-image systematics.
  - **Position:** A baseline for simulation-based map inference, with explicit evidence that model transfer matters as much as nominal prediction precision.
