## High-Redshift Quenching, Cold Gas, and Compact Sources

- [2606.21361](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21361) F. Valentino et al. `Extended [CII] gas emission in and around a massive quiescent galaxy at z=7.3`
  - **Question:** how can a massive galaxy remain quiescent at `z=7.27` if it still contains cold gas?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: RUBIES-UDS-QG-z7, the most distant known massive quiescent galaxy.
    - Instruments: ALMA Band 6 `[CII] 158 micron` and JWST/NIRSpec plus NIRCam context.
    - Measurements: `[CII]` size, rest-frame far-infrared continuum limit, and gas-mass estimates.
  - **Method:**
    - Confirm the JWST spectroscopic redshift with ALMA `[CII]`.
    - Compare `[CII]` extent with the compact stellar body.
    - Convert galaxy-scale `[CII]` luminosity to molecular and atomic gas masses under multiple calibrations.
    - Interpret the halo-scale gas component against previous MgII absorption evidence.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - `[CII]` emission extends to `R_eff,[CII]=8+/-3 kpc`, far beyond the `R_eff=209(+33/-24) pc` stellar body.
    - Roughly `70%` of the `[CII]` flux comes from a circumgalactic halo.
    - The rest-frame `~160 micron` continuum is undetected, giving `L_IR<1.4x10^11 L_sun`.
    - The inferred cold-gas reservoir is substantial: `log(M_mol/M_sun)=9.53(+0.32/-0.31)` and `f_gas>~20%` under most assumptions.
    - Quenching at `700 Myr` after the Big Bang therefore requires very low star-formation efficiency despite abundant fuel.
  - **Position:** read first because it turns the early-quiescent galaxy problem from gas absence into gas suppression and possible prior AGN-driven expulsion.

- [2606.21614](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21614) Sophia Geris et al. `Little Red and Blue Dots: AGN-excited narrow lines, Lyman-alpha emission, and resemblance to standard quasars`
  - **Question:** are Little Red and Blue Dots obscured stellar systems, unusual AGN, or a shared compact-accretion population?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `36` Little Red and Blue Dots in the GOODS fields.
    - Redshift range: `2.26<z<7.89`.
    - Selection: JWST compact sources with broad Balmer lines and weak X-ray emission.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare both classes on optical/near-infrared diagnostic diagrams.
    - Test broad Balmer equivalent widths and Balmer decrements against normal AGN behavior.
    - Use Ly-alpha and narrow-line properties to infer whether ionizing radiation reaches the ISM.
    - Evaluate envelope-like and equatorial or clumpy obscuration scenarios.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Both LRDs and LBDs occupy diagnostic-diagram regions consistent with AGN excitation, with deviations plausibly tied to low metallicity.
    - LRDs show stronger Ly-alpha than normal star-forming galaxies and include a broad component consistent with broad Halpha.
    - Fully enclosing gas-envelope models do not explain the line evidence unless they allow clumpiness or equatorial leakage.
    - Dust-obscured LBD-like scenarios remain broadly consistent with the data.
    - Bolometric luminosities inferred from isotropic emission plus complete covering are likely unreliable for LRDs.
  - **Position:** important LRD paper because it shifts the debate toward accretion geometry and ionizing escape rather than only stellar-continuum fitting.

- [2606.23683](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23683) Niranjan Chandra Roy et al. `Little Red Dots on FIRE: Exploring the formation and observational signatures of ultra-compact early galaxies`
  - **Question:** can ultra-compact early galaxies reproduce LRD-like observables without requiring AGN?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: FIRE cosmological runs with 3D dust radiative transfer and synthetic emission-line cubes.
    - Environments: progenitors of present-day group halos with `M_halo>10^13.5 M_sun`.
    - Candidate phases: compact galaxies at `z~4-8`.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify transient dissipative-inflow phases that build compact stellar cores.
    - Generate synthetic photometry and line widths.
    - Compare simulated observables with LRD Balmer breaks, UV slopes, dust masses, ALMA limits, and broad-line widths.
    - Separate stellar/host-galaxy contributions from properties requiring a central engine.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Simulated compact phases last `~150-400 Myr`.
    - They reach `M_star~10^8.5-10^10.5 M_sun`, `-23<M_UV<-20`, `R_eff<300 pc`, and `V_circ>500 km/s`.
    - Stellar and host-galaxy dynamics can explain blue UV slopes, some Balmer breaks, dust constraints, and Balmer widths up to `~1500 km/s`.
    - They do not explain red rest-optical continua, Balmer breaks `>~2.5`, widths `>~2000 km/s`, or broad-Balmer plus narrow-forbidden AGN signatures.
    - A hybrid compact-star-plus-AGN model is the most natural reading.
  - **Position:** useful companion to Geris because it defines which LRD observables are host-galaxy physics and which still point to accretion.

- [2606.21105](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21105) Shuying Zhou et al. `Little Red Dots as Supermassive Analogs of SS 433`
  - **Question:** can high-inclination hyper-Eddington accretion explain the odd spectral properties of LRDs?
  - **Data:**
    - Analogy: SS 433-like hyper-Eddington accretion scaled from stellar-mass black holes to SMBHs.
    - Targets: high-redshift LRD observables, including V-shaped SEDs, Balmer breaks, X-ray weakness, and broad lines.
    - Predicted counterparts: lower-inclination Little Blue Dots and normal AGN.
  - **Method:**
    - Scale self-shielding disk geometry to supermassive black hole systems.
    - Interpret broad-line regions as gas ionized by anisotropic inner-disk radiation.
    - Derive variability and line-width predictions for LRDs and LBDs.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - A puffed-up accretion disk can naturally produce X-ray weakness, soft optical SEDs, and apparent sub-Eddington ratios.
    - The model predicts a positive relation between Balmer-break strength and broad-line width.
    - Emission lines should vary more strongly than the optical continuum.
    - LBDs should be more variable than LRDs.
  - **Position:** speculative but testable because it gives LRD/LBD unification predictions that can be checked with variability and line-width data.

## JWST Lensed-Galaxy Substructure and Resolved Methods

- [2606.20845](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20845) Gourav Khullar et al. `LEGGOS I: The JWST LEGGOS Survey -- LEnsing and Galaxy Growth: Observing Substructures -- Unpacks the Nature of Clumpy Star Formation and Quenching in Gravitationally Lensed Galaxies beyond Cosmic Noon`
  - **Question:** how can JWST and strong lensing resolve star formation, dust, and quenching on parsec-to-sub-kpc scales in high-redshift galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: LEGGOS, combining JWST Cycle 2 GO programs and Cycle 1 archival observations.
    - Targets: eight highly magnified lensed galaxies at `z~2-4`.
    - Instruments: JWST/NIRCam imaging and NIRSpec integral-field spectroscopy.
  - **Method:**
    - Use strong lensing to reach `~10-200 pc` physical scales.
    - Combine lensing reconstruction, multi-band photometry, and IFS.
    - Map Balmer recombination lines and diagnostic ratios across clumps and diffuse regions.
    - Fit flexible spectrophotometric stellar-population models.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Spectroscopy breaks degeneracies that remain in photometry-only clump studies.
    - Some systems show recent quenching in regions previously treated as UV star-forming.
    - LEGGOS galaxies span uniform metallicity cases and cases with higher clump metallicity and harder ionization conditions relative to diffuse regions.
    - The data bridge local parsec-scale star-formation physics and galaxy assembly beyond cosmic noon.
  - **Position:** main survey-infrastructure entry of the day for JWST resolved high-redshift galaxy physics.

- [2606.20804](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20804) Pedram Abedi et al. `LEGGOS II: A Strong Lens Model and Source-Plane Projection of the Clumpy Star-Forming Galaxy SGASJ111020.0+645950.8 at z=2.48`
  - **Question:** how precisely can JWST-resolved clumps constrain a strong-lens model for a `z=2.481` lensed galaxy?
  - **Data:**
    - Lens: SDSSJ1110+6459 at `z=0.659`.
    - Source: SGASJ111020.0+645950.8 at `z=2.481`.
    - Observations: JWST/NIRCam, JWST/NIRSpec, and archival HST data.
  - **Method:**
    - Confirm multiple-image systems with imaging and spectroscopy.
    - Match more than `20` luminous regions across primary-arc images.
    - Use the dense JWST clump constraints to refine the cluster lens model.
    - Project clumpy structure back into the source plane.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The lens mass within `250 kpc` is `1.21(+0.09/-0.04)x10^14 M_sun`.
    - The Einstein radius is `10.8(+0.3/-0.4) arcsec`.
    - The total effective magnification of the giant arc is `mu_tot=24.2(+3.4/-1.2)`.
    - Individual clump magnifications span `mu_clump~4-19`.
    - Magnification precision improves by `~2-8x` over previous models.
  - **Position:** a necessary foundation paper for LEGGOS because resolved source-plane science is only as reliable as the local magnification model.

- [2606.20801](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20801) Alex Ross et al. `LEGGOS III: Mapping Star Formation and Dust in Gravitationally Lensed Galaxies with SUMAC, a UMAP and Clustering Framework`
  - **Question:** can lensed JWST imaging and IFS data be segmented into physically meaningful star-forming components without manual clump picking?
  - **Data:**
    - Demonstration target: SGAS111020.0+645950.8 at `z=2.481`.
    - Instrument: JWST/NIRSpec PRISM IFS.
    - Inputs: spaxel-level SEDs and spectra from lensed imaging/spectroscopy.
  - **Method:**
    - Build `SUMAC`, an unsupervised segmentation pipeline.
    - Embed spaxel-level spectra with `UMAP`.
    - Cluster the embedded points using `HDBSCAN`.
    - Interpret median SEDs and line features for each cluster.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The pipeline recovers six physically distinct stellar or nebular populations.
    - Cluster medians separate by Hbeta plus `[OIII]`, Halpha plus `[NII]`, UV slope, Balmer-break strength, and Balmer decrement.
    - Bluer clusters trace unobscured star-forming regions.
    - Redder clusters trace progressively dustier star-forming regions.
  - **Position:** practical JWST-methods paper because it replaces subjective clump labels with reproducible spectral clustering.

- [2606.20800](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20800) Guillaume Hewitt et al. `No hidden monsters: Probing recently-quenched galaxies for obscured AGN with JWST-PRIMER MIRI and NIRCam`
  - **Question:** do obscured AGN explain recent quenching in post-starburst galaxies at `1<z<2`?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `65` photometrically selected post-starburst galaxies in PRIMER-UDS.
    - Instruments: JWST/MIRI F770W and F1800W, JWST/NIRCam, and HST/ACS.
    - Diagnostic: mid-infrared excess from hot dust or residual star formation.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare low- and high-mass post-starburst mid-infrared colors.
    - Fit AGN templates to constrain hidden accretion.
    - Test F770W--F1800W color as a passive/star-forming separator.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Massive PSBs with `M_star>10^10 M_sun` show little or no mid-infrared excess.
    - Low-mass PSBs show enhanced `18 micron` emission, consistent with residual star formation.
    - Hidden AGN in massive PSBs are limited to Eddington ratios `<1%`.
    - F770W--F1800W color is a strong passive/star-forming diagnostic at high stellar mass.
  - **Position:** useful quenching paper because it weakens a simple buried-AGN explanation for massive PSBs in this redshift range.

## Gas Cycling, Environment, and Large-Scale Structure

- [2606.22764](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22764) C. Zhang et al. `Highly filamentary HI gas in the circumgalactic medium and intragalactic medium around NGC 4631`
  - **Question:** does low-density CGM/IGM HI contain coherent filamentary structure on kpc scales?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: NGC 4631 group.
    - Observations: FAST FEASTS single-dish HI and VLA THINGS interferometric HI.
    - Structures: diffuse HI in the CGM and intragroup medium.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine single-dish sensitivity with interferometric resolution.
    - Identify filamentary HI features in position and velocity.
    - Classify filament morphologies from position-velocity structure.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Filaments have widths of `0.5-3.3 kpc` and lengths of `6.1-49.8 kpc`.
    - The structures are velocity coherent.
    - Three classes appear: U-shaped, linear, and wavy filaments.
    - The detection links pc-scale ISM filaments, kpc-scale CGM/IGM filaments, and Mpc-scale cosmic-web filaments.
  - **Position:** strong local-gas analog because it gives direct morphology for diffuse baryon cycling outside galaxy disks.

- [2606.23674](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23674) Brenda Namumba et al. `Ram-pressure signatures in the dwarf irregular galaxy SextansB revealed by deep MeerKAT HI observations`
  - **Question:** can the diffuse IGM in the Local Group measurably disturb the HI disk of a dwarf galaxy?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: Sextans B.
    - Observations: MeerKAT HI 21 cm at `1.4 km/s` spectral resolution.
    - Sensitivity: column densities down to `3.3x10^18 cm^-2`.
  - **Method:**
    - Map the extended HI disk and faint outer structures.
    - Compare gas morphology against the stellar distribution.
    - Fit 3D kinematic models with TiRiFiC.
    - Use tailored hydrodynamical simulations to test IGM ram pressure.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The low-column-density HI distribution is asymmetric and filamentary.
    - Gas and stars are offset, while the stellar disk is more symmetric.
    - Outer rotation curves differ between approaching and receding sides.
    - Stellar feedback cannot explain the full morphology and kinematics.
    - Diffuse-IGM ram pressure can reproduce the outer-gas disturbance.
  - **Position:** relevant to CGM/IGM work because it shows that even weak large-scale gas can alter dwarf-galaxy baryon structure.

- [2606.22432](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22432) Sourabh Paul et al. `Tracing Large-scale Structure with the MeerKLASS On-the-Fly Survey: Angular Clustering of Radio Sources at 816 MHz`
  - **Question:** can MeerKLASS DR1 radio-continuum sources provide stable wide-area clustering measurements?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: MeerKLASS UHF on-the-fly continuum DR1.
    - Area: `~800 deg^2` in the DESI footprint.
    - Frequency: reference imaging at `816 MHz`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure the angular two-point correlation function `w(theta)`.
    - Test depth-mask and flux-threshold stability.
    - Fit fixed-slope power-law and projected-matter templates.
    - Infer effective bias under two T-RECS redshift-distribution priors.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Clustering is detected over `0.02 deg < theta < 10 deg`.
    - The intermediate-scale power-law amplitude is `A(1 deg)=(1.434+/-0.475)x10^-3`.
    - The effective bias is `b_eff=1.998+/-0.350` for the AGN prior and `1.530+/-0.265` for the total prior.
    - The dominant modeling uncertainty is the redshift distribution `N(z)`.
  - **Position:** useful LSS infrastructure because radio clustering can become much sharper once optical/IR overlap calibrates redshift distributions.

- [2606.20829](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20829) G. Congedo et al. `Euclid: Quick Data Release (Q1) -- LensMC shear measurement catalogue for cluster lensing science`
  - **Question:** are Euclid Q1 VIS images already suitable for cluster-lensing shear measurements?
  - **Data:**
    - Imaging: Euclid Quick Release 1 VIS images over `63 deg^2`.
    - Catalogue: LensMC shapes, positions, weights, and morphology parameters.
    - Validation: two-point statistics, cluster profiles, and external WISE/DES cluster catalogues.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure galaxy shapes for Euclid VIS sources.
    - Build size- and magnitude-dependent empirical bias corrections.
    - Validate additive biases and cluster stacked profiles.
    - Test residual systematics with random-cluster stacks.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Shape density reaches `26 arcmin^-2` for `I_E<24.5` and `75 arcmin^-2` for `I_E<27`.
    - No spatial additive-bias dependence is detected within this scope.
    - Residual systematics may still affect low-redshift large-radius bins.
    - Euclid can constrain cluster lensing profiles for `10^14 M_sun` clusters to `z~2`.
  - **Position:** high-value survey-methods entry for cluster environment and halo-growth work in the Euclid era.

- [2606.21495](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21495) Franklin Aldas et al. `X-ray emission maps and scaling relations in IllustrisTNG and MillenniumTNG: Differences between cluster and group regimes`
  - **Question:** where do simulated group and cluster X-ray scaling relations depart from self-similar behavior?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: IllustrisTNG300 and MillenniumTNG.
    - Halo mass range: `10^12.5 <= M_500 <= 10^15.5 M_sun`.
    - Products: synthetic X-ray emission maps and spectra.
  - **Method:**
    - Generate X-ray maps using APEC cooling functions.
    - Introduce a metallicity prescription for simulations without individual abundance tracking.
    - Fit `L_X-M_500`, `M_gas-M_500`, and `T-M_500` relations.
    - Quantify hydrostatic-equilibrium and spectroscopic-temperature biases.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Scaling relations match observations overall but prefer broken power laws.
    - The pivot occurs near `M_500=10^13.67 M_sun`.
    - Low-mass systems steepen because AGN feedback is more important.
    - X-ray hydrostatic masses are underestimated by `15%`.
    - Spectroscopic effects raise the high-mass bias to `21%` and create luminosity discrepancies up to `33%` in low-mass systems.
  - **Position:** useful bridge between simulation and cluster observations because it isolates the group-regime failure mode of simple scaling.
