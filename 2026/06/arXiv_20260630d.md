## High-Redshift Galaxies, Structure, and Compact Accretion

- [2606.28590](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28590) Kiyoaki C. Omori et al. `Interaction-induced star formation boosts stellar mass assembly in z~5 galaxies`
  - **Question:** how much stellar-mass growth at `z~5` is driven by galaxy-galaxy interactions and the starbursts they trigger?
  - **Data:**
    - Imaging: JWST/NIRCam observations from CANUCS.
    - Sample: `48` spectroscopically selected galaxies at `5.0<z_spec<5.6`.
    - Interaction subset: `21` systems, or `44%`, visually classified as close interactions within `lesssim 5 kpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Visually classify multi-component close systems.
    - Fit component star-formation histories with Dense Basis.
    - Compare burst mass growth against a no-burst baseline and assume components eventually merge.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Interacting components undergo brief `~0.2 Gyr` star-formation enhancements.
    - The bursts grow component stellar masses by `2.66+/-0.85x`.
    - The burst phase forms `1.71+/-0.37x` excess mass relative to a no-burst path.
    - Mergers contribute `42^{+20}_{-25}%` of total stellar-mass growth at `z~5`, about half through pre-existing stellar mass and half through newly formed stars.
  - **Position:** read first because it directly connects JWST-resolved high-redshift interactions to the mass-assembly budget.

- [2606.30253](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30253) Federica Loiacono et al. `No evolution in the number density of little red dots from cosmic dawn to cosmic noon`
  - **Question:** does the Little Red Dot abundance decline from cosmic dawn to cosmic noon?
  - **Data:**
    - Field: JWST/EIGER J1030 field around the `z~6.3` quasar SDSS J1030+0524.
    - Parent catalog: `154` point-like JWST-selected sources.
    - LRD candidates: `5` broad-line emitters with `FWHM gtrsim 1000 km/s`, red `F200W-F356W>0`, and X-ray non-detections.
  - **Method:**
    - Select point-like red sources in the JWST catalog.
    - Measure broad-line emitters and derive LRD bolometric luminosity functions at `z=2.4` and `z=4.5`.
    - Compare LRD number densities with pre-JWST AGN samples and recent model predictions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At `z=2.4`, the LRD space density is only a factor of `~2` below all pre-JWST AGN above `L_bol gtrsim 3x10^44 erg/s`.
    - At `z=4.5`, the inferred density agrees with larger-area LRD studies.
    - The abundance of LRDs above `L_bol gtrsim 3x10^44 erg/s` shows no significant evolution at `z>2`.
    - The cosmic-noon abundance is `3.4^{+5.6}_{-2.4}x10^-5 Mpc^-3`, about `350x` larger than recent model predictions.
  - **Position:** important LRD demographics paper because it tests whether LRDs are a short early seed-growth phase or a broader high-accretion state.

- [2606.28489](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28489) Madalina N. Tudorache et al. `pop-cosmos: Galaxy size evolution across structural and star-formation classifications in COSMOS-Web`
  - **Question:** how do galaxy size-mass and size-redshift relations change when galaxies are split by star formation versus morphology?
  - **Data:**
    - Catalogs: pop-cosmos stellar masses and sSFRs trained on COSMOS2020 Spitzer/IRAC `Ch.1<26`.
    - Imaging: COSMOS-Web size and morphology measurements.
    - Sample: `99,369` galaxies.
  - **Method:**
    - Build size-mass and size-redshift relations for star-formation and morphology splits.
    - Compare slopes, intercepts, pivot masses, and intrinsic scatter.
    - Relate pivot masses to AGN bolometric fraction and halo-scale baryonic redistribution.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - sSFR and morphology splits produce quantitatively different scaling-relation slopes, intercepts, and scatter behavior.
    - Intrinsic scatter depends on structural morphology but not on sSFR.
    - Quiescent and bulge-dominated size-mass relations both show double-power-law breaks, but at different pivot masses.
    - The quiescent pivot mass is `M_*~10^10.7 M_sun`; the bulge-dominated pivot mass is `M_*~10^11.1 M_sun`.
    - Morphology-dependent trends require space-based imaging.
  - **Position:** useful COSMOS-Web baseline because high-z size evolution depends on classification choices that can masquerade as physical trends.

- [2606.28559](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28559) R. Vaisakh et al. `DESI DR2 Reference Mocks: Clustering results from UCHUU ELGs and QSOs`
  - **Question:** can Uchuu-based DESI DR2 reference mocks reproduce ELG and QSO clustering over the redshift range used for high-z structure measurements?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: Uchuu N-body simulation.
    - Tracers: DESI DR2-like ELGs at `0.8<z<1.6` and QSOs at `0.8<z<2.1`.
    - Observables: number density, clustering statistics, and large-scale bias.
  - **Method:**
    - Populate halos and subhalos with QSOs using modified SHAM.
    - Select ELG satellites with low velocities relative to their associated central halos.
    - Tune the mocks to DESI DR2 clustering and compare redshift evolution.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The mocks reproduce the fitted number-density and clustering evolution over the analyzed scales.
    - The measured large-scale bias improves DESI lightcone construction.
    - The ELG and QSO prescriptions refine galaxy-halo connection modeling for high-redshift tracers.
  - **Position:** practical large-scale-structure entry because DESI mock fidelity controls interpretation of high-z galaxy and quasar clustering.

## Gas, CGM, Feedback, and AGN Diagnostics

- [2606.28874](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28874) Yu Rong et al. `Stellar Surface Density Modulates MgII Cool-gas Outflow Absorption in DESI Star-forming Galaxies`
  - **Question:** is cool-gas outflow absorption controlled only by stellar mass and SFR, or also by galaxy compactness?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: DESI DR1 stacked spectra.
    - Sample: massive star-forming galaxies at `0.35<z<1.0`.
    - Diagnostic: down-the-barrel Mg II cool-gas absorption.
  - **Method:**
    - Define stellar surface density as `Sigma_*=M_*/(2pi R_e^2)`.
    - Build AGN-clean samples matched in stellar mass.
    - Repeat the test in samples matched in both stellar mass and Balmer-line SFR proxy.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Mg II outflow equivalent width rises monotonically with `Sigma_*` in every redshift bin.
    - From the lowest to highest `Sigma_*` tertile, `EW_out` increases by `0.37-0.61 Angstrom`.
    - The absolute outflow velocity changes only weakly.
    - Cool-gas outflow strength depends on how tightly the galaxy is built, not only on stellar mass or star formation.
  - **Position:** read-first feedback result because it adds structure as an empirical axis for interpreting outflow absorption.

- [2606.30088](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30088) Namrata Roy et al. `Lighting Up the CGM: Strong, Jet-Aligned Halpha Emission around Radio Galaxies`
  - **Question:** do radio jets produce anisotropic, localized changes in the cool ionized CGM?
  - **Data:**
    - Tracer: faint Halpha emission from `T~10^4 K` cool ionized CGM.
    - Sightlines: DESI background-quasar spectra around radio galaxies.
    - Geometry: projected distance and position angle relative to radio jet axes.
  - **Method:**
    - Stack background-quasar spectra by jet-aligned and off-axis sightline geometry.
    - Compare Halpha emission with Mg II absorption along the same sightlines.
    - Test radial structure from host-galaxy scales to projected radio-lobe regions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Jet-aligned stacks within `theta<20 deg` detect Halpha at `>5 sigma`.
    - The mean integrated jet-aligned flux is `1.19x10^-17 erg cm^-2 s^-1`.
    - The azimuthally averaged stack over `324` sightline angles gives no detection below `2 sigma`.
    - The jet-aligned Halpha signal is about `100x` brighter than normal halos.
    - Mg II absorption is similar in jet-aligned and off-axis directions, so Halpha isolates localized low-covering-fraction clouds boosted by the jet.
  - **Position:** strong CGM-feedback paper because it uses geometry to separate jet-affected gas from the ambient cool reservoir.

- [2606.28927](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28927) Kana Morokuma-Matsui et al. `Insights into Jet-Induced Cloud Disruption in NGC 1316: ALMA Reveals a Spatially Extended Molecular Gas`
  - **Question:** how do AGN jets reshape molecular gas in a nearby radio galaxy?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: NGC 1316.
    - Instrument: ALMA CO(1-0) at `100 pc` resolution, with archival CO(2-1) and CO(3-2).
    - Structures: `24` giant molecular cloud associations in the NW Shell and SE Blob.
  - **Method:**
    - Map CO morphology and kinematics.
    - Compare interferometric and single-dish CO fluxes.
    - Use line ratios and multiwavelength shell morphology to test a jet-driven bubble model.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Several regions have broad CO line widths above `50 km/s`.
    - Interferometric CO recovers only `34-38%` of the single-dish flux, implying abundant extended molecular gas.
    - GMAs have velocity dispersions about `2x` typical star-forming-galaxy values for their sizes.
    - Elevated line ratios near the jet reach `R_21~1` and `R_31~1`.
    - A jet power of `1.6x10^43 erg/s` can drive the observed `~5 kpc` shell and cloud disruption.
  - **Position:** lower-redshift but valuable because it gives a resolved molecular-gas template for jet-induced negative feedback.

- [2606.29608](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29608) Mauricio Elias-Chavez et al. `The Lockman-SpReSO Project: A Deep X-ray Spectral View of a FIR-selected AGN Population`
  - **Question:** what X-ray spectral properties do FIR-selected AGN show across a wide redshift range?
  - **Data:**
    - Field: Lockman Hole FIR-selected galaxies.
    - Instrument: deep XMM-Newton observations.
    - Sample: `94` AGN over `z=0.07-5` to a `0.3-10 keV` flux limit of `5x10^-16 erg s^-1 cm^-2`.
  - **Method:**
    - Cross-match FIR sources with X-ray detections.
    - Fit absorbed power-law, reflection, soft-excess, and XCLUMPY Compton-thick models.
    - Estimate column densities, rest-frame `2-10 keV` luminosities, and iron-line equivalent widths.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Obscured AGN fraction increases toward higher redshift.
    - The sample contains one strong and two borderline Compton-thick candidates with `N_H gtrsim 10^24 cm^-2`.
    - Soft excess emission appears in `10` AGN, with average blackbody temperature `0.12+/-0.02 keV`.
    - The torus covering factor follows a receding-torus relation with X-ray luminosity.
    - FIR selection favors dusty star-forming hosts and heavily obscured AGN.
  - **Position:** useful AGN-selection paper because dusty high-z hosts complicate X-ray and infrared accretion census work.

- [2606.29988](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29988) Hannah McCall et al. `Kinematics of Weak Cool-Core Cluster A3571 Observed with XRISM: Low Cooling Rate Balanced by Low Heating Rate`
  - **Question:** can weak turbulence offset cooling in a relaxed, AGN-feedback-free cluster core?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: weak cool-core cluster A3571.
    - Instrument: four XRISM Cycle 1 pointings.
    - Region: cluster core out to `120 kpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure velocity dispersion and bulk velocity in core regions.
    - Compare gas sloshing, Mach number, and non-thermal pressure with relaxed and merging cluster samples.
    - Estimate turbulent heating against cooling losses.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Velocity dispersions are mostly uniform at `~100-120 km/s`.
    - The northern sloshing elongation has a `68%` upper limit of `68 km/s`.
    - A3571 has lower Mach number and non-thermal pressure fraction than A2029 and below some cosmological-simulation predictions.
    - Turbulent heating is still sufficient to offset cooling in the studied regions.
    - Merging clusters have average Mach number `0.29+/-0.07`, nearly twice the relaxed sample.
  - **Position:** cluster-gas context entry because XRISM kinematics calibrate how feedback-free or weak-feedback systems regulate cooling.

- [2606.30624](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30624) Elias Kyritsis et al. `Prescriptions for the stochasticity effect on the integrated X-ray luminosity of star-forming galaxies`
  - **Question:** how does stochastic sampling of high-mass X-ray binaries bias X-ray luminosity predictions for star-forming galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Model grid: star-formation rate and metallicity conditions.
    - Redshift test: simulated samples across `z=0.5-5`.
    - Observable: integrated galaxy X-ray luminosity dominated by HMXB populations.
  - **Method:**
    - Run Monte Carlo sampling of the HMXB X-ray luminosity function.
    - Fit surfaces for upper and lower luminosity bounds as functions of SFR and metallicity.
    - Apply prescriptions to local and high-redshift samples and flux-limited survey selection.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Stochasticity must be modeled before interpreting X-ray luminosity differences as intrinsic galaxy evolution.
    - Scatter shows mild redshift evolution and is smallest near `z~2.5`.
    - At low redshift, stochastic effects can raise `L_X` by up to `1 dex`.
    - Stochastic HMXB boosts can overlap the low-luminosity AGN regime and bias source classification.
  - **Position:** practical for high-z X-ray work because AGN selection and star-formation scaling relations share the same luminosity space.

## Reionization, Baryons, Magnetism, and SKA Infrastructure

- [2606.29903](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29903) Rennan Barkana et al. `High-Redshift Signatures from the Cosmic Dawn and the Epoch of Reionization`
  - **Question:** what standard and exotic astrophysical signatures can SKA-Low detect in the high-redshift 21 cm signal?
  - **Data:**
    - Facility: SKA-Low in AA* and AA4 configurations.
    - Epochs: Cosmic Dawn and the Epoch of Reionization.
    - Sources: first stars, galaxies, black holes, primordial black holes, non-standard dark matter, non-standard fluctuations, and strong radio galaxies.
  - **Method:**
    - Review the astrophysical and cosmological processes shaping the 21 cm signal.
    - Separate Lambda-CDM standard signatures from exotic new-physics signatures.
    - Evaluate detectability against expected SKA-Low sensitivity.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The 21 cm signal encodes both early astrophysical heating/ionization and possible beyond-Lambda-CDM physics.
    - SKA-Low AA* and AA4 can test first-galaxy and first-black-hole scenarios.
    - Exotic signatures remain speculative but map cleanly onto observables that SKA-Low can constrain.
  - **Position:** high-priority reionization overview because it links source physics, IGM state, and SKA detectability in one roadmap.

- [2606.29388](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29388) Manisha Caleb et al. `Probing the Baryon Distribution with Fast Radio Bursts`
  - **Question:** how can SKA fast radio bursts trace baryons from galactic to cosmological scales?
  - **Data:**
    - Facility: SKA, especially southern-hemisphere FRB discovery.
    - Tracers: FRB dispersion measure, DM scatter, DM-field statistics, and scattering timescale.
    - Synergies: Rubin Observatory, cosmic shear, and galaxy clustering surveys.
  - **Method:**
    - Forecast DM scatter as a function of redshift.
    - Cross-correlate the DM field with Stage IV galaxy surveys.
    - Use FRB scattering to constrain CGM properties.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - SKA FRBs can directly probe baryonic feedback models.
    - FRB information can improve the cosmological constraining power of Stage IV surveys.
    - Scattering timescales can measure CGM properties.
    - Large-redshift FRB samples can improve constraints on reionization-era electron distributions.
  - **Position:** useful IGM/CGM bridge because FRBs trace ionized baryons that galaxy surveys otherwise model indirectly.

- [2606.30025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30025) S. A. Mao et al. `Probing Magnetic Fields In and Around Galaxies Near and Far`
  - **Question:** what SKA AA4 observations can map magnetic fields in galaxies and their CGM over cosmic time?
  - **Data:**
    - Facility: SKA AA4.
    - Nearby-galaxy observable: diffuse polarized emission plus dense rotation-measure grids within the virial radius.
    - Distant-galaxy observable: polarized lensed quasars and statistical back-illumination.
  - **Method:**
    - Define a homogeneous polarimetric survey of nearby galaxies.
    - Use strong lenses to probe interstellar magnetic fields in distant galaxies.
    - Use background polarized sources to trace redshift evolution of CGM magnetic fields.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - SKA AA4 can build a magnetic-field census from nearby galaxies to high redshift.
    - Dense rotation-measure grids can map halo-scale magnetization.
    - The survey design can constrain galactic dynamo theories and galaxy-magnetic-field coevolution.
  - **Position:** relevant infrastructure paper because magnetic fields may regulate feedback, CGM structure, and star formation but remain poorly measured at high redshift.

- [2606.30307](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30307) Francesca Panessa et al. `The SKA-VLBI Perspective on Radio-Quiet AGN`
  - **Question:** how can SKA-VLBI isolate radio emission mechanisms in the radio-quiet AGN population?
  - **Data:**
    - Facility: SKA-MID phased into global VLBI arrays at full AA4 deployment.
    - Frequency range: `0.35-15 GHz`.
    - Targets: radio-quiet AGN from local systems to cosmic dawn.
  - **Method:**
    - Use high-resolution VLBI imaging to separate compact cores from host-galaxy radio emission.
    - Combine flux, spectral, and polarization monitoring.
    - Map nuclear regions from sub-parsec to kiloparsec scales.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Radio-quiet AGN radio emission mixes star formation, winds, shocks, photoionized gas, low-power jets, and coronal activity.
    - SKA-VLBI can reach sub-milliarcsecond imaging and microJy sensitivity.
    - AA4 enables the first population-level census of radio-quiet AGN nuclei.
    - Earlier AA* operations can support pilot studies of the brightest nearby systems.
  - **Position:** important AGN infrastructure entry because it turns weak nuclear radio emission into a resolved feedback and accretion diagnostic.

- [2606.28512](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28512) Q. D'Amato et al. `Dual AGN and Multiple SMBH Systems in the Era of SKAO`
  - **Question:** how will SKAO find and characterize dual AGN and SMBH binaries across merger stages?
  - **Data:**
    - Facility: SKAO radio interferometry and VLBI.
    - Spatial scales: tens of kiloparsecs to sub-parsec bound SMBH systems.
    - Synergies: JWST, Euclid, Rubin, LISA, and pulsar timing arrays.
  - **Method:**
    - Review current dual-AGN and SMBH-binary detection strategies.
    - Emphasize dust-unbiased high-resolution radio imaging.
    - Define radio follow-up, blind radio selection, compact binary, and gravitational-wave-link science cases.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - SKAO improves the sensitivity, survey speed, imaging fidelity, and angular resolution needed for dual-SMBH census work.
    - Radio imaging can separate accretion-powered emission from star formation.
    - The combined electromagnetic and gravitational-wave context can measure SMBH pairing, accretion, and merger-driven feedback through cosmic history.
  - **Position:** useful roadmap because high-z AGN environments and merger rates need radio constraints independent of dust and optical selection.
