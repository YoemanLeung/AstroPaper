## Executive View

The read-first paper is [2606.00258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00258) Kelcey Davis. OCEANS uses high-resolution JWST/NIRSpec spectra for `10` LRDs at `3<z<7` and finds Balmer-line absorption in `4` of them, with a higher detection rate than lower-resolution surveys. This is directly useful for deciding whether LRD diversity is dominated by geometry, absorbing gas, AGN broad-line regions, or stellar-continuum effects.

[2606.00205](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00205) Elia Cenci is the strongest theory companion. It uses MELIORA simulations to connect rest-frame UV diversity in `z>8.5` LRD candidates to the age of embedded direct-collapse black holes. The key diagnostic is an evolutionary transition over about `30 Myr`, from black-hole-dominated emission to increasingly stellar UV continua.

The early-universe methods entry is [2606.00219](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00219) Daniela Breitman. 21cmEMUv3 emulates multiple 21cmFAST observables over `z~6-30`, including the cylindrical 21 cm power spectrum, neutral fraction, spin temperature, global signal, UV luminosity functions, and Thomson optical depth. It is a high-priority tool paper for connecting first-galaxy assumptions to EoR constraints.

The gas-mapping and black-hole-survey theme is also strong. [2606.00164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00164) Matthew J. Hayes argues that boosted HST operations could uniquely image warm-hot CGM emission lines. [2606.01707](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01707) Erika Ogata shows that black-hole accretion can dominate Halpha and He II line-intensity-mapping shot noise around cosmic noon. [2606.00721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00721) Aditya Sharma models inverse-Compton/CMB effects in high-redshift AGN jets.

For morphology and environment, [2606.00415](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00415) Marco Chiaberge tests VLM ensembles for merger classification against VELA+SUNRISE mock images, while [2606.01297](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01297) Zi-Hua Ho shows how spatial quenching classifications in MaNGA depend on the chosen tracer. These are lower redshift or methods-heavy, but they map onto the profile's morphology and environment axes.

## Little Red Dots and Early Black-Hole Growth

- [2606.00258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00258) Kelcey Davis et al. `OCEANS of Absorption: High-resolution NIRSpec Spectroscopy Reveals Diverse Balmer-line Absorption in Little Red Dots`
  - **Question:** what does high-resolution JWST spectroscopy reveal about Balmer-line absorption and absorbing gas in LRDs?
  - **Data:**
    - Program: OCEANS follow-up spectroscopy in the CEERS/EGS field.
    - Instrument: JWST/NIRSpec high-resolution spectroscopy with Halpha coverage.
    - Sample: `10` LRDs at `3<z<7`.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit Halpha profiles and search for Balmer-line absorption.
    - Compare high-resolution detections with lower-resolution NIRSpec survey results.
    - Interpret absorption diversity with a partial-covering gas model.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Balmer-line absorption is found in `4` of `10` LRDs.
    - `7` of `10` LRDs are best fit by Halpha profiles with exponential wings.
    - Absorbers have median velocity offset `-49 km/s` and median equivalent width `5.3 Angstrom`.
    - The results imply a near-ubiquitous reservoir of dense, excited `n=2` hydrogen around LRDs.
  - **Position:** top daily entry because it constrains the gas geometry and physical interpretation of LRD spectra rather than only their broadband colors.

- [2606.00205](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00205) Elia Cenci et al. `Ultraviolet diversity of Little Red Dots as a probe for direct-collapse black hole ages`
  - **Question:** can rest-frame UV diversity in LRDs diagnose the age of embedded direct-collapse black holes?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation suite: MELIORA large-scale cosmological hydrodynamical simulation.
    - Target population: LRD candidates at `z>8.5`.
    - Wavelength range: rest-frame `0.2-0.6 micron`.
  - **Method:**
    - Track Pop III stellar and accreting DCBH contributions to candidate LRD spectra.
    - Follow host stellar-mass buildup, metallicity enrichment, and accretion-state evolution after DCBH formation.
    - Relate UV colors and spatial extent to black-hole age.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Host-galaxy UV emission evolves rapidly during the first `~30 Myr` after DCBH formation.
    - Young systems are initially black-hole dominated, with low stellar mass, low metallicity, and high accretion rates.
    - Older UV-bright LRDs have higher gas-phase metallicities, lower BH-to-stellar mass ratios, and lower Eddington ratios.
    - The LRD phase is short-lived and should encode an evolutionary sequence in emission lines, metallicity, and accretion state.
  - **Position:** high-priority LRD theory paper because it turns color diversity into a possible clock for early black-hole formation.

- [2606.01870](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01870) Ataru Tanikawa et al. `Mass and Spin Growth of Very Massive Stars in Star Clusters Potentially Associated with Little Red Dots`
  - **Question:** can dense star clusters associated with LRD-like systems grow very massive, rapidly spinning stars that seed intermediate-mass black holes?
  - **Data:**
    - Framework: gravitational `N`-body simulations of dense star clusters.
    - Target objects: very massive stars in collision-rich cluster environments.
    - Connection: possible LRD-associated seed or transient channels.
  - **Method:**
    - Simulate collisional mass growth under different host-cluster initial conditions.
    - Include bloated Hayashi-track states induced by stellar collisions.
    - Convert stellar spin estimates to a dimensionless black-hole spin parameter for interpretation.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - VMS masses can reach `10^3-10^4 Msun`.
    - Accounting for bloated collision states can increase VMS mass by up to a factor of `3` when accretion exceeds `3 x 10^-2 Msun/yr`.
    - Normalized VMS spin exceeds `10` in the modeled cases.
    - Collapse could form an intermediate-mass black hole with a massive accretion disk and explosive or gravitational-wave signatures.
  - **Position:** speculative but relevant seed-channel paper for LRD interpretation and early black-hole growth.

## Cosmic Dawn, Reionization, and Survey Emulators

- [2606.00219](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00219) Daniela Breitman et al. `21cmEMUv3: a hybrid diffusion-LSTM emulator of 21cmFAST summary observables`
  - **Question:** can a hybrid emulator provide fast, accurate EoR and cosmic-dawn summary observables for inference pipelines?
  - **Data:**
    - Training basis: 21cmFASTv3 simulations with atomically and molecularly cooling galaxies.
    - Redshift range: `z~6-30`.
    - Summary observables: cylindrical and spherical 21 cm power spectra, neutral fraction, spin temperature, global signal, UV luminosity functions, and Thomson optical depth.
  - **Method:**
    - Condition the emulator on `sigma_8` and ten astrophysical parameters.
    - Use score-based diffusion for the cylindrical 21 cm power spectrum.
    - Use LSTM networks for the remaining six observables.
    - Reinterpret HERA upper limits and forecast SKA detectability.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The emulator reaches sub-percent median accuracy across its summary outputs.
    - Current HERA limits imply soft-band X-ray luminosity per SFR is not below `10^39.2 erg s^-1 Msun^-1 yr` at `95%` confidence.
    - The framework supports SKA 21 cm power-spectrum forecasts under different array configurations.
  - **Position:** very useful methods entry because it links first-galaxy physics, IGM state, and future 21 cm survey strategy.

- [2606.01907](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01907) Yingxiao Song et al. `CSST large-scale structure analysis pipeline: IV. Cosmic Voids Identified from Galaxy Group Samples as Probes of the Large-scale Structure`
  - **Question:** can voids identified from galaxy-group catalogs recover halo-void statistics in CSST-like surveys?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey context: Chinese Space-station Survey Telescope reference mock galaxy redshift survey.
    - Catalogs: ideal `100%` and conservative `~30%` spectroscopic-redshift completeness group samples.
    - Redshift range: five intervals from `z=0` to `1.0`.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify group-voids from galaxy-group catalogs and halo-voids from underlying halos.
    - Use brightest central galaxies as group centers to improve void-center estimates.
    - Compare void size functions and density profiles under completeness degradation.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Group-void statistics can reproduce halo-void statistics when spectroscopic completeness is at least `40%`.
    - Even `30%` completeness can remain usable when a redshift-error term is included.
    - Group-voids are promising LSS probes and useful for emulator-based analysis.
  - **Position:** lower-redshift than the core profile, but relevant as a survey-pipeline paper for LSS reconstruction from incomplete spectroscopy.

## CGM, IGM, and Line-Intensity Mapping

- [2606.00164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00164) Matthew J. Hayes et al. `Astronomical Advantages of a Boost Mission to Facilitate HST Science into the 2030s: Imaging the Circumgalactic Medium of Galaxies`
  - **Question:** what CGM science would remain uniquely enabled if HST operations are extended by an orbital boost?
  - **Data:**
    - Facility context: HST into the 2030s.
    - Target signals: UV/optical emission from HI Lyalpha and highly ionized metals such as CIV, OVI, and NeVIII.
    - Science target: diffuse CGM around galaxies.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify line-emission diagnostics that require HST's UV capability.
    - Assess how deep, high-resolution mapping would constrain CGM morphology and feedback exchange.
    - Position HST observations as pathfinders for HWO spectroscopy.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - HST remains uniquely able to image diffuse transitions with ionization potentials between `13` and `200 eV`.
    - A boost could enable first statistical constraints on warm-hot CGM spatial distributions.
    - The observations would support target selection and science-case development for future HWO facilities.
  - **Position:** directly aligned with CGM/IGM mapping interests despite being a white paper rather than a new measurement.

- [2606.01707](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01707) Erika Ogata et al. `Signatures of Accreting Black Holes in Line Intensity Mapping`
  - **Question:** how much do accreting black holes contribute to Halpha and He II line-intensity-mapping signals?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation basis: IllustrisTNG cosmological hydrodynamical simulation.
    - Mock observables: Halpha and He II intensity maps.
    - Survey context: SPHEREx and CDIM-like experiments.
  - **Method:**
    - Construct LIM maps with both star-formation and black-hole accretion contributions.
    - Separate mean intensity, shot noise, and small-scale clustering effects.
    - Evaluate voxel-intensity-distribution observability for forthcoming surveys.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Black-hole accretion contributes `~40-60%` of Halpha mean intensity around cosmic noon.
    - It contributes `~60-80%` of He II mean intensity in the same regime.
    - Black-hole-powered emission dominates the shot-noise component and boosts small-scale clustering, especially for He II.
    - SPHEREx can probe BH-influenced bright Halpha voxels at `z<~4`, while a CDIM-like experiment can access BH-dominated He II.
  - **Position:** high-value survey-theory paper because it couples AGN growth to unresolved line mapping of large-scale structure.

## High-Redshift AGN and Quasar Physics

- [2606.00721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00721) Aditya Sharma et al. `The Radio--X-ray Correlation of High-Redshift AGN: A Numerical Study of Inverse-Compton Scattering of the CMB Photons in Relativistic Jets`
  - **Question:** can IC/CMB losses in relativistic jets explain radio and X-ray trends in high-redshift radio-loud quasars?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: three-dimensional relativistic magnetohydrodynamic jet models.
    - Radiation model: synthetic SEDs and intensity maps with synchrotron and IC/CMB losses.
    - Target regime: high-redshift radio-loud quasars.
  - **Method:**
    - Hold jet dynamics and ambient medium fixed across redshifts.
    - Couple MHD simulations to a hybrid Eulerian-Lagrangian particle framework.
    - Track shock acceleration and redshift-dependent CMB energy density.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Simulations reproduce weak redshift dependence in radio luminosity and strong X-ray enhancement toward high redshift.
    - At high redshift, X-ray luminosity follows the expected `(1+z)^4` IC/CMB scaling.
    - The X-ray-to-radio flux ratio increases systematically with redshift.
    - Slower jets show stronger redshift evolution in the X-ray enhancement than faster jets.
  - **Position:** useful AGN-physics entry for interpreting high-z radio sources and jet feedback observables.

- [2606.01496](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01496) Brendon J. Brewer et al. `The Information Content of Quasar Variability Light Curves: How Well Can we Infer Stochastic Model Parameters?`
  - **Question:** which stochastic-model parameters are actually constrained by quasar variability light curves?
  - **Data:**
    - Example dataset: `570` quasar light curves measured over decades.
    - Model class: damped random walk / Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process.
    - Inference framework: information-theory diagnostics and hierarchical Bayesian regression.
  - **Method:**
    - Test effects of fixing versus fitting the mean light-curve level.
    - Use conditional entropy and mutual information to quantify parameter information content.
    - Fit volatility as a function of bolometric luminosity and rest wavelength directly from light curves.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Fixing the mean overconfidently constrains the DRW timescale `tau`.
    - Short-term volatility `eta` is much better constrained than `tau`.
    - Volatility decreases with bolometric luminosity and rest wavelength.
    - Volatility decreases more steeply with redshift than time dilation alone predicts, implying changing intrinsic variability.
  - **Position:** methods-relevant quasar paper, especially for future time-domain AGN selection and accretion-state inference.

- [2606.00992](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00992) Qiaoya Wu et al. `Understanding the Broad-line Region of Active Galactic Nuclei with Photoionization. II. Slim disks, Self-shadowing, and BLR sizes`
  - **Question:** why do high-accretion-rate AGN fall below the canonical BLR radius--luminosity relation?
  - **Data:**
    - Observational anchor: reverberation-mapping AGN samples.
    - Model ingredients: analytic slim-disk SEDs and axisymmetric photoionization grids.
    - Parameter axes: accretion rate, black-hole mass, BLR gas density, and BLR geometry.
  - **Method:**
    - Compute BLR emissivity-weighted radii under slim-disk illumination.
    - Model self-shadowing as disks become geometrically thick near or above Eddington.
    - Add accretion-rate-dependent gas-density enhancements.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Self-shadowing reduces ionizing flux to low-latitude BLR clouds and flattens the high-accretion R-L relation.
    - Self-shadowing alone cannot explain all lag offsets in low-mass, high-accretion systems.
    - Increasing BLR gas density by a factor of `3-5` per dex in accretion rate improves agreement with RM data.
  - **Position:** relevant to interpreting broad-line masses and accretion states in high-z AGN and LRD analogs.

## Morphology, Mergers, and Environmental Quenching

- [2606.00415](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00415) Marco Chiaberge et al. `Vision-Language Model Ensembles Achieve Human-Expert Accuracy for Galaxy Merger Classification`
  - **Question:** can VLM ensembles classify galaxy mergers with accuracy comparable to trained human experts?
  - **Data:**
    - Test set: `41` VELA+SUNRISE mock galaxy images.
    - Models: `15` VLM classifier configurations across four architectures.
    - Reference: human-expert merger labels from prior work.
  - **Method:**
    - Prompt multiple VLM configurations to classify merger probability.
    - Combine classifiers in a Bayesian ensemble.
    - Compare accuracy, merger fractions, and error profiles against human classifications.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Confident classifications reach `83.3%` accuracy.
    - The recovered merger fraction is `0.52 +/- 0.09`, within `0.66 sigma` of the true value `0.585`.
    - Bayesian weighting improves accuracy by `17.1` percentage points over majority voting.
    - The ensemble makes `5` misclassifications, comparable to `6` human-expert misclassifications in the same benchmark.
  - **Position:** relevant methods paper for scalable morphology and merger-fraction work, though real-survey domain shifts still need validation.

- [2606.01297](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01297) Zi-Hua Ho et al. `Inside-Out vs. Outside-In Quenching of MaNGA Galaxies: Dependence on Stellar Mass and Environment`
  - **Question:** how much do inferred inside-out and outside-in quenching patterns depend on the chosen star-formation-suppression tracer?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: MaNGA DR17.
    - Sample: approximately `10,000` galaxies.
    - Diagnostics: sSFR, `Dn4000`, post-starburst spaxels, and LI(N)ER emission.
  - **Method:**
    - Classify quenching modes using the fraction of quenched area and concentration of quenched area.
    - Compare mode fractions across four suppression diagnostics.
    - Examine dependence on stellar mass, satellite status, and halo mass.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - sSFR yields comparable inside-out and outside-in fractions.
    - `Dn4000` and LI(N)ER diagnostics strongly favor inside-out patterns.
    - Outside-in patterns are more common in lower-mass systems, especially satellites.
    - Halo-mass dependence is weaker and less consistent than stellar-mass or tracer dependence.
  - **Position:** local but useful cautionary paper for spatially resolved quenching diagnostics in future high-redshift IFU work.

- [2606.01570](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01570) Jie Tian et al. `Morphology of Optical Changing-Look AGN-host Galaxies: Evidence for an Important Role of Mergers`
  - **Question:** are changing-look AGN preferentially associated with disturbed or merging host galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `63` low-redshift changing-look AGN at `z<0.15`.
    - Imaging: DESI DR10.
    - Morphology measures: non-parametric indices and visual classifications.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure concentration, asymmetry, smoothness, and Gini-M20 diagnostics.
    - Visually identify merger signatures and shell features.
    - Compare changing-look AGN hosts against non-changing-look AGN samples.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - `18` of `63` hosts, or `29%`, are identified as mergers.
    - About `56%` of the merging systems show shell features.
    - Changing-look AGN hosts have about `2x` higher merger likelihood than comparison samples.
    - Mergers or interactions may help drive changing-look behavior.
  - **Position:** lower-redshift AGN paper, but relevant to the recurring merger--accretion connection.

- [2606.02074](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02074) J. Alfonso L. Aguerri et al. `Hierarchical assembly in action: a galaxy tail from a disrupting group in the Virgo cluster outskirts`
  - **Question:** what does the W cloud in Virgo reveal about group accretion and cluster assembly?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: W cloud and surroundings in the southern outskirts of the Virgo cluster.
    - Observables: galaxy spatial distribution, kinematics, and stellar populations.
    - Structure: W group and newly identified W tail.
  - **Method:**
    - Reconstruct three-dimensional structure and dynamical state.
    - Separate compact-group, tail, and cluster-bound components by velocity and distance.
    - Interpret the W tail geometry in relation to the W group orbit.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The W cloud is not a projected large-scale filament, but is dominated by a compact group interacting with Virgo.
    - The W tail is dynamically coherent and connects the W group to the cluster.
    - The tail forms a planar structure aligned with the W group orbital geometry, supporting a tidal origin.
    - The stripped population is dominated by low-mass, star-forming dwarf galaxies still in the blue cloud.
  - **Position:** local benchmark for hierarchical assembly and preprocessing before cluster accretion.
