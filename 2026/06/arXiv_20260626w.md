The week is strongest for high-redshift gas reservoirs, compact accretion, and early galaxy structure. The read-first paper remains [2606.21361](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21361) F. Valentino: ALMA detects extended `[CII]` around RUBIES-UDS-QG-z7 at `z=7.27`, so the most distant known massive quiescent galaxy still has a large cold-gas reservoir and must suppress star formation rather than simply lack fuel.

Little Red Dot physics stayed active for three straight days. [2606.23778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23778) Visal Sok uses NIRSpec grating spectra to disfavor a uniform full-covering gas envelope for many LRDs. [2606.26098](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26098) Quinn O. Casey adds low-redshift SDSS analogs that test how ordinary quasar signatures can be hidden in low-resolution high-z data.

Early morphology is the second major theme. [2606.23792](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23792) Daniel Ivanov and [2606.23793](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23793) Yingjie Cheng identify and interpret a barred spiral at `z=3.159`. [2606.25022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25022) Xiaohan Wang pushes the bar theme to `z=5.102`, with a massive barred spiral, a broad-line AGN, and an overdense environment. [2606.26264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26264) Laura DeGroot adds JWST/HST evidence for inside-out disk growth at `1.5<=z<=3.0`.

Gas, CGM, and environmental processing form the practical backbone. [2606.22764](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22764) C. Zhang finds kpc-scale filamentary HI in the CGM/IGM around NGC 4631. [2606.24085](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24085) Weimin Yi tracks multiscale quasar outflows from nuclear to CGM radii at `z=3.409`. [2606.24159](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24159) John Franklin Crenshaw detects CGM dust reddening with only `4.6 deg^2` of Rubin DP1. [2606.26320](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26320) Jonathan Freundlich measures molecular-gas depletion in a `z~0.7` overdense cosmic-web group.

Reionization and IGM methods are split between sources and large-scale modeling. [2606.23869](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23869) Fujiang Yu presents a lensed ultra-faint `z~10` arc whose F200W excess may trace extremely metal-poor stars or intense UV lines. [2606.26355](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26355) Omkar Bait and [2606.26537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26537) Yi Hang Valerie Wong focus on LyC escape proxies. [2606.26234](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26234) Roger de Belsunce gives the strongest Ly-alpha forest and high-z galaxy cross-correlation methodology paper.

Survey infrastructure is unusually broad. LEGGOS gives JWST lensing plus IFS workflows for `z~2-4` galaxies. COSMOS-Web and CANUCS/Technicolor provide survey-scale morphology catalogs. Rubin, Euclid, DESI, MeerKLASS, and many SKAO roadmap papers show that the next bottleneck is not only source discovery but calibrated redshifts, gas tracers, morphology, and selection functions.

## High-Redshift Gas Reservoirs and Compact Accretors

- [2606.21361](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21361) F. Valentino et al. `Extended [CII] gas emission in and around a massive quiescent galaxy at z=7.3`
  - **Question:** how can a massive galaxy remain quiescent at `z=7.27` if it still contains cold gas?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: RUBIES-UDS-QG-z7, the most distant known massive quiescent galaxy.
    - Instruments: ALMA Band 6 `[CII] 158 micron` plus JWST/NIRSpec and NIRCam context.
    - Measurements: `[CII]` size, far-infrared continuum limit, and gas-mass estimates.
  - **Method:**
    - Confirm the JWST redshift with ALMA `[CII]`.
    - Compare `[CII]` extent with the compact stellar body.
    - Convert `[CII]` luminosity to molecular and atomic gas masses under multiple calibrations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - `[CII]` emission extends to `R_eff,[CII]=8+/-3 kpc`, far beyond the `R_eff=209(+33/-24) pc` stellar body.
    - Roughly `70%` of the `[CII]` flux comes from a circumgalactic halo.
    - The rest-frame `~160 micron` continuum is undetected, with `L_IR<1.4x10^11 L_sun`.
    - The inferred cold-gas reservoir is large: `log(M_mol/M_sun)=9.53(+0.32/-0.31)` and `f_gas>~20%` under most assumptions.
  - **Position:** weekly read-first because it turns early quenching into a star-formation-efficiency and gas-state problem, not a simple fuel-absence problem.

- [2606.23778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23778) Visal Sok et al. `Constraints on the Gas Geometry Surrounding Little Red Dots through Narrow-Line Diagnostics`
  - **Question:** can a uniform optically thick gas envelope around Little Red Dots explain their narrow-line ratios?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `~20` LRDs with high signal-to-noise JWST/NIRSpec grating spectra.
    - Diagnostics: optical narrow-line ratios including OI/Halpha and HeII/Hbeta.
    - Context: gas-envelope models with reprocessed central-engine radiation.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure ionization parameter and electron-temperature diagnostics.
    - Compare line ratios with stellar photoionization and AGN-like excitation.
    - Test full-covering envelope expectations against high- and low-ionization lines.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At least `40%` of the LRDs require high ionization parameter and electron temperature.
    - Another `15%` fall in the AGN regime for OI/Halpha.
    - HeII/Hbeta is usually `less than or about 0.1`.
    - A uniform gas envelope with covering fraction unity is disfavored.
  - **Position:** central LRD paper of the week because it attacks the assumed geometry behind many compact-source models.

- [2606.21614](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21614) Sophia Geris et al. `Little Red and Blue Dots: AGN-excited narrow lines, Lyman-alpha emission, and resemblance to standard quasars`
  - **Question:** are Little Red and Blue Dots unusual stellar systems, obscured AGN, or a shared compact-accretion population?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `36` Little Red and Blue Dots in the GOODS fields.
    - Redshift range: `2.26<z<7.89`.
    - Selection: compact JWST sources with broad Balmer lines and weak X-ray emission.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare optical and near-infrared diagnostic diagrams.
    - Test Balmer equivalent widths and decrements against ordinary AGN behavior.
    - Use Ly-alpha and narrow-line evidence to infer ionizing leakage into the ISM.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - LRDs and LBDs occupy diagnostic regions consistent with AGN excitation, with deviations plausibly tied to low metallicity.
    - LRD Ly-alpha emission is stronger than in ordinary star-forming galaxies.
    - Fully enclosing gas-envelope models need clumpiness or equatorial leakage.
    - Bolometric luminosities inferred under isotropic complete-covering assumptions are likely unreliable.
  - **Position:** useful because it links LRD/LBD taxonomy to accretion geometry and ionizing escape.

- [2606.23683](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23683) Niranjan Chandra Roy et al. `Little Red Dots on FIRE: Exploring the formation and observational signatures of ultra-compact early galaxies`
  - **Question:** can ultra-compact early galaxies reproduce LRD-like observables without requiring AGN?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: FIRE cosmological runs with dust radiative transfer and synthetic emission-line cubes.
    - Environments: progenitors of present-day group halos with `M_halo>10^13.5 M_sun`.
    - Candidate phases: compact galaxies at `z~4-8`.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify dissipative-inflow phases that build compact stellar cores.
    - Generate synthetic photometry and line widths.
    - Compare simulated observables with LRD Balmer breaks, UV slopes, dust constraints, and broad-line widths.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Compact phases last `~150-400 Myr`.
    - Simulated systems reach `M_star~10^8.5-10^10.5 M_sun`, `-23<M_UV<-20`, and `R_eff<300 pc`.
    - Host-galaxy dynamics can explain Balmer widths up to `~1500 km/s`.
    - Red rest-optical continua, Balmer breaks `>~2.5`, widths `>~2000 km/s`, and broad-Balmer plus narrow-forbidden signatures still point to accretion.
  - **Position:** companion to Geris and Sok because it defines the host-galaxy baseline before invoking AGN.

## Early Morphology, Bars, and Environmental Structure

- [2606.25022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25022) Xiaohan Wang et al. `A massive barred spiral galaxy at z = 5.102 discovered by JWST`
  - **Question:** can a massive barred spiral disk already exist at `z>5`?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: M1149-BSG-z5 in the parallel field of MACS J1149+2223.
    - Instruments: JWST and HST imaging.
    - Environment: a nearby companion galaxy and an overdense region.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify disk, bar, spiral-arm, and bulge components in resolved imaging.
    - Fit isophotal ellipses and structural models.
    - Interpret emission-line and broad-line properties with chemical enrichment and AGN activity.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - M1149-BSG-z5 is a barred spiral at `z=5.102`.
    - The bar length is `a_bar~4.5 kpc`, with spiral arms peaking at `r~5.5 kpc`.
    - The galaxy has `M_*=10^10.45 M_sun`, SFR `144 M_sun/yr`, and `R_e=2.61 kpc`.
    - It hosts a broad-line AGN with `M_BH/M_*~10^-3`.
  - **Position:** top morphology result because it joins early disk structure, AGN growth, and environment at `z>5`.

- [2606.23792](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23792) Daniel Ivanov et al. `Discovery of a Barred-Spiral Galaxy at z_spec = 3.16 I: Bar Identification and Properties`
  - **Question:** were massive barred spiral disks present less than `2 Gyr` after the Big Bang?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: COSMOS-74706 at `z_spec=3.159`.
    - Imaging: JWST/NIRCam F200W, F277W, and F356W.
    - Spectroscopy: archival Keck/MOSDEF coverage.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify spiral morphology and an elongated central structure.
    - Inspect residuals from Sersic fitting.
    - Apply ellipse fitting and Fourier decomposition to test bar strength.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - COSMOS-74706 is an unlensed barred spiral at `z_spec=3.159`.
    - Three independent methods support the bar identification.
    - A rotationally supported disk capable of bar formation existed within `2 Gyr`.
  - **Position:** sets the mid-week bar sequence that Thursday then extends to `z=5.102`.

- [2606.23793](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23793) Yingjie Cheng et al. `Discovery of a Barred-Spiral Galaxy at z_spec = 3.16 II. The Star Formation History`
  - **Question:** what does the star-formation history of COSMOS-74706 imply about early bar growth and quenching?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: COSMOS-74706 at `z_spec=3.1591`.
    - Imaging: deep HST and JWST/NIRCam photometry.
    - Components: spatially separated bar and surrounding regions.
  - **Method:**
    - Decompose the galaxy image.
    - Fit spatially resolved SEDs with Prospector.
    - Compare mass, recent star formation, and age inside and outside the bar.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Total stellar mass is `log(M_*/M_sun)=10.63+/-0.13`.
    - The bar region contains about `30%` of stellar mass.
    - The bar region contributes only about `8%` of recent star formation.
    - The bar is older in mass-weighted age than the surrounding regions.
  - **Position:** important because it connects early bar morphology to old stellar mass and possible bar quenching.

- [2606.26252](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26252) Westley Brown et al. `Cluster vs Field: Clear Evidence for a Morphology-Density Relation in All Environments at z~1.6`
  - **Question:** does the morphology-density relation at `z~1.6` depend on cluster-specific processes?
  - **Data:**
    - Cluster sample: `111` SpARCS cluster galaxies.
    - Field sample: `458` 3D-HST galaxies.
    - Measurements: photometry, spectroscopy, local density, and F160W morphologies.
  - **Method:**
    - Classify galaxies by Sersic index.
    - Compare morphology with stellar mass and local galaxy density.
    - Test cluster and field trends separately.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Bulge-like fraction rises with local density in both cluster and field environments.
    - Median Sersic index increases with local density in both samples.
    - The morphology-density relation is already in place at `z~1.6`.
  - **Position:** useful environmental-structure paper because it shifts attention from cluster membership to local density.

- [2606.26264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26264) Laura DeGroot et al. `Quantifying the inside-out formation of disk galaxies at 1.5 <= z <= 3.0`
  - **Question:** do matched JWST and HST sizes support inside-out disk growth at cosmic noon?
  - **Data:**
    - Field: Extended Groth Strip.
    - Redshift range: `1.5<=z<=3.0`.
    - Imaging: JWST rest-frame optical and HST rest-frame UV.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure UV and optical half-light radii at comparable resolution.
    - Compare sizes with dust, stellar mass, SFR, and clumpy morphology.
    - Revisit HST-based inside-out growth claims with JWST resolution.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - UV sizes are generally larger than optical sizes.
    - The difference remains after dust correction.
    - Clumpy galaxies with `n<0.5` usually have larger UV sizes because star formation is distributed over clumps or mergers.
  - **Position:** complements the bar papers by treating disk growth as a resolved radial process.

## CGM, IGM, and Reionization-Scale Probes

- [2606.23869](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23869) Fujiang Yu et al. `A Strongly Lensed Ultra-faint Arc at z approximately 10 with an F200W excess in Abell S1063`
  - **Question:** what powers the F200W excess in a highly magnified ultra-faint `z~10` galaxy?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: GAR10, an F115W-dropout arc in Abell S1063.
    - Instruments: JWST/NIRCam imaging from GLIMPSE and GO-1840.
    - Lensing: median magnification `mu=43(+78/-20)`.
  - **Method:**
    - Infer intrinsic luminosity using cluster lensing.
    - Fit exploratory Prospector SED models.
    - Compare continuum-dominated and emission-line-dominated explanations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The intrinsic UV magnitude is `M_UV~-15.8`.
    - The continuum case gives `z=10.75(-0.34/+0.41)` and `beta=-2.92+/-0.12`.
    - The line-emission case gives `z=10.45(-0.21/+0.11)` with age `1-3 Myr`.
    - CIV, HeII, and CIII] can explain the F200W excess in the line-emission case.
  - **Position:** high-priority reionization-frontier entry because it is an ultra-faint spectroscopy target where Pop III-like and extreme-line interpretations remain separable.

- [2606.26234](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26234) Roger de Belsunce et al. `Lyman-Alpha Forest and its Cross-Correlation with High-Redshift Galaxies in Effective Field Theory at the Field Level`
  - **Question:** can field-level EFT jointly model Ly-alpha forest absorption and high-redshift galaxy cross-correlations?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: AbacusSummit painted Ly-alpha fields and Sherwood hydrodynamic simulations.
    - Redshift range: `z=2.0-3.2`.
    - Forecast context: DESI and DESI-II Ly-alpha forest, LBG, and LAE samples.
  - **Method:**
    - Build a perturbative forward model for the Ly-alpha forest flux decrement.
    - Validate power spectra, counts-in-cells statistics, and halo cross-correlations.
    - Generate DESI and DESI-II clustering mocks.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The model matches simulated 3D power spectra at `1%` to `k<=0.3 h/Mpc`.
    - It matches 1D power spectra at `5%` to `k<=1.0 h/Mpc`.
    - Counts-in-cells agree down to `2 Mpc/h`.
    - Phenomenological flux-power models fail at field level even on quasi-linear scales.
  - **Position:** best IGM methods paper of the week because it links absorption maps to actual high-z galaxy samples.

- [2606.24085](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24085) Weimin Yi et al. `Radial gradients revealed by multiscale outflows from down-the-barrel spectroscopy toward a quasar at redshift 3.4`
  - **Question:** how does quasar feedback couple from nuclear scales to the CGM at `z=3.409`?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: a luminous quasar at `z=3.409`.
    - Observations: down-the-barrel spectroscopy spanning two decades.
    - Tracers: N V and C IV absorption across multiple outflow scales.
  - **Method:**
    - Track spectral variability over epochs.
    - Compare trough depths for outflows at different radii.
    - Interpret opposite N V and C IV behavior as abundance or ionization gradients.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Three freely expanding outflow scales show opposite N V and C IV depth trends.
    - The contrast supports a tenable radial N/C gradient.
    - The system marks a transition from ejective small-scale feedback to regulative large-scale feedback.
  - **Position:** high-value CGM feedback entry because it connects time-domain spectra to radial wind impact.

- [2606.24159](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24159) John Franklin Crenshaw et al. `A First Measurement of Circumgalactic Dust Reddening from Only 4.6 deg^2 of the Rubin Observatory's DP1`
  - **Question:** can early Rubin data already detect circumgalactic dust around foreground galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: Vera C. Rubin Observatory ComCam Data Preview 1.
    - Area: `4.6 deg^2`.
    - Measurement: stacked background-galaxy colors around foreground galaxies.
  - **Method:**
    - Use photometric redshifts for foreground and background selection.
    - Stack reddening profiles by projected radius.
    - Interpret average color excess with a Milky Way extinction curve.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Reddening is detected from `r_perp~10 kpc` to `1 Mpc`.
    - Within `120 kpc`, `A_V=(1.2+/-0.4)x10^-1 (r_perp/20 kpc)^(-1.8+/-0.4)`.
    - The innermost `10-15 kpc` bin reaches `A_V~0.3 mag`.
    - Red foreground galaxies show stronger extinction than blue foreground galaxies.
  - **Position:** important pathfinder because CGM dust becomes measurable before the full LSST footprint.

- [2606.26320](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26320) Jonathan Freundlich et al. `Probing the molecular gas content of galaxies in an over-dense group at z~0.7: a test case for environmental quenching`
  - **Question:** how strongly can a dense group environment suppress molecular gas before mature cluster assembly?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: COSMOS-Gr30 at `z~0.7`.
    - Instruments: IRAM NOEMA and 30m CO(2-1) observations plus MUSE context.
    - Environment: a cosmic-web filament intersection with a `~10^4 kpc^2` ionized gas structure.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure CO detections and upper limits for spectroscopic group members.
    - Stack molecular-gas constraints.
    - Compare gas fractions with field main-sequence scaling relations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Four group galaxies are detected in CO at `S/N>5`.
    - Average molecular-gas content is reduced by `~0.5 dex`.
    - Gas fractions are `20%-40%` of typical main-sequence values.
    - Extended ionized gas has `M_gas<2x10^10 M_sun`, so less than one third is cold and star-forming.
  - **Position:** practical environment paper because it observes molecular-gas depletion in a cosmic-web group.

## Survey Infrastructure and Future Gas Measurements

- [2606.20845](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20845) Gourav Khullar et al. `LEGGOS I: The JWST LEGGOS Survey -- LEnsing and Galaxy Growth: Observing Substructures -- Unpacks the Nature of Clumpy Star Formation and Quenching in Gravitationally Lensed Galaxies beyond Cosmic Noon`
  - **Question:** how can JWST and strong lensing resolve star formation, dust, and quenching on parsec-to-sub-kpc scales in high-redshift galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: LEGGOS.
    - Targets: eight highly magnified lensed galaxies at `z~2-4`.
    - Instruments: JWST/NIRCam imaging and NIRSpec integral-field spectroscopy.
  - **Method:**
    - Use strong lensing to reach `~10-200 pc` scales.
    - Combine lensing reconstruction, photometry, IFS, and Balmer-line diagnostics.
    - Fit flexible spectrophotometric stellar-population models.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Spectroscopy breaks degeneracies in photometry-only clump studies.
    - Some systems show recent quenching in regions previously treated as UV star-forming.
    - LEGGOS bridges local parsec-scale star formation and galaxy assembly beyond cosmic noon.
  - **Position:** key JWST methods entry because it makes resolved high-z clump work more physical.

- [2606.14869](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14869) Lilan Yang et al. `COSMOS-Web morphology catalog`
  - **Question:** what morphology measurements are available for the largest current JWST galaxy imaging fields?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: COSMOS-Web.
    - Sample: about `780000` galaxies.
    - Imaging: four JWST/NIRCam filters.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure structural parameters consistently across the field.
    - Provide morphology products for broad community use.
    - Support size, concentration, and shape analyses across redshift.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - COSMOS-Web now has survey-scale JWST morphology infrastructure.
    - The catalog enables statistical structural work beyond small-field case studies.
    - The release is central for linking JWST morphology to environment and galaxy evolution.
  - **Position:** primary morphology-catalog entry because structure becomes a survey measurement.

- [2606.26640](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26640) Ran Wang et al. `Cm-wavelength Studies of Molecular Gas and Star Formation at High Redshift with the SKA`
  - **Question:** what SKA frequency coverage is needed to measure cold and dense molecular gas in high-redshift galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Facility context: SKA high-frequency capabilities below and beyond `~50 GHz`.
    - Tracers: redshifted low-J CO plus HCN, HCO+, HNC, and related dense-gas lines.
    - Science context: JWST young-galaxy discoveries and ALMA high-J/far-infrared detections.
  - **Method:**
    - Review high-redshift molecular-gas use cases for the updated SKA science book.
    - Compare ALMA high-J tracers with SKA-accessible low-J transitions.
    - Identify frequency requirements for cold-gas and dense-gas work.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - SKA coverage beyond `15 GHz` is needed for low-J molecular-line studies at high redshift.
    - Low-J CO and dense-gas tracers measure the gas reservoir missed by high-J lines alone.
    - SKA molecular-line work will complement JWST source discovery with fuel measurements.
  - **Position:** key Friday infrastructure addition because source discovery needs matched gas diagnostics.

- [2606.26355](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26355) Omkar Bait et al. `Probing the Nature of Lyman Continuum Emitting and Low-metallicity Galaxies Using the SKA`
  - **Question:** can SKA radio-continuum measurements diagnose the feedback physics that lets LyC photons escape?
  - **Data:**
    - Targets: LyC emitters and low-mass metal-poor galaxies.
    - Frequency regime: `1-15 GHz`.
    - Forecast samples: LyC-emitter candidates at `z~1-3`.
  - **Method:**
    - Use radio-continuum slopes and normalization as star-formation and cosmic-ray diagnostics.
    - Compare radio behavior with LyC escape fraction, metallicity, ionization conditions, and SFR surface density.
    - Forecast SKA-Mid surface densities.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - SKA-Mid surveys can assemble `~10-100` LyC candidates per square degree.
