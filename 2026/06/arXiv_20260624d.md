## Executive View

The Wednesday listing is strongest for JWST-era high-redshift galaxies and gas around galaxies. The read-first paper is [2606.23778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23778) Visal Sok. It uses high-S/N NIRSpec grating spectra of `~20` Little Red Dots and shows that at least `40%` require high ionization parameter and electron temperature. This directly tests whether a uniform optically thick gas envelope can hide the central engine.

Early-galaxy morphology is unusually prominent. [2606.23792](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23792) Daniel Ivanov reports a barred spiral galaxy at `z_spec=3.159`; [2606.23793](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23793) Yingjie Cheng uses the same system to infer an old, massive bar component and possible early bar quenching. [2606.23869](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23869) Fujiang Yu adds a reionization-frontier lensing case: an ultra-faint `z~10` arc in Abell S1063 with an F200W excess that may trace extremely metal-poor stars or intense UV lines.

The gas-cycling theme is broad. [2606.24085](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24085) Weimin Yi tracks multiscale quasar outflows from nuclear to CGM scales at `z=3.409`. [2606.24159](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24159) John Franklin Crenshaw makes a first Rubin DP1 measurement of circumgalactic dust reddening from only `4.6 deg^2`. [2606.24735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24735) Yiyuan Zhang uses TNG50 to show that halo assembly history imprints CGM mass, metallicity, and cold-gas angular momentum at fixed halo mass.

Survey and infrastructure entries are useful but less immediately central. [2606.23784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23784) Silvan Fischbacher extends GalSBI to jointly model galaxy population and clustering. [2606.24744](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24744) Kenneth J. Duncan lays out how high-multiplex optical spectroscopy unlocks SKAO extragalactic science. [2606.24697](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24697) Euclid Collaboration: F. Passalacqua quantifies slitless-spectroscopy cross-contamination for Euclid/NISP redshift recovery.

Cluster and group physics fill out the day. [2606.23790](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23790) Makoto Ando reports anisotropic quenching around `0.9<z<1.4` clusters and links it to preprocessing along the central-galaxy major axis. [2606.24142](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24142) Keiichi Umetsu delivers weak-lensing calibration for `41` CHEX-MATE clusters. [2606.23775](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23775) Rainer Weinberger and [2606.23798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23798) Thomas Berlok introduce PICO-Cluster simulations for cool-core assembly, jet feedback, magnetic fields, and plasma heating.

## Compact High-Redshift Sources and Early Galaxy Structure

- [2606.23778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23778) Visal Sok et al. `Constraints on the Gas Geometry Surrounding Little Red Dots through Narrow-Line Diagnostics`
  - **Question:** can a uniform optically thick gas envelope around Little Red Dots explain their narrow-line ratios?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `~20` LRDs with high signal-to-noise JWST/NIRSpec grating spectra.
    - Diagnostics: multiple optical narrow-line ratios, including OI/Halpha and HeII/Hbeta.
    - Context: gas-envelope models in which the continuum comes from reprocessed central-engine radiation.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure line-ratio diagnostics for ionization parameter and electron temperature.
    - Test whether stellar photoionization from a host galaxy can power the narrow lines.
    - Compare high-ionization and low-ionization diagnostics against full-covering envelope expectations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At least `40%` of the LRDs have line ratios requiring high ionization parameter and electron temperature.
    - A further `15%` fall in the AGN regime for the OI/Halpha diagnostic.
    - Most objects lack strong high-ionization emission, with HeII/Hbeta `less than or about 0.1`.
    - A uniform gas envelope with covering fraction unity is disfavored.
    - Lower-density channels can let hard photons escape and excite surrounding narrow-line gas.
  - **Position:** read first because it directly tests the geometry that many LRD interpretations assume.

- [2606.23869](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23869) Fujiang Yu et al. `A Strongly Lensed Ultra-faint Arc at z approximately 10 with an F200W excess in Abell S1063`
  - **Question:** what powers the unusual F200W excess in a highly magnified ultra-faint `z~10` galaxy?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: GAR10, an F115W-dropout arc in Abell S1063.
    - Instruments: JWST/NIRCam imaging from GLIMPSE and GO-1840.
    - Lensing: median magnification `mu=43(+78/-20)`.
  - **Method:**
    - Use cluster lensing to infer the intrinsic luminosity of the source.
    - Fit exploratory Prospector SED models under two physical interpretations.
    - Compare continuum-dominated and emission-line-dominated explanations for the F200W excess.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The intrinsic UV magnitude is `M_UV approximately -15.8`.
    - The continuum case gives `z=10.75(-0.34/+0.41)`, `beta=-2.92+/-0.12`, and `log(Z/Z_sun)=-3.56(-0.85/+0.65)`.
    - The line-emission case gives `z=10.45(-0.21/+0.11)` with age `1-3 Myr`.
    - CIV, HeII, and CIII] can explain the F200W excess in the line-emission case.
    - JWST/NIRSpec spectroscopy is needed to separate Pop III-like continuum from extreme UV-line emission.
  - **Position:** high-priority reionization-frontier case because it pushes ultra-faint spectroscopy-relevant science into the lensing regime.

- [2606.23792](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23792) Daniel Ivanov et al. `Discovery of a Barred-Spiral Galaxy at z_spec = 3.16 I: Bar Identification and Properties`
  - **Question:** were massive barred spiral disks already present less than `2 Gyr` after the Big Bang?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: COSMOS-74706 at `z_spec=3.159`.
    - Imaging: JWST/NIRCam F200W, F277W, and F356W.
    - Spectroscopy: archival Keck/MOSDEF overlapping a blue clump.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify spiral morphology and an elongated central structure in JWST imaging.
    - Inspect residuals from Sersic-profile fitting.
    - Fit isophotal ellipses and search for bar-like ellipticity and position-angle profiles.
    - Apply Fourier decomposition and compare the bisymmetric mode with calibrated bar-strength thresholds.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - COSMOS-74706 is an unlensed barred spiral galaxy at `z_spec=3.159`.
    - Three independent methods support the bar identification.
    - Photometric constraints place the blue clump at the same redshift as the main spiral.
    - A rotationally supported disk capable of bar formation was already in place within `2 Gyr`.
  - **Position:** central morphology result of the day because it makes early disk secular structure observational rather than only inferred.

- [2606.23793](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23793) Yingjie Cheng et al. `Discovery of a Barred-Spiral Galaxy at z_spec = 3.16 II. The Star Formation History`
  - **Question:** what does the star-formation history of COSMOS-74706 imply about early bar growth and quenching?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: COSMOS-74706 at `z_spec=3.1591`.
    - Imaging: deep HST and JWST/NIRCam multi-band photometry.
    - Model inputs: spatially separated bar and surrounding regions.
  - **Method:**
    - Decompose the galaxy image and mask components.
    - Fit spatially resolved SEDs with Prospector.
    - Compare mass, recent star formation, and age in the bar and disk regions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Total stellar mass is `log(M_star/M_sun)=10.63+/-0.13`.
    - The bar region contains about `30%` of the stellar mass.
    - The same region contributes only about `8%` of recent star formation.
    - The potential bar is older in mass-weighted age than surrounding regions.
    - A rapid bar onset at this redshift implies a low dark-matter fraction and baryon-dominated massive galaxy structure.
  - **Position:** companion to Ivanov because it connects early bar morphology to inside-out growth and possible bar quenching.

## CGM, IGM, Quasar Feedback, and Environmental Processing

- [2606.24085](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24085) Weimin Yi et al. `Radial gradients revealed by multiscale outflows from down-the-barrel spectroscopy toward a quasar at redshift 3.4`
  - **Question:** how does quasar feedback couple from nuclear scales to the CGM at `z=3.409`?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: a luminous quasar at `z=3.409`.
    - Observations: multi-epoch down-the-barrel spectroscopy spanning two decades.
    - Tracers: N V and C IV absorption troughs across different outflow scales.
  - **Method:**
    - Track spectral variability and normalization effects across epochs.
    - Compare trough depths for outflows at multiple radial scales.
    - Interpret opposite N V and C IV behavior as an abundance or ionization gradient.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Three freely expanding outflow scales show opposite depth trends in N V and C IV.
    - The contrast supports a tenable radial N/C gradient.
    - The system marks a transition from ejective feedback on small scales to regulative feedback on large scales.
    - The result targets the wind-ISM/CGM coupling problem that remains difficult for simulations.
  - **Position:** high-value quasar feedback entry because it links time-domain spectroscopy to radial CGM impact.

- [2606.24159](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24159) John Franklin Crenshaw et al. `A First Measurement of Circumgalactic Dust Reddening from Only 4.6 deg^2 of the Rubin Observatory's DP1`
  - **Question:** can early Rubin data already detect circumgalactic dust around foreground galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: Vera C. Rubin Observatory ComCam Data Preview 1.
    - Area: `4.6 deg^2`, about `0.03%` of the final LSST footprint.
    - Measurement: stacked background-galaxy colors around foreground galaxies.
  - **Method:**
    - Use photometric redshifts to select foreground and background galaxies.
    - Stack chromatic reddening profiles as a function of projected radius.
    - Interpret average E(g-z) using a Milky Way extinction curve.
    - Split the foreground sample by rest-frame color.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Reddening is detected from `r_perp approximately 10 kpc` to `1 Mpc`.
    - Within `120 kpc`, `A_V=(1.2+/-0.4)x10^-1 (r_perp/20 kpc)^(-1.8+/-0.4)`.
    - The innermost `10-15 kpc` bin reaches `A_V approximately 0.3 mag`.
    - Red foreground galaxies show stronger extinction than blue foreground galaxies.
    - The inferred dust-to-stellar-mass ratio is about `1%`, close to the stellar metal-yield budget.
  - **Position:** important CGM pathfinder because it shows that Rubin can measure halo dust well before the full LSST footprint.

- [2606.24735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24735) Yiyuan Zhang et al. `The dependence of Circumgalactic Medium properties on halo assembly histories in the IllustrisTNG simulations`
  - **Question:** how much does halo assembly history affect CGM properties at fixed halo mass?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: IllustrisTNG TNG50.
    - Halo mass range: `10^10.5-10^12.5 M_sun`.
    - Classification: early-forming and late-forming halos.
  - **Method:**
    - Calculate halo formation times and split halos by assembly history.
    - Compare embedded galaxy properties and CGM gas properties at `z~0`.
    - Track CGM gas origins through fresh accretion and wet mergers.
    - Measure cold-gas angular momentum and rotational support.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Early-forming halos host galaxies with higher stellar mass and metallicity.
    - Early-forming halos have lower CGM gas mass and lower sSFR.
    - CGM metallicity is higher in early-forming halos below `10^12 M_sun`, but the trend reverses in the `10^12-10^12.5 M_sun` bin.
    - Fresh accretion is insensitive to assembly history, while late-forming galaxies experience more wet mergers.
    - Below `10^12 M_sun`, cold gas in late-forming halos has higher specific angular momentum and stronger rotational support.
  - **Position:** useful simulation anchor because it ties CGM observables to assembly bias rather than halo mass alone.

- [2606.23790](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23790) Makoto Ando et al. `Anisotropic quenching beyond z=1 and its implications for preprocessing around high-redshift galaxy clusters`
  - **Question:** does anisotropic satellite quenching around clusters already operate beyond `z=1`?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `12` spectroscopically confirmed clusters at `0.9<z<1.4`.
    - Observable: quiescent satellite fraction as a function of orientation from the central-galaxy major axis.
    - Interpretation support: cosmological simulation and quenching model.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure satellite quenching anisotropy relative to the central-galaxy major axis.
    - Track satellite accretion histories in a cosmological simulation.
    - Combine accretion histories with a delay-then-rapid quenching model.
    - Separate intrahalo quenching from preprocessing.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Anisotropic quenching is detected at modest `~2 sigma` significance.
    - In `z=1.25` clusters, most satellites are recently infalled within `less than or about 2 Gyr`.
    - Satellite orientation angles are randomized in about `2 Gyr` after accretion.
    - Preprocessing dominates the quenching budget in the model.
    - The preprocessing-attributable quenched fraction is higher along the major axis by about `20%`.
  - **Position:** relevant environment paper because it connects high-redshift cluster quenching to large-scale accretion geometry.

## Cluster, Group, and Survey Infrastructure

- [2606.23784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23784) Silvan Fischbacher et al. `GalSBI: Forward Modelling Galaxy Clustering and Population`
  - **Question:** can image-level galaxy population simulations also reproduce clustering and redshift distributions for large-scale-structure surveys?
  - **Data:**
    - Framework: GalSBI with subhalo abundance matching based on optimal transport.
    - Observations: DES Y3 imaging, HSC deep fields, and COSMOS high-precision photometric redshifts.
    - Products: simulated photometry, morphology, clustering, redshift distributions, luminosity function, and galaxy-halo connection.
  - **Method:**
    - Generate UFig image simulations.
    - Use simulation-based inference to constrain galaxy-population parameters.
    - Compare angular power spectra under magnitude and color cuts.
    - Propagate clustering contribution into sample-variance uncertainty.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Simulated photometry and morphology agree with DES and HSC deep-field imaging.
    - Galaxy clustering agrees with data across magnitude and color cuts.
    - Mean redshifts agree within `0.2-1.6 sigma` for different magnitude cuts.
    - Sample variance dominates bright samples at `<24 mag`; galaxy-population uncertainty dominates fainter samples.
    - The public model can support survey tests of sample variance, source clustering, redshift distributions, and blending.
  - **Position:** important methods paper because it joins imaging realism and clustering realism in one forward-modeling framework.

- [2606.24697](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24697) Euclid Collaboration: F. Passalacqua et al. `Euclid preparation. First investigation of the impact of cross-contamination on spectroscopic redshift measurements with pixel-level simulations`
  - **Question:** how strongly does overlapping slitless spectra contamination degrade Euclid/NISP redshift measurements?
  - **Data:**
    - Instrument: Euclid Near-Infrared Spectrometer and Photometer.
    - Simulations: pixel-level NISP images with idealized and contaminated cases.
    - Contaminants: Halpha emitters in the target redshift range and other galaxies.
  - **Method:**
    - Process simulated images through the Euclid spectroscopic pipeline.
    - First measure redshift recovery in non-overlapping spectra.
    - Add contamination with increasing numbers of bright-to-faint neighboring galaxies.
    - Separate target-range Halpha contamination from all-galaxy contamination.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Redshift accuracy and reliability depend on Halpha flux and galaxy size in the idealized case.
    - Sources fainter than magnitude `20` show no clear evidence for additional degradation.
    - The analysis conservatively tracks contaminants down to magnitude `24`.
    - Same-redshift-range Halpha contaminants contribute about `4%` of total degradation from all-galaxy cross-contamination.
  - **Position:** useful Euclid methods note because NISP redshift failures feed directly into large-scale-structure systematics.

- [2606.24744](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24744) Kenneth J. Duncan et al. `Unlocking the Full Potential of SKAO Extra-galactic Science with High-multiplex Optical Spectroscopy`
  - **Question:** what optical spectroscopy is needed to make SKAO extragalactic continuum and HI surveys physically interpretable?
  - **Data:**
    - Facilities: SKAO continuum and HI surveys plus current and future optical multi-object spectrographs.
    - Science cases: radio AGN, star-forming galaxies, HI stacking, local environment, and large-scale structure.
    - Redshift reach: HI statistical work to `z=1` and beyond.
  - **Method:**
    - Map radio observables to required redshift, emission-line, and continuum diagnostics.
    - Identify synergies with existing optical spectroscopic surveys.
    - Specify where future dedicated spectroscopic surveys are needed.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Precise redshifts are required to separate star formation from AGN activity in radio samples.
    - Optical diagnostics are needed to identify accretion modes and host-galaxy histories.
    - HI stacking beyond the local Universe depends on extensive spectroscopic redshifts.
    - Environment and large-scale-structure science are major spectroscopy-limited SKAO products.
  - **Position:** relevant planning paper because it defines the optical-side infrastructure needed for future baryon-cycle surveys.

- [2606.23775](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23775) Rainer Weinberger et al. `Active galactic nucleus driven jet feedback in cosmologically forming cool-core galaxy clusters I: The effect of hierarchical assembly on intra-cluster medium properties`
  - **Question:** how does cosmological assembly change the ICM response to AGN jet feedback in cool-core clusters?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: PICO-Cluster cosmological zooms with explicit AGN jet feedback.
    - Comparisons: PICO-Cluster IllustrisTNG kinetic feedback and isolated cluster jet-feedback simulations.
    - Targets: thermodynamic profiles, gas and stellar fractions, velocity structure, and multiphase gas.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare cosmological and isolated cluster simulations under jet feedback.
    - Test whether AGN heating balances cooling.
    - Separate central jet effects from satellite-driven gas structure at large radii.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Jet-feedback cosmological clusters reproduce observed stellar and gas fractions and local cool-core thermodynamic profiles.
    - IllustrisTNG kinetic feedback runs do not match those observations as well.
    - AGN heating roughly balances cooling and suppresses star formation.
    - Gas beyond `50 kpc` is strongly shaped by satellite galaxies, not only by the central jet.
    - Cosmological simulations produce non-thermal pressure support more consistent with observations than isolated simulations.
  - **Position:** cluster-theory anchor because it shows that realistic assembly histories are part of the feedback problem.

- [2606.24142](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24142) Keiichi Umetsu et al. `CHEX-MATE: AMALGAM weak-lensing analysis of 41 Planck Sunyaev-Zel'dovich-selected galaxy clusters`
  - **Question:** what weak-lensing mass calibration does CHEX-MATE need for multi-probe cluster studies?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `41` Planck SZ-selected CHEX-MATE clusters at `0.11<=z<=0.55`.
    - Imaging: Subaru/Suprime-Cam and CFHT/MegaPrime from AMALGAM.
    - Signals: azimuthally averaged weak lensing around X-ray peaks.
  - **Method:**
    - Detect tangential shear and check the `45 deg` rotated component.
    - Fit NFW profiles to infer weak-lensing mass and concentration.
    - Assess systematic mass-calibration uncertainty.
    - Fit hierarchical Bayesian scaling relations for concentration and Planck SZ mass proxy.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Median cluster weak-lensing signal-to-noise is `6.5`.
    - The rotated component has median signal-to-noise `-0.1`, consistent with zero.
    - Total systematic uncertainty in weak-lensing mass calibration is `8%`.
    - At `M_200=10^15 M_sun` and `z=0.25`, `c_200=3.53+/-0.71` with intrinsic scatter `0.22+/-0.04 dex`.
    - The baseline Planck proxy result gives `M_SZ/M_500=0.83+/-0.09` at `M_500=7x10^14 M_sun`.
  - **Position:** useful cluster-infrastructure result because it anchors X-ray/SZ cluster interpretation to lensing masses.
