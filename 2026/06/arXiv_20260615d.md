## Executive View

The Monday listing is compact but useful for reionization, JWST feedback work, and high-redshift AGN environments. The read-first paper is [2606.14671](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14671) Zebedee Summerfield, which uses more than `35,000` THESAN-ZOOM galaxy realizations to connect LyC escape with bursty star formation, gas clearing, and UV-bright sources at `z=3-16`.

The best observational JWST entry is [2606.13819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13819) L. Ulivi, which uses JWST/NIRSpec IFU data for six galaxies at `3<z<9` to connect ionized outflows with spatially resolved stellar-population histories. Massive AGN hosts with strong outflows show nuclear quenching signatures within the last `~100-300 Myr`, while lower-mass `z>5` starbursts show more continuous growth.

High-redshift quasar environments get a strong simulation anchor in [2606.13784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13784) Akanksha Kapahtia. The paper compares multiple cosmological simulations and finds that luminous quasars usually occupy `10^12-10^12.5 M_sun` haloes in TNG-based runs at `z=3-6`, with large instantaneous AGN-luminosity scatter at fixed halo mass.

The main survey-product paper is [2606.13781](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13781) Maya Merchant, a CANUCS/Technicolor structural-parameter release for `~41,000` galaxies in up to `29` HST+JWST filters, with a focused size-mass analysis for `~4,100` star-forming galaxies at `0.6<z<=4`.

The best CGM-method paper is [2606.13777](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13777) Jonas Biba, which bridges TNG50 CGM turbulence with idealized AREPO boxes and finds subsonic CGM flows with Mach numbers around `0.1-0.7`. It is lower-redshift but directly useful for interpreting multiphase CGM survival and mixing.

The remaining entries are mostly tools, local-Universe AGN, cluster analogs, morphology, and lower-redshift dynamics. They are useful background rather than immediate read-first material for high-redshift galaxy/LSS/IGM work.

## Reionization Sources and Escape Diagnostics

- [2606.14671](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14671) Zebedee Summerfield et al. `From THESAN-ZOOM to JWST: Predicting ionizing photon escape and the rise of UV-bright reionization sources`
  - **Question:** which galaxy properties best predict LyC escape and escaped ionizing emissivity during reionization?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: more than `35,000` galaxy realizations from THESAN-ZOOM cosmological radiation-hydrodynamic simulations.
    - Redshift range: `z=3-16`.
    - Observables: simulation-derived diagnostics plus JWST-accessible photometric quantities.
  - **Method:**
    - Train random-forest regressors for the escape fraction `f_esc`.
    - Train parallel models for escaped ionizing photon rate `N_ion,esc`.
    - Compare full-physics diagnostic sets with JWST-observable restricted sets.
    - Combine UV luminosity functions with inferred `N_ion,esc-M_UV` relations to reconstruct reionization histories.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The `SFR_10/SFR_100` ratio and `M_gas/M_*` are the strongest `f_esc` diagnostics.
    - Rest-frame `1500 A` UV magnitude dominates `N_ion,esc` prediction.
    - Reionization histories are consistent with observational constraints and avoid an ionizing-photon-budget crisis.
    - The dominant contribution comes from UV-bright galaxies with `M_UV<-17` after `z~8`.
  - **Position:** read first because it turns JWST-accessible galaxy properties into a simulation-calibrated reionization-emissivity model.

- [2606.14682](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14682) Sambit K. Giri et al. `Impact of 21-cm foreground mitigation strategies on reionization power spectrum constraints`
  - **Question:** how do foreground avoidance and foreground removal bias 21-cm reionization parameter inference?
  - **Data:**
    - Observable: EoR 21-cm power-spectrum constraints.
    - Foreground strategies: Fourier-space foreground avoidance and Gaussian-process foreground removal.
    - Parameters: minimum star-forming halo mass, ionizing escape fraction, and neutral-fraction history.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare inferred astrophysical parameters under foreground avoidance and foreground removal.
    - Track parameter biases and posterior widths.
    - Test multi-redshift inference when individual bins are poorly mitigated.
    - Recompare strategies at matched length scales.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Both methods can introduce parameter biases up to about `1 sigma`.
    - Foreground avoidance yields broader posteriors because it discards more contaminated modes.
    - Global reionization histories remain within the `95%` credible interval.
    - Poorly mitigated redshift bins can contaminate multi-redshift inference unless independent quality metrics identify them.
  - **Position:** relevant survey-method paper because reionization constraints depend on foreground choices as much as on signal sensitivity.

## JWST Feedback, AGN, and Galaxy Structure

- [2606.13819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13819) L. Ulivi et al. `GA-NIFS: The interplay between feedback and star formation at 3 < z < 9 probed by JWST/NIRSpec IFU`
  - **Question:** how do ionized outflows relate to spatially resolved star-formation histories in early galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Instrument: JWST/NIRSpec IFU.
    - Sample: six starburst- and AGN-dominated galaxies at `3<z<9`.
    - Spectroscopy: low-resolution `R~100` and high-resolution `R~2700` modes.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine spatially resolved stellar-population analysis with previously measured outflow properties.
    - Separate massive AGN-host systems from lower-mass high-redshift starburst systems.
    - Compare nuclear and galaxy-wide star-formation histories against outflow strength.
    - Interpret one rejuvenated AGN host in the context of a recent major merger.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Massive galaxies with `M_*>5x10^10 M_sun` and strong AGN-driven outflows show quenching episodes within `~100-300 Myr`.
    - Quenching signatures are concentrated in nuclear regions with `r<3 kpc`.
    - Lower-mass `z>5` starburst galaxies with powerful outflows show continuous growth.
    - One AGN host shows recent `10-30 Myr` rejuvenation, likely merger-fueled.
  - **Position:** top observational entry because it links high-redshift IFU outflows to resolved stellar-population histories rather than treating feedback as a line-profile measurement alone.

- [2606.14477](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14477) David J. Rosario et al. `GATOS N: Extended circumnuclear dust emission in nearby Seyfert galaxies surveyed by JWST/MIRI`
  - **Question:** how widespread is resolved AGN-heated dust in nearby Seyfert nuclei, and how does it relate to the NLR?
  - **Data:**
    - Instrument: JWST/MIRI MIRIM imaging from `5-25 micron`.
    - Sample: nearby Seyfert galaxies from the GATOS program.
    - Spatial scale: central few `100 pc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Isolate nuclear and extended MIR emission.
    - Compare MIR structures with stars, ionized gas, molecular gas, and absorbing dust.
    - Account for emission-line contamination in MIRI filters.
    - Search for signatures of dusty nuclear outflows.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Resolved AGN-heated dust is widespread in Seyfert galaxies.
    - The dust extends to a few `100 pc` from the nucleus.
    - MIR dust emission strongly correlates with AGN-ionized NLR gas.
    - JWST/MIRI reaches surface-brightness sensitivity beyond ground-based MIR work.
  - **Position:** lower-redshift but useful because it calibrates how JWST resolves dusty AGN feedback structures that may be unresolved in high-redshift compact sources.

- [2606.13781](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13781) Maya Merchant et al. `CANUCS/Technicolor Data Release 2: A Catalogue of Galaxy Structural Parameters in up to 29 HST+JWST bands and a Multi-Wavelength Exploration of the Galaxy Size-Mass Relation at 0.6 < z <= 4`
  - **Question:** how do galaxy sizes depend on stellar mass, redshift, and rest-frame wavelength when measured with multi-band HST+JWST imaging?
  - **Data:**
    - Surveys: CANUCS and Technicolor.
    - Imaging: `19` NIRCam medium- and broadband filters in five CANUCS flanking fields, plus HST bands.
    - Catalog: structural parameters for `~41,000` galaxies in up to `29` filters.
    - Focus sample: `~4,100` star-forming galaxies at `0.6<z<=4` and `8.5<log(M_*/M_sun)<=11.5`.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit galaxy morphologies with GALFIT.
    - Measure size as a function of stellar mass, redshift, and rest-frame wavelength.
    - Parametrize the galaxy size-wavelength relation.
    - Introduce wavelength as a free parameter in the optical/NIR size-mass relation.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The slope of the size-mass relation changes with rest-frame wavelength.
    - A crossover mass near `10^9.5 M_sun` separates diffuse and compact morphology regimes.
    - The data release enables multi-wavelength structural work across HST and JWST filters.
  - **Position:** strong infrastructure entry because high-redshift structure measurements increasingly require wavelength-aware morphology rather than single-band sizes.

## Quasar Hosts, AGN Modeling, and CGM Physics

- [2606.13784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13784) Akanksha Kapahtia et al. `The host halo masses of AGNs and quasars at z ~ 3-7 with TNG-Cluster, FLAMINGO and other cosmological galaxy simulations`
  - **Question:** do cosmological simulations reproduce the observed narrow halo-mass range of high-redshift quasars?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: TNG300, TNG-Cluster, FLAMINGO L1_m8, FLAMINGO L2p8_m9, Illustris, EAGLE, TNG100, and Simba.
    - Redshift range: before cosmic noon, extending to `z~7`.
    - AGN threshold: `L_bol^AGN >= 10^42 erg/s`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure AGN bolometric luminosity as a function of host halo mass.
    - Compare median trends and scatter across simulation suites.
    - Identify halo masses occupied by luminous quasars.
    - Compare simulated host masses with observational clustering estimates.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The luminosity-halo-mass relation is nonlinear and has large scatter.
    - TNG-based runs place quasars with `L_bol^AGN~10^45-10^47 erg/s` in `10^12-10^12.5 M_sun` haloes at `z=3-6`.
    - FLAMINGO quasars extend to median halo masses near `10^12.8 M_sun` at `z~3-4`.
    - Scatter in AGN luminosity at fixed halo mass reaches up to `3 dex`, larger than halo-mass scatter at fixed luminosity.
    - The most luminous AGNs do not usually occupy the most massive haloes up to `z~7`.
  - **Position:** high-priority quasar-environment paper because it tests the halo-mass assumptions behind high-redshift AGN clustering and protocluster searches.

- [2606.14643](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14643) Hao Zhang et al. `A new CIGALE module for modeling AGN emission lines`
  - **Question:** can CIGALE fit BLR and NLR emission lines to infer AGN gas properties alongside host-galaxy SEDs?
  - **Data:**
    - Tool: new CIGALE `[nebular_AGN]` module.
    - Photoionization engine: Cloudy.
    - Benchmarks: quasar composite spectrum, empirical metallicity calibration, and X-ray-selected AGN data.
  - **Method:**
    - Generate AGN incident radiation fields from X-ray and accretion-disk continua implemented in CIGALE.
    - Build a Cloudy model database for BLR and NLR gas.
    - Benchmark line profiles, line ratios, metallicity behavior, and dust attenuation effects.
    - Run line-sensitivity tests for module parameters.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The module reproduces most quasar emission-line profiles approximately.
    - It covers key AGN line ratios seen in observed samples.
    - Some parameter combinations recover metallicities consistent with an empirical calibration.
    - Dust attenuation law choices materially affect SED fitting.
  - **Position:** useful tool paper because JWST high-redshift AGN work increasingly needs joint host plus AGN line modeling.

- [2606.13777](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13777) Jonas Biba et al. `Turbulence in the multi-phase circumgalactic medium: bridging TNG50 and idealized kpc-scale simulations`
  - **Question:** how do turbulence and cooling set the abundance and survival of cold CGM gas?
  - **Data:**
    - Cosmological simulation: TNG50 Milky Way-like galaxies at `z=0`.
    - Idealized simulations: `~1 kpc^3` AREPO turbulent boxes with metal-line cooling.
    - CGM turbulence: subsonic flows with Mach numbers around `0.1-0.7`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure CGM turbulent properties with multi-scale filtering.
    - Use TNG50-motivated turbulence properties as idealized initial and driving conditions.
    - Implement smoothly time-varying random acceleration fields in AREPO.
    - Vary cooling and mixing timescales across turbulent-box simulations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - TNG50 CGM turbulence is roughly balanced between compressive and solenoidal components, with `b~0.3-0.6`.
    - Turbulence seeds density fluctuations that trigger thermal instability.
    - Mixing between hot and cold gas depends on the cooling-to-mixing timescale ratio.
    - Cold `~10^4 K` gas abundance depends strongly on turbulence strength and cooling efficiency.
  - **Position:** lower-redshift simulation paper, but directly relevant for interpreting multiphase CGM survival and absorber structure around galaxies.

## Additional Useful Entries

- [2606.14109](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14109) L. J. Zenocratti et al. `The S-PLUS Fornax Project (S+FP): Fornax-like clusters in cosmological hydrodynamical simulations`
  - **Question:** can EAGLE and IllustrisTNG produce synthetic Fornax-like cluster catalogs comparable to S-PLUS observations?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: EAGLE and IllustrisTNG Fornax analogs.
    - Synthetic photometry: `12` S-PLUS bands generated with SKIRT.
    - Observational reference: Fornax cluster and NGC 1399.
  - **Method:**
    - Select Fornax analogs from observed cluster and central-galaxy properties.
    - Generate mock images, spectra, SEDs, magnitudes, and colors.
    - Compare color-magnitude relations and spatial substructure.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Some simulated systems reproduce broad Fornax color-magnitude and spatial-substructure trends.
    - Simulated galaxies are bluer at larger cluster-centric distance, matching observations.
    - The comparison supports current simulations for broad cluster-population features.
  - **Position:** useful local-cluster analog work, but lower priority for high-redshift-focused reading.

- [2606.13791](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13791) Zahra Sharbaf et al. `A novel data-driven approach to extract stellar population properties from galaxy spectra using absorption indices`
  - **Question:** can PCA on absorption indices recover interpretable stellar-population properties from galaxy spectra?
  - **Data:**
    - Models: composite stellar-population models for galaxy spectra.
    - Indices: six absorption indices, including Balmer features.
    - Observational comparison: SDSS and LEGA-C spectra.
  - **Method:**
    - Build a PCA latent space from model absorption-index data.
    - Use the first three eigenvectors to study age-metallicity separation.
    - Interpret the final eigenvector as a diagnostic of recent bursting behavior.
    - Compare PCA-based results with standard SPS fitting.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The first three eigenvectors can break the age-metallicity degeneracy when all six indices are used.
    - The contrast between `Hgamma_A` and `Hdelta_A` probes recent bursts on `~0.5-1 Gyr` timescales.
    - The method agrees with standard SPS model-fitting results.
  - **Position:** useful stellar-population methodology, especially for survey spectra, but not a high-priority high-redshift environment paper.
