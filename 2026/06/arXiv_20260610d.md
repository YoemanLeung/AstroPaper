## Executive View

The Wednesday listing is led by gas diagnostics around compact high-redshift sources. [2606.09970](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09970) Zhiyuan Ji uses `110` spectroscopically confirmed Little Red Dots at `z>=4` to show that Lyalpha emission is usually host-scale rather than tied to the compact red broad-line component. This is the read-first paper because it connects LRD phenomenology to neutral gas, Lyalpha escape, and CGM-scale redistribution.

Two reionization and early-structure entries are directly useful for survey strategy. [2606.10005](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10005) Abigail E. Ambrose tests SPHEREx cross-correlations among Lyalpha, Halpha, Hbeta, [OII], and [OIII] intensity maps during reionization. [2606.10648](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10648) H. T. J. Bevins models star-formation variability on `10-100 Myr` timescales for `~17000` galaxies at `z=3-8`.

The high-redshift quasar and black-hole set is unusually strong. [2606.10160](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10160) Yoshiki Matsuoka uses JWST/NIRSpec IFU data for six SHELLQs-selected `z~6` candidate obscured quasars and finds a Lyalpha-luminosity-dependent AGN fraction. [2606.11084](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11084) Minghao Yue resolves dust emission in the lensed `z=6.52` quasar J0439+1634 and finds that AGN heating dominates only inside `~100 pc`. [2606.10000](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10000) Eunwoo Chung and [2606.10036](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10036) Jonathan Kho both address how early dense environments can grow SMBH seeds.

The feedback and CGM entries are also high value. [2606.10271](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10271) Rebecca L. Davies presents resolved cold and ionized gas images of a supernova-driven wind at `z=5.3`, with gas removal at twice the star-formation rate. [2606.11089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11089) Yucheng Guo measures cool-CGM coherence lengths of `~2-7 kpc` from background-galaxy and quasar MgII absorption.

For large-scale-structure and simulation infrastructure, [2606.10020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10020) Stuart McAlpine reconstructs a `4 h^-1 Gpc`-scale BOSS/SDSS density and velocity field, and [2606.10038](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10038) Shy Genel releases second-generation CAMELS simulations with larger volumes and ionizing-background parameters relevant to IGM thermal-history inference.

## LRDs, Reionization Mapping, and Early Star Formation

- [2606.09970](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09970) Zhiyuan Ji et al. `Through the Veil: Lyalpha Illuminates the Host Galaxies of Little Red Dots`
  - **Question:** what does Lyalpha emission reveal about the gas and host galaxies around Little Red Dots?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `110` spectroscopically confirmed LRDs at `z>=4`.
    - Spectroscopy: JWST/NIRSpec PRISM coverage of Lyalpha.
    - Imaging: HST/ACS or JWST/NIRCam broadband data for continuum-subtracted Lyalpha maps.
    - Mapped subsample: `13` LRDs at `z>~5.5`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure Lyalpha detections and rest-frame equivalent widths.
    - Compare Lyalpha luminosity, Halpha, [OIII], and continuum properties.
    - Construct Lyalpha maps where imaging allows continuum subtraction.
    - Interpret Lyalpha morphology relative to rest-optical light and compact broad-line components.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Lyalpha is detected at `S/N>=3` in `32` LRDs.
    - Lyalpha luminosities and equivalent widths are consistent with normal star-forming galaxies at similar redshift.
    - Lyalpha/Halpha ratios are systematically low relative to star-forming galaxies.
    - Lyalpha luminosity follows [OIII] luminosity more closely than [OIII] equivalent width.
    - Mapped Lyalpha is extended, asymmetric, and often offset from rest-optical light.
  - **Position:** top Wednesday entry because it uses Lyalpha to separate the compact LRD component from the host-scale ISM/CGM environment.

- [2606.10005](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10005) Abigail E. Ambrose et al. `On Cross-Correlating Line Intensity Maps from SPHEREx during Reionization`
  - **Question:** which SPHEREx line-intensity cross-correlations can detect reionization-era galaxy emission despite interlopers?
  - **Data:**
    - Lines: Lyalpha, Halpha, Hbeta, [OII], and [OIII].
    - Facility context: SPHEREx reionization-era intensity mapping.
    - Redshift focus: `z>=5` cases, with explicit tests at `z=5`.
    - Foregrounds: lower-redshift interloping lines and bright interloper galaxies.
  - **Method:**
    - Simulate line-intensity maps with major emission sources.
    - Include Lyalpha radiative transfer and a dust-extinction model.
    - Cross-correlate line pairs to remove uncorrelated foreground contamination.
    - Compare signal-to-noise across line pairs, dust assumptions, and masking strategies.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Halpha cross-correlated with [OIII] can reach total `S/N` up to `99` at `z=5` in the highest no-dust case.
    - Instrument noise dominates most scales for most lines.
    - Lyalpha and [OII] are more interloper-limited on the largest scales.
    - Intensity mapping can probe galaxies below the `3 sigma` direct-detection threshold of SPHEREx.
    - Most observable signal is still dominated by large directly detectable galaxies.
  - **Position:** high-priority survey-strategy paper because it defines which line pairs can map reionization-era structure with near-term all-sky spectroscopy.

- [2606.10648](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10648) H. T. J. Bevins et al. `Star-formation variability on the star-forming main sequence during the Epoch of Reionization`
  - **Question:** what star-formation variability timescales drive scatter around the high-redshift star-forming main sequence?
  - **Data:**
    - Redshift range: `z=3-8`.
    - Galaxy sample: `~17000` galaxies from Simmonds et al. 2025.
    - Observables: intrinsic main-sequence scatter measured over `10-100 Myr` averaging timescales.
    - Models: Simple Harmonic Oscillator and Extended Regulator power spectral density forms.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit PSD models to scatter as a function of SFR averaging timescale.
    - Use nested sampling and neural-network emulators.
    - Test stellar-mass and redshift trends in inferred variability.
    - Compare regulator-like and dynamical-timescale variability components.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Preferred variability timescales are `~10-30 Myr`.
    - These timescales are consistent with galactic dynamical and stellar-feedback timescales.
    - In the SHO model, lower-mass galaxies show stronger rapid variability than higher-mass systems.
    - Evidence for a redshift transition in PSD form is weak.
    - Selection effects at high redshift limit strong interpretation.
  - **Position:** useful reionization-era population paper because burstiness affects UV luminosities, ionizing output, and interpretation of high-`z` main-sequence scatter.

## High-Redshift Quasars and Black-Hole Growth

- [2606.10160](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10160) Yoshiki Matsuoka et al. `Aether-SHELLQs: JWST integral-field spectroscopy of candidate obscured quasars at z ~ 6`
  - **Question:** what are the AGN fraction, gas structure, and kinematics of SHELLQs-selected `z~6` candidate obscured quasars?
  - **Data:**
    - Instrument: JWST/NIRSpec IFU.
    - Program: Aether GO 5645.
    - Sample: six galaxies at `z~6`.
    - Parent selection: Subaru SHELLQs candidates with luminous narrow Lyalpha.
  - **Method:**
    - Search for broad Balmer components and narrow-line diagnostics.
    - Compare Balmer profiles, forbidden-line profiles, and Lyalpha luminosities.
    - Map ionized-gas morphology and kinematics with IFU data.
    - Combine with similar SHELLQs objects from previous work.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Two of six targets show broad Balmer components with `FWHM>3000 km/s`.
    - AGN fraction is `>77%` above `L_Lyalpha=10^44 erg/s` and `<15%` below that threshold.
    - AGN hosts are compact.
    - Non-broad-line objects show extended ionized gas up to `10 kpc`.
    - Star-formation rates in the extended objects are `60-600 M_sun/yr`.
  - **Position:** high-priority high-`z` AGN paper because it links Subaru quasar selection, JWST low-luminosity AGN, and LRD-like intermediate-luminosity populations.

- [2606.11084](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11084) Minghao Yue et al. `High-Resolution ALMA Imaging for a Gravitationally-lensed Quasar at z=6.5: Constraining the AGN Contribution to Galactic-Scale Dust Heating`
  - **Question:** how much does AGN heating bias far-infrared star-formation estimates in a `z=6.52` quasar host?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: gravitationally lensed quasar J0439+1634 at `z=6.52`.
    - Instrument: ALMA.
    - Observable: rest-frame `162.7 micron` dust continuum.
    - Resolution: source-plane scale down to `~36 pc` near the SMBH.
  - **Method:**
    - Model ALMA visibilities with a pixelated lens reconstruction.
    - Reconstruct the source-plane dust continuum.
    - Fit radiative-transfer models to separate AGN-heated and star-heated dust.
    - Assess the effect on far-infrared-based SFR estimates.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Lens magnification is `4.63+-0.03`.
    - The reconstructed source has a compact bright core with size `<~200 pc`.
    - AGN-heated dust dominates at `r<~100 pc`.
    - AGN heating contributes only `~13%` of the observed sub-mm flux.
    - Far-infrared SFRs for most high-`z` quasars are likely only mildly overestimated.
  - **Position:** important quasar-host measurement because it directly tests AGN contamination in dust-based star-formation rates at reionization-era redshift.

- [2606.10000](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10000) Eunwoo Chung et al. `From Dense Gas Clouds to Supermassive Black Hole Seeds: Hybrid Hydro/Direct N-body Simulations of Runaway Collision-driven Intermediate-mass Black Hole Formation`
  - **Question:** can dense early gas clouds form IMBH seeds through runaway stellar collisions without ad hoc seeding?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: hybrid hydrodynamics plus direct `N`-body calculations.
    - Code: updated Enzo-Abyss.
    - Initial systems: dense metal-poor gas clouds with varied turbulence.
    - Products: dense clusters, very massive stars, and central IMBHs.
  - **Method:**
    - Follow cluster formation self-consistently with gas dynamics, gravity, and stellar evolution.
    - Track runaway stellar collisions and very massive star formation.
    - Collapse very massive stars into IMBHs.
    - Grow IMBHs through Eddington-limited gas accretion and TDEs.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Very massive stars form with masses `343-5108 M_sun`.
    - The result is insensitive to the tested stellar-wind feedback strengths.
    - TDEs contribute `23%` of total accretion over `~10 Myr` in the most optimistic model.
    - A seed with initial mass `6747 M_sun` can grow to `~62000 M_sun` within `100 Myr` under steady supply assumptions.
    - Dense high-redshift clusters can provide viable SMBH seeds.
  - **Position:** strong early-BH seed paper because it connects dense JWST-era stellar systems to IMBH formation and subsequent growth.

- [2606.10036](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10036) Jonathan Kho et al. `Learning the Universe at High Redshifts: Impact of Accretion Modeling on Early Black Hole Growth`
  - **Question:** how do different accretion prescriptions change early black-hole growth in simulations?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation context: Learning the Universe high-redshift galaxy simulations.
    - Targets: early SMBHs with `M_BH>=10^6 M_sun` at `z>=9`.
    - Seed regimes: heavy seeds around `10^5 M_sun` and lower-mass `10^3-10^4 M_sun` seeds.
    - Prescriptions: Bondi-Hoyle and two free-fall accretion scalings.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare accretion-rate dependence on black-hole mass.
    - Test response to AGN and stellar feedback.
    - Evaluate the role of many BH-BH mergers.
    - Identify conditions that reach JWST-inferred early SMBH masses.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Bondi accretion produces stronger runaway growth for heavy seeds.
    - Bondi growth is more vulnerable to local gas heating by feedback.
    - Free-fall models grow lower-mass seeds more effectively in principle.
    - Fiducial stellar feedback suppresses growth for low-mass seeds in all tested prescriptions.
    - `10^6-10^7 M_sun` black holes at `z>=9` require abundant heavy-seed mergers or weak-feedback Bondi growth.
  - **Position:** useful simulation-systematics paper because early SMBH conclusions depend strongly on subgrid accretion choices.

## Winds, CGM Structure, and Gas Regulation

- [2606.10271](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10271) Rebecca L. Davies et al. `Multiphase images of a powerful supernova-driven wind in the early Universe`
  - **Question:** can massive early galaxies drive winds strong enough to quench star formation?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: massive galaxy at `z=5.3`, `1.1 Gyr` after the Big Bang.
    - Observables: resolved cold gas and ionized gas.
    - Physical context: ongoing merger activity and early massive-galaxy quenching.
    - Comparison: nearby starburst-driven superwinds.
  - **Method:**
    - Image multiple gas phases of the outflow.
    - Estimate gas-removal rate relative to star-formation rate.
    - Compare wind mass and energetics to local superwinds.
    - Interpret the result in the context of massive quiescent galaxy formation.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The outflow removes gas at about twice the star-formation rate.
    - The wind could eject all cold gas within `100 Myr`.
    - Merger-driven outflows can plausibly create massive quiescent galaxies in the early Universe.
    - Wind mass and energetics are consistent with nearby starburst-driven superwinds.
    - Stellar-feedback efficiency may remain similar over the last `12 Gyr`.
  - **Position:** high-priority feedback entry because it gives direct multiphase evidence for fast gas depletion at early cosmic time.

- [2606.11089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11089) Yucheng Guo et al. `MusE GAs FLOw and Wind (MEGAFLOW) XIV: Background-Galaxy Absorption Reveals Kiloparsec-Scale Structure in the Cool Circumgalactic Medium`
  - **Question:** what is the coherence scale of cool `T~10^4 K` MgII-bearing CGM clouds?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: MEGAFLOW.
    - Absorber: stacked MgII absorption.
    - Background sources: extended galaxies with kpc-scale half-light radii and point-like quasars with sub-pc angular sizes.
    - Impact-parameter regime: trends measured beyond `100 kpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare mean and median equivalent-width profiles against different background-source sizes.
    - Use incomplete covering and beam averaging as geometric diagnostics.
    - Test trends with background-galaxy size.
    - Interpret observations with a toy model of discrete cool clouds.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Mean and median MgII profiles are broadly consistent for background galaxies.
    - Mean and median profiles differ significantly for background quasars.
    - The difference grows at impact parameters beyond `100 kpc`.
    - Large background galaxies with median half-light radius `~6.6 kpc` show more consistent profiles than small ones with `~1.5 kpc`.
    - MgII cloud coherence lengths are `~2-7 kpc`.
  - **Position:** top CGM paper because it gives a direct geometric constraint on the small-scale structure of cool circumgalactic gas.

- [2606.10116](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10116) Weichen Wang et al. `The Importance of Galaxy-Wide Star Formation in Driving Winds at z~1`
  - **Question:** are `z~1` galaxy winds driven by compact star-forming regions or by galaxy-wide star formation?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `86` star-forming galaxies at `z~1`.
    - Stellar-mass range: `10^9.0-10^11.5 M_sun`.
    - Spectroscopy: deep Keck MgII line profiles.
    - Wind detections: `50` galaxies, or `58%` of the sample.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure wind velocities from MgII profiles.
    - Compare wind detection and velocity to SFR, stellar mass, sSFR, and SFR surface density.
    - Test galaxy-wide versus compact-region SFR surface-density correlations.
    - Compare the wind-velocity relation with local starbursts and IllustrisTNG.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Wind detection declines gradually around `Sigma_SFR=0.1 M_sun/yr/kpc^2` rather than showing a sharp threshold.
    - `z~1` galaxies follow the same wind-velocity versus SFR relation as local starbursts.
    - The combined relation is `log v_wind = 0.16 log SFR + 2.4`.
    - Compact-region SFR surface density does not correlate more strongly with wind velocity than galaxy-wide SFR surface density.
    - Galaxy-wide star formation appears important for lifting entrained gas.
  - **Position:** useful wind-scaling paper because it bridges local starbursts, `z~1` galaxies, and simulation expectations.

- [2606.10557](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10557) Misaki Yamamoto et al. `Molecular Gas Structure and Star Formation Diversity in Stephan's Quintet Revealed by ACA CO(1-0) Mapping`
  - **Question:** how do molecular gas structure and turbulence regulate star formation in a compact interacting group?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: Stephan's Quintet.
    - Instrument: ALMA ACA `12CO(1-0)` mapping.
    - Map scale: `137 kpc x 119 kpc`.
    - Spatial resolution: `~5.5 kpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Map molecular gas across member galaxies and intergalactic regions.
    - Identify CO clumps along the tidal tail and shocked filament.
    - Compare Halpha-derived SFRs with CO properties.
    - Measure SFE trends with CO velocity dispersion.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Molecular gas resides in NGC 7319 and intergalactic regions.
    - Four discrete CO clumps have molecular masses of order `10^7-10^8 M_sun`.
    - SFEs span `~2.2 dex`.
    - Regions with `~50-150 km/s` velocity dispersion near the shocked filament show suppressed star formation.
    - Turbulence likely regulates star formation in interacting systems.
  - **Position:** lower-redshift but relevant environment paper because it connects group-scale interaction, molecular gas, and inefficient star formation.

## LSS, Simulation Infrastructure, and Survey Methods

- [2606.10020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10020) Stuart McAlpine et al. `The Manticore Project II: Bayesian digital twins of cosmic structure across the SDSS and BOSS volumes`
  - **Question:** can field-level inference reconstruct the three-dimensional density and velocity structure across major low-redshift galaxy surveys?
  - **Data:**
    - Volume: `(4 h^-1 Gpc)^3` to `z~0.7`.
    - Resolution: `~4 Mpc/h`.
    - Surveys: 2M++, 6dFGS, 2dFGRS, SDSS, and BOSS.
    - Framework: BORG hierarchical Bayesian inference.
  - **Method:**
    - Infer initial conditions jointly from multiple galaxy redshift surveys.
    - Evolve initial conditions forward under gravitational dynamics.
    - Validate posterior density and velocity fields with external observables.
    - Compare reconstructed structures with LCDM expectations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Posterior realizations are statistically consistent with LCDM.
    - Cross-correlation with Planck CMB lensing gives `7.4 sigma` detection significance.
    - Velocity-weighted cluster stacking gives a `3.5 sigma` kSZ detection.
    - The BOSS Great Wall appears as a `~3 sigma` overdensity.
    - The method produces a full posterior ensemble of density and velocity fields.
  - **Position:** useful LSS infrastructure because it turns survey volumes into probabilistic cosmic-web and velocity-field maps.

- [2606.10038](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10038) Shy Genel et al. `Learning the Universe with the 2nd Generation of CAMELS: Varying 35 parameters of the IllustrisTNG model in (50Mpc/h)^3 boxes`
  - **Question:** what does a larger CAMELS simulation suite add for cosmological and astrophysical parameter inference?
  - **Data:**
    - Suite: `1192` simulations.
    - Volume: `(50 Mpc/h)^3`.
    - Parameter space: `35` cosmological, astrophysical, and numerical parameters around IllustrisTNG.
    - Outputs: matter power spectra, projected maps, galaxy spatial graphs, and massive-halo thermodynamics.
  - **Method:**
    - Train neural networks and Gaussian processes on multiple summary statistics.
    - Compare inference against the previous `(25 Mpc/h)^3` CAMELS generation.
    - Test effects of larger volumes, massive halos, and lower sample variance.
    - Vary ionizing-background parameters tied to IGM thermal statistics.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Larger volumes generally tighten marginal parameter constraints.
    - Constraint improvements are weaker than simple square-root volume scaling.
    - Mode coupling and complex parameter degeneracies likely limit the gain.
    - Four new ionizing-background parameters affect IGM temperature statistics.
    - Public simulation outputs and ancillary data are released.
  - **Position:** high-value simulation resource because the larger boxes and ionizing-background variations are relevant to galaxy formation and IGM inference.

- [2606.10024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10024) Richard Stiskalek et al. `Learning the Universe with cosmological rescaling of merger trees and semi-analytic galaxy formation models`
  - **Question:** can cosmological rescaling of merger trees cheaply expand training sets for galaxy-survey inference?
  - **Data:**
    - Models: Santa Cruz semi-analytic galaxy formation model.
    - Inputs: rescaled halo merger trees.
    - Parameter space: `Omega_m` and `sigma_8`.
    - Summary statistics: stellar mass function and two-point correlation function.
  - **Method:**
    - Extend cosmological rescaling to operate directly on merger trees.
    - Apply a halo-profile correction to reduce halo-mass bias.
    - Generate many rescaled training samples from a small number of base simulations.
    - Compare inference precision with dedicated simulation suites.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Halo-mass bias is suppressed below the percent level.
    - `64` base simulations rescaled to `~1000` training samples can match the accuracy of `750` dedicated simulations.
    - Rescaling to `3200` realizations improves `Omega_m` prediction by `~25%`.
    - Rescaling one CAMELS-SAM simulation costs `~0.1 CPUh`.
    - The method offers a practical route to dense emulator training sets.
  - **Position:** useful survey-method entry because it reduces the simulation cost of galaxy-clustering and stellar-mass-function inference.

## Lower-Priority Methods, AGN Catalogs, and Near-Field Entries

- [2606.10447](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10447) Vaidehi S. Paliya identifies `18749` new NLSy1 galaxies from DESI DR1 spectra at `z<0.9`. The catalog expands low-mass, high-accretion AGN demographics for future multiwavelength work.

- [2606.10455](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10455) Alberto Dominguez maps the same DESI DR1 NLSy1 population onto the quasar main sequence. A larger fraction of DESI NLSy1s exceeds the Eddington limit than in SDSS, making the sample a local analog set for rapid early black-hole growth.

- [2606.09981](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09981) B. Luo reinterprets PHL 1811 as a heavily obscured super-Eddington quasar rather than an intrinsically X-ray-weak source. The work links X-ray weakness to clumpy dust-free accretion-disk winds.

- [2606.10027](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10027) Laura Sommovigo derives a four-parameter symbolic-regression attenuation-curve model from TNG50/TNG100 plus SKIRT. It is useful for SED-fitting systematics and forward modeling.

- [2606.10022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10022) Jan D. Burger introduces PRFM-vol as a pressure-regulated subgrid model for star formation in cosmological simulations. It connects ISM-scale regulation to galaxy morphology in multizoom runs.

- [2606.10026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10026) Sarah M. R. Jeffreson presents the companion PRFM-int/PRFM-vol subgrid formulation for Arepo. The model gives shorter depletion times than IllustrisTNG prescriptions in high-pressure regions.

- [2606.10197](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10197) Zehao Peng trains a probabilistic model to infer IFU-like spectra from broadband images and single-fiber DESI spectra. It may become a useful survey-scale tool for spatially resolved galaxy diagnostics.

- [2606.10655](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10655) Qiqi Wu builds an external morphology score for Gaia-CRF3 quasars from DESI, SDSS, SkyMapper, and PS1. Host-galaxy morphology induces astrometric systematics relevant to quasar reference frames.

- [2606.10823](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10823) Maria Paola Vaccaro models BBH formation in AGN disks across SMBH mass, Eddington ratio, and viscosity. Low-viscosity disks produce stronger hierarchical growth into high-mass remnants.

- [2606.09975](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09975) K. Overdeck reports DELVE 8/Gemini I, a compact Milky Way satellite at `120 kpc` with `M_V=-2.1` and `r_1/2=8.6 pc`. It is mainly near-field archaeology rather than core profile science.
