## Executive View

The week is dominated by Little Red Dots, early black-hole growth, high-redshift gas diagnostics, and environment-dependent galaxy evolution. The strongest through-line is that nuclear gas geometry, accretion state, dense environment, and line-ratio calibration are now inseparable in JWST-era interpretation.

The read-first weekly paper is [2606.03753](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03753) Luke Conaboy. It directly tests the connection between [OIII] emitters and Lyalpha forest transmission at `z~6`, and it gives the clearest caution for the profile: current galaxy-IGM samples are still sample-variance limited enough that broad ionizing-source models survive.

For LRDs and early black holes, the anchor set is [2606.00258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00258) Kelcey Davis, [2606.03522](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03522) Makoto Ando, and [2606.04711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04711) Chang-Hao Chen. Together they show that Balmer absorption, rest-UV compactness, Fe II/Mg II, Balmer decrements, and high-density gas geometry all point to a nuclear-structure problem rather than a simple color-selection problem.

For cosmic-noon gas and environments, [2605.30513](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30513) Natalie Lam gives a large JADES high-z MZR/FMR measurement, [2605.30410](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30410) Nikko J. Cleri calibrates ionization-parameter evolution, [2606.05284](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05284) Visal Sok ties clumps to metal-poor accretion, and [2606.05283](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05283) Monica Natalia Isla Llave ties protocluster DSFGs to enhanced AGN incidence.

For AGN feedback and SMBH-host coupling, [2606.03037](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03037) Weizhe Liu places a `z~6.2` quasar on the local `M_BH-sigma_*` relation while detecting a strong outflow. [2605.30424](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30424) F. Valentino detects [NeV] and broad Halpha in recently quenched high-z galaxies. [2606.06226](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06226) Lucas M. Seaton adds an extreme variable UV outflow at `z=2.6781`.

The survey and inference tools are also strong: [2606.00219](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00219) Daniela Breitman releases 21cmEMUv3 for EoR inference, [2606.04208](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04208) Erin Mentuch Cooper releases HETDEX PDR1 with hundreds of thousands of LAEs and [O II] emitters, [2606.04793](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04793) Weiwen Kong uses DESI stacking for multiphase CGM profiles, and [2606.01707](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01707) Erika Ogata shows that black-hole accretion can dominate small-scale Halpha and He II line-intensity-mapping signals.

## Galaxy-IGM Coupling, CGM, and Survey Infrastructure

- [2606.03753](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03753) Luke Conaboy et al. `The impact of source and survey modelling on the connection between [O III] emitters and Ly alpha forest transmission at z ~ 6`
  - **Question:** how strongly can current JWST [OIII]-emitter samples constrain the connection between galaxies and Lyalpha forest transmission at `z~6`?
  - **Data:**
    - Observations: [OIII]-emitter clustering and galaxy-Lyalpha transmission cross-correlations.
    - Models: empirical halo-to-galaxy assignment for observed [OIII] emitters.
    - Survey geometry: mock JWST catalogues with depth and geometry included.
  - **Method:**
    - Construct mock [OIII]-emitter surveys from numerical models.
    - Compare one- and two-dimensional galaxy-Lyalpha transmission cross-correlations with observations.
    - Test how source modeling and sample variance alter the apparent galaxy-IGM signal.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Mock surveys can match the observational data statistically because current survey scatter is large.
    - The modeled one-dimensional cross-correlation peak lies about `10 cMpc` below the observed peak.
    - Present samples have limited power to rule out broad ionizing-source models.
    - Larger samples and boxes above `250 cMpc` are needed for stronger inference.
  - **Position:** weekly read-first because it sits directly at the galaxy-IGM interface and quantifies how fragile current `z~6` environmental conclusions remain.

- [2606.04793](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04793) Weiwen Kong et al. `Cooler Phases of the Circumgalactic Medium Are More Centrally Concentrated: Constraints from Multiphase Absorption Lines`
  - **Question:** how do cold, cool, and warm CGM phases vary with radius around galaxies and quasars?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: DESI Year 1.
    - Tracers: Ca II, Mg II, and C IV doublets.
    - Background sources: stacked spectra behind galaxies and quasars.
  - **Method:**
    - Stack background-source spectra around foreground hosts.
    - Measure equivalent-width radial profiles for each ion.
    - Compare phase-dependent trends with simulation predictions.
    - Test host stellar mass and AGN-environment dependence.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Cooler ions show steeper radial equivalent-width profiles.
    - Emission-line galaxy halos show a strong cool-to-warm radial transition.
    - Quasar halos show a more uniform phase distribution, likely tied to AGN feedback.
    - Host stellar mass primarily sets the radial scaling, especially for the cool phase.
  - **Position:** top weekly CGM entry because it brings DESI-scale absorption statistics to multiphase halo gas.

- [2606.04208](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04208) Erin Mentuch Cooper et al. `HETDEX Public Data Release 1: Source Catalog 2 and Data Cubes from ~90 sq deg of Integral-Field Optical Spectroscopy`
  - **Question:** what public HETDEX products are available for LAE, [O II], AGN, and cosmic-noon large-scale structure work?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: HETDEX IFU spectroscopy.
    - Area: `86.67 deg^2`.
    - Observations: `431,713` IFU observations.
    - Catalogs: HPSC2 plus data cubes across major fields.
  - **Method:**
    - Reprocess four additional years of HETDEX data.
    - Apply improved quality control and machine-learning classifiers.
    - Release source catalogs, spectra, cubes, and candidate products.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - HPSC2 contains `426,654` LAEs and `491,411` [O II] emitters.
    - It also includes `18,303` AGN.
    - The appendix adds a `1.6 million`-candidate LAE sample.
    - The products directly support LAE LSS and target-selection work at `1.88<z<3.52`.
  - **Position:** major infrastructure entry for LAE mapping and cosmic-noon emission-line samples.

## Little Red Dots and Early Black-Hole Growth

- [2606.00258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00258) Kelcey Davis et al. `OCEANS of Absorption: High-resolution NIRSpec Spectroscopy Reveals Diverse Balmer-line Absorption in Little Red Dots`
  - **Question:** what does high-resolution JWST spectroscopy reveal about Balmer-line absorption and absorbing gas in LRDs?
  - **Data:**
    - Program: OCEANS follow-up spectroscopy in CEERS/EGS.
    - Instrument: JWST/NIRSpec high-resolution spectroscopy with Halpha coverage.
    - Sample: `10` LRDs at `3<z<7`.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit Halpha profiles and search for Balmer-line absorption.
    - Compare high-resolution detections with lower-resolution NIRSpec survey results.
    - Interpret absorption diversity with a partial-covering gas model.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Balmer-line absorption appears in `4/10` LRDs.
    - `7/10` LRDs are best fit by Halpha profiles with exponential wings.
    - Absorbers have median velocity offset `-49 km/s` and median equivalent width `5.3 Angstrom`.
    - The results imply a near-ubiquitous dense, excited `n=2` hydrogen reservoir around LRDs.
  - **Position:** top LRD spectroscopy entry because it constrains gas geometry rather than only broadband colors.

- [2606.03522](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03522) Makoto Ando et al. `The UV Side of Little Red Dots: Red, Compact, and Iron-Enhanced Rest-UV Emission with a Strong Downturn around Lyalpha`
  - **Question:** can ordinary host-galaxy star formation explain the rest-UV emission of LRDs?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `~100` LRDs from the JWST spectral archive.
    - Measurements: UV continuum slopes, line strengths, Fe II and Mg II features, and UV morphology.
    - Comparison set: star-forming galaxies at similar redshifts and UV magnitudes.
  - **Method:**
    - Stack spectra to measure UV continuum and line trends.
    - Compare UV sizes and slopes against normal star-forming galaxies.
    - Model whether host-galaxy emission can reproduce the red continuum.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - LRDs are redder and more compact in the UV than comparable star-forming galaxies.
    - Balmer-break strength, UV slope, Lyalpha downturn depth, and Fe II equivalent width are correlated.
    - Fe II/Mg II is about `8-10`.
    - A red compact component with roughly `beta_UV~0` is needed beyond host-galaxy light.
  - **Position:** key LRD morphology and spectroscopy paper because it links the UV side to the Balmer-break problem.

- [2606.04711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04711) Chang-Hao Chen et al. `ABCD: The Nuclear Structure of the Little Red Dots Revealted through Absorption, Break, Continuum, and Decrement`
  - **Question:** what nuclear gas geometry explains the Balmer-line, absorption, continuum, and decrement properties of LRDs?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `14` LRDs at `2.2<z<6.7`.
    - Instrument: JWST/NIRSpec MSA prism and medium-resolution grating spectra.
    - Diagnostics: Balmer emission, absorption, broad and narrow components, velocity-resolved decrements, and continuum luminosity.
  - **Method:**
    - Decompose broad, narrow, and absorption components.
    - Measure integrated and velocity-resolved Balmer decrements.
    - Fit virialized broad-line-region models.
    - Compare incident luminosity inferred from lines with UV and optical continua.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Broad-line Balmer decrements require high-density gas with `n_H>=10^9 cm^-3`.
    - Six sources show Balmer absorption with absorber covering factors above `50%`.
    - Velocity-resolved decrements are centrally peaked in the five highest-S/N sources.
    - A viewing-angle-dependent, optically thick, clumpy gaseous torus can explain the spectra and continua.
  - **Position:** strong weekly anchor because it unifies several LRD observables under one nuclear-structure model.

## High-Redshift Metallicity, Clumps, and Environments

- [2605.30513](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30513) Natalie Lam et al. `The JADES Mass-Metallicity and Fundamental Metallicity Relations at z>=2 Using New High-Redshift Metallicity Calibrations`
  - **Question:** how do the MZR and FMR evolve across `1.4<z<7.0` with high-redshift metallicity calibrations?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: JADES.
    - Sample: `601` star-forming galaxies with JWST/NIRSpec spectra.
    - Measurements: stacked spectra by stellar mass, redshift, and SFMS offset.
  - **Method:**
    - Stack spectra in stellar-mass and redshift bins.
    - Derive gas-phase metallicities with high-redshift strong-line calibrations.
    - Compare MZR and FMR trends with observations and simulations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The MZR slope evolves weakly to `z~5`, with `gamma~0.21+/-0.03`.
    - The normalization declines at about `d log(O/H)/dz~-0.1` to `z~4`.
    - At `z>~5`, low-mass galaxies continue declining in metallicity while high-mass galaxies remain closer to lower-redshift relations.
    - A shallow anti-correlation between MZR residuals and SFMS offset appears by `z~5`.
  - **Position:** strongest weekly metallicity calibration because it anchors high-z gas-phase abundance work with a large JWST sample.

- [2605.30410](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30410) Nikko J. Cleri et al. `RUBIES: The Evolution of the Ionization Parameter from 0 < z < 9`
  - **Question:** how does the ionization parameter evolve in high-redshift galaxies when rest-frame optical lines are available?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: RUBIES.
    - Instrument: JWST/NIRSpec PRISM and G395M spectroscopy.
    - Sample: `434` galaxies at `3<z<9`.
    - Comparison samples: SDSS, LEGA-C, and KBSS.
  - **Method:**
    - Infer `U=q/c` from O32 ratios.
    - Use Cloudy photoionization grids.
    - Build predictors using redshift, stellar mass, and sSFR.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - `U` increases with redshift and sSFR and decreases with stellar mass.
    - At fixed mass and sSFR, `U` rises by about a factor of `4` from `z=2` to `z=6`.
    - Photoionization degeneracies create a systematic floor of about `0.3 dex` in `log U`.
  - **Position:** essential companion to high-z metallicity papers because line diagnostics depend on evolving nebular conditions.

- [2606.05284](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05284) Visal Sok et al. `Metal-Poor Gas Accretion Drives Giant Clump Formation at 0.6 < z < 2.6`
  - **Question:** are giant clumps in cosmic-noon star-forming galaxies tied to metal-poor gas accretion?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `~300` star-forming galaxies at `0.6<z<2.6`.
    - Surveys: LEGA-C, MOSDEF, and CANUCS near-infrared spectroscopy.
    - CANUCS subset: resolved stellar properties of clumps.
    - Morphology: stellar-mass maps and Gini-M20 merger statistics.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare gas-phase metallicities of clumpy and nonclumpy galaxies.
    - Measure offsets from the mass-metallicity relation.
    - Link clump ages, masses, and SFRs to host metallicity residuals.
    - Test whether stellar-mass morphology classifies clumpy systems as mergers.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Clumpy galaxies generally fall below the MZR, while nonclumpy galaxies sit above it.
    - Clumps in hosts with `Delta Z<=0` are younger and have higher SFRs.
    - The two clump populations show no significant mass difference.
    - Most clumpy galaxies are not stellar-mass-map mergers.
  - **Position:** strong weekly environment-gas entry because it links clumps, inflow, metallicity dilution, and morphology at cosmic noon.

- [2606.05283](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05283) Monica Natalia Isla Llave et al. `Strong environmental AGN enhancement among DSFGs in z > 2 protoclusters`
  - **Question:** does the dense protocluster environment enhance SMBH growth in DSFGs beyond host-property effects?
  - **Data:**
    - Population: DSFGs in protoclusters at `z>2`.
    - Control sample: field DSFGs matched in stellar mass, SFR, and dust mass.
    - AGN tracer: X-ray AGN fraction.
  - **Method:**
    - Build a homogeneous protocluster-field DSFG comparison.
    - Match host properties between environments.
    - Compare X-ray AGN fractions across the full sample and redshift bins.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Protocluster DSFGs show an X-ray AGN fraction enhancement of `~2.7x`.
    - The enhancement remains `~2.7x` at `z=2-3` and `~2.6x` at `z=3-4.5`.
    - Matched stellar mass, SFR, and dust mass reduce host-selection explanations.
    - Dense environments likely increase SMBH fueling through gas availability and interactions.
  - **Position:** closest Friday match to the profile because it combines protoclusters, DSFGs, and AGN fueling at `z>2`.

## Quasars, AGN Feedback, and SMBH-Host Coupling

- [2606.03037](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03037) Weizhe Liu et al. `A z ~ 6.2 Quasar on the Local M_BH-sigma_* Relation Quenching Its Host Galaxy from the Aether Survey`
  - **Question:** can a luminous `z~6.2` quasar already lie on the local black-hole-host relation while quenching through feedback?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: quasar J1512+4422 at `z~6.2`.
    - Instrument: JWST/NIRSpec IFU from Aether.
    - Epoch: about `900 Myr` after the Big Bang.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure black-hole mass and stellar velocity dispersion.
    - Map nuclear outflow kinematics and extent.
    - Compare outflow rate and kinetic power with SFR and quasar luminosity.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The quasar has `M_BH~8.9 x 10^8 Msun` and `sigma_*~288 km/s`.
    - The outflow reaches `~478 km/s` and extends to `~3.2 kpc`.
    - The mass outflow rate is `92.6 Msun/yr`, far above the reported current SFR.
    - The kinetic power is about `0.6%` of quasar luminosity.
  - **Position:** top high-z quasar entry because it links SMBH-host scaling, feedback, and early quenching.

- [2605.30424](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30424) F. Valentino et al. `High-ionization coronal lines trace quasar-like activity in recently quenched galaxies at high redshift`
  - **Question:** do recently quenched high-redshift galaxies host strong nuclear activity missed by star-formation-contaminated diagnostics?
  - **Data:**
    - Instrument: JWST/NIRSpec archival spectroscopy.
    - Parent sample: `87` massive quenched galaxies.
    - Detection sample: `6` [NeV]-detected systems at `z~1.5-4.5`.
  - **Method:**
    - Search for high-ionization [NeV] lambda3427.
    - Use broad Halpha detections to estimate black-hole masses.
    - Compare [NeV] incidence with post-starburst age indicators.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Broad Halpha in `4/6` [NeV] systems implies `M_BH=10^8.5-10^9.5 Msun`.
    - [NeV] luminosities imply `L_bol=10^45-10^46 erg/s`.
    - Eddington ratios are about `10-50%`.
    - Strong [NeV] emitters prefer the youngest post-starburst systems.
  - **Position:** important AGN-quenching entry because it ties recent quenching to still-active SMBH growth.

- [2606.06226](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06226) Lucas M. Seaton et al. `A New Member of the Fast and Furious Family: A Relativistic and Time-Variable UV Outflow in a Luminous Quasar`
  - **Question:** how extreme can UV-detected quasar outflows become, and how do they vary with time?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: SDSS J231854.31+243954.2.
    - Redshift: `z=2.6781+-0.0004` from Gemini/GNIRS Halpha.
    - Diagnostics: variable C IV and Si IV absorption.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure outflow velocities from variable UV absorption.
    - Use near-infrared spectroscopy to establish systemic redshift.
    - Place the source within the fast relativistic quasar-outflow class.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Absorption velocities span `-77,000 km/s` to at least `-90,000 km/s`.
    - The quasar is radio quiet and weak-lined in the rest-frame UV.
    - The source is an extreme time-variable UV outflow case.
  - **Position:** useful feedback and wind-launching entry for the cosmic-noon quasar context.

## Reionization, Cosmic Dawn, and Inference Tools

- [2606.00219](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00219) Daniela Breitman et al. `21cmEMUv3: a hybrid diffusion-LSTM emulator of 21cmFAST summary observables`
  - **Question:** can a hybrid emulator provide fast, accurate EoR and cosmic-dawn observables for inference pipelines?
  - **Data:**
    - Training basis: 21cmFASTv3 simulations.
    - Redshift range: `z~6-30`.
    - Outputs: 21 cm power spectra, neutral fraction, spin temperature, global signal, UV luminosity functions, and Thomson optical depth.
  - **Method:**
    - Condition the emulator on `sigma_8` and ten astrophysical parameters.
    - Use score-based diffusion for the cylindrical 21 cm power spectrum.
    - Use LSTM networks for the remaining summary observables.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The emulator reaches sub-percent median accuracy across summary outputs.
    - Current HERA limits imply soft-band X-ray luminosity per SFR is not below `10^39.2 erg s^-1 Msun^-1 yr` at `95%` confidence.
    - The framework supports SKA 21 cm forecasts under different array configurations.
  - **Position:** high-value methods paper because it links first-galaxy physics, IGM state, and future 21 cm constraints.

- [2606.04827](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04827) Zihan Wang and Huanyuan Shan `Steep Redshift Evolution of the Ionizing Escape Fraction at z = 5--12: Empirical Constraints and Comparison with Simulations`
  - **Question:** how does the ionizing escape fraction vary with halo mass and redshift across reionization?
  - **Data:**
    - Observables: HST and JWST UV luminosity functions at `z=5-12`.
    - Integral constraints: Planck optical depth and seven neutral-fraction measurements.
    - Comparison: THESAN simulation outputs.
  - **Method:**
    - Model `f_esc` as a halo-mass and redshift dependent function.
    - Use abundance matching to connect UV magnitude and halo mass.
    - Solve reionization histories and constrain parameters with grid and MCMC scans.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Profile likelihood gives `f_0=0.061`, `alpha_M=0.18`, and `alpha_z=1.98`.
    - Population-averaged `f_esc` rises from `~2%` at `z=5` to `~9%` at `z=12`.
    - Sub-threshold halos contribute more than `80%` of the ionizing budget at `z>=10`.
    - The steep-evolution model predicts `tau_e=0.047`.
  - **Position:** useful empirical input paper for reionization simulations and source-budget work.
