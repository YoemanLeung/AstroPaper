## High-Redshift Structure, Compact Sources, and AGN Abundances

- [2606.25022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25022) Xiaohan Wang et al. `A massive barred spiral galaxy at z = 5.102 discovered by JWST`
  - **Question:** can a massive barred spiral disk already exist at `z>5`?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: M1149-BSG-z5 in the parallel field of MACS J1149+2223.
    - Instruments: JWST and HST imaging.
    - Environment: a nearby companion galaxy and an overdense region.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify disk, bar, spiral-arm, and bulge components in the resolved imaging.
    - Fit isophotal ellipses and structural models.
    - Interpret emission-line and broad-line properties in the context of chemical enrichment and AGN activity.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - M1149-BSG-z5 is a barred spiral at `z=5.102`.
    - The bar has length `a_bar~4.5 kpc`, with spiral arms peaking at `r~5.5 kpc`.
    - The stellar mass is `10^10.45 M_sun`, the star-formation rate is `144 M_sun/yr`, and `R_e=2.61 kpc`.
    - The galaxy hosts a broad-line AGN with `M_BH/M_*~10^-3`.
    - The overdense environment and companion favor an interaction-assisted route to early bar formation.
  - **Position:** read first because it joins early disk morphology, AGN growth, chemical maturity, and environment at `z>5`.

- [2606.26098](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26098) Quinn O. Casey et al. `A Population of Little Red Dot-like Quasars in SDSS`
  - **Question:** can lower-redshift quasars reproduce the photometric and spectral behavior of JWST Little Red Dots?
  - **Data:**
    - Parent sample: SDSS quasars selected through `ugriz` filters at `z~0.4` and `z~0.8`.
    - Analog sample: `~1300` Local Red Dots with LRD-like colors.
    - Subsample: `N=244` objects with V-shaped continua.
  - **Method:**
    - Match SDSS color selections to the JWST filters used for `z~5` LRDs.
    - Stack spectra and SEDs for Local Red Dots.
    - Compare quasar, host-galaxy, reddening, and cool-blackbody components.
    - Test whether X-ray non-detections would persist under high-redshift LRD upper limits.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The V-shaped-continuum subset shows higher-order Balmer absorption and [NeV]3426 emission.
    - Those features would likely be missed in low-resolution JWST/PRISM spectra.
    - Local Red Dot SEDs can be modeled with a reddened AGN plus host galaxy, with or without a cool blackbody.
    - The X-ray detection rate is comparable to typical quasars.
    - Typical quasars and LoRDs would have greater than `50%` probability of non-detection under current LRD X-ray limits.
  - **Position:** important LRD interpretation paper because it tests whether low-resolution high-z data can erase ordinary AGN signatures.

- [2606.25890](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25890) O. L. Dors et al. `Cosmic evolution of the helium and oxygen abundances in type 2 Active Galactic Nuclei: Helium-loud AGNs`
  - **Question:** how do helium and oxygen abundances in type 2 AGN narrow-line regions evolve from the local universe to `z~7`?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `84` type 2 AGN, including `65` at `z<0.2` and `19` at `2.8<z<6.8`.
    - Spectroscopy: rest-frame optical coverage from JADES and the literature.
    - Diagnostics: `He/H` and `O/H` from the `T_e` method and strong-line calibrations.
  - **Method:**
    - Estimate NLR abundance ratios with direct and calibrated line methods.
    - Compare ionization degree and abundance behavior across redshift.
    - Test sensitivity to electron-density assumptions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The AGN ionization degree increases toward higher redshift.
    - Most AGN at `z>2.8` show oversolar helium and subsolar oxygen abundances.
    - The object goods-s-mediumhst-58850 at `z=6.2615` has `12+log(He/H)=11.64`, or `He/He_sun~4.4`.
    - The extreme helium abundance remains robust for `N_e=500-10000 cm^-3`.
    - He/H declines toward lower redshift at about `0.04 dex` per redshift unit, while O/H increases by about `0.06 dex` per redshift unit.
  - **Position:** useful high-z AGN diagnostic because chemical abundance anomalies can bias NLR interpretation in JWST-era spectra.

## Ly-alpha Emitters, Halos, and Ionized Gas

- [2606.25723](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25723) Manish Kataria et al. `Ly-alpha halos and UV continuum morphologies of Tadpole Galaxies at z>3`
  - **Question:** do disturbed high-redshift tadpole morphologies affect Ly-alpha halo extent and photon transport?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `12` tadpole galaxies at `z~3-5.5` in the Hubble Ultra Deep Field.
    - Observations: deep MUSE Ly-alpha data and UV continuum morphologies.
    - Measurements: Ly-alpha surface-brightness profiles, effective radii, spatial offsets, and line profiles.
  - **Method:**
    - Build morphology-aware Ly-alpha and UV continuum profiles.
    - Measure Ly-alpha halo sizes and offsets relative to the stellar component.
    - Inspect line-profile structure for double peaks and asymmetries.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Extended Ly-alpha halos are detected in `10/12` galaxies.
    - About `40%` of the sample shows double-peaked Ly-alpha profiles.
    - Ly-alpha effective radii broadly follow UV continuum extent, but the halos are more symmetric and often offset.
    - Several systems show asymmetric or outflow-like Ly-alpha structures.
    - Disturbed morphology likely affects anisotropic Ly-alpha escape and CGM radiative transfer.
  - **Position:** high-priority Ly-alpha morphology entry because it links resolved structure to halo-scale emission at `z>3`.

- [2606.25126](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25126) I. Laferte-Urrutia et al. `The new era of Lyman alpha emitters (LAEs): Typical star formation histories of LAEs in the ILLUSTRIS simulation`
  - **Question:** are LAEs in simulations dominated by one typical star-formation history or by multiple classes?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: IllustrisTNG100.
    - Sample: `6051` LAEs at `z=2.0`.
    - Classifier: KMeans grouping of star-formation histories.
  - **Method:**
    - Apply a recent LAE selection criterion to simulated galaxies.
    - Cluster their star-formation histories.
    - Compare mass, Ly-alpha luminosity, and total star-formation rate among classes.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The four classes contain `35%`, `33%`, `21%`, and `11%` of the LAE sample.
    - The largest class has peak star formation at the time of observation.
    - Other classes have bursts `0.3`, `0.7`, and `1.3 Gyr` before observation.
    - The classical low-mass, low-dust, single-burst LAE picture represents only `35%` of the sample.
  - **Position:** useful LAE interpretation paper because it warns against treating LAE selection as a single SFH population.

- [2606.25228](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25228) P. Layana-Astudillo et al. `The new era of Lyman alpha emitters (LAEs): Efficiency in the selection criteria`
  - **Question:** how deep must broadband imaging be relative to narrowband imaging to efficiently recover LAEs?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: ODIN observations in the SHELA field.
    - Imaging: DECam N501 and N419 narrowbands plus `g` and `r` broadbands.
    - Area: about `15 deg^2` in the SHELA field, within ODIN's nearly `100 deg^2` program.
  - **Method:**
    - Apply a dual-continuum LAE selection criterion.
    - Vary the broadband depth relative to the narrowband depth.
    - Measure the resulting LAE recovery fraction.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Narrowband depths reach about `25.3 AB mag`, while broadbands reach about `26.3 AB mag`.
    - Broadband data about `1 mag` deeper than narrowband data recover nearly `80%` of LAEs.
    - Equal-depth broadband and narrowband data recover only about `20%`.
    - Deeper broadband exposures beyond the `1 mag` offset give progressively smaller recovery gains.
  - **Position:** practical survey-design result for LAE samples selected from wide-field narrowband imaging.

- [2606.25214](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25214) Lluis Mas-Ribas et al. `Fast Radio Bursts probe Galaxy Evolution: Evidence and implications of a redshift-dependent FRB host DM`
  - **Question:** can localized FRBs measure redshift evolution in ionized gas across galaxies and halos?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `90` localized FRBs, including `69` with confirmed host redshifts.
    - Surveys: DSA and ASKAP/CRAFT ICS.
    - Observable: host dispersion measure evolution, `DM_host(z) proportional to (1+z)^n_z`.
  - **Method:**
    - Forward model localized FRB samples with instrumental effects.
    - Infer `n_z` and its degeneracies with mean host DM and cosmological parameters.
    - Forecast constraints from future MeerTRAP, DSA, and CRAFT host samples.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The inferred evolution is `n_z=1.62(+1.48/-1.57)`.
    - Both datasets independently favor `n_z>0`, and the non-evolving case is disfavored at `1 sigma`.
    - Ignoring host evolution can overestimate DM-based redshifts by up to `Delta z~0.3` for `DM_EG~1000-2000 pc cm^-3`.
    - Future samples of about `100` to `350` localized hosts can reduce `n_z` uncertainties to about `0.7`.
  - **Position:** relevant CGM/IGM tracer paper because FRBs probe all ionized phases along host sightlines rather than one emission or absorption phase.

## HI, Cosmic Web, Metallicity, and Survey Infrastructure

- [2606.25523](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25523) Hengxing Pan et al. `Studying HI and the Cosmic Web in the Era of SKA`
  - **Question:** how can SKA connect neutral-gas fueling in galaxy disks to cosmic-web-scale gas accretion?
  - **Data:**
    - Facility: SKA HI surveys.
    - Science target: neutral atomic hydrogen from resolved galactic disks to large-volume cosmic-web environments.
    - Context: recent observational and theoretical progress on gas accretion.
  - **Method:**
    - Review the role of HI in galaxy evolution and disk fueling.
    - Identify SKA capabilities needed for high column-density sensitivity and volume.
    - Connect radio HI work to multiwavelength facilities.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - SKA can resolve galactic disks while surveying volumes large enough to probe cosmic-web impact.
    - HI accretion onto disks remains poorly understood without linking disk, halo, and web scales.
    - Coordinated radio and multiwavelength data are required to make the cosmic-web gas connection physical.
  - **Position:** most profile-aligned SKAO entry because it directly connects galaxy gas supply to large-scale structure.

- [2606.25995](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25995) Piyush Sharda et al. `The evolution of the galaxy gas-phase mass-metallicity relation from z=15 to z=0 in the COLIBRE cosmological simulations`
  - **Question:** when does the gas-phase mass-metallicity relation appear, and what physical processes shape it?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: COLIBRE cosmological hydrodynamical runs.
    - Redshift range: `z=15` to `z=0`.
    - Resolution and volume: particle masses `10^5-10^7 M_sun` and box sizes `25-400 cMpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Track star-forming galaxy gas-phase oxygen abundances.
    - Compare MZR evolution with observational compilations and other simulations.
    - Test feedback, star-formation efficiency, and dust depletion effects.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - COLIBRE reproduces the observed MZR across cosmic time within systematic abundance uncertainties.
    - The MZR is already in place at `z~10` and shows little evolution until `z~5`.
    - The low-redshift MZR slope becomes shallower.
    - AGN feedback largely controls the high-mass turnover.
    - Core-collapse supernova feedback strongly affects the low-mass end.
  - **Position:** useful simulation benchmark because it ties high-redshift abundance trends to feedback physics across the full observable mass range.

- [2606.25689](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25689) Hiddo S. B. Algera et al. `Tracing the Star Formation History of the Universe through Thermal Free-Free Emission with the SKA`
  - **Question:** can SKA thermal free-free measurements recover dust-unbiased star-formation histories to the early universe?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: T-RECS faint radio sky.
    - Survey design: matched-depth SKA-Mid Bands 1 through 5b over `0.25 deg^2`.
    - Frequency range: `0.35-15.4 GHz`.
  - **Method:**
    - Forecast detections in an ambitious AA4 SKA-Mid survey.
    - Fit radio SEDs to separate thermal free-free and synchrotron components.
    - Combine thermal fractions with broader low-frequency luminosity functions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The survey would detect about `1.5e4` star-forming galaxies in all bands to `z~7`.
    - Thermal fractions and synchrotron spectral indices can be recovered without bias in the forecast.
    - Thermal SFR uncertainties are `less than or about 0.1 dex` for galaxies near the radio luminosity-function knee.
    - The method can constrain cosmic star-formation-rate density to `z~7`.
  - **Position:** relevant survey-planning entry because free-free emission is a dust-insensitive SFR tracer for high-redshift galaxy evolution.

- [2606.25096](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25096) Shane P. O'Sullivan et al. `An SKA-Low RM Grid for constraining the origin of cosmic magnetism`
  - **Question:** how dense can an SKA-Low rotation-measure grid become, and what cosmic-magnetism problems would it address?
  - **Data:**
    - Facility: SKA-Low wide-area survey concepts.
    - Forecast survey: `10000 deg^2` in about `3200 hr`.
    - Observable: polarized source counts and Faraday rotation measures.
  - **Method:**
    - Model meter-wavelength polarized source counts.
    - Forecast RM density as a function of polarized-intensity threshold.
    - Compare expected data products with cosmological MHD simulations and LOFAR RM grids.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The predicted RM grid density is `N(>P)~5(P/100 microJy)^-0.75 deg^-2`.
    - A wide AA4 survey would produce more than `50000` RMs.
    - Expected RM precision is about `0.05 rad m^-2`.
    - Combining wide and all-sky data could yield up to `100000` meter-wavelength RMs.
  - **Position:** useful for CGM and LSS readers because RM grids are a route to magnetized plasma in halos and the cosmic web.

## AGN Feedback, Lensed Quasars, and Cluster-Scale Context

- [2606.25856](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25856) B. Sebastian et al. `JWST resolves jet-driven H2 and ionized outflows in radio galaxy 3C305`
  - **Question:** how efficiently does a compact radio jet shock and accelerate multiphase gas in its host galaxy?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: radio galaxy 3C 305.
    - Instruments: JWST MIRI MRS, NIRSpec, NIRCam, and MIRI imaging.
    - Tracers: H2 rotational lines, mid-IR fine-structure lines, and PAH emission.
  - **Method:**
    - Map H2 and ionized-gas kinematics across jet termination regions.
    - Fit multiple Gaussian components to warm H2 emission.
    - Model H2 excitation with a power-law temperature distribution.
    - Interpret mid-IR line ratios with MAPPINGS shock-plus-precursor models.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - H2/PAH and [Fe II] tracers show shocks at the jet termination locations.
    - Ionized gas reaches higher outflow velocities than H2 gas.
    - Velocity increases sharply at the jet hotspots.
    - Hotspots have flatter H2 excitation slopes, implying a larger warm/hot gas mass fraction.
    - Radiative losses and moderate outflow kinetic power can account for the estimated jet power, indicating high jet coupling efficiency.
  - **Position:** strong feedback paper because JWST resolves how a compact jet drives kiloparsec-scale multiphase outflows.

- [2606.25164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25164) Luke R. Holden et al. `Dust destruction signals shock-accelerated outflows in the nearby active galaxy NGC 1068`
  - **Question:** are AGN outflows in NGC 1068 accelerated by fast shocks that compress gas and destroy dust?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: nearby active galaxy NGC 1068.
    - Observations: deep spectroscopy of coronal and lower-ionization forbidden lines.
    - Diagnostics: [NeV]3425, [FeVII]6087, and refractory versus non-refractory line ratios.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare refractory-element depletion in disk gas and outflowing gas.
    - Use forbidden-line ratios to test dust destruction.
    - Infer outflowing and non-outflowing gas density contrasts.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Non-outflowing disk gas shows strong depletion of refractory elements onto dust.
    - Outflowing gas just above the disk is largely dust-free.
    - Outflowing gas density is `19-110` times higher than non-outflowing gas density.
    - Fast shocks can explain both compression and dust destruction.
  - **Position:** important local anchor for feedback physics because coronal lines trace the destructive impact of AGN-driven shocks directly.

- [2606.25028](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25028) Kriti Kamal Gupta et al. `Constraining the Physical Properties of Quadruply Lensed Quasars using Optical-to-X-Ray Data`
  - **Question:** how accurately can optical-to-X-ray data constrain physical properties of quadruply lensed AGN?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `27` quadruply lensed AGN at `0.6<z<3.1`.
    - Coverage: optical/UV photometry, spectroscopy, and X-ray data.
    - Measurements: bolometric luminosities, virial black-hole masses, and Eddington ratios.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit broadband SEDs with phenomenological models.
    - Fit spectral emission lines for virial black-hole masses.
    - Compare X-ray and optical bolometric-correction prescriptions.
    - Use the UV-X-ray luminosity relation as a lensing-magnification diagnostic.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Luminosity-dependent `2-10 keV` X-ray bolometric corrections give unbiased bolometric-luminosity predictions.
    - The maximum scatter is about `0.5 dex`.
    - Optical bolometric corrections are generally overestimated, though microlensing treatment reduces scatter.
    - UV-X-ray luminosity relations can help quantify micro- and millilensing magnification uncertainties.
  - **Position:** useful quasar reference because lensed AGN samples probe inner structure while demanding careful magnification and bolometric calibration.

- [2606.25367](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25367) Shuanghao Shu et al. `A Statistical Study of HI Gas in AGN-Hosting and Satellite Galaxies from ALFALFA and FASHI`
  - **Question:** is cold-gas depletion in AGN-hosting satellites driven mainly by internal feedback or environment?
  - **Data:**
    - Surveys: ALFALFA and FASHI HI samples.
    - Optical spectroscopy: SDSS DR7/DR8 and DESI.
    - Environments: group-centric positions of AGN-hosting and non-AGN satellite galaxies.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare HI gas fractions and star-formation rates for AGN hosts and star-forming counterparts.
    - Measure radial trends relative to group virial radius.
    - Check beam-confusion artifacts at small group-centric radius.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - AGN-hosting galaxies have SFRs and HI gas fractions about `1 dex` below star-forming counterparts.
    - AGN-hosting satellites remain gas- and SFR-deficient even near `R/R180~1`.
    - Flat gas-fraction and sSFR radial profiles favor internal feedback over environment as the primary driver.
    - The apparent HI increase at `R/R180<0.3` is a beam-confusion artifact.
  - **Position:** practical baryon-cycle paper because it separates AGN-host gas depletion from satellite environmental processing.

- [2606.25727](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25727) Ramananda Santra et al. `Probing High-redshift Intracluster Medium Using SKA`
  - **Question:** can SKA test rapid magnetic-field amplification in high-redshift intracluster plasma?
  - **Data:**
    - Target system: El Gordo, ACT-CL J0102-4915, at `z~0.87`.
    - Facility concept: SKA-Low and SKA-Mid staged deployments.
    - Science target: diffuse radio halos, relics, cosmic rays, and ICM magnetic fields.
  - **Method:**
    - Use broadband continuum and polarization measurements to probe ICM magnetic-field structure.
    - Treat El Gordo as an extreme merging cluster science-verification target.
    - Forecast staged SKA physical resolution of about `20-30 kpc`.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - High-redshift clusters already show diffuse radio powers comparable to nearby systems.
    - Such detections imply efficient magnetic amplification despite inverse Compton losses.
    - El Gordo can test magnetic-field and cosmic-ray models under extreme merger conditions.
    - Future SKA samples can make the high-redshift ICM test statistical rather than anecdotal.
  - **Position:** relevant cluster-scale entry because it links radio plasma physics to structure formation at high redshift.
