## Early Galaxies, Ly-alpha, and Reionization

- [2606.27427](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27427) Lewis R. Prole et al. `SEEDZ: Rapid Galaxy Assembly as a Pathway to Supermassive Stars, Dense Stellar Environments and Massive Black Hole Seeds`
  - **Question:** what early galaxy assembly histories produce the inflow rates needed for supermassive stars and heavy black-hole seeds?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: SEEDZ hydrodynamic simulations of early galaxy assembly.
    - Formation criterion: `>1 M_sun/yr` flowing into `10 pc` regions for heavy seeds.
    - Epoch: heavy-seed formation tracked to `z=10`.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare halos that meet the heavy-seed criterion with halos that never meet it.
    - Track gas flow from virial scales of a few hundred parsecs into the inner parsec.
    - Apply a supermassive-star condition using metallicity, accretion rate, and lifetime thresholds.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Heavy seeds form in halos that grow rapidly relative to non-seed halos.
    - Halos with virial-scale growth rates `gtrsim 1 M_sun/yr` sustain `0.1 M_sun/yr` into the inner `1 pc`.
    - Heavy seeds typically form about `100 Myr` after the first stars and stellar-mass black holes.
    - By `z=10`, most heavy seeds form in near-solar-metallicity regions, with a low-metallicity minority at `10^-2 Z_sun`.
    - The inferred supermassive-star number density is `0.1 cMpc^-3`; a visible fraction of only `10^-4` could match the JWST Little Red Dot population.
  - **Position:** read first because it ties high-redshift galaxy assembly, dense stellar systems, and black-hole seed demographics into one simulation framework.

- [2606.27426](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27426) Floor van Donkelaar et al. `Too shy to spin? Cosmic wallflowers as proto-globular clusters`
  - **Question:** can isolated circumgalactic star-forming clusters at high redshift evolve into globular-cluster-like systems?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: MassiveBlackPS high-resolution simulation.
    - Epoch: star-forming clusters at `z~7.6`.
    - Formation channels: disc clusters from gravitational instability and isolated circumgalactic "cosmic wallflowers" born from cosmic filaments.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure rotational velocity `v_rot` and rotational support `v/sigma`.
    - Compare cluster kinematics across the two formation channels.
    - Combine kinematics with stellar surface densities and gas content.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Cosmic wallflowers have systematically lower rotational velocities than disc clusters.
    - Low-`v_rot` wallflowers overlap the observed Milky Way globular-cluster population in surface-density space.
    - Gas-rich wallflowers preferentially occupy the weakly rotating regime.
    - Denser, more rotationally supported wallflowers may follow a separate path linked to massive black-hole seed formation.
  - **Position:** useful high-redshift structure paper because it links filament-fed circumgalactic environments to possible proto-globular-cluster initial conditions.

- [2606.27423](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27423) Pengfei Li et al. `Analytical and fitting formulae for solutions to Lyman-alpha radiative transfer equations: the effects of geometry, recoil, and velocity gradients`
  - **Question:** how can analytic Ly-alpha radiative-transfer solutions be extended across geometry, recoil, and bulk velocity gradients?
  - **Data:**
    - Geometries: slab, sphere, and cylindrical symmetry for static, uniform gas clouds.
    - Physics: atomic recoil and constant gas velocity gradients.
    - Validation: Ly-alpha Monte Carlo radiative-transfer simulations.
  - **Method:**
    - Solve Ly-alpha radiative-transfer equations for cylindrical geometry.
    - Empirically modify recoil-free spectra to include recoil effects.
    - Derive series solutions for small velocity gradients and fitting formulae for large gradients.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The cylindrical solution completes a simple-geometry set alongside slab and spherical cases.
    - Series solutions agree with simulations for small velocity gradients where edge velocity is comparable to the thermal velocity `b`.
    - Extended fitting formulae improve large-gradient spectra at `v_E/b~100` under large optical depths.
    - The formulae provide compact Ly-alpha inputs for theoretical studies that cannot run full radiative-transfer simulations.
  - **Position:** important methods entry for Ly-alpha emitter and Ly-alpha forest interpretation because line shapes encode geometry and gas kinematics.

- [2606.27858](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27858) Anshuman Acharya et al. `Inferring Cosmology and Astrophysics from the High-redshift 21cm Signal with SKA-Low`
  - **Question:** which statistics and inference tools can SKA-Low use to recover Cosmic Dawn and reionization physics from the 21 cm signal?
  - **Data:**
    - Facility: SKA-Low, including the initial AA* configuration.
    - Observable: redshifted 21 cm signal during Cosmic Dawn and the Epoch of Reionization.
    - Statistics: power spectrum, higher-order statistics, distribution moments, morphology, and field-level summaries.
  - **Method:**
    - Review analytical, semi-numerical, numerical, and emulator-based signal models.
    - Compare Bayesian, simulation-based, and field-level inference approaches.
    - Assess complementary statistics for breaking degeneracies among galaxy and IGM parameters.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The non-Gaussian 21 cm signal carries information beyond the power spectrum.
    - Combining morphology, higher-order statistics, and field-level tools is needed to break parameter degeneracies.
    - The initial SKA-Low configuration can already target early constraints on galaxy and IGM properties.
  - **Position:** high-priority reionization infrastructure paper because it specifies how SKA-Low measurements become astrophysical parameters.

- [2606.27903](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27903) Zihan Wang et al. `A hidden reionization prior biases cosmological inference`
  - **Question:** does assuming a single smooth reionization history bias cosmological inference?
  - **Data:**
    - Constraints: Planck optical depth, SPT and ACT patchy kSZ limits, and the Ly-alpha forest endpoint.
    - Reconstruction inputs: non-parametric reionization histories and Planck EE polarization.
    - Cosmological targets: summed neutrino mass and `sigma_8`.
  - **Method:**
    - Test whether monotonic ionization histories reproduce the combined observational constraints.
    - Allow an additional early ionization component at `z gtrsim 12`.
    - Propagate the reionization-shape freedom through the `A_s-tau_e` degeneracy.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - A single smooth reionization transition cannot simultaneously match the listed constraints.
    - An additional early ionization phase relaxes the summed-neutrino-mass upper bound to `sum m_nu < 0.39 eV` at `95%` confidence.
    - The same freedom shifts `sigma_8` toward weak-lensing-preferred values.
    - Reionization shape acts as an implicit prior in precision cosmology.
  - **Position:** relevant because high-redshift ionization history is not only astrophysics; it also controls cosmological parameter inference.

## JWST Galaxy Dynamics, Compact Starbursts, and Nebular Diagnostics

- [2606.27853](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27853) Juan M. Espejo Salcedo et al. `MSA-3D: Rotation Curves and Dark Matter Fractions at z~0.5-1.7 with JWST/NIRSpec`
  - **Question:** what range of inner dark-matter fractions and rotation-curve shapes do star-forming galaxies show at `0.5<z<1.7`?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: MSA-3D Cycle 1.
    - Instruments: JWST/NIRSpec ionized-gas kinematics and JWST/NIRCam imaging.
    - Sample: `30` star-forming galaxies, with `23` in the primary statistical sample.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine spatially resolved kinematics with imaging.
    - Forward-model baryonic and dark-matter contributions.
    - Cross-check with stellar mass maps and SFR-derived gas profiles.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Most primary-sample galaxies are rotationally supported disks with `sigma_0~31-65 km/s`.
    - Inner dark-matter fractions span `f_DM(R_e)~0.1-0.9`, with median `0.63`.
    - Among `19` galaxies reaching at least `2R_e`, the rotation curves split into six rising, six flat, and seven falling cases.
    - Falling curves tend toward lower `V_rot/sigma_0~2` and `f_DM lesssim 0.55`, while dark-matter-rich disks have `V_rot/sigma_0~4` and `f_DM gtrsim 0.7`.
    - The sample extends high-resolution dynamics down to stellar masses of `~10^9 M_sun`.
  - **Position:** important JWST kinematics result because it measures the dynamical diversity of cosmic-noon disks below the mass range of many earlier surveys.

- [2606.27468](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27468) William Matzko et al. `JWST Reveals Compact Nuclear Starbursts Masquerading as AGNs in Metal-Poor Dwarfs: Where Are the Accreting Intermediate-Mass Black Holes?`
  - **Question:** can compact metal-poor nuclear starbursts mimic AGN diagnostics without accreting black holes?
  - **Data:**
    - Instrument: JWST/NIRSpec spectroscopy.
    - Target: SDSS J160135.95+311353.7, a low-mass, metal-poor galaxy selected by extreme mid-infrared colors.
    - Comparison: SDSS J120122.30+021108.3, a lower-mass and more metal-poor system from prior work.
  - **Method:**
    - Search for coronal lines, X-ray emission, and variability associated with accretion.
    - Compare stellar populations, dust properties, Wolf-Rayet features, PAH structure, and CO bandhead absorption.
    - Use photoionization models to test whether low metallicity alone explains weak high-ionization emission.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - J1601 shows no evidence for coronal lines, X-ray emission, or variability from accretion.
    - J1601 contains a developed compact nuclear starburst with red-supergiant CO bandhead absorption, weak Wolf-Rayet features, and a circumnuclear PAH ring.
    - The red mid-infrared colors in both systems originate from compact unresolved nuclear emission.
    - Low metallicity alone cannot explain the lack of high-ionization emission.
    - Common AGN color diagnostics may not uniquely identify accreting black holes in metal-poor dwarf galaxies.
  - **Position:** useful for JWST compact-source interpretation because starbursts can masquerade as accretion in the same diagnostic space used for IMBH and LRD searches.

- [2606.28129](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28129) Yu Rong et al. `A DESI Calibration of the [O II]--[S II] Electron-density Offset in Integrated Star-forming Galaxies`
  - **Question:** can [O II] and [S II] doublet densities be used interchangeably in integrated star-forming galaxy spectra?
  - **Data:**
    - Catalog: DESI DR1 Emission Line Catalog.
    - Diagnostics: [O II] `lambda lambda 3726,3729` and [S II] `lambda lambda 6716,6731`.
    - Galaxy set: star-forming galaxies with fiducial emission lines.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure electron densities from both low-ionization doublets.
    - Fit a binned median calibration between [O II]- and [S II]-based densities.
    - Test trends with stellar mass, H-alpha SFR, attenuation, N2, O32, and specific SFR.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - [O II] gives systematically higher densities than [S II], with median offset `0.228 dex`.
    - The calibration is `log n_e(OII)=0.752 log n_e(SII)+0.832` within the reported uncertainties.
    - The offset is larger at higher stellar mass, H-alpha SFR, attenuation, and N2.
    - The offset is smaller at higher O32 and shows no significant trend with specific SFR.
    - [O II] and [S II] likely weight different low-ionization gas phases in integrated spectra.
  - **Position:** practical spectroscopy result because density evolution studies often mix diagnostics across redshift when only one doublet is observable.

- [2606.27479](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27479) N. Deg et al. `The Bright Future of the Dark and Dim Universe`
  - **Question:** how can SKA-Mid test the low-mass frontier of galaxy formation with starless HI clouds and faint gas-rich galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Populations: Reionization-Limited HI Clouds and faint gas-rich galaxies.
    - Facility: SKA-Mid Array Assembly 4.
    - Observables: HI mass function, HI velocity function, and low-mass baryonic Tully-Fisher relation.
  - **Method:**
    - Frame RELHICs as dark-matter tests on sub-galactic scales.
    - Separate RELHIC candidates from tidal or pressure-confined contaminants.
    - Forecast low-mass galaxy observables reachable with deep and wide radio data.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - RELHICs provide direct tests of Lambda-CDM predictions for low-mass halos.
    - SKA-Mid AA4 can resolve nearby RELHIC gas structure.
    - The same datasets can constrain the HIMF, HIVF, and bTFR down to `10^6 M_sun`.
  - **Position:** lower-redshift but useful because the faint end and reionization-limited gas reservoirs set boundary conditions for early galaxy formation.

## HI, Hot Gas, AGN Feedback, and Radio Jets

- [2606.28150](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28150) W. J. G. de Blok et al. `Deep HI observations of cold gas inflow and outflow`
  - **Question:** what deep HI observations are needed to connect cold gas inflow and outflow to galaxy evolution?
  - **Data:**
    - Facility context: SKA-Mid AA4.
    - Targets: nearby individual disk and dwarf galaxies.
    - Sensitivity goal: high angular resolution with very low HI column-density sensitivity.
  - **Method:**
    - Review gas-accretion and gas-removal processes in nearby galaxies.
    - Identify where current HI data lack sensitivity or angular resolution.
    - Connect local resolved measurements to sub-grid physics in hydrodynamical simulations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Deep HI observations are needed to explain how galaxies maintain star formation.
    - Nearby resolved HI inflow/outflow measurements calibrate interpretation of high-redshift galaxy evolution.
    - SKA-Mid AA4 can provide the missing combination of resolution and low-column-density sensitivity.
  - **Position:** important infrastructure entry because cold atomic gas regulates the baryon cycle that high-redshift observations infer indirectly.

- [2606.28304](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28304) C. Spingola et al. `SKA-VLBI view of AGN jets in the early Universe`
  - **Question:** how can SKA-VLBI constrain the first jetted AGN and their impact on early host galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Facility: SKA-VLBI using SKA-Mid and SKA-Low frequencies.
    - Targets: compact high-brightness-temperature radio cores in jetted AGN at `z>6`.
    - Physical scale: milliarcsecond angular resolution and sub-microJy sensitivity.
  - **Method:**
    - Use VLBI imaging to constrain Doppler boosting, duty cycles, and jet-environment coupling.
    - Compare observations with models of `>10^6 M_sun` SMBH formation and evolution.
    - Combine radio imaging with panchromatic host-galaxy and environment data.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - SKA-VLBI can directly image and characterize jetted AGN in the early universe.
    - The data can test jet-assisted super-Eddington growth scenarios.
    - High-resolution radio cores provide probes of both feedback and cosmological geometry.
  - **Position:** high-priority AGN infrastructure paper because early jetted sources are both black-hole growth tracers and feedback laboratories.

- [2606.28311](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28311) Fergus R. Donnan et al. `Kinematic detection of dusty outflows from AGN: PAH kinematics of type 2 quasars with JWST/MIRI spectroscopy`
  - **Question:** can JWST/MIRI measure dusty AGN outflows directly through PAH kinematics?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: five type 2 quasars at `z~0.1` from QSOFEED.
    - Instrument: JWST/MIRI spectroscopy.
    - Tracers: PAH features, ionized gas, and molecular gas emission lines.
  - **Method:**
    - Apply PCA tomography to velocity-map PAH features.
    - Compare PAH kinematics with ionized and molecular gas velocity maps.
    - Subtract disk kinematics to isolate residual outflow signatures.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Velocity maps of the `11.3 micron` PAH feature are recovered for three of five objects.
    - All three PAH maps show outflow signatures.
    - H2 rotational transitions show residuals consistent with an outflow after disk subtraction.
    - Dusty outflows may be more common at `lambda_Edd gtrsim 0.1`, though the sample is small.
  - **Position:** useful feedback paper because it turns dust from a static attenuation component into a kinematic outflow tracer.

- [2606.28257](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28257) Swetha Sankar et al. `When Jets Don't Quench: Near-Infrared H2 in Star Forming Low-Excitation Radio Galaxies`
  - **Question:** can jet-mode radio AGN coexist with molecular-rich, star-forming host galaxies before quenching?
  - **Data:**
    - Instrument: Gemini/GNIRS near-infrared spectroscopy.
    - Sample: eight low-redshift blue low-excitation radio galaxies at `z<0.1`.
    - Host type: rare BLERGs, about `2.5%` of low-excitation radio galaxies.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure ro-vibrational warm H2 emission.
    - Compare mass-normalized H2 luminosity with radio power and host morphology.
    - Interpret merger-driven and jet-driven excitation channels.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - BLERGs show significant warm H2 at `T~2000-4000 K`.
    - Strong H2 emission preferentially occurs in disturbed and advanced-merger systems.
    - Compact radio sources with sizes `lesssim 20 kpc` remain plausible sites of localized jet-ISM interaction.
    - BLERGs may be a short-lived phase where radio AGN activity coexists with molecular-rich, interaction-driven ISM conditions.
  - **Position:** relevant feedback result because it challenges the simple mapping between radio jets and immediate host quenching.

- [2606.28019](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28019) Gourab Giri et al. `Simulating megaparsec-scale jets of radio galaxies: Magneto-hydrodynamics of jets reaching 5 Mpc`
  - **Question:** what physical conditions let radio-galaxy jets remain collimated over megaparsec scales?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: 3D high-resolution MHD jet simulations in low-density cosmic environments.
    - Jet classes: continuous injections at different powers.
    - Scale target: radio jets reaching `5 Mpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Vary kinetic power, collimation, and magnetic stabilization.
    - Track pinch and kink instabilities.
    - Build a synchrotron-emission proxy from compressed and amplified magnetic regions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - High thrust, suppressed transverse distortions, and stronger poloidal fields allow a jet to reach `5 Mpc` in `15 Myr`.
    - The successful model injects `2.3e61 erg` into the environment.
    - A less stabilized jet reaches only `~3 Mpc` over `~35 Myr`.
    - Jet-head propagation transitions from about `0.5c` to `0.2c-0.05c`.
    - Such jets can transport energy and magnetic flux into underdense cosmic regions.
  - **Position:** useful for LSS feedback because extreme radio jets redistribute energy and magnetization beyond the host halo.

- [2606.28099](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28099) Guandi Zhao et al. `Thermal Sunyaev-Zel'dovich cross-correlations with unWISE galaxies: disentangling radio contamination, dust properties, and electron pressure`
  - **Question:** how much foreground modeling is required before tSZ-galaxy correlations can measure hot gas in low-mass halos?
  - **Data:**
    - Galaxy samples: two low-redshift unWISE samples with mean redshifts `0.14` and `0.23`.
    - Microwave maps: Planck PR4 and ACT DR6 temperature maps.
    - Components: tSZ, cosmic infrared background, and radio emission.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit harmonic-space cross-correlations with multi-component foreground models.
    - Use nine Planck bands for model selection.
    - Apply a radio-inclusive recipe to ACT+Planck `90`, `150`, and `220 GHz` maps.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - A model including radio contamination is strongly preferred at `9.5 sigma` for unWISE Low-z and `11 sigma` for unWISE Mid-z.
    - Radio cleaning removes the apparent negative galaxy-tSZ cross-correlation seen in no-radio fits.
    - Cleaned spectra remain positive to `ell~6000`.
    - The cleaned measurements are consistent with a halo model and two-parameter generalized NFW pressure profile.
  - **Position:** relevant hot-CGM methods paper because small-scale baryon measurements can be foreground-dominated without explicit radio modeling.

- [2606.28314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28314) Om Gupta et al. `The Role of Scintillation in Detecting HI Absorption in FRB Spectra`
  - **Question:** when can FRB spectra reveal intervening or host-galaxy HI 21 cm absorption despite scintillation?
  - **Data:**
    - Signal: HI 21 cm absorption in FRB spectra.
    - Model: diffractive scintillation from a thin scattering screen.
    - Catalog cross-check: repeating FRB positions and Milky Way molecular clouds traced by CO.
  - **Method:**
    - Simulate scintillation-dominated spectra for realistic FRBs.
    - Compare scintillation decorrelation bandwidth with absorption-profile width.
    - Test stacked repeat bursts separated by more than the diffractive scintillation timescale.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Absorption is detectable in high-S/N scintillation-dominated spectra when scintillation bandwidth and line width differ strongly.
    - Repeat bursts improve the signal only if their scintillation modulations are not time-correlated.
    - FRB 20180916B may intersect a Galactic molecular cloud.
    - Current and planned telescopes require `gtrsim 1000` bursts stacked for a `5 sigma` detection when both scintillation and noise are present.
  - **Position:** useful HI/CGM technique paper because FRBs could probe neutral gas in hosts, intervening galaxies, or high-redshift minihalos if the selection function is understood.

- [2606.27812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27812) Aritra Basu et al. `Unraveling the Imprints of Fluctuation-dynamo on the Intracluster Medium with the SKA`
  - **Question:** can SKA polarization imaging recover fluctuation-dynamo signatures in intracluster magnetic fields?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: high-resolution MHD fluctuation-dynamo maps.
    - Facility context: SKA-Low and SKA-Mid AA4.
    - Observables: polarized synchrotron emission from cluster halos.
  - **Method:**
    - Generate broad-band synthetic maps.
    - Quantify filamentary and sheet-like polarized structures.
    - Use statistical morphology, including Minkowski functionals, to infer turbulence driving scales.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Fluctuation dynamos produce filamentary polarized synchrotron structures spanning hundreds of kiloparsecs.
    - Robust identification requires angular resolution `lesssim 1 arcsec` and sensitivity around `0.25-1 microJy/beam`.
    - Frequencies above about `3 GHz`, especially SKA-Mid Band 5a, are favorable.
    - A Band 4 receiver covering `2-4 GHz` would substantially improve ICM polarization work.
  - **Position:** lower-priority for high-z galaxies but relevant for magnetized plasma in cluster environments and the large-scale baryon cycle.
