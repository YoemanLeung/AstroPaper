## Executive View

The Friday listing is weaker on direct JWST high-redshift galaxy observations than the rest of the week, but strong for protocluster fate, reionization modeling, cosmic-web dynamics, SPHEREx cluster photometry, and environment-driven galaxy evolution.

The read-first paper is [2606.19463](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19463) Seunghwan Lim. It uses FLAMINGO simulations to test whether observationally selected `z>=5` protocluster candidates become present-day clusters. The main result is cautionary: current selections mix real cluster progenitors with interlopers, and later merging depends strongly on neighbors within `5-10 cMpc`.

The top reionization-method paper is [2606.19449](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19449) Christopher Cain. It builds an analytic model that predicts both ionized fraction and hydrogen photoionization rate. The model reaches percent-level accuracy in ionized fraction and `20-30%` accuracy in `Gamma_HI` at `z<=6`, making Ly-alpha forest photoionization-rate data usable in fast inference.

The strongest early-Universe cosmology entry is [2606.19459](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19459) Yash Aggarwal. It tests whether a Pop III.1 flash at `z=20` can raise CMB optical depth to `tau=0.087` while keeping the patchy kSZ signal below conservative South Pole Telescope limits.

Large-scale environment work is useful. [2606.19443](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19443) Hannah Jhee tracks evolving cosmic filaments and shows that halo accretion and spin alignment depend on time-dependent filament structure. [2606.19875](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19875) Hyeonguk Bahk validates SPHEREx forced photometry for cluster galaxies from `z~0.02-1.1`. [2606.20335](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20335) Chong Ge uses radio tails and arcs in A2142 as ICM-weather tracers of a weak merger shock.

Modeling infrastructure appears in two forms. [2606.19447](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19447) Dhruv T. Zimmerman trains a simulation-based machine-learning SED tool, `Phot-Gal`, on 3D radiative-transfer photometry. [2606.20370](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20370) Bruna R. Braganca de Lima releases `ELMA`, an ellipse-based bar-length estimator demonstrated on JWST/NIRCam GOODS-S imaging.

## High-Redshift Protoclusters and Reionization Models

- [2606.19463](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19463) Seunghwan Lim `On the later evolution of observationally selected protocluster candidates at z greater than about 5`
  - **Question:** do observationally selected `z>=5` protocluster candidates reliably become present-day galaxy clusters?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: FLAMINGO, resolving protocluster cores up to `z~10`.
    - Samples: three selection methods compared with observational protocluster candidate samples.
    - Scale: neighbor counts and mass sums inside `10 cMpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Track selected high-redshift candidates forward to `z=0`.
    - Compare mass-selected, abundance-matched, and observationally motivated selections.
    - Measure nearby candidate clustering and later merger probabilities.
    - Test which selection quantities predict final cluster fate.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Current searches select both genuine cluster progenitors and interlopers that do not reach cluster masses by `z=0`.
    - Candidates at `z>=5` host `2-10` neighbors within `10 cMpc`.
    - A neighbor at `5 cMpc` implies a later merger probability of `>=50%`; a neighbor at `10 cMpc` implies `>=30%`.
    - Each selected candidate undergoes roughly `2-6` later major mergers, mostly at `z<=2`.
    - Robust progenitor identification needs the total galaxy mass down to faint limits within `10 cMpc`.
  - **Position:** read first for high-redshift LSS because it turns protocluster selection into a fate-calibration problem rather than a single overdensity measurement.

- [2606.19449](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19449) Christopher Cain et al. `A self-consistent analytical model for both the photoionization rate and reionization history`
  - **Question:** can a fast analytic reionization model predict both ionized fraction and photoionization rate for inference against Ly-alpha forest data?
  - **Data:**
    - Observables: ionized fraction `x_i(z)` and hydrogen photoionization rate `Gamma_HI(z)`.
    - Constraint context: high-redshift quasar and galaxy Ly-alpha forest transmission at `5<=z<=6`.
    - Validation: detailed radiative-transfer simulations.
  - **Method:**
    - Build a formalism from the cosmological radiative-transfer equation.
    - Predict `x_i` and `Gamma_HI` self-consistently.
    - Compare against radiative-transfer simulations.
    - Test sensitivity to ionizing photon output and reionization endpoint.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The model is percent-level accurate in `x_i`.
    - The model is `20-30%` accurate in `Gamma_HI` at `z<=6`.
    - Modest changes in galaxy ionizing output or reionization endpoint shift `Gamma_HI` by more than the model uncertainty.
    - The framework makes photoionization-rate measurements usable in fast Bayesian inference.
  - **Position:** important reionization infrastructure because the Ly-alpha forest constrains the tail end of reionization through `Gamma_HI`, not only through neutral-fraction summaries.

- [2606.19459](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19459) Yash Aggarwal et al. `Fireworks at Cosmic Dawn: relieving BAO-CMB tensions with the Pop III.1 Flash`
  - **Question:** can an early weakly clustered ionization phase raise CMB optical depth without violating Ly-alpha forest and patchy-kSZ constraints?
  - **Data:**
    - Cosmology context: CMB optical depth, DESI BAO, Planck low-ell polarization, Ly-alpha forest, and SPT patchy-kSZ limits.
    - Source model: supermassive Pop III.1 stars as dark-matter-powered metal-free stars.
    - Fiducial event: flash ionization centered at `z=20`.
  - **Method:**
    - Model early ionization from self-regulated Pop III.1 sources.
    - Impose a minimum source separation of `~1 cMpc`.
    - Compute optical depth and patchy-kSZ consistency.
    - Compare with late-reionization constraints from Ly-alpha forest observations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The fiducial model reaches `tau=0.087`.
    - Weak source clustering suppresses large-scale ionization fluctuations.
    - The model stays below conservative `2 sigma` SPT patchy-kSZ upper limits.
    - Early reionization phases remain viable only if they avoid excessive late-time duration and large-scale patchiness.
  - **Position:** useful but speculative because it connects high-redshift source physics to current BAO-CMB parameter tension.

## Cosmic Web, Cluster Selection, and Environmental Gas

- [2606.19443](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19443) Hannah Jhee et al. `The impact of evolving cosmic filaments on mass and spin evolution of dark matter halos`
  - **Question:** how do time-evolving cosmic filaments affect halo accretion and angular momentum?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: cosmological N-body run with filaments identified at `z=0` using DisPerSE.
    - Tracked structures: individual filament progenitors through time.
    - Halo sample: systems screened to remove major-merger contamination.
  - **Method:**
    - Reconstruct filament histories using spatial similarity between descendant and progenitor candidates.
    - Measure filament drift, density profiles, splashback radii, and core overdensities.
    - Recompute halo phase-space trajectories in evolving filament-centric coordinates.
    - Compare halo accretion and spin alignment with stochastic expectations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Halo mass accretion rates are suppressed as halos approach high-density filaments.
    - The suppression begins near filament outskirts, consistent with tidal stripping or reduced net accretion.
    - Halo spin alignment departs from random-walk expectations.
    - Tracking co-evolving filaments and halos is needed to characterize environmental effects accurately.
  - **Position:** strong cosmic-web methods entry because it treats filaments as moving structures rather than static present-day backdrops.

- [2606.19875](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19875) Hyeonguk Bahk et al. `The SPHEREx View of Galaxy Clusters: A Simulation-based Validation of the Forced Photometry Pipeline for Extended Sources`
  - **Question:** can SPHEREx forced photometry deliver cluster-galaxy photometry and cluster redshifts with useful precision?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: eight galaxy clusters spanning `z~0.02-1.1`.
    - Inputs: DESI Legacy Survey and COSMOS ancillary data.
    - Mock observations: SPHEREx Sky Simulator images.
  - **Method:**
    - Generate end-to-end mock SPHEREx observations.
    - Run forced photometry with The Tractor.
    - Quantify biases from source blending, survey depth, and sample selection.
    - Combine selected member redshifts to recover cluster redshifts.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Photometry is generally unbiased when blending is controlled.
    - Source blending is the main catastrophic-outlier driver.
    - SPHEREx detects members to `K_s~20 AB` at `5 sigma`, but depth relative to BCGs worsens toward `z~1`.
    - Quality-selected cluster galaxies reach photometric-redshift precision `sigma_NMAD~0.003-0.01`.
    - Combined member redshifts recover `z<=0.5` cluster redshifts with bias `|Delta z|/(1+z)<0.002` and scatter `sigma~0.002`.
  - **Position:** high-value survey-infrastructure paper for cluster and environment work with SPHEREx-era low-resolution spectroscopy.

- [2606.20335](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20335) Chong Ge et al. `A merger shock traced by radio arcs and ultra-long radio tails in galaxy cluster A2142`
  - **Question:** can radio arcs and very long radio tails independently trace weak merger shocks in the intracluster medium?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: Abell 2142, a nearby merging massive cluster.
    - X-ray data: XMM-Newton observations.
    - Radio context: arc-shaped filaments behind head-tail radio galaxies T1 and T2.
  - **Method:**
    - Detect the northwestern X-ray shock front.
    - Compare shock and cold-front structure with off-axis merger simulations.
    - Interpret radio arcs as vortex-ring structures formed when shocks interact with jet cocoons.
    - Model re-acceleration and stretching of aged tail plasma by post-shock winds.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The merger shock has Mach number `M~1.3`.
    - An off-axis merger with a large impact parameter reproduces the shock and cold fronts.
    - Radio arcs can trace shock-rolled jet cocoons.
    - Merger shocks can elongate radio tails beyond `500 kpc`.
  - **Position:** useful cluster-physics entry because tailed radio galaxies become sensitive probes of ICM weather and merger shocks.

- [2606.20006](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20006) A. Boselli et al. `A Virgo Environmental Survey Tracing Ionised Gas Emission (VESTIGE) XX. Star formation in the tidal tail of NGC 4254`
  - **Question:** what happens to molecular clouds and star formation inside stripped HI tails in cluster environments?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: NGC 4254 in Virgo.
    - Observations: ALMA `12CO(1-0)` for `42` star-forming regions outside the disk.
    - Detected clouds: ten GMCs in four stripped-tail regions.
  - **Method:**
    - Resolve GMC properties at `~160 pc` angular-resolution scale.
    - Compare gas column density, velocity dispersion, and star-formation activity with disk clouds.
    - Use analytic calculations and tuned simulations to test cloud survival.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - GMC masses are `0.8-2.0x10^6 M_sun`.
    - Gas column densities are around `10 M_sun pc^-2`.
    - Velocity dispersions are `3-12 km/s`.
    - The clouds are unstable and likely dissolve in `10-30 Myr`.
    - The intracluster medium is too diffuse locally to confine feedback-driven gas loss.
  - **Position:** lower-redshift but useful because it gives a resolved laboratory for stripped gas, cloud survival, and star formation in hostile environments.

## Modeling and Galaxy-Structure Infrastructure

- [2606.19447](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19447) Dhruv T. Zimmerman et al. `Reimagining SED Fitting with Cosmological Galaxy Simulations and Machine Learning`
  - **Question:** can galaxy properties be inferred from photometry using machine learning trained on 3D radiative-transfer simulations instead of conventional scalar SED assumptions?
  - **Data:**
    - Training source: simulated-galaxy photometry from 3D radiative transfer with varied implemented physics.
    - Tool: `Phot-Gal`.
    - Predicted quantities: redshift, stellar mass, dust mass, and star-formation rate.
  - **Method:**
    - Train a machine-learning inverse model on simulated photometry.
    - Accept arbitrary filter coverage through `K`-nearest-neighbor imputation.
    - Compare against `prospector`.
    - Inspect which photometric components drive the predictions and uncertainties.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The fiducial model predicts redshift, stellar mass, dust mass, and SFR with uncertainty estimates.
    - It outperforms standard SED-fitting accuracy on the test set used in the study.
    - With fewer photometric constraints, uncertainties can underrepresent the true offset from ground truth.
    - Generalization to novel data remains the key risk.
  - **Position:** useful for JWST and survey work because photometric physical-property inference is becoming the limiting step for very large galaxy samples.

- [2606.20175](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20175) Harley J. Brown et al. `A Consistent Comparison of Intracluster Light Assembly in Simulations I. Redshift Evolution and Progenitor Galaxies`
  - **Question:** how robust are simulated intracluster-light assembly histories to the adopted ICL definition?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: Horizon-AGN, TNG100, The Three Hundred Gizmo-Simba 7K, and Hydrangea.
    - Halo mass range: `M_178c~10^14-10^15 M_sun`.
    - Tracked history: cluster progenitors back to `z>=2`.
  - **Method:**
    - Apply a homogenized ICL identification framework across simulations.
    - Compare fiducial and alternative ICL definitions.
    - Trace `z~0` ICL stars back to progenitor galaxies.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Fiducial `z~0` ICL stellar mass fractions are broadly consistent at `~0.1-0.2`.
    - Average ICL mass fractions show no significant evolution under the fiducial definition.
    - Absolute ICL fractions are highly sensitive to the definition.
    - Most ICL stars from satellites originate from infall stellar masses above `~10^10 M_sun`, mainly `10^10.5-10^11.5 M_sun`.
  - **Position:** relevant for cluster assembly and environmental evolution because ICL conclusions require definition-controlled comparisons.

- [2606.20370](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20370) Bruna R. Braganca de Lima et al. `ELMA: ELlipse-based bar MAjor axis estimator`
  - **Question:** can bar lengths be measured automatically and homogeneously in large imaging surveys?
  - **Data:**
    - Tool: `elma`, a standalone Python package.
    - Imaging demonstration: JWST/NIRCam barred galaxies in GOODS-South.
    - Inputs: two-dimensional imaging data, WCS information, and user-supplied redshift.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit iterative elliptical isophotes.
    - Trace radial ellipticity profiles.
    - Identify a projected bar-length estimate from the local maximum in ellipticity.
    - Convert angular length to projected physical length.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - `elma` automates a measurement that is usually manual and method-sensitive.
    - The package is designed for galaxies already identified as candidate barred systems.
    - Survey-scale bar measurements become easier to homogenize across JWST imaging fields.
  - **Position:** useful morphology infrastructure because bar lengths connect secular evolution to central mass growth and disk transformation.
