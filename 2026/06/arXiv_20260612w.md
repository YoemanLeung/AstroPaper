## Executive View

The week is dominated by compact high-redshift sources, gas diagnostics, and the physical conditions around early black holes. The most important cross-day pattern is that LRDs and related compact populations are no longer only color-selected anomalies. They are being dissected through Lyalpha morphology, Balmer absorption, dense-gas covering factors, broad-line scaling, medium-band selection, metallicity, and simulation analogs.

The read-first weekly paper is [2606.09970](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09970) Zhiyuan Ji. It uses `110` spectroscopically confirmed LRDs at `z>=4` to show that Lyalpha emission is commonly host-scale, extended, asymmetric, and offset from the compact rest-optical component. This is the clearest galaxy-gas bridge for the LRD theme.

The strongest direct high-redshift gas entries are [2606.08782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08782) Minami Nakane on low-ionization absorption and abundance ratios in `z=7.2-10.6` galaxies, [2606.06609](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06609) Keerthi Vasan G.C. on neutral-gas covering fractions and kinematics in `z=5-9` galaxies, [2606.10271](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10271) Rebecca L. Davies on a resolved `z=5.3` multiphase outflow, and [2606.12686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12686) Tucker Jones on `~100 pc` gas flows in a lensed `z=3.2` galaxy.

Early black-hole growth is the second major anchor. [2606.10160](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10160) Yoshiki Matsuoka places `z~6` SHELLQs-selected objects between luminous quasars and JWST low-luminosity AGN/LRDs. [2606.11345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11345) Yuki Isobe ties high-redshift metallicity calibration to extremely metal-poor galaxies and two LRDs. [2606.12851](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12851) Raul G. Cacho and [2606.10000](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10000) Eunwoo Chung provide seed-growth channels through AGN disks and dense star clusters.

For survey and environment work, [2606.10005](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10005) Abigail E. Ambrose tests SPHEREx reionization line-intensity cross-correlations, [2606.11089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11089) Yucheng Guo measures `~2-7 kpc` cool-CGM coherence lengths, [2606.12522](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12522) Shane W. Davis targets magnetized gas in a `z=2.47` protocluster, and [2606.13359](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13359) Aritra Ghosh builds cluster-catalog infrastructure for MOONRISE.

The best Friday addition to the weekly set is [2606.13393](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13393) Emanuele Daddi, because a CO(6-5) molecular reservoir in a low-SFR `z=7.31` galaxy sharpens the distinction between gas exhaustion, inefficient star formation, and recent shutdown at early times.

## Compact High-Redshift Sources and LRD/LBD Physics

- [2606.09970](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09970) Zhiyuan Ji et al. `Through the Veil: Lyalpha Illuminates the Host Galaxies of Little Red Dots`
  - **Question:** what does Lyalpha emission reveal about the gas and host galaxies around Little Red Dots?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `110` spectroscopically confirmed LRDs at `z>=4`.
    - Spectroscopy: JWST/NIRSpec PRISM coverage of Lyalpha.
    - Imaging: HST/ACS or JWST/NIRCam broadband data for continuum-subtracted Lyalpha maps.
    - Mapped subset: `13` LRDs at `z>~5.5`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure Lyalpha detections and rest-frame equivalent widths.
    - Compare Lyalpha luminosity, Halpha, [OIII], and continuum properties.
    - Construct Lyalpha maps where imaging allows continuum subtraction.
    - Interpret Lyalpha morphology relative to rest-optical light and compact broad-line components.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Lyalpha is detected at `S/N>=3` in `32` LRDs.
    - Lyalpha luminosities and equivalent widths are consistent with normal star-forming galaxies at similar redshift.
    - Lyalpha/Halpha ratios are systematically low relative to star-forming galaxies.
    - Mapped Lyalpha is extended, asymmetric, and often offset from rest-optical light.
    - The compact red component and the Lyalpha-emitting host-scale gas are not the same physical structure.
  - **Position:** weekly read-first because it moves LRD interpretation from color and broad lines into ISM/CGM geometry.

- [2606.09721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09721) Zhiwei Pan et al. `NEXUS: Abundance, Environments, and Spectral Diversity of Little Red Dots from the NIRSpec MSA Sample`
  - **Question:** what do NIRSpec spectra show about LRD abundance, selection performance, spectral diversity, and environments?
  - **Data:**
    - Program: NEXUS.
    - Instruments: JWST/NIRCam photometry and NIRSpec MSA/PRISM spectra.
    - Redshift range: `2.3<z<7.4`.
    - Spectroscopic sample: `36` confirmed LRDs.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine multiple photometric LRD selection methods.
    - Measure selection completeness and purity at `F444W<26`.
    - Classify NIRSpec spectral diversity.
    - Compare broad Halpha, continuum, narrow [OIII], space density, and clustering.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Combined photometric selection reaches `~85%` completeness.
    - Overall purity is `~60%`.
    - More than `90%` of spectroscopically confirmed LRDs have robust broad-line detections.
    - Clustering suggests host halos of several `10^11 h^-1 M_sun`, with large uncertainties.
  - **Position:** key demographics paper because it connects LRD spectra, selection functions, and environment.

- [2606.12509](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12509) Fabio Fontanot et al. `THESAN view on the recent JWST Little Blue Dots`
  - **Question:** what are Little Blue Dots in simulations, and how do they relate to compact JWST populations?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: THESAN radiation-hydrodynamic reionization simulations.
    - Redshift range: `4<z<7`.
    - Population: compact blue galaxies selected to match JWST Little Blue Dot criteria.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify simulated LBD analogs.
    - Compare size, mass, age, and star-formation properties.
    - Estimate abundance and observability.
    - Contrast LBDs with LRDs and line-contaminated compact galaxies.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - LBD analogs are abundant compact systems in the simulation.
    - Young stars and strong nebular emission can reproduce blue compact-source colors.
    - LBDs provide a star-forming baseline for separating compact stellar systems from AGN-dominated red systems.
  - **Position:** important because compact-source demographics need joint LBD/LRD selection modeling.

## Reionization-Era Gas, CGM, and Feedback

- [2606.08782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08782) Minami Nakane et al. `JWST Absorption-Line Analysis of UV-Bright Galaxies at z=7.2-10.6: Early Chemical Enrichment Traced by C, O, Mg, Al, Si, and Fe`
  - **Question:** what do low-ionization UV absorption lines reveal about cool gas, covering fraction, and chemical enrichment in bright reionization-era galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Instrument: JWST/NIRSpec medium-resolution spectroscopy.
    - Sample: eight UV-bright galaxies at `z=7.2-10.6`.
    - Diagnostics: low-ionization absorption from C, O, Mg, Al, Si, and Fe.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify low-ionization absorption lines in archival spectra.
    - Compare equivalent widths against compactness and star-formation intensity.
    - Fit covering fraction and column density.
    - Place abundance ratios in DLA-like diagnostic planes.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Five of eight galaxies show multiple low-ionization absorption lines.
    - Compact galaxies with high Hbeta-to-UV luminosity and high `Sigma_SFR` show weaker absorption.
    - Two `z~7` galaxies have `[Fe/Si]~-0.2`.
    - Near-solar `[Al/Si]` disfavors a strong pair-instability supernova odd-even signature.
  - **Position:** top absorption-line entry because it links reionization-era cool gas to early chemical clocks.

- [2606.06609](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06609) Keerthi Vasan G.C. et al. `JWST absorption line spectroscopy with SPURS: ISM covering fractions and kinematics in individual galaxies at z=5-9`
  - **Question:** what do deep rest-UV absorption spectra reveal about neutral-gas porosity and outflow kinematics in individual reionization-era galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Program: JWST Cycle 4 SPURS.
    - Sample: six luminous galaxies in Abell 2744 at `z=5-9`.
    - Diagnostics: low- and high-ionization interstellar metal absorption lines.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure low-ionization absorption depths.
    - Compare low- and high-ionization velocity centroids.
    - Identify systemic, blueshifted, and multiphase absorption components.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Low-ionization covering fractions span `0.2-0.9`.
    - Low-ionization centroids range from `+70 km/s` to `-140 km/s`.
    - High-ionization absorption is mostly blueshifted.
    - Deep JWST spectra can resolve enriched ISM geometry and kinematics during reionization.
  - **Position:** high-priority gas diagnostic because covering fraction and kinematics control Lyalpha and LyC escape.

- [2606.10271](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10271) Rebecca L. Davies et al. `Resolving a Powerful Galactic Wind in a z=5.3 Star-Forming Galaxy`
  - **Question:** can a high-redshift galaxy drive a resolved multiphase wind strong enough to regulate star formation?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: star-forming galaxy at `z=5.3`.
    - Observations: resolved cold and ionized gas diagnostics.
    - Wind measurement: gas removal rate compared with SFR.
  - **Method:**
    - Map cold and ionized outflow components.
    - Estimate outflow mass, velocity, and removal rate.
    - Compare depletion timescale with the ongoing SFR.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The outflow is spatially resolved in multiple gas phases.
    - The gas removal rate is about twice the star-formation rate.
    - Cold gas could be depleted within `100 Myr` if the outflow persists.
  - **Position:** strong feedback paper because it measures an early wind with direct regulatory power.

- [2606.12686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12686) Tucker Jones et al. `Gas Flows in a Strongly Lensed Galaxy at z=3.2`
  - **Question:** how do outflows, possible inflows, and metal enrichment appear on resolved scales in a typical star-forming galaxy at `z=3.2`?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: SGAS J003341.5+024217 at `z=3.2`.
    - Lensing: source-plane sampling near `~100 pc`.
    - Diagnostics: absorption and emission lines tracing gas kinematics and enrichment.
  - **Method:**
    - Map resolved gas velocities.
    - Separate outflowing and redshifted absorption components.
    - Compare metallicity and dust across the source.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Fast outflowing gas is consistent with supernova-driven feedback.
    - Enrichment is spatially inhomogeneous.
    - Redshifted absorption points to candidate inflow or recycled accretion.
  - **Position:** direct galaxy-gas connection paper for cosmic-noon and high-redshift ISM/CGM work.

## Early Black Holes, Quasars, and Molecular Gas

- [2606.10160](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10160) Yoshiki Matsuoka et al. `Aether-SHELLQs: JWST integral-field spectroscopy of candidate obscured quasars at z ~ 6`
  - **Question:** what are the AGN fraction, gas structure, and kinematics of SHELLQs-selected `z~6` candidate obscured quasars?
  - **Data:**
    - Instrument: JWST/NIRSpec IFU.
    - Program: Aether GO 5645.
    - Sample: six galaxies at `z~6`.
  - **Method:**
    - Search for broad Balmer components and narrow-line diagnostics.
    - Map ionized-gas morphology and kinematics with IFU data.
    - Compare Balmer profiles, forbidden-line profiles, and Lyalpha luminosities.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Two of six targets show broad Balmer components with `FWHM>3000 km/s`.
    - AGN fraction is `>77%` above `L_Lyalpha=10^44 erg/s` and `<15%` below that threshold.
    - Non-broad-line objects show extended ionized gas up to `10 kpc`.
  - **Position:** central high-`z` AGN paper because it links Subaru quasar selection, JWST low-luminosity AGN, and LRD-like populations.

- [2606.13393](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13393) Emanuele Daddi et al. `Strong CO(6-5) emission in a quiescent galaxy at z=7.31 reveals a massive molecular reservoir`
  - **Question:** can a galaxy at `z=7.31` with low current star formation still retain a large molecular gas reservoir?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: JADES-GS-z7-01-QU at `z=7.31`.
    - Line: CO(6-5).
    - Inferred gas: molecular mass near `3x10^10 M_sun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Detect and measure CO(6-5).
    - Convert high-J CO emission to molecular gas mass.
    - Compare gas, stellar mass, and SFR.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The inferred gas fraction is about `65%`.
    - A large molecular reservoir can coexist with low current SFR.
    - Early quiescence may reflect inefficient star formation or recent shutdown rather than gas exhaustion.
  - **Position:** key early-gas result because it tests quenching mechanisms in the reionization era.

- [2606.12851](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12851) Raul G. Cacho et al. `Massive black-hole mergers from accretion disks in AGNs: The case for high-redshift heavy seeds`
  - **Question:** can AGN disks around high-redshift heavy seeds produce the massive black-hole mergers seen by gravitational-wave detectors?
  - **Data:**
    - Model context: compact objects embedded in AGN accretion disks.
    - Seed scenario: high-redshift heavy black-hole seeds.
    - Observable comparison: massive black-hole merger populations.
  - **Method:**
    - Model orbital migration and pairing inside AGN disks.
    - Compare merger production against seed-mass and disk-property assumptions.
    - Evaluate whether high-redshift heavy seeds improve massive merger production.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - AGN disks can enhance black-hole pairing and growth.
    - Heavy seeds at high redshift make the most massive merger products easier to form.
    - The channel links early SMBH seeding to gravitational-wave demographics.
  - **Position:** useful because it connects high-redshift seed models to a measurable merger channel.

## Environments, Metallicity, and Survey Infrastructure

- [2606.11089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11089) Yucheng Guo et al. `The Physical Coherence of Cool Circumgalactic Gas`
  - **Question:** what are the coherence lengths of cool CGM clouds around galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey context: MEGAFLOW.
    - Probes: background-galaxy and quasar MgII absorption.
    - Scale: paired sightlines across galaxy halos.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare absorption coherence across multiple background sightlines.
    - Infer characteristic cool-gas cloud sizes.
    - Relate cloud structure to halo and galaxy properties.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Cool-CGM coherence lengths are about `2-7 kpc`.
    - Background galaxies provide a path to resolving CGM structure beyond quasar-only pencil beams.
    - The inferred scales are small enough to matter for interpreting galaxy-gas correlations.
  - **Position:** high-priority CGM entry because it constrains the spatial granularity behind absorption statistics.

- [2606.12522](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12522) Shane W. Davis et al. `A Search for Magnetic Fields in the CARLA J1018.6+1058 Galaxy Protocluster`
  - **Question:** can radio observations constrain magnetic fields in a dense `z=2.47` protocluster environment?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: CARLA J1018.6+1058 protocluster.
    - Redshift: `z=2.47`.
    - Observables: radio polarization and rotation-measure constraints.
  - **Method:**
    - Search for magnetic-field signatures around the protocluster.
    - Compare observed rotation or depolarization constraints with foreground expectations.
    - Interpret limits in terms of intraprotocluster gas conditions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Protoclusters can be used to test early magnetization of dense environments.
    - Magnetic-field constraints add gas-physics context beyond galaxy overdensity alone.
    - The method is relevant for feedback and turbulence in assembling cluster environments.
  - **Position:** useful dense-environment paper because it links protocluster selection to magnetized gas.

- [2606.13078](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13078) Mario Candeloro et al. `Chemical signatures of Population III stars in high-z galaxies: predictions and detectability`
  - **Question:** which chemical signatures can identify Pop III enrichment in high-redshift galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Models: high-redshift galaxies with Pop III chemical contribution.
    - Observables: abundance-sensitive UV and optical line ratios.
    - Facility context: JWST and future large telescopes.
  - **Method:**
    - Predict abundance patterns for Pop III-enriched populations.
    - Translate chemical patterns into observable line diagnostics.
    - Test detectability under realistic sensitivity limits.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Pop III signatures are easiest to detect in extremely metal-poor systems.
    - JWST struggles below the critical metallicity threshold without strong lensing.
    - Future facilities are needed for robust Pop III chemical archaeology.
  - **Position:** strong first-stars entry because it defines observational requirements instead of only proposing candidate sources.
