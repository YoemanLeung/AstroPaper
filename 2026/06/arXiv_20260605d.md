## Protoclusters, DSFGs, and AGN Growth

- [2606.05283](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05283) Monica Natalia Isla Llave et al. `Strong environmental AGN enhancement among DSFGs in z > 2 protoclusters`
  - **Question:** does the dense protocluster environment enhance SMBH growth in dusty star-forming galaxies beyond host-property effects?
  - **Data:**
    - Population: DSFGs in protoclusters at `z>2`.
    - Control sample: field DSFGs matched in stellar mass, SFR, and dust mass.
    - AGN tracer: X-ray AGN fraction.
    - Redshift bins: full sample plus `z=2-3` and `z=3-4.5` subsets.
  - **Method:**
    - Build a homogeneous protocluster and field DSFG comparison.
    - Match host galaxy properties to reduce selection and mass-driven biases.
    - Compare X-ray AGN fractions in protocluster and field environments.
    - Evaluate enhancement significance with Poisson statistics.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Protocluster DSFGs show an X-ray AGN fraction enhancement of `~2.7x` with `p=3e-4`.
    - The enhancement is `~2.7x` at `z=2-3` with `p=0.003`.
    - The enhancement is `~2.6x` at `z=3-4.5` with `p=0.03`.
    - Matched stellar mass, SFR, and dust mass make host-property selection an unlikely driver.
    - Dense protocluster environments likely increase SMBH fueling through gas availability and interactions.
  - **Position:** top Friday entry because it directly connects protoclusters, DSFGs, AGN incidence, and environmental SMBH growth at the redshifts central to the profile.

## Gas Accretion, Clumps, and Cosmic-Noon Morphology

- [2606.05284](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05284) Visal Sok et al. `Metal-Poor Gas Accretion Drives Giant Clump Formation at 0.6 < z < 2.6`
  - **Question:** are giant clumps in cosmic-noon star-forming galaxies tied to metal-poor gas accretion?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `~300` star-forming galaxies at `0.6<z<2.6`.
    - Surveys: LEGA-C, MOSDEF, and CANUCS near-infrared spectroscopy.
    - CANUCS subset: resolved stellar properties of clumps.
    - Morphology: stellar-mass maps and Gini-M20 merger statistics.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare integrated gas-phase metallicities of clumpy and nonclumpy star-forming galaxies.
    - Measure offsets from the mass-metallicity relation.
    - Relate clump stellar ages, masses, and SFRs to host metallicity residuals.
    - Test whether clumpy systems are mostly merger-classified in stellar-mass morphology.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Clumpy galaxies generally sit below the mass-metallicity relation, while nonclumpy galaxies sit above it.
    - Clumps in hosts with `Delta Z<=0` are younger and have higher SFRs than clumps in hosts with `Delta Z>0`.
    - The two clump populations show no significant mass difference.
    - Most clumpy galaxies are not classified as mergers in stellar-mass maps.
    - Metal-poor accretion can both trigger star formation in clumps and dilute host gas metallicities.
  - **Position:** high-priority companion to environment-metallicity work because it links resolved clumps, gas inflow, and chemical dilution at cosmic noon.

## Quasar Outflows and AGN Feedback Context

- [2606.06226](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06226) Lucas M. Seaton et al. `A New Member of the Fast and Furious Family: A Relativistic and Time-Variable UV Outflow in a Luminous Quasar`
  - **Question:** how extreme can UV-detected quasar outflows become, and how do they vary with time?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: radio-quiet luminous quasar SDSS J231854.31+243954.2.
    - Redshift: Halpha redshift `z=2.6781+-0.0004` from Gemini/GNIRS.
    - Diagnostics: variable C IV and Si IV absorption.
    - Publication context: Astrophysical Journal, Volume `1004`, Number `1`, article `49`.
  - **Method:**
    - Use rest-frame UV absorption variability to identify and characterize outflowing gas.
    - Measure outflow velocities from C IV and Si IV absorption troughs.
    - Use near-infrared spectroscopy to set the systemic redshift from Halpha.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The outflow is detected in the ultraviolet rather than only through X-ray or broad absorption selection.
    - Absorption velocities span `-77,000 km/s` to at least `-90,000 km/s`.
    - The quasar is weak-lined in the rest-frame ultraviolet.
    - The source joins the "Fast and Furious" class of relativistic, time-variable quasar outflows.
  - **Position:** useful AGN-feedback entry because the outflow velocity is extreme enough to test wind-launching and quasar-selection models.

## Local Star Formation and Galactic-Structure Benchmarks

- [2606.05668](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05668) T. A. Rector et al. `Mapping Active Star-Formation in Serpens and the Aquila Rift`
  - **Question:** can Herbig-Haro outflows map active star formation across the Serpens-Aquila Rift?
  - **Data:**
    - Instrument: DECam on the Blanco `4 m` telescope.
    - Region: Serpens-Aquila Rift.
    - Detections: `88` new Herbig-Haro objects.
    - Supporting data: literature dust maps and Gaia astrometry.
  - **Method:**
    - Run a high-sensitivity imaging survey for Herbig-Haro outflows.
    - Identify likely progenitors for the detected outflows.
    - Use HH positions, dust maps, and astrometry to split the region into cloud components.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The survey more than triples the known HH object count in the region.
    - HH objects trace five spatially distinct active star-forming clouds.
    - In Serpens and western Aquila, HH objects follow gas structures near low-density cavities.
    - A nearer Serpens cloud at `~250 pc` may mark a new stellar generation connected to the Scutum North Association.
  - **Position:** lower priority for the profile, but useful as a local star-formation mapping example using outflow tracers.

- [2606.06248](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06248) Hanna Parul et al. `Bar-induced deflection of open cluster tidal tails`
  - **Question:** can open-cluster tidal-tail orientations constrain the Milky Way bar pattern speed?
  - **Data:**
    - Parent catalog: `~1450` observed open clusters from Hunt & Reffert 2024.
    - Models: axisymmetric reference potential and eight barred potentials.
    - Pattern speeds: `Omega_b=20-55 km/s/kpc`.
    - Comparison: observed tidal tails including NGC 2632 and the Hyades.
  - **Method:**
    - Run test-particle simulations for each open cluster.
    - Measure deflection angles between barred and axisymmetric tail orientations.
    - Classify clusters as bar-sensitive or bar-insensitive.
    - Compare model tail orientations with observed extended tails.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Deflection varies systematically with bar pattern speed and cluster guiding radius.
    - The largest deflections occur near the outer Lindblad resonance.
    - Tail orientation depends on orbital pericentre relative to the bar major axis.
    - Extended tails of NGC 2632 and the Hyades disfavor moderate bar pattern speeds.
  - **Position:** Galactic-structure methods entry; low direct priority for high-z work but useful as a dynamics-and-survey observable.

- [2606.06457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06457) Sergio Sanchez-Sanjuan et al. `Dynamical evolution and dissolution timescale of young stellar clusters in the Orion star-forming complex`
  - **Question:** which young Orion clusters remain bound, and which dissolve rapidly into the Galactic field?
  - **Data:**
    - Region: Orion star-forming complex.
    - Sample: `13` young clusters.
    - Measurements: Gaia DR3 astrometry, complementary radial velocities, structural parameters, and mass-function corrections.
    - Simulations: direct `N`-body integrations for `300 Myr` in a Galactic potential.
  - **Method:**
    - Derive three-dimensional velocity distributions and cluster structure.
    - Estimate present-day virial states.
    - Initialize `N`-body simulations from observed global parameters.
    - Compare cluster evolution with and without cluster-cluster interactions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - All studied clusters are supervirial.
    - Seven clusters with `alpha_vir<=7` retain bound cores for `>=170 Myr`.
    - Six clusters with `alpha_vir>=7` dissolve before `120 Myr`.
    - Cluster-cluster interactions are negligible under current Orion conditions.
    - Long-lived clusters show bound-fraction modulations linked to Galactic vertical motion and disk-crossing tidal heating.
  - **Position:** local benchmark for cluster survival and dissolution, but outside the main high-z galaxy/IGM/AGN priority lane.
