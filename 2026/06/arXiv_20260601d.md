## Executive View

The read-first paper is [2605.30513](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30513) Natalie Lam. It uses stacked JWST/NIRSpec spectra from JADES to measure the mass-metallicity relation and fundamental metallicity relation across `1.4<z<7.0`. The scale is large enough to matter: `601` star-forming galaxies, strong-line calibrations tuned to high-redshift samples, and explicit comparison against simulations.

[2605.30410](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30410) Nikko J. Cleri is the strongest nebular-physics companion. RUBIES constrains the ionization parameter for `434` galaxies at `3<z<9` and finds an increase by a factor of about `4` from `z=2` to `z=6` at fixed stellar mass and sSFR. This is directly useful for interpreting metallicity and line-ratio work in JWST samples.

The high-redshift AGN and quenching theme is unusually strong. [2605.30424](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30424) F. Valentino detects [NeV] in massive quenched galaxies at `z~1.5-4.5`, linking recently quenched systems to quasar-like accretion. [2605.30414](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30414) Ansh R. Gupta remeasures host and black-hole scaling in broad-line AGN at `z~3.5-7`. [2605.31052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31052) Angel Chandro-Gomez uses COLIBRE to connect early massive quenched galaxies to AGN feedback and overdense environments.

The early-universe and LRD entries are [2605.31077](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31077) Yangyao Chen and [2605.30436](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30436) Samantha E Hardin. The first links LRDs to super-Eddington black-hole growth during nuclear bursts. The second uses Renaissance simulations to interpret `z>10` galaxy photometry, sizes, and stellar masses.

The morphology and environment entries are useful but secondary. [2605.31415](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31415) Si-Yue Yu measures COSMOS-Web rest-frame near-IR size and surface-brightness evolution since `z=3`. [2605.31420](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31420) Sergey S. Mirzoyan extends U-Net VAE morphology classification to JWST galaxies out to `z=4`. [2605.31008](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31008) Wesley Van Kempen is lower redshift, but it is relevant as a clean environment-regulation and superstructure study.

## High-Redshift Metallicity and Nebular Conditions

- [2605.30513](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30513) Natalie Lam et al. `The JADES Mass-Metallicity and Fundamental Metallicity Relations at z>=2 Using New High-Redshift Metallicity Calibrations`
  - **Question:** how do the mass-metallicity relation and fundamental metallicity relation evolve across `1.4<z<7.0` when high-redshift metallicity calibrations are used?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: JWST Advanced Deep Extragalactic Survey.
    - Sample: `601` star-forming galaxies with JWST/NIRSpec spectra.
    - Measurements: stacked spectra binned by stellar mass, redshift, and star-forming-main-sequence offset.
  - **Method:**
    - Stack NIRSpec spectra in stellar-mass and redshift bins.
    - Derive gas-phase metallicities with updated strong-line calibrations based on high-redshift galaxies.
    - Compare MZR and FMR trends with lower-redshift observations and cosmological simulations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The MZR slope evolves weakly out to `z~5`, with `gamma~0.21+/-0.03`.
    - The MZR normalization declines smoothly at about `d log(O/H)/dz~-0.1` out to `z~4`.
    - At `z>~5`, the low-mass end keeps declining in metallicity while the high-mass end remains closer to lower-redshift relations.
    - A shallow anti-correlation between MZR residuals and SFMS offset appears by `z~5`.
  - **Position:** top daily entry because it directly supports high-z metallicity, galaxy-growth, and simulation-comparison work.

- [2605.30410](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30410) Nikko J. Cleri et al. `RUBIES: The Evolution of the Ionization Parameter from 0 < z < 9`
  - **Question:** how does the ionization parameter evolve in high-redshift galaxies when rest-frame optical emission lines are available from JWST?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: RUBIES.
    - Instrument: JWST/NIRSpec PRISM and G395M spectroscopy.
    - Sample: `434` galaxies at `3<z<9`.
    - Comparison samples: SDSS, LEGA-C, and KBSS.
  - **Method:**
    - Infer dimensionless ionization parameter `U=q/c` from O32 ratios.
    - Use Cloudy photoionization models to map line ratios to nebular conditions.
    - Build multivariate predictors using redshift, stellar mass, and sSFR.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - `U` increases with redshift and sSFR, and decreases with stellar mass.
    - At fixed stellar mass and sSFR, `U` increases by about a factor of `4` from `z=2` to `z=6`.
    - Degeneracies among photoionization models introduce a systematic floor of about `0.3 dex` in `log U`.
  - **Position:** essential companion to high-z metallicity papers because line-ratio calibrations depend on evolving nebular conditions.

- [2605.30427](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30427) Naveen A. Reddy et al. `The AURORA Survey: Multiple Balmer and Paschen Emission Lines for Individual Star-forming Galaxies at z=1.5-4.4. II. Implications for Nebular Dust Corrections, Nebular SFRs, and Differential Reddening`
  - **Question:** how do Balmer and Paschen recombination lines change nebular dust corrections and nebular SFR estimates at `z=1.5-4.4`?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: JWST/AURORA.
    - Sample: `24` galaxies at `z=1.5-4.4`.
    - Measurements: multiple HI Balmer and Paschen emission lines.
  - **Method:**
    - Derive nebular attenuation curves from multiple recombination-line ratios.
    - Compare nebular-line SFRs with SED-based SFRs.
    - Test stellar versus nebular reddening behavior.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Halpha attenuation is about `0.20 dex` larger than values from the common Balmer-decrement plus Galactic-extinction prescription.
    - Nebular-line SFRs and SED-based SFRs agree on average when MOSDEF attenuation or SMC extinction curves are used for the stellar continuum.
    - Stellar attenuation curves are steep while nebular attenuation curves are flatter, consistent with a porous medium shaped by feedback.
  - **Position:** useful calibration paper for translating JWST emission-line samples into SFR, dust, and gas-property constraints.

## High-Redshift AGN, Quenching, and Early Black Holes

- [2605.30424](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30424) F. Valentino et al. `High-ionization coronal lines trace quasar-like activity in recently quenched galaxies at high redshift`
  - **Question:** do recently quenched high-redshift galaxies host strong nuclear activity that is missed by star-formation-contaminated diagnostics?
  - **Data:**
    - Instrument: JWST/NIRSpec archival spectroscopy.
    - Parent sample: `87` massive quenched galaxies.
    - Detection sample: `6` [NeV]-detected systems at `z~1.5-4.5`.
  - **Method:**
    - Search for high-ionization [NeV] lambda3427.
    - Use broad Halpha detections to estimate black-hole masses.
    - Compare [NeV] incidence with post-starburst age indicators and theoretical models.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - [NeV] provides a clean AGN tracer because its ionization potential is about `97 eV`.
    - Broad Halpha in `4` of `6` [NeV] systems implies `M_BH=10^8.5-10^9.5 Msun`.
    - [NeV] luminosities imply `L_bol=10^45-10^46 erg/s` and Eddington ratios of about `10-50%`.
    - Strong [NeV] emitters prefer the youngest post-starburst systems with `D_n4000<~1.3`.
  - **Position:** high-priority AGN-quenching entry because it ties rapid quenching to still-active SMBH growth.

- [2605.30414](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30414) Ansh R. Gupta et al. `A Rapid Evolution in the Observed Mbh/M* Relation at z > 3 Revealed via Spectro-photometric SED-Modeling`
  - **Question:** does improved spectro-photometric SED modeling reduce the apparent elevation of `M_BH/M*` in high-redshift broad-line AGN?
  - **Data:**
    - Surveys: CEERS and RUBIES.
    - Instrument: JWST/NIRSpec PRISM and G395M spectra.
    - Sample: `39` broad-line AGN at `z~3.5-7`.
  - **Method:**
    - Decompose kinematics using higher-resolution NIRSpec/G395M spectra.
    - Fit continuum and narrow-line fluxes with BEAGLE-AGN.
    - Compare stellar-mass estimates with and without AGN line and continuum components.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Non-LRD host stellar masses are only modestly affected by AGN narrow-line-region and continuum components.
    - Non-LRD AGN at `z<3.5` are consistent with the local `M_BH/M*` relation.
    - Non-LRD AGN at `z>4.5` remain elevated, with the transition occurring over about `500 Myr`.
    - The transition is driven mainly by host stellar-mass assembly rather than by a changing black-hole-mass distribution.
  - **Position:** important early-BH entry because it tests whether the high-z black-hole scaling tension survives better host modeling.

- [2605.31052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31052) Angel Chandro-Gomez et al. `Unveiling the population of massive quenched galaxies at z>=2 in the COLIBRE simulations -- II. The role of AGN feedback and environment on their emergence`
  - **Question:** what drives massive galaxies at `z>=2` to become quenched in the COLIBRE simulations?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation suite: COLIBRE cosmological hydrodynamical simulations.
    - Target population: early massive quenched galaxies with `M_star>~10^10 Msun`.
    - Feedback variants: thermal AGN feedback and hybrid thermal-plus-jet AGN feedback.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify massive quenched galaxies in COLIBRE.
    - Compare quenching histories across AGN feedback models.
    - Link black-hole growth, gas inflow, and environment on `0.3-1.0 cMpc` scales.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - AGN feedback is the primary quenching mechanism in both modeled feedback channels.
    - Thermal feedback quenches massive galaxies efficiently at `z>3`; the hybrid model is slower because early black-hole growth is slower and jet feedback acts on longer timescales.
    - Massive quenched galaxies occupy denser environments before quenching, where gas inflow and black-hole accretion are enhanced.
    - At `z=3`, about `54%` and `20%` of simulated MQGs survive as main progenitors of `z=0` galaxies in the two models, while many undergo rejuvenation.
  - **Position:** directly relevant to the profile's environment, quenching, and AGN-feedback axis.

- [2605.31077](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31077) Yangyao Chen and Houjun Mo `Super-Eddington accretion of black holes in early nuclear bursts gives birth to Little Red Dots`
  - **Question:** can Little Red Dots arise naturally from early black holes undergoing super-Eddington accretion during nuclear bursts?
  - **Data:**
    - Framework: physically motivated black-hole seeding and growth model in a Lambda-CDM context.
    - Target class: JWST Little Red Dots at high redshift.
    - Outputs: predicted black-hole, host-galaxy, host-halo, progenitor, and descendant distributions.
  - **Method:**
    - Select predicted black holes using criteria tied to nuclear bursts.
    - Model super-Eddington growth episodes.
    - Connect observed LRDs to seed channels and descendant populations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - LRDs emerge from a subset of black holes with super-Eddington accretion during nuclear bursts.
    - Most modeled LRDs at `z~5` are seeded at `z>~20` through direct-collapse black holes or pair-instability supernovae from Pop III stars.
    - Modeled LRD black holes grow to `M_BH~10^5-10^7 Msun` by the observed epoch.
    - Descendants range from compact dwarf galaxies to brightest cluster galaxies by `z=0`.
  - **Position:** high-priority LRD theory paper because it makes testable links among seeds, bursts, hosts, and descendants.

## First Galaxies, Morphology, and Structural Evolution

- [2605.30436](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30436) Samantha E Hardin et al. `JWST Predictions for z > 10 Galaxies from the Renaissance Simulations -- I: Photometry and Sizes`
  - **Question:** how do Renaissance simulations compare with JWST observations of `z>10` galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: Renaissance high-redshift galaxy simulations.
    - Observational comparison: `14` spectroscopically confirmed JWST galaxies at `z>10`.
    - Outputs: mock photometry, stellar masses, SFRs, sizes, colors, and Sersic indices.
  - **Method:**
    - Generate physical properties and mock observations for simulated `z>10` galaxies.
    - Compare simulated stellar masses, SFRs, sizes, and colors with JWST discoveries.
    - Extend interpretation to lower-mass systems below current JWST limits.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Simulated stellar masses span about `10^3-10^8 Msun`, overlapping the low end of JWST-inferred `z>10` galaxy masses.
    - Simulated SFRs span `10^-4` to `10^1 Msun/yr`, overlapping the lower range of JWST samples.
    - Compact sizes dominate, with many half-light radii between `1` and `10 pc` and half-stellar-mass radii near `0.1 kpc`.
    - The simulations broadly track JWST high-redshift galaxy trends while extending to lower masses.
  - **Position:** useful first-galaxy simulation paper for interpreting compact JWST sources and extrapolating below current detection limits.

- [2605.31415](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31415) Si-Yue Yu et al. `COSMOS-Web: Galaxy Size and Surface Brightness Evolution at Rest-Frame 1.22 microns Since z=3`
  - **Question:** how do rest-frame near-IR galaxy sizes and surface brightnesses evolve over `0.5<=z<=3`?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: JWST COSMOS-Web.
    - Sample: `15,420` galaxies with `M_star=10^10-10^11.5 Msun`.
    - Observable: rest-frame `1.22 micron` size and surface brightness.
  - **Method:**
    - Map available NIRCam filters to rest-frame J-band effective radii.
    - Correct surface brightness for dust extinction and cosmological dimming.
    - Fit size and surface-brightness evolution for star-forming and quiescent galaxies.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Star-forming galaxies follow `R_e,J proportional to (1+z)^-0.92+/-0.04` at characteristic mass.
    - Quiescent galaxies evolve faster, with `beta=-1.34+/-0.05`.
    - Surface brightness brightens toward high redshift, with `gamma=3.07+/-0.08` for star-forming galaxies and `3.70+/-0.08` for quiescent galaxies.
    - Surface-brightness evolution reflects combined luminosity and size evolution.
  - **Position:** strong morphology and structure entry because COSMOS-Web directly supports high-z stellar-mass-distribution studies.

- [2605.31420](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31420) Sergey S. Mirzoyan and Arno Avagyan `Enhancing Galaxy Classification with U-Net Variational Autoencoders. III. Disk-like Galaxy Identification in JWST Samples of up to redshift 4`
  - **Question:** can U-Net VAE denoising and classification identify disk-like massive galaxies in JWST imaging to `z=4`?
  - **Data:**
    - Imaging: JWST galaxy samples.
    - Sample: `1,380` galaxies at `0.5<z<4` with `M_star>10^10 Msun`.
    - Output class: disk-like morphology.
  - **Method:**
    - Apply a denoising and classification pipeline based on U-Net variational autoencoders.
    - Identify disk-like systems and uncertain cases.
    - Examine disk-like incidence across redshift and stellar mass.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The classifier finds `382` disk-like galaxies in the massive high-redshift sample.
    - Disk-like structure persists across a broad redshift and mass range.
    - Massive disks may be more common in the early universe than traditional expectations imply.
  - **Position:** useful methods-plus-science entry for JWST morphology, though classifier systematics need direct visual and simulation validation.

## Environment, Large-Scale Structure, and Survey Tools

- [2605.31008](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31008) Wesley Van Kempen et al. `The Galactic Squeeze: How Aggregate and Highly Dynamical Environments Shape Star Formation in the Local Universe`
  - **Question:** how do group-scale halos, cluster-centric position, and superstructure context regulate star formation in the nearby universe?
  - **Data:**
    - Regions: Southern Galactic Pole reference volume and the Nexus region around Abell 4038.
    - Metrics: quenched fraction and sSFR for star-forming galaxies.
    - Environmental axes: stellar mass, group-scale halo mass, projected radial zone, and projected phase space.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare quenched fractions and star-forming sSFR across reference and superstructure regions.
    - Separate stellar-mass and halo-mass dependencies.
    - Use projected phase space to connect quenching with orbital history.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Quenched fraction increases with stellar mass in field and group environments.
    - Group galaxies show an additional halo-mass dependence.
    - The Nexus differs from the SGP baseline, consistent with heterogeneous accretion and pre-processing in a forming superstructure.
    - Environmental regulation is spatially nonuniform around Abell 4038.
  - **Position:** lower-redshift analog for disentangling mass, halo, and cosmic-web effects in future large-area surveys.

- [2605.30433](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30433) Cian Roche et al. `A Consistent Implementation of Cluster Strong Lensing in Cosmological Simulation Light Cones`
  - **Question:** how can cluster strong-lensing predictions include correlated and uncorrelated line-of-sight structure self-consistently?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation basis: IllustrisTNG particle data.
    - Lens regime: cluster-scale strong lensing with high-redshift source galaxies.
    - Output: light cones and simulated strong-lensing images.
  - **Method:**
    - Remap simulation volumes into a lensing-appropriate geometry.
    - Use multi-plane ray tracing through resolved structures.
    - Quantify line-of-sight perturbations to image positions and critical curves.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Uncorrelated line-of-sight structure can shift lensed-image positions by several arcseconds.
    - It introduces about `6%` scatter in primary critical-curve area.
    - It changes total critical area within `100 arcsec` by `16^{+20}_{-14}%` for `z_s=4`.
  - **Position:** relevant survey-method paper because lensing fields are central to high-z source discovery and volume interpretation.

- [2605.30500](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30500) Guangwen Chen et al. `Transformer-Based Source Detection and Morphological Classification in LOFAR Deep-Field Continuum Images`
  - **Question:** can transformer-based detection produce scalable, morphology-aware radio-source catalogs for deep radio fields?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey data: LOFAR Deep Fields at `150 MHz`.
    - Training field: ELAIS-N1.
    - Validation fields: all four LOFAR Deep Fields.
  - **Method:**
    - Adapt RF-DETR set-prediction detection to interferometric continuum images.
    - Train with five morphology classes.
    - Compare with PyBDSF and visually identified extended radio galaxies.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The model reaches `F1~91%` in the training field.
    - It transfers to other LOFAR Deep Fields with field-to-field differences tied to depth and calibration.
    - It treats multi-component radio galaxies as source-level detections rather than fragmented Gaussian components.
  - **Position:** relevant for AGN and radio-survey workflows, especially for SKA-Low-scale source finding.
