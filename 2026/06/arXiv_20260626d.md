## Executive View

The Friday listing is another SKAO-heavy day, but several entries are directly useful for high-redshift galaxy and gas work. The read-first paper is [2606.26640](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26640) Ran Wang. It argues that SKA frequency coverage above `15 GHz` is crucial for low-J CO and dense-gas tracers in high-redshift galaxies, especially as JWST keeps expanding the census of young systems.

Reionization proxy work is strong. [2606.26355](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26355) Omkar Bait connects SKA radio-continuum measurements to LyC escape, low metallicity, cosmic-ray feedback, and extreme ionization. [2606.26537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26537) Yi Hang Valerie Wong outlines how SPRITE can measure low-redshift LyC emitters at `0.16<z<0.4` as direct analogs for reionization-era sources.

The best IGM/LSS methodology entry is [2606.26234](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26234) Roger de Belsunce. It builds a field-level EFT forward model for the Ly-alpha forest and its cross-correlation with high-redshift halos, LBGs, and LAEs, with DESI and DESI-II applications. This is the most profile-aligned cross-list because it ties IGM absorption to galaxy surveys.

Environment and morphology appear in a compact set. [2606.26252](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26252) Westley Brown finds a morphology-density relation already in place at `z~1.6` in both SpARCS cluster and 3D-HST field galaxies. [2606.26264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26264) Laura DeGroot uses JWST and HST imaging to support inside-out disk formation at `1.5<=z<=3.0`. [2606.26320](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26320) Jonathan Freundlich measures depleted molecular gas in a `z~0.7` overdense group at a cosmic-web filament intersection.

AGN and feedback entries are practical rather than high-z flagship results. [2606.26305](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26305) Randall C. Dannen shows that detached high-velocity CIV absorption requires both high terminal velocity and short wind acceleration length. [2606.26813](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26813) Santanu Mondal reports tentative nuclear ultra-fast and galaxy-scale outflows in dwarf galaxy Arp 151.

## High-Redshift Gas, LyC Escape, and Reionization Proxies

- [2606.26640](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26640) Ran Wang et al. `Cm-wavelength Studies of Molecular Gas and Star Formation at High Redshift with the SKA`
  - **Question:** what SKA frequency coverage is needed to measure cold and dense molecular gas in high-redshift galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Facility context: SKA high-frequency capabilities below and beyond `~50 GHz`.
    - Tracers: redshifted low-J CO plus HCN, HCO+, HNC, and related dense-gas lines.
    - Science context: JWST-discovered young galaxies and ALMA high-J CO and far-infrared line detections.
  - **Method:**
    - Review the high-redshift molecular-gas use case for the updated SKA science book.
    - Compare ALMA high-J and far-infrared line strengths with SKA-accessible low-J transitions.
    - Identify the frequency coverage required for cold-gas and dense-gas work in early galaxies.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - SKA coverage beyond `15 GHz` is needed for low-J molecular-line studies at high redshift.
    - Low-J CO and dense-gas tracers measure the cold molecular reservoir that high-J lines alone do not capture.
    - SKA molecular-line work will complement JWST galaxy discovery by measuring the gas fueling early star formation.
  - **Position:** read first because it links high-redshift galaxy discovery to the molecular-gas measurements needed to interpret it physically.

- [2606.26355](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26355) Omkar Bait et al. `Probing the Nature of Lyman Continuum Emitting and Low-metallicity Galaxies Using the SKA`
  - **Question:** can SKA radio-continuum measurements diagnose the feedback physics that lets LyC photons escape?
  - **Data:**
    - Targets: low-redshift LyC emitters and low-mass metal-poor galaxies.
    - Frequency regime: SKA and precursor mid-frequency observations across `1-15 GHz`.
    - Forecast samples: candidate LyC emitters at `z~1-3`.
  - **Method:**
    - Use radio-continuum spectral slopes and normalization as star-formation and cosmic-ray diagnostics.
    - Compare LyC escape fraction, ionization conditions, metallicity, and SFR surface density.
    - Forecast SKA-Mid candidate surface densities for a dedicated Large Programme.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - SKA-Mid surveys can assemble `~10-100` LyC-emitter candidates per square degree.
    - The forecast SFR range is `1-100 M_sun/yr`.
    - Radio-continuum data can separate thermal and nonthermal components in extreme low-metallicity systems.
    - The measurements directly target supernova, radiative, and cosmic-ray feedback channels for LyC escape.
  - **Position:** high-priority reionization-proxy entry because it proposes a radio path to the escape-fraction problem.

- [2606.26537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26537) Yi Hang Valerie Wong et al. `Predicted Capabilities of the SPRITE SmallSat for a Low-Redshift Lyman Continuum Emission Survey`
  - **Question:** can a small far-ultraviolet mission expand direct LyC escape measurements at low redshift?
  - **Data:**
    - Mission: SPRITE SmallSat with a far-ultraviolet imaging spectrograph.
    - Target regime: star-forming galaxies at `0.16<z<0.4`.
    - Commissioning set: eight confirmed LyC emitters from the Low-redshift Lyman Continuum Survey.
  - **Method:**
    - Forecast SPRITE sensitivity for known LyC emitters.
    - Use commissioning observations to calibrate detection limits.
    - Connect low-redshift LyC measurements to high-redshift analog interpretation.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Low-redshift LyC emitters avoid the severe IGM absorption that blocks direct measurements beyond `z~3`.
    - SPRITE can validate its LyC sensitivity on known emitters before a broader survey.
    - The survey will constrain the physics of ionizing-photon escape and inform future Lyman-UV mission design.
  - **Position:** useful because direct low-z LyC measurements remain the cleanest empirical anchor for reionization analogs.

## IGM, Environment, and Galaxy Structure

- [2606.26234](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26234) Roger de Belsunce et al. `Lyman-Alpha Forest and its Cross-Correlation with High-Redshift Galaxies in Effective Field Theory at the Field Level`
  - **Question:** can a field-level EFT model jointly describe Ly-alpha forest absorption and high-redshift galaxy cross-correlations?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: AbacusSummit N-body fields with painted Ly-alpha absorption and Sherwood hydrodynamic simulations.
    - Redshift range: `z=2.0-3.2`.
    - Forecast context: DESI and DESI-II Ly-alpha forest, LBG, and LAE samples.
  - **Method:**
    - Build a perturbative forward model for the Ly-alpha forest flux decrement.
    - Validate 3D and 1D power spectra, counts-in-cells statistics, and halo cross-correlations.
    - Generate DESI clustering mocks and DESI-II galaxy-forest mocks.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The model matches simulated 3D power spectra at `1%` to `k<=0.3 h/Mpc`.
    - It matches 1D power spectra at `5%` to `k<=1.0 h/Mpc`.
    - Halo cross-correlations and counts-in-cells statistics are accurately recovered down to `2 Mpc/h`.
    - Field-level phenomenological flux-power models fail on quasi-linear scales.
  - **Position:** the strongest IGM/LSS entry because it directly supports joint Ly-alpha forest and high-redshift galaxy analyses.

- [2606.26252](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26252) Westley Brown et al. `Cluster vs Field: Clear Evidence for a Morphology-Density Relation in All Environments at z~1.6`
  - **Question:** does the morphology-density relation at `z~1.6` depend on cluster-specific processes?
  - **Data:**
    - Cluster sample: `111` SpARCS cluster galaxies.
    - Field sample: `458` 3D-HST galaxies.
    - Measurements: multiband photometry, spectroscopy, local density, and F160W morphology.
  - **Method:**
    - Classify galaxies by Sersic index into disk-like, bulge-like, and intermediate categories.
    - Compare morphology with stellar mass and local density in cluster and field samples.
    - Test whether cluster membership alone explains morphology-density trends.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The fraction of bulge-like galaxies rises with local density in both cluster and field environments.
    - Median Sersic index increases with local density in both samples.
    - Most galaxies remain disk-like until `M_*>10^10.25 M_sun` in clusters or `M_*>10^10.8 M_sun` in the field.
    - The morphology-density relation is already in place at `z~1.6` and may not be cluster-specific.
  - **Position:** important environment paper because it separates local density from global cluster membership.

- [2606.26264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26264) Laura DeGroot et al. `Quantifying the inside-out formation of disk galaxies at 1.5 <= z <= 3.0`
  - **Question:** do JWST-resolution rest-frame optical sizes support inside-out disk growth at cosmic noon?
  - **Data:**
    - Field: Extended Groth Strip.
    - Redshift range: `1.5<=z<=3.0`.
    - Imaging: JWST rest-frame optical and HST rest-frame UV data.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure UV and optical half-light radii at comparable spatial resolution.
    - Compare size differences with dust attenuation, stellar mass, SFR, and morphology.
    - Inspect clumpy systems separately from smoother disks.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - UV sizes are on average larger than optical sizes across the sample.
    - The size difference persists after accounting for dust attenuation.
    - Most clumpy galaxies with `n<0.5` have larger UV sizes because star formation is distributed across clumps or mergers.
    - JWST improves the rest-optical size-mass relation relative to lower-resolution HST-only work.
  - **Position:** useful structural-evolution result because it makes inside-out growth measurable with matched UV and optical resolution.

- [2606.26320](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26320) Jonathan Freundlich et al. `Probing the molecular gas content of galaxies in an over-dense group at z~0.7: a test case for environmental quenching`
  - **Question:** how strongly can a dense group environment suppress molecular gas before cluster assembly?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: COSMOS-Gr30 at `z~0.7`.
    - Instruments: IRAM NOEMA and 30m CO(2-1) observations plus MUSE context.
    - Environment: a dense group at a cosmic-web filament intersection with a `~10^4 kpc^2` ionized gas structure.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure CO detections and upper limits for spectroscopic group members.
    - Stack molecular-gas constraints across the group.
    - Compare gas fractions with field main-sequence scaling relations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Four group galaxies are detected in CO at `S/N>5`.
    - Average molecular-gas content is reduced by `~0.5 dex` relative to field scaling relations.
    - Gas fractions are only `20%-40%` of typical main-sequence values.
    - The extended ionized structure has `M_gas<2x10^10 M_sun`, so less than one third is in a cold star-forming phase.
  - **Position:** relevant environment case because it catches molecular-gas depletion in a group linked to larger-scale filaments.

## AGN Feedback, HI, and SKAO Survey Infrastructure

- [2606.26305](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26305) Randall C. Dannen et al. `Wind Acceleration as a Driver of Detached Blueshifted Absorption in Quasar Disk Winds`
  - **Question:** what wind structure produces extremely detached blueshifted CIV absorption in quasar spectra?
  - **Data:**
    - Models: biconical quasar disk winds with different acceleration lengths.
    - Observable: CIV absorption in extremely high-velocity outflow quasars.
    - Velocity scale: blueshifted absorption reaching `~0.2c`.
  - **Method:**
    - Generate synthetic spectra across viewing angles.
    - Vary the wind acceleration length `R_v`.
    - Include continuum-source size, attenuation, scattering, reprocessing, and emission with Monte Carlo radiative transfer.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Detached highly blueshifted CIV absorption requires both high terminal velocity and small `R_v`.
    - Larger `R_v` models produce broader, less detached troughs even at high terminal velocity.
    - Viewing angle selects the wind region, but internal acceleration structure controls absorption morphology.
  - **Position:** useful AGN-wind diagnostic because absorption profiles can constrain acceleration, not only orientation.

- [2606.26999](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26999) Jing Wang et al. `HI Galaxy Science with the SKA`
  - **Question:** what HI survey strategy best connects galaxy gas accretion, consumption, removal, and feedback?
  - **Data:**
    - Facility: SKA HI surveys under AA* and AA4 development.
    - Targets: sub-kpc nearby HI morphology, HI mass-function evolution, and `z>1` atomic-gas balance.
    - Context: multiwavelength southern-hemisphere surveys expected in `2028+`.
  - **Method:**
    - Update SKA HI science goals and survey tiers.
    - Connect spatially resolved nearby work to higher-redshift statistical work.
    - Frame HI measurements around baryon-cycle processes.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Key goals include complete nearby HI morphology and kinematics at sub-kpc and `1 km/s` resolution.
    - SKA should measure cosmic HI mass density and HI mass-function evolution at least to `z~1`.
    - The high-redshift goal is the balance between cold molecular and cool atomic gas at `z>1`.
  - **Position:** important infrastructure entry because HI is the neutral-gas backbone for cosmic baryon-cycle work.

- [2606.27026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27026) Fatemeh S. Tabatabaei et al. `Unveiling the roles of thermal and nonthermal processes in the ISM & IGM structure formation and evolution of galaxies with SKAO`
  - **Question:** how can SKAO separate thermal and nonthermal gas processes in galaxies across cosmic time?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: radio continuum and HI emission from local galaxies shifted to higher redshift.
    - Facilities: SKA pathfinders and SKAO AA4.
    - Targets: M51- and NGC 6946-like analogs.
  - **Method:**
    - Use radio continuum to trace thermal and nonthermal emission.
    - Use HI to trace gas content.
    - Forecast detectability of nearby-galaxy analogs at higher redshift.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - AA4 surveys can trace continuum emission from M51-like and NGC 6946-like analogs beyond cosmic noon at `z=2-3`.
    - HI gas content can be traced beyond `z=1`.
    - Magnetic fields and cosmic rays can slow massive-star formation and help launch winds.
    - Nonthermal feedback is likely important at cosmic noon.
  - **Position:** useful survey forecast because it links radio observables to physical ISM/IGM processes rather than only source counts.

- [2606.26773](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26773) M. Giulietti et al. `Semi-empirical Predictions for Ultra-deep Radio Counts of Star-forming Galaxies with the SKAO`
  - **Question:** how much of the cosmic star-formation-rate density can SKA radio surveys recover?
  - **Data:**
    - Model: SEMPER semi-empirical radio-galaxy framework.
    - Inputs: redshift-dependent stellar mass functions, main sequence, and IR/radio correlation.
    - Forecast: SKA-Mid Band 2 number counts at `1.4 GHz`.
  - **Method:**
    - Extend SEMPER with evolving starburst fractions.
    - Compute differential number counts for lensed and unlensed star-forming galaxies.
    - Forecast radio-traced SFR density to `z~10`.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - SKA surveys will probe faint flux regimes dominated by star-forming galaxies.
    - The model predicts a substantial population of massive dust-obscured galaxies at early epochs.
    - Less than `20 hr` of SKA-Mid Band 2 can recover at least `~20%` of the SEMPER total SFR density.
    - The recovered population includes optically/NIR-dark systems to `z~6`.
  - **Position:** relevant for high-z surveys because radio counts bypass dust and optical selection losses.

- [2606.26813](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26813) Santanu Mondal et al. `Feeding and Feedback in Dwarf Galaxies (FeeD) -- I. Evidence of nuclear ultra-fast and galaxy-scale outflows in the dwarf galaxy Arp 151`
  - **Question:** can low-mass galaxies launch both nuclear ultra-fast and galaxy-scale AGN outflows?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: dwarf galaxy Arp 151.
    - Instruments: NuSTAR X-ray observations and MaNGA integral-field spectroscopy.
    - Tracers: nuclear ultra-fast outflow and optical galaxy-scale outflow.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit NuSTAR spectra for a fast nuclear outflow signature.
    - Use MaNGA data to identify and characterize optical outflowing gas.
    - Compare kinetic coupling between the nuclear and galaxy-scale components.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - NuSTAR gives tentative `~2 sigma` evidence for a nuclear outflow at `~0.18c`.
    - The optical galaxy-scale outflow has mass outflow rate `~0.43 M_sun/yr`.
    - The nuclear outflow rate is `~0.015 M_sun/yr`.
    - The two components are consistent with a momentum-conserving phase.
  - **Position:** lower-priority for high-z work but useful because AGN feedback may operate even in dwarf hosts.
