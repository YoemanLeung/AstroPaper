## Executive View

The Tuesday listing is strong for reionization-field mapping, high-redshift quasar cold gas, JWST structural catalogs, obscured high-redshift galaxies, and multiscale AGN feedback.

The read-first paper is [2606.14959](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14959) Xiangyu Jin. It uses JWST/NIRCam and NIRSpec in the `z=6.5412` quasar field J0226+0302 to connect line-emitting galaxies, AGN, and IGM transmission during reionization. The field adds `65` line-emitting galaxies at `5.3<z<6.4`, measures an IGM-galaxy cross-correlation excess at `~10-40 cMpc`, and identifies four broad-Halpha AGN among `49` NIRSpec-observed emitters.

The top high-redshift quasar-ISM paper is [2606.16012](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16012) Claudia Maria Pierro. New ALMA Band 3 CO(6-5) and `~100 GHz` continuum observations of ten HYPERION `z>6` QSOs show heterogeneous molecular gas reservoirs, dust temperatures, gas-to-dust ratios, and feedback signatures. The result is useful because it breaks the assumption that rapid SMBH growth, star formation, gas consumption, and enrichment are synchronized.

JWST survey infrastructure is unusually strong. [2606.14869](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14869) Lilan Yang releases COSMOS-Web morphology measurements for `~780,000` galaxies across four NIRCam bands. [2606.16853](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16853) Pralay Biswas uses JWST/UNCOVER and MegaScience imaging in Abell 2744 to find `113` `z>2` HST-dark galaxies and a lower-redshift highly extincted low-mass analog population.

AGN feedback is anchored by [2606.16967](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16967) Mayank Sharma, which combines HST/COS absorption, VLT/KMOS IFU spectroscopy, and SDSS data for a `z=0.9655` quasar to trace an outflow from `~9 pc` to beyond `8 kpc`. The data show deceleration from about `-3800 km/s` to `-1000 km/s` while maintaining broadly consistent momentum flux across scales.

The remaining useful entries are mostly AGN demographics, type-2 variability, stellar-feedback modeling, DESI and Euclid morphology catalogs, and lower-redshift dwarf or Milky Way assembly work. They are lower priority for immediate high-redshift galaxy/LSS/IGM reading but useful as methods and comparison background.

## Reionization, High-Redshift Quasars, and Early Gas

- [2606.14959](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14959) Xiangyu Jin et al. `Probing Direct Contributions of Galaxies and AGN to Cosmic Reionization in a Quasar Field J0226+0302 with JWST NIRCam and NIRSpec`
  - **Question:** how do galaxies and AGN in a `z=6.5412` quasar field contribute to the local ionizing background during reionization?
  - **Data:**
    - Field: quasar field J0226+0302 at `z=6.5412`.
    - Instruments: JWST Cycle 2 NIRCam and NIRSpec, building on ASPIRE observations.
    - Sample: `65` additional line-emitting galaxies at `5.3<z<6.4`; `49` line emitters observed with NIRSpec.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify line-emitting galaxies in the quasar field.
    - Measure the IGM-galaxy cross-correlation function.
    - Compare the correlation signal with THESAN simulations.
    - Identify broad-Halpha AGN and measure IGM transmission around them.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - IGM transmission shows a `~2 sigma` excess at `~10-40 cMpc` from galaxies relative to the average IGM transmission.
    - The IGM-galaxy correlation is consistent with THESAN models with neutral fraction `5%-7%` and average galaxy `f_esc=6%`.
    - Four broad-Halpha AGN are found among `49` NIRSpec-observed line emitters, giving an AGN fraction of `(8+/-4)%`.
    - The AGN environment shows higher IGM transmission within `5 cMpc/h` than most [OIII] emitters, implying AGN `f_esc=50%-100%`.
  - **Position:** read first because it directly connects galaxy overdensities, AGN, and IGM transmission in a reionization-era quasar field.

- [2606.16012](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16012) Claudia Maria Pierro et al. `HYPERION. The cold ISM of rapidly growing z>6 quasars: diverse gas reservoirs, dust enrichment, and feedback signatures`
  - **Question:** how diverse are the cold ISM, dust, star formation, and feedback properties of rapidly growing `z>6` QSO hosts?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: ten HYPERION QSOs at `z>6`.
    - Instrument: new ALMA Band 3 observations of CO(6-5) and `~100 GHz` continuum.
    - Ancillary data: archival and literature ALMA/NOEMA measurements.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure CO(6-5) and continuum detections.
    - Infer molecular gas masses and dust properties.
    - Combine gas and dust masses to estimate gas-to-dust ratios.
    - Compare dust temperature, X-ray photon index, and C IV velocity shift.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - `~100 GHz` continuum is detected in eight targets.
    - CO(6-5) is detected in four QSO hosts and one companion, with molecular gas masses of order `10^10 M_sun`.
    - Non-detections imply upper limits of a few `10^9 M_sun`, indicating broad gas-reservoir diversity.
    - J083+11 has gas-to-dust ratio `16^{+5}_{-4}`, among the lowest measured in a high-redshift QSO host.
    - HYPERION QSOs show that SMBH growth, star formation, gas consumption, enrichment, and feedback need not be synchronized.
  - **Position:** top quasar-ISM entry because it turns luminous `z>6` QSO hosts into a heterogeneous cold-gas population rather than a single evolutionary phase.

- [2606.16853](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16853) Pralay Biswas et al. `Optically Invisible Galaxies at Cosmic Noon and beyond with JWST/UNCOVER`
  - **Question:** what high-redshift galaxy population is missed by rest-frame-UV-selected surveys and recovered by JWST near-infrared imaging?
  - **Data:**
    - Fields: JWST UNCOVER and MegaScience imaging of Abell 2744.
    - Filters: `20` JWST/NIRCam and `7` HST filters.
    - Sample: `113` HST-dark galaxies at `z>2` and `94` low-redshift analogs at `z<2`.
  - **Method:**
    - Select HST-dark galaxies with a red `1.50-3.56 micron` color criterion.
    - Fit multiwavelength photometry to derive stellar masses, dust extinction, sizes, and SFRs.
    - Use gravitational magnification and ultradeep JWST imaging to push to low stellar masses.
    - Compare the high-redshift sample with low-redshift highly extincted low-mass analogs.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - JWST detects HST-dark `z>2` galaxies down to stellar mass `10^7.5 M_sun`.
    - The population is moderately dusty, compact, and follows the star-forming main sequence.
    - The inferred star-formation-rate density peaks at `z~4.5`.
    - The `z<2` analogs have median `log M_*/M_sun~7.10` and median `A_V~1.97 mag`.
  - **Position:** important census paper because JWST recovers a low-mass, dust-obscured high-redshift population missed by optical selection.

## JWST, DESI, and Survey-Scale Structure

- [2606.14869](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14869) Lilan Yang et al. `COSMOS-Web: A Multi-wavelength Morphological Catalog of ~780,000 Galaxies`
  - **Question:** how do galaxy structures and morphology indicators evolve across COSMOS-Web when measured consistently in multiple JWST bands?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: COSMOS-Web.
    - Catalog: `~780,000` galaxies from COSMOS2025.
    - Imaging: four independent JWST/NIRCam bands.
  - **Method:**
    - Perform single- and double-Sersic parametric modeling.
    - Measure non-parametric morphology indicators, including Gini-`M20`, asymmetry, and concentration.
    - Compare structure with star-formation activity, stellar mass, and redshift.
    - Cross-check non-parametric classifications against Sersic-index-based classifications.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Sersic index and bulge-to-total ratio correlate strongly with position on the SFR-stellar-mass plane up to `z~4`.
    - Massive galaxies with `M_*>10^10.5 M_sun` evolve from median `n_sersic~1` at `z~6` to `~2.5` at `z~2`.
    - Lower-mass galaxies remain near `n_sersic~1.2` over the same interval.
    - UV Sersic indices are systematically smaller than optical values but follow similar evolutionary trends.
  - **Position:** top survey-product paper because COSMOS-Web morphology now supports structural-transformation and galaxy-SMBH coevolution studies at JWST scale.

- [2606.16507](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16507) Ashley Spindler et al. `Deep Learning Segmentation of Spiral Arms and Bars for 600,000 Galaxies in DESI`
  - **Question:** can deep learning produce public pixel-level arm and bar maps for large photometric galaxy samples?
  - **Data:**
    - Imaging: DESI Legacy Survey.
    - Catalog: `639,636` galaxies.
    - Training source: Galaxy Zoo: 3D pixel masks and Galaxy Zoo: DESI classifications.
  - **Method:**
    - Train a U-net-style neural network named ZooBot:3D.
    - Produce soft segmentation maps for spiral arms and bars.
    - Validate performance across face-on disks.
    - Cross-match products to MaNGA and SAMI IFU samples.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The public catalog maps spiral arms and bars for more than `600,000` galaxies.
    - The network identifies rings despite limited ring examples in the training set.
    - The products enable arm/bar-aware photometric and IFU studies.
  - **Position:** useful morphology infrastructure because arm and bar masks can turn survey images into spatially resolved structural priors.

- [2606.16734](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16734) Alexander A. Marchuk et al. `The Edge-on Galaxies in the DESI survey (EGIDE): sample building and photometry`
  - **Question:** how can DESI Legacy imaging support a homogeneous large catalog of edge-on galaxy candidates?
  - **Data:**
    - Imaging: DESI Legacy Imaging Survey DR10.
    - Catalog: `149,215` edge-on galaxy candidates.
    - Redshift coverage: redshift values for `98%` of the sample.
  - **Method:**
    - Use Galaxy Zoo annotations and a fine-tuned Zoobot model to select edge-on systems.
    - Apply manual supervision for quality control.
    - Derive homogeneous SExtractor `griz` photometry, stellar masses, SFRs, and ancillary properties.
    - Compare blue-sequence and red-cloud edge-on populations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - EGIDE is about ten times larger than its predecessor and covers more than half the sky.
    - Redder edge-on systems decline more quickly with increasing axis ratio than bluer systems.
    - Red-sequence galaxies are thicker than blue-cloud galaxies.
    - Flattening ratio increases with stellar mass at the high-mass end in redder systems.
  - **Position:** lower-redshift but useful because it provides a large controlled geometry sample for dust, disks, and structural systematics.

## AGN Growth, Feedback, and Variability

- [2606.16967](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16967) Mayank Sharma et al. `Mapping a Quasar Outflow from Parsec to Kiloparsec Scales: A Combined HST Absorption and VLT Emission Investigation`
  - **Question:** can a single quasar outflow be traced continuously from nuclear absorption scales to galactic emission-line scales?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: PKS J0352-0711 at `z=0.9655`.
    - Instruments: VLT/KMOS IFU spectroscopy, HST/COS absorption spectroscopy, and SDSS spectroscopy.
    - Tracers: mini-BAL absorption systems and [OIII] emission.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine emission-line maps with absorption-line outflow properties.
    - Compare outflow velocities and radii across parsec, hundred-parsec, and kiloparsec scales.
    - Test whether momentum flux remains coherent across scales.
    - Interpret velocity changes as mass loading and breakout.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The inner wind is traced in absorption at `~9 pc` and in emission at `>40 pc` with velocity `~-3800 km/s`.
    - An intermediate component near `~500 pc` decelerates to `~-2100 km/s`.
    - A wide-angle blueshifted outflow extends beyond `8 kpc` at about `-1000 km/s`.
    - Momentum fluxes remain consistent within uncertainties across three orders of magnitude in scale.
  - **Position:** strong AGN-feedback entry because it links the nuclear wind and galaxy-scale outflow in one system instead of comparing unmatched samples.

- [2606.15365](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15365) Rafael Ortiz III et al. `PEARLS: NuSTAR and XMM-Newton Extragalactic Survey of the JWST North Ecliptic Pole Time Domain Field VI: Multiwavelength SED Analysis`
  - **Question:** how do X-ray-selected AGN SFRs and black-hole accretion rates relate to host-galaxy growth in the JWST North Ecliptic Pole field?
  - **Data:**
    - Field: JWST North Ecliptic Pole Time Domain Field.
    - Sample: `261` X-ray sources to `z~5`.
    - Observatories: NuSTAR, XMM-Newton, and multiwavelength photometry.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit multiwavelength SEDs for X-ray sources.
    - Place hosts relative to the star-forming main sequence.
    - Compare SFR, BHAR, X-ray luminosity, and specific AGN luminosity.
    - Separate low-mass growth-spurt systems from high-mass maintenance-mode systems.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - SFR relative to the main sequence correlates with specific AGN luminosity at `rho=+0.73`.
    - X-ray luminosity correlates with SFR at `rho=+0.80`.
    - A star-forming and X-ray-luminous cold-quasar population indicates short-timescale accretion episodes.
    - Low-mass galaxies show BHARs above population averages, while high-mass SMBHs accrete closer to maintenance mode.
  - **Position:** useful AGN-galaxy coevolution paper because X-ray selection recovers short accretion phases hidden by standard AGN labels.

- [2606.16528](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16528) Kaizheng Guo et al. `The Narrow-Line Seyfert 1 Phenomenon: Accretion State Versus Host Galaxy Properties`
  - **Question:** are narrow-line Seyfert 1 galaxies a distinct host population or the high-accretion end of a continuous Type 1 AGN distribution?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `~12,000` SDSS Type 1 AGN.
    - Subsample: `767` host-matched NLS1-BLS1 pairs.
    - Measurements: CIGALE host and AGN properties.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare NLS1 and BLS1 host masses, black-hole masses, Eddington ratios, SFRs, and continua.
    - Examine the FWHM(Hbeta)-AGN-luminosity plane.
    - Match hosts by stellar mass, black-hole mass, and redshift.
    - Test whether accretion-state differences remain after host matching.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - NLS1s globally have lower black-hole masses, higher Eddington ratios, lower stellar masses, and higher sSFRs than BLS1s.
    - The `2000 km/s` FWHM boundary is better treated as an empirical division inside a continuous space.
    - Host-matched NLS1s still show higher Eddington ratios, stronger Fe II, bluer continua, elevated SFR, and higher dust attenuation.
  - **Position:** useful low-redshift AGN benchmark because it separates accretion state from host-galaxy demographics.

## Feedback and Emission Modeling Tools

- [2606.15184](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15184) Anand Utsav Kapoor et al. `TODDLERS 2.0: Stellar feedback and observables across diverse IMFs, binary populations, and cloud environments`
  - **Question:** how do IMF choice, binaries, cloud structure, and dust physics alter stellar-feedback observables in star-forming regions?
  - **Data:**
    - Tool: TODDLERS 2.0.
    - Stellar-population inputs: pySTARBURST99 and BPASS.
    - Emission modeling: Cloudy post-processing.
  - **Method:**
    - Extend 1D shell dynamics to broader stellar populations and cloud conditions.
    - Include arbitrary IMFs, upper-mass limits to `500 M_sun` or `300 M_sun`, binary evolution, and stochastic IMF sampling.
    - Add non-uniform cloud density profiles and dynamic cloud-density evolution.
    - Include modified grain size distributions and diffuse ionized gas.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Centrally concentrated density profiles fragment earlier.
    - Constant star formation delays fragmentation relative to instantaneous bursts.
    - Top-heavy IMFs produce stronger feedback and earlier fragmentation than a Kroupa IMF.
    - Dynamic cloud density evolution can reduce unswept cloud density by up to three orders of magnitude at low metallicity.
    - Low-mass clusters with `M_*<~10^4 M_sun` show order-of-magnitude stochastic feedback variations.
  - **Position:** useful modeling infrastructure because high-redshift nebular and feedback interpretations depend on stellar-population and birth-cloud assumptions.

- [2606.14946](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14946) Tejahni Desire et al. `A Comparative Study of Isothermal Turbulence Statistics: Fourier Space Driving vs. Point Source Driving`
  - **Question:** can the turbulence driving parameter `b` uniquely diagnose the energy-injection mode in interstellar turbulence?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: isothermal hydrodynamic AthenaK boxes.
    - Driving modes: Fourier-space driving and point-source driving.
    - Diagnostics: density PDFs, velocity statistics, power spectra, and Mach-number measures.
  - **Method:**
    - Match Mach numbers between Fourier-space and point-source driving runs.
    - Compare morphology, density and velocity statistics, and density-Mach correlations.
    - Test sensitivity to forcing correlation time.
    - Recompute `b` with mass-weighted Mach numbers and excluded bubble interiors.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Point-source driving yields `b=0.33-0.49` despite injecting compressive radial momentum.
    - Mass-weighted estimates reach only `b_M=0.74-0.79`, still below the expected `b~1`.
    - Varying forcing correlation time changes `b` by more than a factor of `3` for compressive driving.
    - `b` is degenerate with injection locality and temporal correlation.
  - **Position:** useful methods paper because turbulence diagnostics feed directly into ISM and CGM interpretation.

## Additional Useful Entries

- [2606.15016](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15016) J. Ceron-Meneses et al. `Seeing through the dust: Unraveling near-infrared variability in type 2 active galactic nuclei`
  - **Question:** can long-baseline near-infrared variability identify hidden or misclassified AGN components in type 2 systems?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: UltraVISTA DR6.
    - Baseline: `14` years of multiepoch `YJHKs` data.
    - Sample: `533` type 2 AGN in COSMOS.
  - **Method:**
    - Build calibrated NIR light curves.
    - Model variability with damped random walks.
    - Classify variables by wavelength-dependent DRW amplitude.
    - Compare NIR variability with X-ray obscuration classes.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - `7%-17%` of type 2 AGN are NIR-variable, with higher fractions toward `Ks`.
    - About one third of X-ray-unobscured type 2 AGN vary in the NIR, consistent with weak or misclassified type 1 AGN.
    - X-ray-obscured type 2 AGN show NIR variability in `21.4%` of cases.
  - **Position:** useful COSMOS AGN-classification background for separating obscuration, variability, and weak type 1 contamination.

- [2606.14852](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14852) Stefano Torniamenti et al. `Intermediate-mass black hole seeding in galactic nuclei from star cluster migration`
  - **Question:** can migrating star clusters seed galactic nuclei with intermediate-mass black holes by `z~6`?
  - **Data:**
    - Model: inSpyral semi-analytic cluster-migration model.
    - Galaxy context: star cluster populations drawn from L-Galaxies 2020.
    - Host regime: galaxies with `M_star,gal<~5x10^10 M_sun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Model dynamical-friction inspiral of massive clusters.
    - Follow black-hole mergers inside migrating clusters.
    - Include effects of cluster compactness and relativistic kicks.
    - Compare predicted merger remnants with high-mass gravitational-wave events.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Massive clusters can seed galactic nuclei with IMBHs as early as `z~6`.
    - Migrating-cluster merger products reach `~300 M_sun`, five times above the in-situ formation limit.
    - Sub-parsec cluster radii can produce IMBHs above `10^4 M_sun`.
  - **Position:** relevant to early black-hole seeding, though it is more gravitational-wave and nuclear-cluster focused than high-redshift galaxy survey focused.
