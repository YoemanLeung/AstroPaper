## Executive View

The read-first paper is [2606.03753](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03753) Luke Conaboy. It directly targets the connection between [OIII]-emitting galaxies and Lyalpha forest transmission at `z~6`, using empirical halo-to-galaxy modeling and mock JWST survey geometry. The key caution is that current samples have enough survey scatter to allow broad ionizing-source models, even when the observed one-dimensional correlation peak is offset from the mocks by about `10 cMpc`.

The LRD anchor is [2606.03522](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03522) Makoto Ando. It analyzes rest-UV spectra and sizes for `~100` JWST-selected LRDs and finds redder UV slopes, compact UV emission, strong Fe II relative to Mg II, and a downturn around Lyalpha. This is a high-value complement to recent optical LRD papers because it tests whether the host galaxy alone can explain the UV side of the SED.

The high-redshift quasar paper is [2606.03037](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03037) Weizhe Liu. Aether JWST/NIRSpec IFU observations place a `z~6.2` quasar on the local `M_BH-sigma_*` relation, detect a kiloparsec-scale outflow, and argue that the outflow rate exceeds the current SFR by a large factor. This is directly relevant to early SMBH-host coevolution and quenching.

The cosmic-dawn feedback entry is [2606.02711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02711) Olof Nebrin. First multidimensional Lyalpha radiation-hydrodynamics simulations find Lyalpha pressure can dominate pre-supernova feedback in dense, metal-poor gas, with force multipliers `M_F~10-60`. This matters for first-galaxy simulations and any reionization model that currently omits Lyalpha pressure.

The local statistical anchor for black-hole demographics is [2606.02699](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02699) Ragadeepika Pucha. DESI DR1 yields a very large dwarf-AGN census and a broad-line AGN sample that extends `M_BH-M_*` down to low masses. It is low-redshift, but it gives a baseline for interpreting overmassive high-redshift black holes and LRD descendants.

## Galaxy-IGM Coupling and Cosmic-Dawn Feedback

- [2606.03753](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03753) Luke Conaboy et al. `The impact of source and survey modelling on the connection between [O III] emitters and Ly alpha forest transmission at z ~ 6`
  - **Question:** how strongly can current JWST [OIII]-emitter samples constrain the connection between galaxies and Lyalpha forest transmission at `z~6`?
  - **Data:**
    - Observations: recent measurements of [OIII]-emitter clustering and galaxy-Lyalpha transmission cross-correlations.
    - Models: empirical halo-to-galaxy assignment for observed [OIII] emitters.
    - Survey geometry: mock JWST catalogues with survey depth and geometry included.
  - **Method:**
    - Construct mock [OIII]-emitter surveys from numerical models.
    - Compare one- and two-dimensional galaxy-Lyalpha transmission cross-correlations against the data.
    - Test how source modeling and sample variance affect the apparent galaxy-IGM signal.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Mock surveys can match the observational data statistically because the current survey scatter is large.
    - The peak of the modeled one-dimensional cross-correlation occurs at a scale about `10 cMpc` below the observed peak.
    - Present galaxy-IGM samples have limited power to rule out broad ionizing-source models.
    - Larger samples and boxes larger than `250 cMpc` are needed for stronger inference.
  - **Position:** top daily entry because it sits exactly at the galaxy-IGM interface and tests how robust current `z~6` environmental interpretations are.

- [2606.02711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02711) Olof Nebrin et al. `Lyman-alpha Pressure Strongly Enhances Pre-Supernova Feedback at Cosmic Dawn: The First Multi-Dimensional Lyman-alpha Radiation Hydrodynamics Simulations`
  - **Question:** does Lyalpha radiation pressure dynamically dominate feedback before supernovae in dense, metal-poor cosmic-dawn environments?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation code: Lydion, using an M1 moment method for Lyalpha transfer.
    - Targets: star clusters and isolated stars in dense, metal-poor clouds.
    - Metallicity regime: `Z/Z_sun<=0.01`.
  - **Method:**
    - Run the first two-dimensional Lyalpha radiation-hydrodynamics simulations.
    - Include self-consistent dust dynamics.
    - Compare Lyalpha pressure with direct and infrared radiation pressure.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The method gives an order-`100` speed-up over Monte Carlo transfer in simple benchmarks.
    - Lyalpha feedback strongly boosts outflows in dense, metal-poor clouds.
    - Radiative forces reach about `(2-16) L_bol/c`.
    - Lyalpha force multipliers reach `M_F~10-60`.
    - Simulations that omit Lyalpha pressure may miss the strongest radiation-pressure channel in dense metal-poor gas.
  - **Position:** high-priority cosmic-dawn methods paper because it changes the feedback budget before the first supernovae.

- [2606.02720](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02720) A. Bolamperti et al. `A Cosmic Archipelago of lensed metal-poor galaxies at z~6`
  - **Question:** what can strongly lensed metal-poor galaxies at `z~6` reveal about chemically young systems near reionization?
  - **Data:**
    - Target class: lensed metal-poor galaxies.
    - Redshift: `z~6`.
    - Observables: rest-frame optical emission-line properties and lensing-enabled faint-galaxy access.
  - **Method:**
    - Use lensing magnification to study intrinsically faint, metal-poor galaxies.
    - Classify the systems through emission-line and photometric diagnostics.
    - Place the sample in the context of reionization-era low-metallicity galaxies.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Lensed systems provide access to faint metal-poor galaxies that are difficult to observe in blank fields.
    - Such objects are useful analogs or constituents of the ionizing-source population near reionization.
    - The paper is a strong target list for follow-up spectroscopy and source-plane reconstruction.
  - **Position:** relevant because it combines reionization-era galaxies, metallicity, and lensing selection.

## Little Red Dots and Early Black-Hole Growth

- [2606.03522](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03522) Makoto Ando et al. `The UV Side of Little Red Dots: Red, Compact, and Iron-Enhanced Rest-UV Emission with a Strong Downturn around Lyalpha`
  - **Question:** can the rest-UV emission of LRDs be explained by ordinary host-galaxy star formation?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `~100` LRDs selected from the JWST spectral archive.
    - Measurements: UV continuum slopes, emission-line strengths, Fe II and Mg II features, and UV morphology.
    - Comparison set: star-forming galaxies at similar redshifts and UV magnitudes.
  - **Method:**
    - Stack spectra to measure UV continuum and line trends.
    - Compare UV sizes and slopes against normal star-forming galaxies.
    - Model whether host-galaxy emission alone can reproduce the observed red continuum.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - LRDs have systematically redder UV slopes and more compact UV sizes than comparable star-forming galaxies.
    - Balmer-break strength, UV slope, Lyalpha downturn depth, and Fe II equivalent width are positively correlated.
    - UV size anticorrelates with Balmer-break strength.
    - Fe II/Mg II is about `8-10`, higher than in quasars at similar redshift.
    - The red UV continuum requires an additional red compact component with roughly `beta_UV~0`.
  - **Position:** top LRD paper because it adds rest-UV spectral and morphological constraints to the optical/Balmer-line interpretation problem.

- [2606.03375](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03375) Matias Liempi et al. `Little Red Dot progenitors from Compact Starbursts: A Natural Path to Early AGN Formation`
  - **Question:** can compact starbursts naturally create LRD progenitors and early AGN?
  - **Data:**
    - Target class: compact starbursts that can precede LRD or early-AGN phases.
    - Context: early black-hole formation in dense, gas-rich systems.
  - **Method:**
    - Model the physical path from compact starburst conditions to black-hole growth.
    - Connect dense stellar and gas configurations to early AGN formation.
    - Interpret the resulting phase as an LRD progenitor channel.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Compact starbursts are proposed as a natural route to early AGN formation.
    - The paper strengthens the link between dense nuclear star formation and the LRD phenomenon.
    - It should be read alongside the UV-side constraints from [2606.03522](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03522) Makoto Ando.
  - **Position:** useful theory companion because LRD models need channels that connect stellar density, gas supply, and early accretion.

- [2606.02773](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02773) Chenxuan Zhang et al. `Why Little Red Dots Disappear at z < 3: Evolution of Number Density and Halo Mass`
  - **Question:** what number-density and halo-mass evolution explains the disappearance of LRDs below `z<3`?
  - **Data:**
    - Target class: LRD populations across redshift.
    - Quantities: number density and host halo mass.
  - **Method:**
    - Track LRD abundance evolution with redshift.
    - Connect observed LRD demographics to halo-mass evolution.
    - Interpret why the LRD phase declines at lower redshift.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - LRD disappearance is framed as an evolution problem in both abundance and halo context.
    - The result is directly useful for linking JWST LRD samples to descendant systems.
    - The paper is lower priority than [2606.03522](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03522) Makoto Ando because the listing does not expose the same level of spectral detail.
  - **Position:** relevant demographic entry for turning LRDs from a selection class into an evolutionary phase.

## High-Redshift Quasars, AGN, and Dust

- [2606.03037](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03037) Weizhe Liu et al. `A z ~ 6.2 Quasar on the Local M_BH-sigma_* Relation Quenching Its Host Galaxy from the Aether Survey`
  - **Question:** can a luminous quasar host at `z~6.2` already lie on the local black-hole--host relation while quenching through feedback?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: quasar J1512+4422 at `z~6.2`.
    - Instrument: JWST/NIRSpec integral-field spectroscopy from the Aether survey.
    - Epoch: about `900 Myr` after the Big Bang.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure black-hole mass and stellar velocity dispersion.
    - Map nuclear outflow kinematics and projected extent.
    - Compare outflow rate and kinetic power with SFR and quasar luminosity.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The quasar has `M_BH~8.9 x 10^8 Msun` and `sigma_*~288 km/s`.
    - The nuclear outflow reaches about `478 km/s` and likely extends to `~3.2 kpc`.
    - The outflow dynamical time is about `9 Myr`.
    - The mass outflow rate is `92.6 Msun/yr`, far above the reported current SFR.
    - The kinetic energy outflow rate is about `0.6%` of quasar luminosity, meeting a common negative-feedback threshold.
  - **Position:** daily highlight because it links early SMBH-host scaling relations, feedback, and the emergence of massive quenched galaxies.

- [2606.02685](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02685) Mingyu Li et al. `A Steep-Extinction QSO at z=4.6: JWST Evidence for Abundant Small Dust Grains`
  - **Question:** what does steep extinction in a `z=4.6` quasar imply about early dust production and grain-size distributions?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: a QSO at `z=4.6`.
    - Instrument: JWST spectroscopy or photometry reported in the listing title.
    - Observable: steep extinction consistent with abundant small dust grains.
  - **Method:**
    - Model the quasar continuum and extinction behavior.
    - Interpret the dust curve in terms of grain-size distribution.
    - Compare the result with standard quasar or galaxy dust assumptions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The quasar shows evidence for a steep extinction law.
    - Small dust grains are likely abundant in the system.
    - The result is relevant for interpreting high-redshift QSO continua, UV colors, and inferred intrinsic luminosities.
  - **Position:** important for high-z AGN work because dust physics can bias luminosity, reddening, and host-galaxy inferences.

- [2606.03592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03592) Sude Baltaci et al. `Varstrometry for Off-nucleus and Dual sub-Kpc AGN (VODKA): Radio Classification of High-Redshift Dual AGN Candidates with the Very Large Array`
  - **Question:** can VLA radio classification validate high-redshift off-nucleus or dual sub-kpc AGN candidates selected by varstrometry?
  - **Data:**
    - Program: VODKA.
    - Instrument: Very Large Array radio observations.
    - Target class: high-redshift dual or off-nucleus AGN candidates.
  - **Method:**
    - Classify radio counterparts to candidate dual AGN systems.
    - Use radio morphology or compactness to evaluate AGN interpretations.
    - Test varstrometry as a route to sub-kpc AGN pair discovery.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The paper provides a needed follow-up step for astrometric dual-AGN candidates.
    - Radio classification can separate true AGN activity from ambiguous optical/IR offsets.
    - The method is relevant to merger-triggered growth and future high-z dual-AGN searches.
  - **Position:** profile-relevant because dual AGN connect mergers, accretion, and high-redshift structure growth.

## Galaxy Structure, Quenching, and Survey-Scale Methods

- [2606.02699](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02699) Ragadeepika Pucha et al. `A New Record Census of Dwarf AGN and a Bimodal M_BH-M_* Scaling Relation with DESI DR1`
  - **Question:** what does DESI DR1 imply for AGN incidence and black-hole scaling relations in low-mass galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: DESI DR1 spectroscopy.
    - Parent sample: `1,678,787` low-redshift line-emitting galaxies.
    - Redshift range: `0.001<=z<=0.45`.
    - Broad-line sample: `17,949` broad-line AGN candidates with broad Halpha.
  - **Method:**
    - Select AGN with [NII]-BPT emission-line ratios.
    - Estimate black-hole masses from broad Halpha using single-epoch virial methods.
    - Extend the `M_BH-M_*` relation into the dwarf-galaxy regime.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - AGN are identified in `314,245/1,211,573` high-mass line emitters.
    - AGN are identified in `9,648/467,214` dwarf line emitters.
    - The AGN fraction rises from `~1.4%` at the low-mass end to `~93.3%` at the high-mass end.
    - The broad-line sample reaches `log(M_*/Msun)~7.8` and `log(M_BH/Msun)~4.4`.
    - The released EmFit catalog contains emission-line decompositions for `~7.4 million` galaxies.
  - **Position:** low-redshift but useful because high-z overmassive black-hole claims need a robust local low-mass baseline.

- [2606.02698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02698) Sam E. Cutler et al. `Unbreaking the Universe: MINERVA Measurements of Color Gradients in Massive Quiescent Galaxies Can Help Ease Too-Early Star Formation Tensions`
  - **Question:** can color gradients in massive quiescent galaxies reduce apparent tensions in early star-formation histories?
  - **Data:**
    - Program: MINERVA.
    - Target class: massive quiescent galaxies.
    - Observables: spatial color gradients and inferred stellar-population structure.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure color gradients in massive quiescent systems.
    - Assess how gradients affect stellar-population and formation-time inference.
    - Connect spatially resolved structure to early-formation tension.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Color gradients can change the interpretation of early massive quiescent galaxy formation.
    - Spatially unresolved measurements may overstate or misframe early star-formation tension.
    - The paper is useful for interpreting compact quiescent systems and post-starburst descendants at high redshift.
  - **Position:** relevant because massive quenched galaxies at high redshift are a recurring bridge between early assembly, AGN feedback, and environment.

- [2606.03224](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03224) Vahid Asadi and Najmeh Sheikhi `COSMOS2025: Machine Learning Classification of Early- and Late-type Galaxies at 0 < z < 3`
  - **Question:** can broadband colors classify early- and late-type galaxies in COSMOS2025 without image labels or structural fitting?
  - **Data:**
    - Catalog: COSMOS2025.
    - Application sample: `44,132` galaxies at `0<z<3`.
    - Training basis: Santa Cruz semi-analytic model labels using bulge-to-total mass ratio and sSFR.
  - **Method:**
    - Inject COSMOS2025-like photometric noise into simulated galaxies.
    - Train a CatBoost classifier on `66` broadband colors.
    - Validate against independent bulge+disk decompositions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Simulated-domain performance reaches `98%` precision/recall for late types.
    - Early-type simulated performance reaches `91%` precision and `88%` recall.
    - Only about `6%` of COSMOS2025 galaxies have intermediate probabilities.
    - Independent structural validation gives `70%` overall accuracy.
    - The full pipeline runs in under `30 min` on standard hardware.
  - **Position:** useful survey-methods paper for scalable morphology in COSMOS and next-generation wide surveys.
