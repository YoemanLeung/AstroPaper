## Executive View

The Thursday listing is strongest for JWST-era galaxy diagnostics rather than direct cosmic-web mapping. The read-first paper is [2606.11345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11345) Yuki Isobe, which uses deep JWST/NIRSpec spectra to recalibrate high-redshift metallicity measurements across `z=1-10`, identifies extremely metal-poor galaxies to `z=9.1`, and flags two LRDs in metal-poor environments.

The Little Red Dot entry is also high value. [2606.12355](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12355) Rosa M. Merida tests the BH-star model on `66` LRDs at `2<z<6` with UV-to-optical NIRSpec PRISM spectra. The result is a useful caution: broad priors make pure BH-star interpretations rare, while priors that suppress AGN continua can force many more BH-star-like solutions.

High-redshift quenching and obscured black-hole growth form the second anchor. [2606.12156](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12156) Katherine E. Whitaker and Rachel Bezanson provide an ARA&A synthesis of massive-galaxy quenching pathways. [2606.12076](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12076) Angel Ruiz measures the Compton-thick AGN luminosity function at `3<z<6` and finds a stable CTK fraction after IR-informed obscuration priors.

The best gas-regulation papers are observational. [2606.11444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11444) B. A. Westoby maps molecular gas and dust in `z~3` dusty star-forming galaxies with ALMA and JWST. [2606.12249](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12249) Peter J. Watson uses JWST/NIRISS to calibrate spatially resolved nebular-stellar reddening at `1.0<z<2.4`, explicitly connecting the result to future Roman grism work. [2606.11506](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11506) Sanchayeeta Borthakur is a lower-redshift but profile-relevant HST white paper for disk-CGM interface spectroscopy.

The method and morphology set is useful but secondary. [2606.11308](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11308) Benedict Van den Bussche uses a VAE on pop-cosmos SPS parameters to show that five dimensions capture the main rest-optical SED degrees of freedom. [2606.11328](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11328) G. Scarpe builds a large `z>3` quiescent-galaxy morphology and size sample. [2606.11315](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11315) Vicki Kuhn measures JWST spiral-arm pitch angles to `z~3.5`.

## High-Redshift Metallicity, LRDs, and Early Black Holes

- [2606.11345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11345) Yuki Isobe et al. `JADES: the mass-metallicity relation at z=1-10. New calibrations, extremely metal-poor galaxies, and chemical diversity`
  - **Question:** how do high-redshift metallicity calibrations change the mass-metallicity relation and the census of extremely metal-poor galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Spectroscopy: deep JWST/NIRSpec spectra from JADES full data release, Dark Horse, and OASIS.
    - Sample: stacked spectra for `~1500` star-forming galaxies at `z=1-10`.
    - Calibration feature: [OIII] `lambda4363` auroral-line detections down to `12+log(O/H)=7.0`.
    - EMPG candidates: `50` objects with `12+log(O/H)=6.7-7.3` at `z=1.2-9.1`.
  - **Method:**
    - Stack medium-resolution NIRSpec spectra by galaxy properties.
    - Derive strong-line calibrations over `12+log(O/H)=7.0-8.7`.
    - Compare stack-based calibrations with individual high-`z` auroral-line emitters.
    - Use the calibrations to infer MZRs and identify EMPG candidates.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Stack-based [OIII]/Hbeta and [OIII]/[OII] ratios are lower than calibrations based only on individual auroral-line emitters.
    - Metallicities decrease from `z~0` to `z~4-10` without a strong slope change.
    - EMPG candidates span `1-4%` solar metallicity.
    - Low-metallicity EMPGs often have lower sSFRs, opposite to the local FMR expectation.
    - Two EMPG candidates are LRDs with broad Halpha and prominent Lyalpha.
  - **Position:** read first because it links JWST metallicity calibration, stochastic low-mass galaxy growth, LRDs, and early black-hole environments.

- [2606.12355](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12355) Rosa M. Merida et al. `Between Degeneracy and Evolution: UV-to-optical Insights into the BH* Model in Little Red Dots`
  - **Question:** can the BH-star model explain the UV-to-optical continua of the LRD population?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `66` LRDs at `2<z<6`.
    - Spectroscopy: JWST/NIRSpec PRISM UV-to-optical continuum coverage.
    - Model ingredients: blackbody emission affected by Balmer absorption, stellar and nebular emission, dust attenuation, and AGN continuum.
    - Robust-fit subset: `52` objects with statistically robust solutions.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit LRD continua with a modified Bagpipes framework.
    - Use broad priors that do not impose a preferred physical scenario.
    - Repeat fits with a prior that suppresses strong AGN continua.
    - Compare optical and UV dominance by blackbody, stellar, and AGN components.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - With broad priors, only `~6%` of robust LRDs prefer a BH-star optical component plus UV host galaxy.
    - `~8%` show blackbody-dominated optical continua without a clear stellar component or with AGN UV leakage.
    - Most LRDs are dominated by stellar and/or AGN optical emission with minor blackbody contribution.
    - Suppressing strong AGN continua raises BH-star-like solutions to `~40%`.
    - Enforced BH-star solutions often still require stellar-dominated optical continua.
  - **Position:** important LRD model-selection paper because it separates data-driven support for BH-star solutions from prior-driven degeneracy.

- [2606.12076](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12076) Angel Ruiz et al. `X-ray luminosity function of Compton-thick AGN in the early Universe (z > 3). Robustness and biases of the CTK population`
  - **Question:** how abundant are Compton-thick AGN at `z>3` after correcting X-ray obscuration estimates with infrared information?
  - **Data:**
    - Parent sample: `811` high-redshift X-ray-selected AGN from XMM-Newton XXL-N, Chandra CCLS, and CDF-S/N.
    - CTK candidates: ten high-probability sources selected through X-ray spectral fitting.
    - Redshift range: `3<z<6` for the CTK luminosity-function inference.
    - Additional constraints: mid-infrared photometry and SED modeling.
  - **Method:**
    - Test CTK candidate robustness with multiwavelength SED fits.
    - Compare inferred X-ray luminosities with IR luminosities.
    - Update column-density posteriors using IR-informed priors.
    - Recompute the CTK AGN XLF with revised obscuration probabilities.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Only three of the ten initial high-probability candidates remain consistent with the CTK regime after IR-informed priors.
    - IR priors reduce inferred CTK number densities.
    - CTK AGN constitute `17%` of the total AGN population at `3<z<6`.
    - The CTK fraction shows no significant evolution up to `z~6`.
    - The broader obscured fraction with `N_H>10^23 cm^-2` increases toward high redshift.
  - **Position:** high-priority obscured-growth entry because it constrains early SMBH demographics while exposing selection bias in CTK classifications.

## Quenching, Dust, and Resolved Gas Regulation

- [2606.12156](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12156) Katherine E. Whitaker and Rachel Bezanson `Quenching of Star Formation in Massive Galaxies`
  - **Question:** what physical pathways trigger and maintain quiescence in massive galaxies across cosmic time?
  - **Data:**
    - Review scope: massive quiescent galaxies and their evolving stellar, gas, dust, and dynamical properties.
    - Literature basis: empirical color selections, sSFR thresholds, stellar-population constraints, gas reservoirs, dust reservoirs, and kinematic measurements.
    - Early-quiescent focus: massive systems with rapid formation histories and high metallicities.
  - **Method:**
    - Synthesize observational evidence for identifying quiescent systems.
    - Compare stellar-population and chemical-enrichment histories.
    - Organize gas, dust, and outflow observations into quenching pathways.
    - Separate rapid ejective and slower preventative or exhaustion modes.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Evolving sSFR thresholds provide a more physical quiescent/star-forming split than fixed color selections.
    - Early massive quiescent galaxies often require rapid formation and enriched stellar populations.
    - Gas and dust observations show both fast ejective and slow regulatory modes.
    - Central density is established in situ, while later minor mergers reshape massive quiescent galaxies.
    - Two broad quenching modes are rapid SMBH-outflow shutdown and slower gas exhaustion, virial heating, or preventative feedback.
  - **Position:** useful synthesis paper for interpreting high-`z` quiescent samples, dusty reservoirs, and feedback-driven shutdown.

- [2606.11444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11444) B. A. Westoby et al. `Investigating the role of turbulence in the interstellar medium in z~3 dusty star-forming galaxies using kpc-resolution ALMA dust and gas maps`
  - **Question:** how do molecular gas, dust-obscured star formation, and turbulence regulate star formation in `z~3` dusty star-forming galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Targets: three `z~3` ALESS submillimetre-selected dusty galaxies.
    - ALMA: CO(5-4), CO(4-3), and `870 micron` continuum data.
    - Spatial resolution: `~0.25 arcsec`, about `2 kpc`.
    - Imaging context: JWST/NIRCam and MIRI from ALESS-JWST.
  - **Method:**
    - Map molecular gas, dust continuum, and stellar light on comparable spatial scales.
    - Confirm redshift association for two interacting sources.
    - Fit rotating-disk models to the highest-S/N kinematics.
    - Compare kpc-scale star-formation scaling relations with turbulence-dependent models.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Molecular gas broadly follows dust-obscured star-forming structures.
    - Gas reservoirs are more extended than dust emission and comparable to rest-near-infrared stellar light.
    - The two best sources are consistent with rotating disks with `Vmax/sigma=5+-1` and `6+-1`.
    - The kpc-scale data are offset from star-formation relations without turbulence.
    - Gravo-turbulent models better match the resolved scaling relations.
  - **Position:** strong gas-regulation paper because it combines ALMA and JWST to test high-redshift star-formation physics on resolved scales.

- [2606.12249](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12249) Peter J. Watson et al. `Spatially Resolved Nebular-Stellar Reddening with JWST/NIRISS`
  - **Question:** how does resolved nebular reddening relate to stellar-continuum reddening, stellar mass, and SFR at cosmic noon?
  - **Data:**
    - Instrument: JWST/NIRISS from GLASS-JWST ERS.
    - Sample: `99` galaxies at `1.0<z<2.4`.
    - Stellar-mass range: `7.0<log10(M*/M_sun)<10.5`.
    - Observables: sub-kpc Halpha and Hbeta emission-line maps.
  - **Method:**
    - Extract multiregion Balmer-line maps.
    - Measure Halpha/Hbeta Balmer decrements.
    - Compare nebular and continuum reddening.
    - Fit relations with SFR, stellar mass, and SFR surface density.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The Balmer-decrement to stellar-mass relation matches local trends.
    - There is no measurable redshift difference within `1.0<z<2.4`.
    - Attenuation is negligible below `log10(M*/M_sun)~8.5`.
    - The best-fit relation is `E(B-V)_SED=(0.46+-0.02)E(B-V)_neb`.
    - The resolved calibration can support attenuation corrections for Roman grism studies where Balmer lines are inaccessible.
  - **Position:** high-priority methods bridge because it connects JWST resolved spectroscopy to future Roman high-redshift survey interpretation.

- [2606.11506](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11506) Sanchayeeta Borthakur et al. `Revealing Cosmic Ecosystems with the Hubble Space Telescope in 2030s and Beyond`
  - **Question:** what CGM science remains uniquely dependent on HST ultraviolet spectroscopy in the 2030s?
  - **Data:**
    - Facility: HST ultraviolet spectroscopy.
    - Radial focus: disk-CGM interface around `20 kpc`.
    - Diagnostics: H I, O VI, C II-IV, Si II-IV, N V, Ne VIII, and related metal transitions.
    - Science context: gas inflow, recycling, feedback, metallicity, ionization, and kinematics.
  - **Method:**
    - Define ultraviolet absorption-line observations required for CGM circulation.
    - Combine CGM diagnostics with host-galaxy stellar-population and feedback measurements.
    - Frame HST as the only currently comprehensive UV pathway for this measurement space.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - UV spectroscopy remains the direct route to multiphase CGM diagnostics inaccessible from the ground.
    - Disk-CGM interface measurements can connect column density, density, metallicity, ionization, and kinematics.
    - Host outflow velocities and mass loading are needed to interpret gas circulation.
    - The 2030s HST program would preserve critical low-redshift CGM calibration for galaxy-growth studies.
  - **Position:** relevant white paper because low-redshift CGM absorption calibrates the gas-regulation physics used to interpret higher-redshift systems.

## Morphology, Structure, and SED Inference

- [2606.11308](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11308) Benedict Van den Bussche et al. `pop-cosmos: Disentangling galaxy properties from observables using data-driven approaches`
  - **Question:** how many independent physical degrees of freedom are encoded in rest-frame optical galaxy SEDs?
  - **Data:**
    - Model: pop-cosmos generative galaxy population model.
    - Inputs: `16` SPS parameters.
    - Observable space: rest-frame optical SED features.
    - Inference tool: beta-variational autoencoder latent representation.
  - **Method:**
    - Compress SPS descriptions into a disentangled latent space.
    - Interpret latent dimensions through mutual information.
    - Tie each dimension to spectral features.
    - Compare latent drivers with standard nebular-model assumptions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Five independent dimensions capture the main SED information.
    - The dimensions correspond to stellar mass, recent star formation, dust, and two gas-ionization degrees of freedom.
    - Stellar metallicity and stellar age are not primary independent drivers in this representation.
    - The decomposition recovers gas physical conditions more cleanly than standard line-ratio diagnostics.
  - **Position:** useful inference paper because it targets the degeneracies that affect stellar mass, dust, metallicity, and gas diagnostics in high-redshift SED work.

- [2606.11328](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11328) G. Scarpe et al. `Morphology, sizes, and scatter in a large sample of distant quiescent galaxies`
  - **Question:** what sets the size and morphology scatter of massive quiescent galaxies at `z>3`?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `137` massive quiescent candidates across `825 sq. arcmin`.
    - Redshift range: `3<z<7`.
    - Imaging: multiwavelength JWST-field photometry in five bands.
    - Measurements: Sersic sizes, concentrations, ellipticities, axis ratios, colors, and environmental terms.
  - **Method:**
    - Model surface-brightness profiles with Sersic fits across UV-to-NIR rest-frame wavelengths.
    - Derive size-mass relations and wavelength-dependent structural parameters.
    - Use forward/backward Bayesian fitting to test secondary predictors of size.
    - Compare size trends with redshift, mass, filter, and environment.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The size-mass relation is consistent with previous studies and extends to `z_spec=4.9`.
    - Size decreases with increasing redshift.
    - Size also decreases toward longer observed wavelength.
    - Stellar mass and redshift predict size better than axis ratio, Sersic index, UVJ color, or environment.
    - The large intrinsic size scatter requires additional physical variables.
  - **Position:** relevant morphology entry because it expands the early quiescent population where environment, merger history, and internal structure remain degenerate.

- [2606.11315](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11315) Vicki Kuhn et al. `Spiral arms across cosmic time: JWST measurements of the pitch angles of spiral galaxies at z<3.5`
  - **Question:** how do spiral-arm pitch angles evolve and correlate with internal galaxy properties out to `z~3.5`?
  - **Data:**
    - Imaging: JWST CEERS and JADES.
    - Sample: `593` spiral galaxies with `M*>10^10 M_sun`.
    - Redshift range: `z<3.5`.
    - Measurements: Zoobot classifications and SpArcFiRe pitch-angle fits.
  - **Method:**
    - Fine-tune a Zoobot deep-learning model to identify spiral galaxies.
    - Measure spiral arms and pitch angles with SpArcFiRe.
    - Test correlations with stellar mass, bulge mass, disk mass, sSFR, and tidal strength.
    - Compare behavior above and below `z~1`.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Mean pitch angle shows no significant redshift evolution for the full sample.
    - The most massive systems show mild winding with time.
    - At `z>1.25`, pitch angle does not correlate strongly with tested internal properties.
    - At `z<1.25`, pitch angle weakly anti-correlates with stellar, bulge, and disk mass.
    - A transition near `z~1` separates locally driven spiral structure from structure regulated by the global gravitational potential.
  - **Position:** useful high-redshift morphology paper because it uses JWST to test when ordered disk structure becomes tied to galaxy-scale mass assembly.

- [2606.11313](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11313) Haotian Lyu et al. `Comparison and verification methods to trace interaction-driven disturbances in galaxies`
  - **Question:** which automated method better identifies merger-driven low-surface-brightness disturbances?
  - **Data:**
    - Visual reference: high-confidence disturbance fraction of `25.1+-1.5%`.
    - Methods: self-supervised learning and Concentration-Asymmetry-Smoothness.
    - Targets: galaxies with tidal tails, streams, shells, and other merger signatures.
    - Survey context: LSST-scale image volumes.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare automated disturbance flags with visual classifications.
    - Measure recall and contamination for SSL.
    - Test the classical CAS threshold `A>0.35`.
    - Evaluate stellar-mass trends in disturbance fraction.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - SSL reaches recall `0.86+-0.04` with contamination `0.2`.
    - CAS has higher precision `0.77` but low recall `0.20`.
    - SSL captures a broader disturbed population, including faint tidal debris.
    - Visual and SSL classifications show a significant positive mass-disturbance correlation.
    - CAS produces a weaker mass trend because it selects a cleaner but incomplete sample.
  - **Position:** lower-priority but useful method paper for merger-environment studies and future wide-field disturbance catalogs.

## Lower-Priority AGN, Local-Galaxy, and Calibration Entries

- [2606.11900](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11900) Xiaopeng Cheng detects a compact two-sided jet in the nearly dormant SMBH of M60 at an Eddington ratio of `~10^-8`. The paper is valuable for jet-launching physics but is a low-redshift accretion laboratory rather than a direct high-`z` profile match.

- [2606.11378](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11378) Brenna Mockler models wind-reprocessed TDE light curves and finds an optical peak delayed by `~3 weeks` relative to the bolometric peak. This is mainly relevant as a super-Eddington reprocessing analog.

- [2606.11394](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11394) Paola Marziani discusses Eigenvector-1/main-sequence diagnostics for super-Eddington candidates among jetted and gamma-ray AGN. It is conceptually relevant to rapid accretion but less data-rich than the main AGN entries.

- [2606.11939](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11939) Chao Li reports a fading parsec-scale radio ejecta from a dwarf-galaxy massive black hole candidate. The result is useful for episodic IMBH accretion searches.

- [2606.12137](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12137) Andrew J. Benson builds a unified halo mass function across dark-matter models from simulations spanning `10^6-10^16 M_sun`. It is a useful modeling ingredient for small-scale structure and environment inference.

- [2606.11587](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11587) Zenghua Zhou recalibrates SDSS photometric zero-points with the IRFM temperature scale. The result is a survey-calibration entry with indirect relevance to large photometric samples.

- [2606.11594](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11594) Zenghua Zhou presents SDSS color-temperature relations from GALAH and APOGEE IRFM temperatures. It is mainly stellar-calibration infrastructure.

- [2606.11872](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11872) Alistair H. Nelson compares Milky Way gas and stellar rotation curves and argues that gas rotation is not a direct circular-velocity tracer. This is near-field dynamics, not core profile science.

- [2606.11323](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11323) Iryna S. Butsky and [2606.11359](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11359) Minjie Lei focus on phase-dependent magnetic structure in the local ISM. They are methodologically interesting for multiphase gas but outside the main extragalactic high-redshift lane.
