## Executive View

The week is strongest for reionization-era sources, high-redshift environments, and gas diagnostics. The central pattern is that early galaxies, compact sources, AGN, and large-scale structure are being tied directly to their gas environments: LyC escape, IGM transmission, protocluster fate, ram-pressure stripping, dense gas, Ly-alpha morphology, and photoionization-rate modeling all appear in the same week.

The weekly read-first paper is [2606.14959](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14959) Xiangyu Jin. It uses JWST/NIRCam and NIRSpec in the `z=6.5412` quasar field J0226+0302 to connect `65` line-emitting galaxies, broad-Halpha AGN, and IGM transmission at `5.3<z<6.4`. It is the best single bridge between galaxies, AGN, and reionization-era large-scale environment.

The main simulation-calibrated reionization source paper is [2606.14671](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14671) Zebedee Summerfield. THESAN-ZOOM links LyC escape to burstiness and gas clearing across `z=3-16`, while [2606.17571](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17571) Peter Ziwei Hu asks how often galaxy populations at `z~13` produce large ionized bubbles. Friday adds [2606.19449](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19449) Christopher Cain, a fast analytic bridge from reionization history to the Ly-alpha forest photoionization rate.

High-redshift environment work is unusually direct. [2606.18229](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18229) Dazhi Zhou reports extreme ram-pressure stripping in a `z=4.30` protocluster galaxy. [2606.19463](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19463) Seunghwan Lim shows that `z>=5` protocluster candidates have severe fate scatter unless the surrounding `10 cMpc` mass distribution is measured deeply. [2606.19443](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19443) Hannah Jhee adds the cosmic-web view by tracking evolving filaments and their effect on halo accretion and spin.

Compact high-redshift source work continues from the prior week. [2606.17271](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17271) Andrea Weibel selects `241` BH-star-dominated Little Red Dot candidates, [2606.18342](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18342) Zhiyuan Ji resolves a bipolar kpc-scale structure around an LRD at `z=5.482`, and [2606.18334](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18334) Vadim Rusakov interprets `76` nitrogen-enhanced JWST galaxies as short-lived post-starburst systems.

Gas diagnostics are the practical anchor. [2606.16012](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16012) Claudia Maria Pierro measures diverse cold-ISM reservoirs in `z>6` HYPERION QSOs. [2606.19282](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19282) Jianhang Chen reports dense gas in BX610 at `z=2.21`. [2606.17270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17270) Caitlin M. Casey shows UV/optical attenuation underpredicts IR luminosity by `~3x` for stacked COSMOS-Web galaxies across `0.5<z<7`.

Survey and modeling infrastructure is broad: COSMOS-Web morphology for `~780,000` galaxies, CANUCS/Technicolor structural parameters for `~41,000` galaxies, SPHEREx forced photometry for clusters, DESI DR2 ELG imaging-systematics weights, and simulation-trained SED fitting.

## Reionization Sources, IGM Transmission, and Ionized Bubbles

- [2606.14959](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14959) Xiangyu Jin et al. `Probing Direct Contributions of Galaxies and AGN to Cosmic Reionization in a Quasar Field J0226+0302 with JWST NIRCam and NIRSpec`
  - **Question:** how do galaxies and AGN in a `z=6.5412` quasar field affect local IGM transmission during reionization?
  - **Data:**
    - Field: J0226+0302 around a `z=6.5412` quasar.
    - Instruments: JWST/NIRCam and NIRSpec, with ASPIRE context.
    - Sample: `65` added line-emitting galaxies at `5.3<z<6.4`; `49` line emitters observed with NIRSpec.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify line-emitting galaxies and broad-Halpha AGN.
    - Measure the IGM-galaxy cross-correlation.
    - Compare the transmission signal with THESAN simulations.
    - Estimate AGN and galaxy escape-fraction implications.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - IGM transmission has a `~2 sigma` excess at `~10-40 cMpc` from galaxies.
    - The signal is consistent with THESAN models with neutral fraction `5%-7%` and average galaxy `f_esc=6%`.
    - Four broad-Halpha AGN among `49` NIRSpec-observed emitters imply AGN fraction `(8+/-4)%`.
    - The AGN environment shows higher IGM transmission within `5 cMpc/h`, implying AGN `f_esc=50%-100%`.
  - **Position:** weekly read-first because it directly joins galaxies, AGN, overdensity, and IGM transmission in one reionization-era field.

- [2606.14671](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14671) Zebedee Summerfield et al. `From THESAN-ZOOM to JWST: Predicting ionizing photon escape and the rise of UV-bright reionization sources`
  - **Question:** which galaxy properties predict LyC escape and escaped ionizing emissivity during reionization?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: more than `35,000` THESAN-ZOOM galaxy realizations.
    - Redshift range: `z=3-16`.
    - Predictors: intrinsic simulation diagnostics and JWST-observable photometric quantities.
  - **Method:**
    - Train random-forest regressors for escape fraction and escaped ionizing photon rate.
    - Compare full-physics and JWST-observable predictor sets.
    - Combine UV luminosity functions with inferred `N_ion,esc-M_UV` relations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - `SFR_10/SFR_100` and `M_gas/M_*` are the strongest `f_esc` diagnostics.
    - Rest-frame `1500 A` UV magnitude dominates escaped ionizing photon-rate prediction.
    - UV-bright galaxies with `M_UV<-17` dominate after `z~8`.
    - The inferred reionization histories avoid an ionizing-photon-budget crisis.
  - **Position:** essential weekly theory anchor because it maps JWST-observable galaxy properties onto reionization emissivity.

- [2606.17571](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17571) Peter Ziwei Hu et al. `The Incidence of Large Ionized Bubbles at Redshift 13`
  - **Question:** how common are galaxy-generated ionized bubbles large enough to explain early Ly-alpha transmission at `z~13`?
  - **Data:**
    - Inputs: JWST UV luminosity functions.
    - Benchmark: a Witstok-sized Ly-alpha ionized region.
    - Bubble threshold: `R>=2.5 cMpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Model ionized-bubble incidence around galaxy populations.
    - Convert model incidence into surface density per `Delta z=1`.
    - Test whether ordinary galaxy populations can explain the required regions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The fiducial surface density is `1.33e-2 arcmin^-2` per `Delta z=1`.
    - Large ionized regions at `z~13` are plausible but not guaranteed.
    - Individual cases may need high ionizing efficiency, burstiness, fading, or non-stellar ionizing photons.
  - **Position:** important because single `z~13` Ly-alpha detections need population-level bubble statistics.

- [2606.19449](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19449) Christopher Cain et al. `A self-consistent analytical model for both the photoionization rate and reionization history`
  - **Question:** can fast inference use both reionization history and Ly-alpha forest photoionization-rate constraints?
  - **Data:**
    - Observables: `x_i(z)` and `Gamma_HI(z)`.
    - Constraint regime: Ly-alpha forest transmission at `5<=z<=6`.
    - Validation: radiative-transfer simulations.
  - **Method:**
    - Derive a cosmological-radiative-transfer analytic formalism.
    - Predict `x_i` and `Gamma_HI` self-consistently.
    - Compare model outputs to detailed simulations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The model is percent-level accurate in `x_i`.
    - The model is `20-30%` accurate in `Gamma_HI` at `z<=6`.
    - Photoionization-rate evolution is sensitive to galaxy ionizing output and the reionization endpoint.
  - **Position:** key Friday addition because `Gamma_HI` gives the tail of reionization a quantitative link to high-redshift quasar and galaxy spectra.

## Protoclusters, Cosmic Web, and Environmental Quenching

- [2606.18229](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18229) Dazhi Zhou et al. `An extreme ram-pressure stripping event in a protocluster at redshift 4.3`
  - **Question:** can dense protocluster gas strip a massive galaxy strongly enough to trigger environmental quenching at `z>4`?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: SPT2349-56-C26 at `z=4.30`.
    - Instruments: ALMA and JWST.
    - Observable: offset cold-gas and stellar morphology.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare cold-gas emission with the stellar body.
    - Measure gas displacement and infer stripping geometry.
    - Interpret the event in the context of dense protocluster-core dynamics.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - More than half of the observed cold-gas reservoir is displaced from the galaxy.
    - The gas-emission peak is offset by `6 kpc` from the stellar body.
    - Ram-pressure stripping can remove cold gas in a massive protocluster galaxy at `z=4.3`.
  - **Position:** top environment result of the week because it makes environmental quenching observable before mature cluster assembly.

- [2606.19463](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19463) Seunghwan Lim `On the later evolution of observationally selected protocluster candidates at z greater than about 5`
  - **Question:** how reliably do observed `z>=5` protocluster candidates map to final cluster descendants?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: FLAMINGO with protocluster cores resolved to `z~10`.
    - Selection: three simulated selections compared with observational samples.
    - Environment scale: neighbors and mass within `10 cMpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Track selected candidates forward to `z=0`.
    - Measure nearby candidate clustering.
    - Quantify later merger probabilities and final-mass scatter.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Current selections include both true cluster progenitors and interlopers.
    - Candidates have `2-10` neighbors within `10 cMpc`.
    - A neighbor at `5 cMpc` gives later merger probability `>=50%`.
    - Reliable fate prediction needs deep total galaxy mass within `10 cMpc`.
  - **Position:** complements Zhou by showing that high-redshift overdensity needs fate calibration, not only discovery.

- [2606.19443](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19443) Hannah Jhee et al. `The impact of evolving cosmic filaments on mass and spin evolution of dark matter halos`
  - **Question:** how do evolving filaments modulate halo accretion and spin?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: cosmological N-body data with DisPerSE filaments.
    - Structures: individual `z=0` filaments traced backward through progenitors.
    - Halo sample: major-merger-screened systems.
  - **Method:**
    - Track filament drift, density profiles, splashback radii, and core overdensities.
    - Re-express halo trajectories in evolving filament-centric coordinates.
    - Compare mass-accretion and spin-alignment histories.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Halo mass accretion is suppressed near high-density filament outskirts.
    - Halo spin alignment departs from stochastic random-walk behavior.
    - Static filament models bias environmental interpretation.
  - **Position:** useful for LSS readers because it treats cosmic-web environment as time-dependent dynamics around halos.

## High-Redshift Gas, AGN, and Compact Sources

- [2606.16012](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16012) Claudia Maria Pierro et al. `HYPERION. The cold ISM of rapidly growing z>6 quasars`
  - **Question:** how diverse are the cold gas, dust, and feedback properties of rapidly growing `z>6` QSO hosts?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: ten HYPERION QSOs at `z>6`.
    - Instrument: ALMA Band 3 CO(6-5) and `~100 GHz` continuum.
    - Ancillary data: ALMA and NOEMA literature measurements.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure CO(6-5) and continuum detections.
    - Infer molecular-gas and dust masses.
    - Compare gas-to-dust ratios, dust temperature, X-ray photon index, and C IV velocity shift.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - `~100 GHz` continuum is detected in eight targets.
    - CO(6-5) is detected in four QSO hosts and one companion.
    - Molecular gas masses are of order `10^10 M_sun` for detections and a few `10^9 M_sun` for upper limits.
    - SMBH growth, star formation, gas consumption, enrichment, and feedback are not synchronized across the sample.
  - **Position:** best quasar-ISM paper of the week because it breaks a single-track view of rapid early SMBH and host growth.

- [2606.19282](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19282) Jianhang Chen et al. `The first detection of dense gas in a massive main-sequence galaxy at cosmic noon`
  - **Question:** can dense star-forming gas be detected directly in a typical massive main-sequence galaxy at `z>2`?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: BX610 at `z=2.21`.
    - Lines: HNC `J=5-4` and CN `N=4-3`.
    - Comparison: starburst and quasar/AGN host galaxies.
  - **Method:**
    - Decompose dense-gas line emission.
    - Measure HNC/CN flux ratio.
    - Use radiative-transfer analysis for density and temperature.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - HNC and CN emission is concentrated in the central star-forming region.
    - HNC/CN flux ratio is `1.05+-0.23`.
    - Dense gas has density `(2-4)x10^6 cm^-3` and kinetic temperature `50-80 K`.
  - **Position:** practical gas anchor because it directly measures the dense ISM in a normal cosmic-noon disk.

- [2606.18342](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18342) Zhiyuan Ji et al. `Compact Core, Extended Reach: A Bipolar kpc-Scale Elongation in a Little Red Dot at z approx 5.5`
  - **Question:** can the compact central source in an LRD affect the host on resolved kpc scales?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: LRD-204851 at `z=5.482`.
    - Spectroscopy: VLT/MUSE rest-frame UV cube.
    - Imaging: JWST/NIRCam emission-line maps.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare UV continuum and rest-optical line morphology.
    - Model Ly-alpha velocity structure.
    - Interpret line maps and Ly-alpha profiles jointly.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - A thin bipolar structure passes through the optical continuum centroid.
    - A bright `[O III]` clump lies `~2 kpc` southeast of the centroid.
    - Ly-alpha has a faint blue peak shifted by `~430 km/s`.
    - The geometry supports a biconical low-column-density cavity.
  - **Position:** top compact-source morphology result because it moves LRDs from SED classes toward resolved gas and feedback structure.

- [2606.17271](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17271) Andrea Weibel et al. `A search for Little Red Dots dominated by accreting black-hole stars`
  - **Question:** how common are central-engine-dominated Little Red Dot candidates in large JWST imaging samples?
  - **Data:**
    - Imaging: about `1000 arcmin^2` of JWST data.
    - Selection: BH-star templates in `eazy`.
    - Sample: `241` candidates spanning `z~1.7-9.3`.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit photometry with central-engine and galaxy templates.
    - Estimate candidate redshifts, masses, and number densities.
    - Compare with other compact red source selections.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Candidate number density peaks at `z~5-6`.
    - Eddington-limited assumptions imply `M_BH~10^4-10^7 M_sun`.
    - Central-engine-dominated templates can explain a significant subset of compact red JWST sources.
  - **Position:** important demographics paper because LRD interpretations depend on separating stellar, AGN, and hybrid central-engine components.

## Survey and Modeling Infrastructure

- [2606.14869](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14869) Lilan Yang et al. `COSMOS-Web morphology catalog`
  - **Question:** what morphology measurements are available for the largest current JWST galaxy imaging fields?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: COSMOS-Web.
    - Sample: about `780,000` galaxies.
    - Bands: four JWST/NIRCam filters.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure structural parameters consistently across the field.
    - Provide morphology products for broad user reuse.
    - Support size, concentration, and shape analyses across redshift.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - COSMOS-Web now has survey-scale JWST morphology infrastructure.
    - The catalog enables statistical structural work rather than small-field case studies.
    - The release is central for connecting JWST imaging morphology to environment and galaxy evolution.
  - **Position:** primary survey-product paper because morphology becomes a scalable measurement for high-redshift galaxy populations.

- [2606.18581](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18581) T. Hagen et al. `DESI Data Release 2 ELGs: Property-dependent subsamples, imaging systematics, and clustering`
  - **Question:** do ELG-property-dependent imaging-systematics weights improve DESI DR2 clustering measurements?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: DESI Data Release 2 emission-line galaxies.
    - Regions: DES and non-DES footprints.
    - Subsamples: ELGs split in `g-r` versus `r-z` color space.
  - **Method:**
    - Derive systematic weights with linear regression on ELG subsamples.
    - Compare with the fiducial neural-network correction.
    - Test residual spurious clustering.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The DES footprint has higher density but lacks some extreme-color targets.
    - ELG redshift distributions differ across color-position subsamples in DES.
    - Property-dependent weights reduce spurious clustering in about `10%` of subsamples.
    - The fiducial scheme remains best for the full sample.
  - **Position:** useful LSS infrastructure because ELG selection systematics propagate into environment and clustering science.

- [2606.19875](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19875) Hyeonguk Bahk et al. `The SPHEREx View of Galaxy Clusters`
  - **Question:** can SPHEREx forced photometry recover cluster-galaxy photometry and redshifts accurately enough for cluster science?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: eight clusters at `z~0.02-1.1`.
    - Inputs: DESI Legacy Survey, COSMOS, and simulated SPHEREx imaging.
    - Tool: The Tractor forced photometry.
  - **Method:**
    - Simulate SPHEREx cluster observations.
    - Measure blending, depth, and redshift recovery.
    - Combine selected member redshifts into cluster redshifts.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Photometry is generally unbiased, but blending dominates catastrophic failures.
    - Quality-selected galaxies reach `sigma_NMAD~0.003-0.01`.
    - Combined members recover `z<=0.5` cluster redshifts with bias `<0.002` in `|Delta z|/(1+z)`.
  - **Position:** useful because SPHEREx cluster samples will need clear photometric limits and blending diagnostics.
