## Executive View

The Friday listing is led by a useful mix of high-redshift gas physics, early black-hole growth, and survey infrastructure. The read-first paper is [2606.12686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12686) Tucker Jones, which uses spatially resolved absorption and emission diagnostics in a strongly lensed `z=3.2` galaxy to connect `~100 pc` gas flows, supernova-driven winds, metal enrichment, and possible recycled accretion.

The second anchor is high-redshift molecular gas. [2606.13393](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13393) Emanuele Daddi reports a strong CO(6-5) detection in JADES-GS-z7-01-QU at `z=7.31`, deriving a molecular reservoir near `3x10^10 M_sun` and a gas fraction near `65%`. It directly informs how quiescent or transitioning galaxies retain fuel in the reionization era.

The early-black-hole and first-star set is unusually relevant. [2606.12851](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12851) Raul G. Cacho tests whether massive black-hole mergers can arise from AGN disks and heavy seeds at high redshift. [2606.13078](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13078) Mario Candeloro models Pop III chemical signatures in JWST galaxies and argues that future facilities will need strong gravitational lensing to reach below the Pop III critical metallicity threshold.

For high-redshift compact populations, [2606.12509](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12509) Fabio Fontanot uses THESAN to interpret Little Blue Dots as abundant, compact, low-mass systems with young stellar populations at `4<z<7`. [2606.12511](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12511) Mitch Begelman reframes AGN unification around radiation confinement in compact dusty nuclear disks, a useful physical lens for the LRD/LBD nuclear-geometry problem even though the paper is theoretical.

The best environment and survey entries are [2606.12522](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12522) Shane W. Davis on magnetized gas in the CARLA J1018.6+1058 protocluster at `z=2.47`, [2606.13124](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13124) Shenli Tang on resolved metallicity gradients and pristine inflow candidates in local galaxies, and [2606.13359](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13359) Aritra Ghosh on the VIDEO Galaxy Cluster Catalogue as MOONRISE preparatory infrastructure.

## High-Redshift Gas Flows, Molecular Gas, and Enrichment

- [2606.12686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12686) Tucker Jones et al. `Gas Flows in a Strongly Lensed Galaxy at z=3.2`
  - **Question:** how do outflows, possible inflows, and metal enrichment appear on resolved scales in a typical star-forming galaxy at `z=3.2`?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: galaxy SGAS J003341.5+024217 at `z=3.2`.
    - Lensing: strong magnification enabling `~100 pc` source-plane sampling.
    - Spectroscopy: high spatial and spectral resolution observations of absorption and emission diagnostics.
    - Tracers: neutral and ionized gas kinematics, metallicity, and dust extinction.
  - **Method:**
    - Map the gas velocity field across the lensed source.
    - Separate outflowing absorption from systemic and redshifted components.
    - Compare metal enrichment and dust extinction across the resolved star-forming regions.
    - Interpret redshifted absorption as possible recycled accretion or inflow.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The system shows fast outflowing gas consistent with supernova-driven feedback.
    - Resolved enrichment varies across the galaxy, indicating inhomogeneous metal mixing.
    - Redshifted absorption components point to candidate inflowing or recycled gas.
    - The combination of lensing and spectroscopy resolves gas cycling on scales normally inaccessible at `z>3`.
  - **Position:** read first because it directly matches the galaxy-gas connection at high redshift and provides the most concrete ISM/CGM interface result in the Friday listing.

- [2606.13393](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13393) Emanuele Daddi et al. `Strong CO(6-5) emission in a quiescent galaxy at z=7.31 reveals a massive molecular reservoir`
  - **Question:** can a galaxy at `z=7.31` with low current star formation still retain a large molecular gas reservoir?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: JADES-GS-z7-01-QU at `z=7.31`.
    - Line: CO(6-5).
    - Facility: millimeter spectroscopy.
    - Inferred gas: molecular mass near `3x10^10 M_sun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Detect and measure CO(6-5) emission.
    - Convert the high-J CO line into a molecular gas mass with assumed excitation and conversion factors.
    - Compare gas mass, stellar mass, and current SFR.
    - Interpret the result in the context of early quenching or suppressed star formation.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - CO(6-5) is detected strongly in a galaxy only `~700 Myr` after the Big Bang.
    - The inferred gas fraction is about `65%`.
    - A large molecular reservoir can coexist with low current SFR.
    - Early quiescence may reflect inefficient star formation, recent shutdown, or a transition state rather than simple gas exhaustion.
  - **Position:** high-priority molecular-gas entry because it tests whether early massive galaxies are quenched by gas removal or by suppressed gas conversion.

- [2606.13124](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13124) Shenli Tang et al. `The Size of Inflowing Pristine Gas from Metallicity Gradients in MaNGA Galaxies`
  - **Question:** what spatial scales do metallicity gradients imply for inflowing low-metallicity gas?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: SDSS-IV MaNGA integral-field spectroscopy.
    - Sample: nearby star-forming galaxies with resolved gas-phase abundance maps.
    - Diagnostics: radial metallicity gradients and localized low-metallicity features.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure spatially resolved oxygen-abundance gradients.
    - Identify metallicity drops inconsistent with smooth disk evolution.
    - Infer characteristic sizes for inflowing low-metallicity gas patches.
    - Compare inflow signatures with galaxy properties.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Local metallicity depressions provide a direct way to estimate the scale of pristine gas inflow.
    - The inferred inflow sizes are large enough to affect resolved star formation and chemical mixing.
    - Nearby IFU maps provide analog constraints for interpreting high-redshift metallicity dilution.
  - **Position:** lower-redshift but useful because metallicity-gradient inflow diagnostics are directly portable to cosmic-noon gas-accretion work.

## Early Black Holes, Compact Nuclear Geometry, and First Stars

- [2606.12851](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12851) Raul G. Cacho et al. `Massive black-hole mergers from accretion disks in AGNs: The case for high-redshift heavy seeds`
  - **Question:** can AGN disks around high-redshift heavy seeds produce the massive black-hole mergers seen by gravitational-wave detectors?
  - **Data:**
    - Model context: compact objects embedded in AGN accretion disks.
    - Seed scenario: high-redshift heavy black-hole seeds.
    - Observable comparison: massive black-hole merger populations.
  - **Method:**
    - Model orbital migration and pairing inside AGN disks.
    - Compare merger production against seed-mass and disk-property assumptions.
    - Evaluate whether high-redshift heavy seeds improve the match to massive merger events.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - AGN disks can enhance black-hole pairing and growth by providing gas drag and migration traps.
    - Heavy seeds at high redshift make the most massive merger products easier to produce.
    - The channel links early SMBH seeding to later gravitational-wave demographics.
  - **Position:** relevant seed-growth paper because it connects high-redshift black-hole formation with an observable merger channel.

- [2606.13078](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13078) Mario Candeloro et al. `Chemical signatures of Population III stars in high-z galaxies: predictions and detectability`
  - **Question:** which chemical signatures can identify Pop III enrichment in high-redshift galaxies, and can current facilities detect them?
  - **Data:**
    - Models: chemically enriched high-redshift galaxies with Pop III contribution.
    - Observables: rest-frame UV and optical abundance-sensitive line ratios.
    - Facility context: JWST and future large telescopes.
  - **Method:**
    - Predict abundance patterns for Pop III-enriched stellar populations.
    - Translate chemical patterns into observable line diagnostics.
    - Test detectability under realistic sensitivity limits.
    - Assess the role of gravitational lensing for low-metallicity systems.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Pop III chemical signatures are easiest to detect in extremely metal-poor systems.
    - JWST can constrain some enriched cases but struggles below the critical metallicity threshold without lensing.
    - Strong gravitational magnification will be important for direct tests of the most pristine regimes.
    - Future facilities are needed for robust Pop III chemical archaeology.
  - **Position:** high-value first-stars paper because it turns Pop III interest into concrete abundance and observability requirements.

- [2606.12511](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12511) Mitch Begelman `Unifying AGN Unification with radiation confinement in compact dusty nuclear disks`
  - **Question:** can radiation confinement in compact dusty disks provide a physical basis for AGN unification?
  - **Data:**
    - Theory target: compact dusty nuclear disks around accreting black holes.
    - Phenomenology: type-dependent obscuration, radiation trapping, and anisotropic emission.
  - **Method:**
    - Model radiation confinement in optically thick dusty gas.
    - Connect disk geometry with observed AGN classes.
    - Interpret obscuration as a dynamical consequence of compact nuclear structure.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Compact dusty nuclear disks can confine radiation and reshape observed AGN appearance.
    - Orientation and nuclear-gas structure become coupled rather than independent parameters.
    - The framework is useful for compact red high-redshift sources where dust, dense gas, and broad lines coexist.
  - **Position:** theoretical but relevant because LRD and early-AGN interpretation increasingly depends on dense nuclear geometry.

## Compact High-Redshift Populations and Dense Environments

- [2606.12509](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12509) Fabio Fontanot et al. `THESAN view on the recent JWST Little Blue Dots`
  - **Question:** what are Little Blue Dots in cosmological reionization simulations, and how do they relate to other compact high-redshift populations?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: THESAN radiation-hydrodynamic reionization simulations.
    - Redshift range: `4<z<7`.
    - Population: compact blue galaxies selected to match JWST Little Blue Dot criteria.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify simulated analogs of observed Little Blue Dots.
    - Compare size, stellar mass, stellar age, and star-formation properties.
    - Estimate abundance and observability.
    - Contrast LBDs with Little Red Dots and broader faint-galaxy populations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - LBD analogs are abundant compact systems in the simulation.
    - Their colors can arise from young stellar populations and strong nebular emission.
    - Simulated LBDs provide a baseline for separating star-forming compact galaxies from AGN-dominated compact red systems.
    - JWST compact-source demographics need joint treatment of LBDs, LRDs, and line-contaminated selections.
  - **Position:** important compact-population entry because it broadens the LRD discussion to blue, star-forming analogs in reionization simulations.

- [2606.12522](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12522) Shane W. Davis et al. `A Search for Magnetic Fields in the CARLA J1018.6+1058 Galaxy Protocluster`
  - **Question:** can radio polarization or Faraday-rotation signatures reveal magnetic fields in a `z=2.47` protocluster?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: CARLA J1018.6+1058 protocluster.
    - Redshift: `z=2.47`.
    - Observables: radio polarization and rotation-measure constraints.
    - Environment: dense galaxy protocluster around a radio-loud AGN field.
  - **Method:**
    - Search for magnetic-field signatures toward and around the protocluster.
    - Compare observed rotation or depolarization constraints with foreground expectations.
    - Interpret non-detections or detections in terms of intraprotocluster gas conditions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The work targets magnetic fields as an additional state variable in high-redshift dense environments.
    - Protocluster radio fields can constrain gas turbulence, feedback, and magnetization before cluster virialization.
    - Even limits are useful for models of early intracluster-medium assembly.
  - **Position:** profile-relevant because it extends protocluster work from galaxy overdensities to magnetized gas physics.

- [2606.13359](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13359) Aritra Ghosh et al. `The VIDEO Galaxy Cluster Catalogue: an input catalogue for the MOONRISE survey`
  - **Question:** what cluster and protocluster candidate catalog supports MOONRISE target selection?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: VIDEO near-infrared imaging.
    - Product: galaxy cluster candidate catalog.
    - Application: MOONRISE survey input selection.
  - **Method:**
    - Build a cluster catalog from VIDEO photometric data.
    - Characterize candidate richness, redshift, and selection properties.
    - Frame the catalog for follow-up and survey planning.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - VIDEO provides a structured input catalog for MOONRISE cluster science.
    - The catalog supports environmental target selection over a wide redshift range.
    - It is useful infrastructure for future dense-environment studies.
  - **Position:** survey-infrastructure entry because robust input catalogs set the target selection for protocluster and cluster-evolution programs.
