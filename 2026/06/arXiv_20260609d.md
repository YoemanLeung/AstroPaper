## Executive View

The Tuesday listing is led by direct JWST high-redshift gas diagnostics. [2606.08782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08782) Minami Nakane uses JWST/NIRSpec medium-resolution spectra of eight UV-bright `z=7.2-10.6` galaxies to measure low-ionization absorption, covering fractions, column densities, and abundance ratios. It is the read-first paper because it links reionization-era galaxies to early chemical enrichment, neutral-gas structure, and possible early Type-Ia enrichment.

The Little Red Dot cluster remains important. [2606.09721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09721) Zhiwei Pan presents the NEXUS NIRSpec MSA sample, with `36` spectroscopically confirmed LRDs, broad-line statistics, selection completeness and purity, and an environmental/clustering estimate. [2606.09726](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09726) Hiroto Yanagisawa finds that LRD broad Halpha is enhanced by `~40x` relative to low-redshift Type 1 AGN at fixed bolometric luminosity, favoring a high-covering-factor dense-gas picture.

Two AGN-host measurement papers matter for survey-scale work. [2606.07750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07750) Euclid Collaboration builds reliability values for SED-derived AGN and host properties across `~1 million` Euclid Q1 AGN candidates. [2606.09413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09413) Mi Chen combines COSMOS-Web SED decomposition and deep-learning imaging decomposition to break AGN-host degeneracies in JWST photometry.

The gas and feedback entries are also useful. [2606.07741](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07741) Zirui Chen explains the near-universal X-ray-to-Halpha surface-brightness ratio in winds, stripped tails, and cluster filaments through 3D multiphase mixing geometry. [2606.09616](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09616) Rogerio Riffel connects circumnuclear stellar populations, gas inflow, and AGN fueling in local AGN hosts. [2606.09665](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09665) Aman N. Raju is a scalable LSST-era AGN reverberation-mapping method.

## Reionization-Era Absorption and Little Red Dots

- [2606.08782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08782) Minami Nakane et al. `JWST Absorption-Line Analysis of UV-Bright Galaxies at z=7.2-10.6: Early Chemical Enrichment Traced by C, O, Mg, Al, Si, and Fe`
  - **Question:** what do low-ionization UV absorption lines reveal about cool gas, covering fraction, and chemical enrichment in bright reionization-era galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Instrument: JWST/NIRSpec medium-resolution spectroscopy.
    - Sample: eight UV-bright galaxies at `z=7.2-10.6`.
    - Luminosity range: `-21.9<M_UV<-20.5`.
    - Diagnostics: low-ionization absorption from C, O, Mg, Al, Si, and Fe.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify low-ionization absorption lines in archival spectra.
    - Compare average low-ionization equivalent widths against compactness and star-formation intensity.
    - Fit multiple absorption lines with covering fraction and column density as free parameters.
    - Place abundance ratios in DLA-like diagnostic planes.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Five of eight galaxies show multiple low-ionization absorption lines.
    - Compact galaxies with `r_e<=100 pc`, high Hbeta-to-UV luminosity, and high `Sigma_SFR` show weaker absorption.
    - The detected systems follow the local covering-fraction versus column-density relation.
    - Two `z~7` galaxies have high iron enrichment with `[Fe/Si]~-0.2`.
    - Their near-solar `[Al/Si]` disfavors a strong pair-instability supernova odd-even signature and favors early Type-Ia enrichment.
  - **Position:** top Tuesday entry because it directly probes reionization-era cool gas, chemical clocks, and absorption-line suppression in compact high-`z` systems.

- [2606.09721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09721) Zhiwei Pan et al. `NEXUS: Abundance, Environments, and Spectral Diversity of Little Red Dots from the NIRSpec MSA Sample`
  - **Question:** what do NIRSpec spectra show about LRD abundance, selection performance, spectral diversity, and environments?
  - **Data:**
    - Program: NEXUS.
    - Instruments: JWST/NIRCam photometry and NIRSpec MSA/PRISM spectra.
    - Redshift range: `2.3<z<7.4`.
    - Spectroscopic sample: `36` confirmed LRDs.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine multiple published photometric LRD selection methods.
    - Measure selection completeness and purity at `F444W<26`.
    - Classify the NIRSpec spectral diversity.
    - Compare broad Halpha, 5100 Angstrom continuum, narrow [O III], space density, and clustering.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Combined photometric selection reaches `~85%` completeness over the stated redshift and flux range.
    - Overall purity is `~60%`, with contamination from emission-line galaxies, normal AGN, and dwarf stars.
    - More than `90%` of spectroscopically confirmed LRDs have robust broad-line detections.
    - The `36` LRDs span extreme Balmer-break objects and moderately reddened blackbody-like continua.
    - Clustering suggests host halos of several `10^11 h^-1 M_sun`, with large uncertainties.
  - **Position:** high-priority LRD demographics paper because it ties spectra, selection functions, and environment to the accreting-enveloped-SMBH interpretation.

- [2606.09726](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09726) Hiroto Yanagisawa et al. `A Scaling Relation of LRDs between Broad Halpha and Bolometric Luminosities: Enhanced Broad Halpha Emission Relative to Low-z Type 1 AGN`
  - **Question:** how do LRD broad Balmer-line luminosities scale with bolometric luminosity compared with low-redshift Type 1 AGN?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `37` LRDs at `z~3-7`.
    - Instruments: JWST/NIRSpec PRISM and grating spectra from multiple programs.
    - Measurements: broad Halpha, broad Hbeta where available, and modified-blackbody bolometric luminosity.
  - **Method:**
    - Isolate broad-line quantities from host-contaminated total line luminosities.
    - Fit broad Halpha and broad Hbeta luminosity against bolometric luminosity.
    - Compare with low-redshift Type 1 AGN scaling relations.
    - Model the line enhancement with Cloudy within a locally optimally emitting cloud framework.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Broad Halpha tightly scales with bolometric luminosity.
    - LRDs are enhanced by `~40x` in broad Halpha relative to low-`z` Type 1 AGN at fixed bolometric luminosity.
    - Broad Hbeta shows a smaller enhancement of `~10x`.
    - Cloudy models reproduce the enhancement with covering factor rising from `~20%` to `~100%`.
    - Preferred gas properties include `N_H>=10^24 cm^-2` and density `~10^10 cm^-3`.
  - **Position:** key physical-interpretation paper for LRDs because it connects broad lines, dense gas, and stuffed or extended BLR scenarios.

## AGN Host Decomposition, Surveys, and Black-Hole Growth

- [2606.07750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07750) Euclid Collaboration et al. `Euclid Quick Data Release (Q1): The impact of AGN emission on SED-derived physical properties`
  - **Question:** how reliably can Euclid Q1 derive AGN and host-galaxy properties from multi-component SED fitting?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: Euclid Quick Data Release Q1.
    - Sample: `~1 million` AGN candidates.
    - Photometry: Euclid near-infrared data plus multi-wavelength SED information.
    - Validation: mock SEDs built from observed QSO and galaxy SEDs.
  - **Method:**
    - Build AGN+host mock SEDs.
    - Fit AGN bolometric luminosity, monochromatic luminosity, stellar mass, SFR, and sSFR.
    - Assign reliability values to derived parameters.
    - Test an AGN-fraction lower-limit selection and compare against other AGN selection methods.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - A reliability threshold of `0.5` yields robust stellar masses for `88%` of sources.
    - The same threshold yields reliable AGN luminosities for `76%` of sources.
    - A lower-limit criterion `f_AGN,low>0.075` gives `85%` completeness and purity.
    - Different AGN identification methods probe different stellar-mass and AGN-luminosity ranges.
    - Survey-scale AGN evolution work must carry reliability and selection-bias information forward.
  - **Position:** important survey-method paper because Euclid-scale AGN-host measurements will set the selection function for environment and growth studies.

- [2606.09413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09413) Mi Chen et al. `Towards a consistent framework of determining active galactic nucleus contribution fraction and host galaxy properties`
  - **Question:** can independent morphological information reduce AGN-host degeneracies in multi-wavelength SED fitting?
  - **Data:**
    - Field: COSMOS-Web.
    - Photometry: ultraviolet to far-infrared multi-wavelength data.
    - Imaging: JWST/NIRCam F150W.
    - Methods: CIGALE, GRAHSP, and deep-learning imaging decomposition.
  - **Method:**
    - Estimate AGN contribution fractions with two SED-decomposition codes.
    - Derive independent AGN fractions from imaging decomposition.
    - Compare SED-based and image-based AGN fractions in NIRCam F150W.
    - Assess how empirical and theoretical AGN templates drive parameter degeneracies.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Current SED-fitting approaches retain significant degeneracies in AGN contribution estimates.
    - Independent morphology helps break these degeneracies.
    - Combining SED and image decomposition improves AGN and host-galaxy property estimates.
    - COSMOS-Web is a useful testbed for JWST-era AGN-host decomposition.
  - **Position:** high-value methods entry for LRD/AGN-host work because compact red sources require robust separation of nuclear and stellar light.

- [2606.08961](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08961) Yining Sun et al. `Rapid intermediate-mass black hole formation via runaway mergers of black holes`
  - **Question:** can dense black-hole clusters rapidly form intermediate-mass black-hole seeds for high-redshift SMBH growth?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: direct `N`-body models of dense stellar-mass black-hole clusters.
    - Density regime: `>=5x10^9 M_sun/pc^3`.
    - Target product: `~10^3 M_sun` IMBHs.
    - Event-rate comparison: GW190521-like and GW231123-like mergers.
  - **Method:**
    - Simulate repeated binary black-hole exchanges and hardening.
    - Compare runaway merger efficiency against three-body scattering expectations.
    - Vary cluster density, total mass, initial mass function, and stellar halo potential.
    - Track IMBH spin and gravitational-wave event rates.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Runaway gravitational-wave mergers can produce `~10^3 M_sun` IMBHs within `10 Myr`.
    - The mechanism proceeds through exchanged soft binaries that accumulate hardening.
    - IMBH spin remains low after repeated mergers.
    - Systems that form IMBHs can expand to densities comparable with present-day nuclear star clusters.
    - The channel offers a seed-growth route for high-redshift SMBHs.
  - **Position:** useful black-hole seed paper because it connects early SMBH assembly to dense stellar-dynamical environments.

- [2606.09665](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09665) Aman N. Raju et al. `A Meta-Learning Framework for Multitask Reverberation Mapping in Active Galactic Nuclei`
  - **Question:** can meta-learning scale photometric AGN reverberation mapping to LSST-like light-curve samples?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey context: LSST AGN sky density of about `1000-4000 deg^-2`.
    - Training data: simulated AGN light curves with varied cadences and transfer functions.
    - Test data: Zwicky Transient Facility AGN light curves.
    - Model: Attentive Latent Neural Processes with mixture-density outputs.
  - **Method:**
    - Cluster light-curve topologies with self-organizing maps.
    - Learn latent representations of SMBH properties and accretion-disk transfer functions.
    - Compare reconstruction and transfer-function recovery against ensemble baseline regressors.
    - Test transfer from simulations to real AGN light curves.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Light-curve reconstruction improves by `60-70%` relative to ensemble-trained baselines.
    - Transfer-function recovery improves by `~35%` in a low-variability cluster.
    - Intrinsic SMBH and red-noise parameter recovery improves by `~34%`.
    - The framework is designed for heterogeneous AGN populations in future time-domain surveys.
  - **Position:** methods entry for survey-scale AGN growth because LSST photometric reverberation mapping can expand SMBH demographic samples.

## Multiphase Gas, Feedback, and Environmental Processing

- [2606.07741](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07741) Zirui Chen et al. `Clumps in a Cocoon: Geometry and Mixing Set the Universal X-ray to Halpha Surface Brightness Ratio`
  - **Question:** why do winds, stripped tails, and cluster filaments show a near-universal X-ray-to-Halpha surface-brightness ratio?
  - **Data:**
    - Observational target: systems with `SB_X/SB_Halpha~3`.
    - Simulations: 3D wind-tunnel models of multiphase gas.
    - Density contrast: `chi~10^3`.
    - Gas phases: cold `~10^4 K` Halpha-emitting clumps and hot `~10^6-10^7 K` X-ray gas.
  - **Method:**
    - Model high-contrast cloud disruption and mixing.
    - Track the temperature probability distribution.
    - Smooth model emission on the tail-width scale.
    - Decompose the luminosity ratio into atomic-physics and residence-time terms.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Cold gas shatters into many Halpha-emitting clumps.
    - X-ray-emitting gas forms a volume-filling cocoon around the clumps.
    - The smoothed surface-brightness ratio converges to the observed order-unity value.
    - Halpha luminosity fraction is set mainly by atomic physics.
    - X-ray luminosity fraction is set by the residence time in the X-ray-emitting temperature band.
  - **Position:** strong CGM/IGM-adjacent paper because it gives a geometry-based explanation for multiphase emission ratios in winds, tails, and cluster filaments.

- [2606.09616](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09616) Rogerio Riffel et al. `Blowing star formation away in AGN hosts (BAH) -- V: The Feeding-Feedback Cycle in local AGNs as revealed by their stellar populations`
  - **Question:** do spatially resolved stellar populations in local AGN hosts preserve evidence for gas inflow, star formation, and AGN fueling?
  - **Data:**
    - Targets: NGC 3884, 3C 293, and CGCG 012-070.
    - Scale: inner kiloparsec.
    - Observations: near-infrared spectroscopy.
    - Synthesis models: M13, XSL, and FSPS.
  - **Method:**
    - Reconstruct spatially resolved star-formation histories.
    - Compare population fits across multiple SPS models.
    - Map featureless continuum and hot-dust components.
    - Interpret radial stellar metallicity and population gradients.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Stellar light is dominated by intermediate-age to old populations with `Z>=Z_sun`.
    - All models indicate recent circumnuclear rejuvenation.
    - NGC 3884 and 3C 293 show `0.2<t<=0.7 Gyr` ring-like stellar structures.
    - 3C 293 has two spatially separated featureless-continuum regions, possibly indicating dual AGN or a reddened starburst.
    - Central metallicity drops are consistent with metal-poor gas inflow that fuels accretion and recent star formation.
  - **Position:** local but profile-relevant AGN feedback paper because it links inflow, circumnuclear star formation, and black-hole fueling.

- [2606.09817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09817) Abhner P. De Almeida et al. `Satellite compaction pathways: environmental drivers shaping dwarf galaxy corpulence in the TNG50 simulation`
  - **Question:** what environmental pathways create compact dwarf satellites in a cosmological simulation?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: TNG50.
    - Sample: `z=0` dwarf galaxies with `8.4<log(M_star/M_sun)<9.2`.
    - Classification: compact and normal satellites from the size-mass relation.
    - Environment diagnostics: dark-matter richness, merger history, tidal field, gas content, and pericentric passage.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify compact satellites on the lower-size envelope or secondary branch.
    - Separate dark-matter-rich and dark-matter-poor compaction channels.
    - Track gas inflow, outer stellar stripping, and ram-pressure compression.
    - Compare stellar metallicity, color, and environmental histories.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Dark-matter-rich compact satellites live in poorer environments and compact through low-angular-momentum gas inflow.
    - Most dark-matter-poor compact satellites become red and metal-rich for their stellar mass.
    - Dark-matter-poor compaction is mainly caused by tidal stripping of outer stars.
    - Gas accelerates stripping-driven compaction by at least `15%` after tidal-field correction.
    - A tentative channel begins with ram-pressure starbursts near first pericenter.
  - **Position:** environment paper for the profile because it separates gas-driven, tidal, and ram-pressure structural pathways in satellites.

## Lower-Priority Methods, ISM, and Near-Field Entries

- [2606.09325](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09325) Lars L. Thomsen presents a unified radiative-transfer framework for TDE spectral diversity. Optically thick outflowing envelopes reproduce H-dominated, He-dominated, H+He/Bowen, and featureless classes, with ionization state controlled by injected luminosity per envelope mass.

- [2606.09629](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09629) Noemi Anau Montel builds a differentiable forward model for weakly perturbed stellar streams. Adding kinematics tightens substructure power-spectrum constraints by `~3-5x` and improves the dark-matter free-streaming cutoff precision from `~1.2 dex` to `~0.25 dex` for a `5 Gyr` GD-1-like stream.

- [2606.09576](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09576) Alexandros Pratsos introduces `SCREAM`, an error-aware weakly supervised machine-learning method for stellar-stream membership. On GD-1, it reaches `F1=0.745` and recovers diffuse cocoon and faint main-sequence members missed by stricter classical selections.

- [2606.07771](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07771) Karla Tame-Narvaez benchmarks uncertainty quantification on AION-1 embeddings for galaxy-property regression. Conformal approaches give near-nominal coverage, while locally valid discriminative intervals adapt to local prediction difficulty.

- [2606.07779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07779) Dimitrios Tanoglidis tests vision-language models on ultra-faint dwarf candidate diagnostics. Zero-shot models reproduce aggregate human calibration on easier cases but provide unreliable object-level uncertainty estimates.

- [2606.09651](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09651) Ningyu Tang uses FAST HI narrow self-absorption toward Orion to infer low-energy cosmic-ray ionization rates. The rate scales with local SFR and supports in-situ production by star-forming activity.

- [2606.09284](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09284) Rebeca Canales applies phylogenetic trees to one-zone chemical-evolution models. The outflow mass-loading parameter dominates branch separation, making the method a possible tool for reconstructing divergent enrichment histories.

- [2606.09352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09352) E. Caffau reports TYC 170-1218-1 as an extremely metal-poor r-II halo star with `[Fe/H]=-3.52`, `[Eu/Fe]=+1.84`, and `[Th/Fe]=+1.85`, likely accreted through the Sequoia event.

- [2606.09425](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09425) V. S. Veena detects widespread class I methanol maser emission at the Milky Way bar-CMZ interface. The masers trace shock-processed gas over a `24 pc` mapped region and may analogize shock-dominated maser environments in barred galactic nuclei.

- [2606.08778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08778) Zhen-Xing Ma uses synthetic Herschel observations to identify `8832` filaments and `110193` branches in simulations. Filaments host `94%` of synthetic clumps and have column-density trends consistent with Hi-GAL.
