## Executive View

The Wednesday listing is led by a direct environmental-quenching case at `z=4.3`, a reionization-bubble incidence calculation at `z~13`, and a JWST selection of central-engine-dominated Little Red Dots spanning cosmic noon to the reionization era.

The read-first paper is [2606.18229](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18229) Dazhi Zhou. It reports ALMA and JWST evidence for extreme ram-pressure stripping in SPT2349-56-C26 at `z=4.30`. More than half of the observed cold gas is displaced from the stellar body, with a `6 kpc` gas-stellar offset. This is a direct hydrodynamic quenching channel in a protocluster core at `z>4`.

The best reionization-theory entry is [2606.17571](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17571) Peter Ziwei Hu. It uses JWST-calibrated UV luminosity functions to estimate how often galaxy populations at `z~13` produce ionized bubbles with `R>=2.5 cMpc`. The fiducial surface density is `1.33e-2 arcmin^-2` per `Delta z=1`, making Witstok-sized ionized regions plausible but not guaranteed for any single source.

The top JWST population paper is [2606.17271](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17271) Andrea Weibel. It selects `241` BH-star-dominated Little Red Dot candidates from `~1000 arcmin^2` of JWST imaging using BH-star templates in `eazy`. The candidates span `z~1.7-9.3`, have inferred `M_BH~10^4-10^7 M_sun` under Eddington-limited assumptions, and peak in number density at `z~5-6`.

Dust and PAH work is unusually strong. [2606.17270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17270) Caitlin M. Casey stacks more than `500,000` COSMOS-Web galaxies and finds UV/optical attenuation underpredicts IR luminosity by `~3x` at `0.5<z<7`. The PAHSPECS pair, [2606.18244](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18244) Fergus R. Donnan and [2606.18230](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18230) Cristina Maria Lofaro, uses JWST/MIRI MRS to resolve and calibrate PAH diagnostics at `z~1.1`.

AGN entries are lower redshift but useful. [2606.17144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17144) Kyuseok Oh maps decoupled ionized-gas kinematics and excitation in a Compton-thick AGN. [2606.17599](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17599) J. Wang extends changing-look behavior to the optical FeII complex. [2606.17490](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17490) Nobuyuki Sakai argues AGN disk winds contribute only small fractions of the cosmic gamma-ray and neutrino backgrounds.

## High-Redshift Environments and Reionization

- [2606.18229](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18229) Dazhi Zhou et al. `An extreme ram-pressure stripping event in a protocluster at redshift 4.3`
  - **Question:** can ram-pressure stripping remove enough cold gas to quench a massive protocluster galaxy at `z>4`?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: SPT2349-56-C26 in the SPT2349-56 protocluster at `z=4.30`.
    - Instruments: ALMA and JWST observations of the stellar body and cold-gas emission.
    - Context: earlier high-redshift stripping examples at `z=2.51` and `z=3.06`.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare gas-emission morphology with the stellar body.
    - Measure the displacement of the gas reservoir.
    - Interpret the displaced cold gas as active stripping in a dense protocluster core.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - More than half of the observed cold-gas reservoir is displaced from the galaxy.
    - The gas-emission peak is offset by `6 kpc` from the stellar body.
    - Ram-pressure stripping can remove most of the cold gas from a massive galaxy as early as `z=4.3`.
  - **Position:** read first because it gives a direct hydrodynamic route to environmental quenching in a forming cluster before mature low-redshift cluster conditions exist.

- [2606.17571](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17571) Peter Ziwei Hu et al. `The Incidence of Large Ionized Bubbles at Redshift 13`
  - **Question:** how often can galaxy populations at `z~13` generate large ionized bubbles around early sources?
  - **Data:**
    - Inputs: JWST UV luminosity functions.
    - Benchmark: the Ly-alpha source reported by Witstok et al. (2025).
    - Bubble scale: regions with comoving radius `R>=2.5 cMpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Model galaxy-driven ionized bubbles at `z~13`.
    - Convert bubble incidence into sky surface density per `Delta z=1`.
    - Use independent-sphere source placement as a conservative baseline.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The fiducial case gives `Sigma_{>=2.5}=1.33e-2 arcmin^-2` per `Delta z=1`.
    - Witstok-sized ionized regions are plausible in UVLF-calibrated galaxy-driven models.
    - A specific source may still require higher ionizing efficiency, recent fading or burstiness, or non-stellar ionizing photons.
  - **Position:** important for reionization interpretation because it turns isolated `z~13` Ly-alpha detections into a population-level bubble-incidence question.

- [2606.17210](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17210) J. Clarke et al. `Testing masking effectiveness using multi-line image cubes based on COSMOS2020 for [CII] line intensity mapping at z_[CII] > 3.5`
  - **Question:** when can CO masking recover `[CII]` line-intensity mapping power at `3.5<z<8.2`?
  - **Data:**
    - Input catalog: COSMOS2020 over `1.44 deg^2`.
    - Instrument concept: Prime-Cam EoR-Spec on the Fred Young Submillimeter Telescope.
    - Frequency range: four `40 GHz` EoR-Spec bands from `205-420 GHz`, plus possible `150` and `90 GHz` extensions.
  - **Method:**
    - Build empirical CO and `[CII]` intensity cubes.
    - Add white and correlated noise.
    - Test targeted masking using bright COSMOS2020 foreground galaxies.
    - Test blind masking by matching bright voxels across frequency bands.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - CO cube predictions are consistent with ALMA, VLA, and NOEMA observations.
    - In ideal conditions, masking can recover `[CII]` above `300 GHz`.
    - Noise keeps `[CII]` recovery below `S/N<5` until near the end of a planned `2000 hr` observing period without extra time.
    - CO can be recovered below `300 GHz`, but `[CII]` likely needs cross-correlation techniques there.
  - **Position:** useful survey-feasibility work because foreground masking limits how `[CII]` maps can be used for reionization-era structure.

## JWST Populations, Dust, and Cosmic Noon ISM

- [2606.17271](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17271) Andrea Weibel et al. `Black Hole Stars Across the Universe: Identifying Central Engine Dominated Little Red Dots at z~1.5-9.5`
  - **Question:** can photometric fitting with central-engine templates identify BH-star-dominated Little Red Dots beyond standard V-shaped SED selection?
  - **Data:**
    - Imaging: legacy and pure-parallel JWST imaging over `~1000 arcmin^2`.
    - Sample: `241` compact BH-star-dominated candidates.
    - Redshift range: `z~1.7-9.3`.
  - **Method:**
    - Add BH-star templates to the `eazy` redshift-fitting code.
    - Select compact sources where the BH-star template contributes `>80%` of the rest-optical best-fit SED.
    - Estimate optical and bolometric luminosities.
    - Compare with V-shaped LRD samples.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The selected candidates span `log L_5100~42-44.5`.
    - Median Balmer break strength is `~3`, with some breaks `>10`.
    - Eddington-limited bolometric luminosities imply `M_BH~10^4-10^7 M_sun`.
    - Number density peaks at `z~5-6` near `1e-5 Mpc^-3` and declines by an order of magnitude toward `z~2`.
  - **Position:** high-priority JWST population paper because it treats LRD central engines as a broad cosmic-noon-to-cosmic-dawn population rather than a purely earliest-Universe class.

- [2606.17270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17270) Caitlin M. Casey et al. `Dust in the Average Galaxy: Attenuation, Emission, and Opacity from 0<z<7`
  - **Question:** how do UV/optical attenuation and FIR dust emission compare for typical galaxies from `0<z<7`?
  - **Data:**
    - Imaging: JWST COSMOS-Web.
    - FIR/submm data: Spitzer, Herschel, SCUBA-2, NIKA-2, and ALMA.
    - Sample: more than `500,000` galaxies.
  - **Method:**
    - Stack FIR spectral energy distributions.
    - Independently constrain UV/optical attenuation and dust emission.
    - Derive empirical relations for attenuation, dust temperature, unobscured star-formation fraction, and dust-to-stellar mass ratio.
    - Compare UV, FIR, and dust-mass constraints to separate geometry from grain-property effects.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - UV/optical attenuation underpredicts IR luminosity by `~3x` at `0.5<z<7`.
    - The mismatch reaches up to an order of magnitude for `M_*>10^10.5 M_sun`.
    - `kappa_UV/kappa_FIR` drops by more than an order of magnitude from `z~0` to `z~7`.
    - The ratio is inversely related to `Sigma_SFR`, consistent with a deficit of small grains under intense high-z radiation fields.
  - **Position:** key calibration paper because high-redshift SFR, dust mass, and obscuration estimates cannot rely on UV/optical attenuation alone.

- [2606.18244](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18244) Fergus R. Donnan et al. `PAHSPECS: Spatially Resolved PAH Spectroscopy at cosmic noon with JWST MIRI MRS`
  - **Question:** how do spatially resolved PAH properties vary across normal star-forming galaxies at cosmic noon?
  - **Data:**
    - Program: PAHSPECS.
    - Instrument: JWST/MIRI MRS.
    - Sample: representative normal star-forming galaxies at `z~1.1`.
    - Features: `3.3`, `6.2`, `7.7`, and `11.3 micron` PAHs plus `[ArII]`.
  - **Method:**
    - Forward model MIRI data cubes with non-parametric spatial distributions.
    - Account for PSF convolution.
    - Produce PAH ratio maps.
    - Use HST and JWST photometry to map UV radiation-field hardness.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - PAHs become larger and more neutral with increasing galactocentric radius.
    - The radial trend is opposite to local-galaxy behavior.
    - The `3.3/11.3` PAH ratio decreases with harder UV radiation fields, while `11.3/7.7` increases.
    - Photo-destruction of small or ionized PAHs may drive the lower `3.3/11.3` ratios at cosmic noon.
  - **Position:** strong ISM paper because MIRI turns PAH ratios into resolved physical diagnostics at the peak epoch of galaxy growth.

- [2606.18230](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18230) Cristina Maria Lofaro et al. `PAHSPECS: Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon properties at cosmic noon with JWST/MIRI MRS`
  - **Question:** do integrated PAH band ratios at `z~1.1` trace star formation and ISM conditions differently than in local LIRGs?
  - **Data:**
    - Program: PAHSPECS.
    - Instrument: JWST/MIRI MRS.
    - Sample: five ASPECS galaxies in the HUDF at `z~1.1`.
    - Ancillary data: photometry modeled for dust emission, stellar masses, and SFRs.
  - **Method:**
    - Extract wavelength-dependent integrated spectra.
    - Model spectra with CAFE.
    - Fit stellar masses and SFRs with Prospector.
    - Compare PAH luminosities and ratios with local LIRGs.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Most sources have higher `6.2/7.7` and lower `11.3/7.7` than local LIRGs.
    - The ratios imply an ionized PAH component weighted toward smaller grains.
    - The `7.7 micron` luminosity follows the local `L_7.7-SFR` relation at `z~1`.
    - The AGN-hosting source ASPECS-15 has the lowest `6.2/7.7` ratio and highest sSFR.
  - **Position:** companion calibration paper because it checks whether PAH-based SFR indicators remain usable at cosmic noon.

- [2606.17146](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17146) Bren E. Backhaus et al. `MEGA and SMILES Find Fewer Dusty Galaxies than Expected at Cosmic Noon`
  - **Question:** does the faint end of the infrared luminosity function at cosmic noon contain as much dusty star formation as previous extrapolations assumed?
  - **Data:**
    - Surveys: MEGA and SMILES with JWST/MIRI.
    - Sample: `634` galaxies over `105 arcmin^2`.
    - Redshift range: `0.2<z<2`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure the `7.7 micron` PAH feature using MIRI `F1000W`, `F1500W`, or `F2100W` photometry.
    - Convert `7.7 micron` PAH luminosity to total IR luminosity.
    - Build IR luminosity functions two orders of magnitude fainter than previous work.
    - Integrate luminosity functions to infer SFR density.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The faint-end IR luminosity function flattens strongly, with average slope `alpha~0.147`.
    - Less luminous galaxies have less dust-obscured star formation than prior extrapolations predicted.
    - The inferred SFR density is slightly lower than previous ALMA, Herschel, and Spitzer estimates.
    - LIRGs and ULIRGs still dominate dust-obscured star formation at `z~1-2`.
  - **Position:** useful cosmic-noon census paper because it revises the faint dusty-galaxy contribution with direct JWST/MIRI constraints.

## AGN, Outflows, and Accretion Diagnostics

- [2606.17144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17144) Kyuseok Oh et al. `Decoupled Kinematics and Excitation in the Compton-thick AGN NGC 6552: Spatially Resolved KOOLS-IFU Observations`
  - **Question:** are ionized-gas kinematics locally coupled to excitation in a Compton-thick AGN?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: NGC 6552.
    - Instrument: KOOLS-IFU on the `3.8 m` Seimei Telescope.
    - Region: central `~2 kpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Map spatially resolved optical emission-line ratios.
    - Measure non-parametric `[O III]5007` kinematics.
    - Compare `W80`, velocity asymmetry, and `[O III]/Hbeta`.
    - Estimate ionized outflow energetics.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - `[O III]` widths are elevated across the inner region with `W80~530-830 km/s`.
    - Width decreases with galactocentric distance, consistent with a decelerating centrally concentrated outflow.
    - Neither `W80` nor velocity asymmetry shows a significant global correlation with `[O III]/Hbeta`.
    - Ionized-phase energetics give `E_dot,K/L_bol~0.01%-0.28%` for `n_e=50-1000 cm^-3`.
  - **Position:** useful AGN-feedback benchmark because kinematics and excitation need not trace the same physical structure even in spatially resolved IFU data.

- [2606.17599](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17599) J. Wang et al. `OQ 208: A New Fe II Changing-look Active Galactic Nucleus and Implications for the Nature of the Changing-look Phenomenon`
  - **Question:** can changing-look AGN behavior extend from Balmer emission to the optical FeII complex?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: OQ 208, a local flat-spectrum radio source.
    - Observations: multiepoch optical spectroscopy.
    - Comparison sample: dozens of recent changing-look AGN from Panda and Sniegowska (2024).
  - **Method:**
    - Track FeII and Balmer broad-line changes over time.
    - Measure FeII strength relative to Hbeta.
    - Compare FeII response with optical continuum and Eddington ratio.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - OQ 208 shows disappearance of strong FeII emission with initial `RFe=0.64`.
    - Broad Balmer emission disappears on the same `~14 yr` timescale.
    - FeII strength anticorrelates with continuum and Eddington ratio during changing-look events.
    - The behavior is consistent with a negative FeII response driven by Comptonization in a hot, optically thin accretion flow.
  - **Position:** useful accretion-physics entry because it links FeII and Balmer changing-look behavior to a shared ionizing-continuum response.

- [2606.17490](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17490) Nobuyuki Sakai et al. `Revisiting Disk Winds in Active Galactic Nuclei as an Origin of Cosmic Gamma-ray and Neutrino Backgrounds`
  - **Question:** can AGN disk winds dominate the cosmic gamma-ray and neutrino backgrounds?
  - **Data:**
    - Calibration set: nearby Fermi-LAT-detected Seyfert galaxies.
    - Constraints: radio, GeV gamma-ray data, and recent XRISM wind measurements.
    - Model input: AGN population synthesis.
  - **Method:**
    - Build lepto-hadronic wind models for forward and reverse shocks.
    - Calibrate synchrotron, external-Compton, and hadronic emission.
    - Apply the calibrated models to an AGN population.
    - Estimate the cosmic gamma-ray and neutrino background contributions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Disk winds contribute at most `<=5%` of the cosmic gamma-ray background above `10 GeV`.
    - They contribute at most `<=10%` of the cosmic neutrino background around `100 TeV`.
    - Nearby Seyferts with ultrafast outflows are better targets for TeV gamma-ray and TeV-PeV neutrino tests than a dominant diffuse-background explanation.
  - **Position:** lower-priority for high-z galaxy work but useful because it bounds the energetic importance of AGN winds in multi-messenger backgrounds.

## Methods, Simulations, and Lower-Redshift Context

- [2606.18082](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18082) Chaitra et al. `Variability in Cosmological Hydrodynamical Simulations: how Stochastic Processes, Numerical Effects, and Reproducibility Limits impact Predictability`
  - **Question:** how large is run-to-run stochastic variation in nominally identical cosmological hydrodynamical simulations?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation suite: four identical realizations of a zoom-in galaxy-cluster simulation.
    - Code: OpenGadget3.
    - Controls: compiler, library, and hardware settings held fixed.
  - **Method:**
    - Match galaxies across repeated runs.
    - Separate run-to-run variation from within-run noise with a mixed linear model.
    - Compare dark-matter, stellar, and black-hole mass scatter.
    - Test the role of feedback in regulating variability.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Baseline galaxy dark-matter and stellar masses vary by `~10%-25%`.
    - Stochastic star formation and feedback amplify variation above the shot-noise floor.
    - Feedback reduces scatter in stellar and black-hole masses in the tested setup.
    - The simulations remain statistically reproducible even when individual realizations differ.
  - **Position:** useful methods paper because interpretation of simulation predictions needs a quantitative reproducibility floor.

- [2606.17189](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17189) Eden Wise et al. `Exploring the Relationship Between Bars, Star Formation Activity, and Host Galaxy Properties from z~0 to z~2`
  - **Question:** how does the connection between bars, star formation, and host properties evolve from `z~2` to the local universe?
  - **Data:**
    - Primary sample: `1171` CEERS galaxies with `M_*>10^10 M_sun`.
    - Check sample: COSMOS-Web data.
    - Simulation comparison: TNG50-1 bars with `a_bar>1.5 kpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure bar demographics against sSFR, Sersic index, redshift, and quiescence.
    - Compare barred star-forming and barred quiescent fractions over time.
    - Compare observations with TNG50-1.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At `z~1-2`, barred galaxies tend to have high sSFR and low `n<=2`.
    - The contribution of barred quiescent galaxies rises steeply from `z~2` to `z~0`.
    - The fraction of quiescent galaxies that are barred rises over the last `10 Gyr`.
    - Observations agree well with TNG50-1 for resolved bars larger than `1.5 kpc`.
  - **Position:** useful structural-evolution background because it links disk secular evolution to quenching across the JWST redshift range.

- [2606.17754](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17754) Manuel Bayer et al. `Tracing the very early disruption of the Sagittarius dwarf galaxy in the distant Milky Way halo`
  - **Question:** can distant halo red giants trace the earliest stripped material from the Sagittarius dwarf galaxy?
  - **Data:**
    - Tracers: red-giant-branch stars beyond `80 kpc`.
    - Instrument: Magellan Inamori Kyocera Echelle spectrograph on the Clay Telescope.
    - Comparison data: DESI DR1 stellar tracers and RR Lyrae catalogs.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure radial velocities and metallicities.
    - Test clustering in full `6D` phase space.
    - Compare with Sagittarius disruption models.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Four RGB stars form a tight phase-space clump in a larger Sagittarius-stream spur.
    - The group has metallicity dispersion `0.15^{+0.17}_{-0.08}` around `[Fe/H]=-1.46^{+0.11}_{-0.09}`.
    - The spur is likely early-stripped halo material from Sagittarius.
  - **Position:** lower-priority local-universe entry, but useful as an example of combining spectroscopy, phase space, and disruption models for structure assembly.
