## Executive View

The read-first paper is [2606.04711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04711) Chang-Hao Chen. It gives a direct spectroscopic model for Little Red Dot nuclear structure using `14` LRDs at `2.2<z<6.7`, with Balmer emission, Balmer absorption, continuum, and decrement constraints. The paper is central for deciding whether LRD spectra require dense BLR-scale gas, polar absorbers, and viewing-angle structure around an accretion disk.

The reionization anchor is [2606.04827](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04827) Zihan Wang. It constrains `f_esc(M_h,z)` across `z=5-12` using UV luminosity functions, Planck optical depth, neutral-fraction data, and abundance matching. The profile-likelihood result favors steep redshift evolution, with population-averaged escape fraction rising from `~2%` at `z=5` to `~9%` at `z=12`.

The high-redshift black-hole theory paper is [2606.04081](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04081) Riccardo Caleno. High-resolution zoom simulations of a `z~11` major merger in `M_halo~10^9 Msun` halos find that the merger drives inflow but does not sustain super-Eddington growth when BH feedback is active. This is a useful counterweight to simple merger-driven explanations for early massive black holes.

The survey-product highlight is [2606.04208](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04208) Erin Mentuch Cooper. HETDEX PDR1 releases `86.67 deg^2` of IFU data and HPSC2 catalogs with `426,654` LAEs, `491,411` [O II] emitters, and `18,303` AGN. It is directly useful for LAE large-scale structure, cosmic-noon mapping, and follow-up target construction.

The CGM priority entry is [2606.04793](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04793) Weiwen Kong. DESI Year 1 stacking of Ca II, Mg II, and C IV absorption around galaxies and quasars shows phase-dependent radial structure, with cooler gas more centrally concentrated and quasar halos showing a more uniform distribution likely tied to AGN feedback.

## Little Red Dots and Early Black-Hole Growth

- [2606.04711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04711) Chang-Hao Chen et al. `ABCD: The Nuclear Structure of the Little Red Dots Revealted through Absorption, Break, Continuum, and Decrement`
  - **Question:** what nuclear gas geometry explains the Balmer-line, absorption, continuum, and decrement properties of LRDs?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `14` LRDs at `2.2<z<6.7`.
    - Instrument: JWST/NIRSpec MSA prism and medium-resolution grating spectra.
    - Spectral diagnostics: Halpha, Hbeta, Hgamma, Balmer absorption, broad and narrow line components, velocity-resolved Balmer decrements, and continuum luminosity.
  - **Method:**
    - Decompose broad, narrow, and absorption components of Balmer lines.
    - Measure integrated line ratios and velocity-resolved Balmer decrements.
    - Fit virialized broad-line-region models to the decrement profiles.
    - Compare incident luminosity inferred from line emission with UV and optical continuum.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Broad-line Balmer decrements require high-density gas with `n_H>=10^9 cm^-3`.
    - Velocity-resolved decrements are centrally peaked in the five highest signal-to-noise sources.
    - Three sources are consistent with radial density profiles shallower than `beta<2`.
    - Six sources show Balmer absorption with absorber covering factors above `50%`.
    - A viewing-angle-dependent structure with an optically thick clumpy gaseous torus can explain the distinctive LRD spectra and continua.
  - **Position:** top daily LRD entry because it links the Balmer-break, absorption, continuum, and decrement phenomenology to one nuclear-structure model.

- [2606.04712](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04712) Jinyi Shangguan et al. `The Extreme Rarity and Physical Properties of Low-redshift AGNs with Balmer Absorption`
  - **Question:** how rare are low-redshift type 1 AGN with Balmer absorption, and what do they imply for LRD-like absorbers?
  - **Data:**
    - Parent sample: `14,584` type 1 AGN at `z<0.35` from SDSS.
    - Selected class: robust Balmer-absorption systems.
    - Diagnostics: Halpha, Hbeta, Hgamma absorption depth, absorber covering factor, velocity offset, Fe II emission, Eddington ratio, and gas-phase metallicity.
  - **Method:**
    - Search homogeneously analyzed SDSS spectra for Balmer absorption.
    - Fit a partially covering absorber model with tied theoretical optical-depth ratios.
    - Use multi-epoch spectroscopy to test variability.
    - Compare the low-redshift systems with high-redshift LRD absorber properties.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Only `7` robust Balmer-absorption AGN are found, an occurrence of `~0.05%`.
    - Robust models require optically thick Halpha absorption and typical covering factors `C_f~0.2-0.6`.
    - The LRD analog J1025 has `C_f>=0.8`, consistent with recent high-redshift LRD measurements.
    - Absorber velocity offsets are `~150-850 km/s` with intrinsic widths `~20-200 km/s`.
    - Low metallicity and high accretion rate may increase the Balmer-absorption incidence to `~10%`.
  - **Position:** companion to [2606.04711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04711) Chang-Hao Chen because it tests whether dense neutral absorbers are an extreme but continuous part of AGN phenomenology.

- [2606.04081](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04081) Riccardo Caleno et al. `The role of major mergers in triggering super-Eddington accretion`
  - **Question:** can high-redshift major mergers between low-mass halos trigger sustained super-Eddington black-hole accretion?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation target: a major merger at `z~11`.
    - Halo scale: two relatively low-mass halos with `M_halo~10^9 Msun`.
    - Physics variants: BH seeding and feedback prescriptions across ADAF, sub-Eddington, and super-Eddington regimes.
  - **Method:**
    - Run high-resolution cosmological zoom-in simulations of the merger.
    - Vary radiative, wind, and jet feedback channels.
    - Compare BH accretion histories with and without feedback.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The merger drives central gas inflows but does not produce sustained super-Eddington accretion when feedback is active.
    - Substantial BH growth is suppressed by the adopted feedback model.
    - Post-merger super-Eddington episodes occur only when BH feedback is switched off.
    - Kinetic feedback is the primary regulator of BH growth in these low-mass halos.
    - The only significant super-Eddington episodes occur immediately after BH seeding rather than because of the merger.
  - **Position:** important counterexample for early-BH growth models because merger-driven fueling is not enough when feedback coupling is efficient.

- [2606.04082](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04082) Christopher Tiede et al. `Dynamics and detectability of long-lived non-accretion phases for massive black hole binaries in cold, thermally regulating disks`
  - **Question:** do non-accreting phases of massive black-hole binaries survive in thermally evolved circumbinary disks, and can they be observed?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: grid-based hydrodynamics of circumbinary accretion.
    - Disk model: viscous and hydrodynamic heating coupled to blackbody radiative cooling.
    - Survey context: LSST and Roman optical/near-infrared detectability.
  - **Method:**
    - Evolve high-Mach-number circumbinary disks with an energy equation.
    - Track gas heating, cavity structure, stream launching, and accretion-rate suppression.
    - Estimate electromagnetic signatures during low-accretion phases.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Gas accumulates and heats at the far edge of the cavity, but stream-launching regions stay comparatively cold.
    - Reduced large-scale feeding can lead to long-lived suppressed binary accretion near the Eddington scale.
    - The runaway non-accretion problem is weaker than in locally isothermal simulations.
    - Non-accreting systems can remain luminous and variable enough for LSST and Roman detection.
    - Such binaries may appear X-ray weak with weak or absent emission-line features.
  - **Position:** relevant to AGN demographics because survey selection may include massive binaries that are optically variable but weak in ionizing output.

## Reionization and High-Redshift Survey Products

- [2606.04827](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04827) Zihan Wang and Huanyuan Shan `Steep Redshift Evolution of the Ionizing Escape Fraction at z = 5--12: Empirical Constraints and Comparison with Simulations`
  - **Question:** how does the ionizing escape fraction vary with halo mass and redshift across reionization?
  - **Data:**
    - Observables: HST and JWST UV luminosity functions at `z=5-12`.
    - Integral constraints: Planck Thomson optical depth and seven neutral-fraction measurements.
    - Model comparison: THESAN simulation outputs.
  - **Method:**
    - Model escape fraction as `f_esc=f_0(M/10^10 Msun)^alpha_M[(1+z)/10]^alpha_z`.
    - Use abundance matching to connect UV magnitude with halo mass.
    - Solve reionization histories with an ODE model validated against Planck.
    - Constrain parameters using a dense grid scan and ensemble MCMC.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Profile likelihood gives `f_0=0.061`, `alpha_M=0.18`, and `alpha_z=1.98`.
    - Marginal posteriors are broader because of a strong `f_0-alpha_M-alpha_z` degeneracy.
    - Population-averaged `f_esc` rises from `~2%` at `z=5` to `~9%` at `z=12`.
    - Sub-threshold halos contribute more than `80%` of the ionizing budget at `z>=10`.
    - The steep-evolution model predicts `tau_e=0.047`, consistent with Planck at `0.7 sigma`.
  - **Position:** high-priority reionization methods paper because it turns JWST luminosity functions into tabulated empirical inputs for simulations.

- [2606.04136](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04136) Ilias Goovaerts et al. `HST's Deep Blue: extremely deep UV imaging to reveal the contributors to reionization`
  - **Question:** what HST UV imaging program can calibrate ionizing escape tracers for reionization-era galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Instrument: HST WFC3/UVIS.
    - Redshift window: direct ionizing-photon searches at `2<z<4`.
    - Program scale: deep imaging across `20` fields with supporting spectroscopy.
  - **Method:**
    - Use very deep UV imaging to detect escaping ionizing photons.
    - Spread the survey over many fields to average over uncertain IGM transmission.
    - Tie low-redshift direct escape detections to tracers used at higher redshift.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Escape fraction remains the largest uncertainty in connecting galaxies to reionization.
    - A `20`-field deep-UV HST program would build a sample comparable to low-redshift state-of-the-art escape studies.
    - Field multiplicity is needed because line-of-sight IGM transmission dominates uncertainty.
    - The program would calibrate indirect tracers for epoch-of-reionization sources.
  - **Position:** relevant as observing-strategy work because high-redshift reionization inference depends on lower-redshift escape calibrators.

- [2606.04208](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04208) Erin Mentuch Cooper et al. `HETDEX Public Data Release 1: Source Catalog 2 and Data Cubes from ~90 sq deg of Integral-Field Optical Spectroscopy`
  - **Question:** what public HETDEX products are available for LAE, [O II], AGN, and cosmic-noon large-scale structure work?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: HETDEX integral-field spectroscopy with the Hobby-Eberly Telescope.
    - Coverage: `86.67 deg^2` from `431,713` IFU observations.
    - Spectral range: `3500-5500 Angstrom` at `R~800`.
    - Catalogs: HPSC2 and data cubes from Spring, Fall, COSMOS, GOODS-N, NEP, and SA22 regions.
  - **Method:**
    - Reprocess four additional years of HETDEX data.
    - Apply improved quality control and machine-learning classifiers.
    - Release source catalogs, 1D spectra, data cubes, and candidate LAE products.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - HPSC2 contains `426,654` LAEs and `491,411` [O II] emitters.
    - It also includes `19,457` low-redshift galaxies, `18,303` AGN, and `150,608` stars.
    - The appendix includes a `1.6 million`-candidate LAE sample and raw detection databases.
    - The cubes are not optimized for absolute surface-brightness measurements or very extended nearby galaxies.
  - **Position:** major survey product for LAE large-scale structure and emission-line target selection at `1.88<z<3.52`.

## CGM, IGM, and Multiphase Gas

- [2606.04793](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04793) Weiwen Kong et al. `Cooler Phases of the Circumgalactic Medium Are More Centrally Concentrated: Constraints from Multiphase Absorption Lines`
  - **Question:** how do cold, cool, and warm CGM phases vary with radius around galaxies and quasars?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: DESI Year 1.
    - Absorption tracers: Ca II, Mg II, and C IV doublets.
    - Background sources: stacked spectra behind galaxies and quasars.
  - **Method:**
    - Stack background-source spectra around foreground hosts.
    - Measure equivalent-width radial profiles for each ion.
    - Compare phase-dependent trends with cosmological simulation predictions.
    - Test how radial scaling depends on host stellar mass and AGN environment.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Cooler ions show steeper radial equivalent-width profiles.
    - Emission-line galaxy halos show a strong radial transition from cool to warm gas.
    - Quasar halos show a more uniform phase distribution, likely regulated by AGN feedback.
    - Ca II cold gas in low-redshift galaxies is tightly confined to inner regions.
    - The radial scaling is primarily set by host stellar mass, especially for the cool phase.
  - **Position:** top CGM entry because it uses DESI-scale stacking to connect multiphase absorption, host mass, and AGN feedback.

- [2606.04083](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04083) P. Aparicio Marcos et al. `Studying the absorption signatures of H I Lyman-alpha in the warm-hot circumgalactic medium with TNG50`
  - **Question:** how much H I Lyalpha absorption arises from warm-hot CGM gas around Milky Way-like galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: TNG50.
    - Sample: `75` sightlines through the CGM of `15` galaxies.
    - Halo masses: `10^11.7<=M_200/Msun<=10^12.3`.
    - Absorber class: coronal broad Lyalpha absorbers from gas at `T>10^5 K`.
  - **Method:**
    - Generate synthetic absorption spectra.
    - Identify and characterize broad Lyalpha absorbers from warm-hot CGM gas.
    - Measure covering fractions, column densities, temperature ranges, and baryon contribution.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Coronal broad Lyalpha absorbers have covering fraction `f_c~0.8` for `log N_HI>=13`.
    - Their column densities span `log N_HI=11.6-15.4`.
    - About `50%` of CGM absorbers are dominated by this absorption component.
    - The gas traces `T=10^5.2-10^6.4 K`.
    - Warm-hot gas contributes `~7%` of the galaxy baryon budget and `~25%` of CGM mass in the sample.
  - **Position:** useful low-redshift CGM methods paper because Lyalpha forest-style absorption interpretation needs this warm-hot absorber class.

- [2606.04087](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04087) Lachlan Lancaster et al. `Ceci n'est pas une Couche de Melange: The Meaning of Resolved Turbulent Radiative Mixing`
  - **Question:** when are turbulent radiative mixing layer simulations physically resolved enough to trust their observable predictions?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: high-resolution AthenaK turbulent radiative mixing layers.
    - Diagnostics: micro and macro layer properties, total cooling, phase structure, and transition widths.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure the resolution behavior of cooling and phase structure.
    - Identify the scale at which turbulent diffusion and cooling balance.
    - Test whether total cooling convergence reflects physical convergence.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Apparent resolution independence of total cooling may result from cancellation between numerical dissipation and viscosity.
    - Resolving phase structure requires resolving the turbulent Field length.
    - Correctly resolved layers need spatially resolved transitions between gas phases.
    - Some previous total-cooling convergence claims may not guarantee robust observables.
  - **Position:** relevant to CGM and feedback modeling because mixing-layer observables are sensitive to phase-structure convergence.

- [2606.04093](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04093) Lachlan Lancaster et al. `The Origin of Da Scaling: Suppressed Cooling in Fast-Cooling Mixing Layers`
  - **Question:** why does total cooling in turbulent radiative mixing layers change from `Da^1/2` to `Da^1/4` scaling in the fast-cooling regime?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: turbulent radiative mixing layer experiments.
    - Regime: `Da=t_eddy/t_cool>>1`, where cooling is fast compared with turbulence.
  - **Method:**
    - Analyze how inflowing-gas ram pressure affects turbulent folding of the interface.
    - Connect interface fractal structure to total cooling-rate scaling.
    - Derive a scaling argument for the fast-cooling regime.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Fast cooling suppresses turbulent folding because inflowing-gas ram pressure exceeds turbulent pressure.
    - Suppressed interface folding changes the cooling-rate behavior to `dot(E)_cool proportional to Da^1/4`.
    - The scaling argument reproduces the observed fast-cooling behavior.
  - **Position:** companion to [2606.04087](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04087) Lachlan Lancaster because both papers clarify how to model multiphase interfaces in CGM-like gas.

## AGN, Nuclear Environments, and Galaxy Environments

- [2606.04090](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04090) Daniel Kynoch et al. `Mapping the nuclear environments of extreme coronal line emitting galaxies`
  - **Question:** what do extreme coronal line emitters reveal about nuclear gas around TDE-linked and AGN-linked systems?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `33` extreme coronal line emitters.
    - Spectroscopy: multi-epoch SDSS, DESI, and other optical spectra.
    - Classes: variable TDE-linked systems and non-variable AGN-linked systems.
  - **Method:**
    - Track temporal changes in high-ionization and lower-ionization forbidden lines.
    - Measure emission-line profiles.
    - Convert line widths into characteristic radii under virial assumptions.
    - Compare gas-distance relations with black-hole mass.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Variable ECLEs often show [Fe X]-[Fe XIV] fading before [Fe VII], with [O III] brightening.
    - Coronal lines generally arise between the broad-line region and low-ionization narrow-line region.
    - Ionization stratification appears in both variable and non-variable ECLEs.
    - Gas distance correlates with black-hole mass for [O III] and [Fe VII], with slopes `0.63+-0.08` and `0.69+-0.12`.
  - **Position:** relevant for AGN gas-structure work because ECLEs map circumnuclear gas on scales between BLR and NLR regimes.

- [2606.04655](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04655) Xiaolan Hou and Heng Yu `Environmental Dependence of Star Formation and Galaxy Colors around Abell 2029`
  - **Question:** how do local density and stellar mass shape quenching and galaxy colors around Abell 2029?
  - **Data:**
    - Field: `10 deg x 10 deg` region around A2029.
    - Surveys: DESI and SDSS spectroscopy.
    - Mass bins: `9.5<=log M_star/Msun<10.0`, `10.0<=log M_star/Msun<10.5`, and `log M_star/Msun>=10.5`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure quenched fraction and red fraction as functions of local surface density `log10 Sigma_5`.
    - Compare environmental trends across stellar-mass intervals.
    - Use galaxy colors as tracers of local large-scale structure.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Star-formation activity and color correlate strongly with local density at all masses.
    - Environmental dependence is weaker for low-mass galaxies than for high-mass galaxies but remains significant.
    - Galaxy colors can trace large-scale structure even in low-mass systems.
  - **Position:** lower-redshift environment entry that is useful for interpreting density-quenching observables in survey-scale analyses.

- [2606.04084](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04084) Lena Jlassi et al. `Simulating realistic radio morphologies of Fanaroff-Riley I jets in a self-regulating cool-core cluster`
  - **Question:** can self-regulating AGN jet simulations reproduce observed Fanaroff-Riley I radio morphologies in cool-core clusters?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation target: idealized Perseus-like cool-core cluster.
    - Physics: MHD, cosmic-ray protons and electrons, Lagrangian tracer trajectories, and Crest electron-spectrum evolution.
    - Mock observations: radio synchrotron maps from `150 MHz` to `1.4 GHz`.
  - **Method:**
    - Inject accretion-powered low-density jets.
    - Evolve cosmic-ray electron spectra and magnetic fields.
    - Produce mock radio observations in a blazar-like viewing configuration.
    - Compare self-regulated jets with fixed-power single outbursts.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Self-regulated jets stabilize the cool core against cooling flows.
    - They produce realistic FRI and disturbed-lobe morphologies.
    - Cold gas deflects light jets enough that kpc-scale radio lobe shape does not directly reveal small-scale BH and jet properties.
    - Both fresh and hundreds-of-Myr-old electrons can contribute across `150 MHz-1.4 GHz` in `1-50 microG` magnetic fields.
  - **Position:** relevant AGN feedback paper for cluster-scale gas regulation and radio selection.

- [2606.05157](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05157) SungWon Kwak et al. `SMUGGLE-Ring: Evolutionary link between nuclear star cluster and nuclear disk`
  - **Question:** how do bars, gas inflows, nuclear stellar disks, and nuclear star clusters coevolve in a Milky Way-mass galaxy?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: high-resolution hydrodynamical Milky Way-mass galaxy model.
    - Physics: SMUGGLE multiphase ISM and stellar feedback.
    - Structures: bar, nuclear stellar disk, and nuclear star cluster.
  - **Method:**
    - Follow isolated bar formation and sustained gas inflow.
    - Isolate stars born after bar formation.
    - Track nuclear disk and cluster growth histories.
    - Compare the effect of massive cluster accretion on nuclear scaling relations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - A bar of length `~5 kpc` drives gas inflow and forms both a nuclear stellar disk and nuclear star cluster.
    - The nuclear stellar disk grows inside-out.
    - Stellar feedback repeatedly shocks and regulates the nuclear gas disk.
    - Accretion of a `~3x10^7 Msun` star cluster perturbs the otherwise similar mass growth and star-formation histories.
    - Bar timescale and star-cluster accretion history are key for nuclear-structure scaling relations.
  - **Position:** useful for nuclear galaxy structure, but lower priority than the LRD and AGN entries because it is Milky Way-mass and low-redshift oriented.

## Other Profile-Relevant Mentions

- [2606.05144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05144) Yimeng Tang et al. tests the NGC 1052 dwarf trail with HST surface-brightness-fluctuation distances and JWST TRGB work. The key result is that candidate trail dwarfs are near `~20 Mpc`, not in the foreground NGC 1035 group, while DF2 has a JWST TRGB distance of `17.6+-0.6 Mpc` that conflicts with the older HST TRGB value.
- [2606.04309](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04309) Daniel R. Rybarczyk et al. constrains CO-dark molecular gas with HCO+ absorption in five diffuse-ISM directions. It is mostly local-ISM work, but the result that HCO+ traces gas down to `N(H2)>few x 10^18 cm^-2` is relevant for absorption-line gas diagnostics.
- [2606.04462](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04462) Hai-Feng Wang et al. uses DESI and a clustering algorithm to split the Gaia-Sausage-Enceladus region into four substructures. This is Galactic archaeology rather than the main profile, but it is a DESI structure-analysis example.
