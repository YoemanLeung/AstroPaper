[2607.26177](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26177) Sophie Koudmani is the main high-redshift AGN-formation entry. The observational environment and survey-method set is broad. [2607.26149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26149) SDSS Collaboration presents DR20 with first all-sky BOSS spectra, eROSITA-SDSS-V coordination, and a Local Volume Mapper preview.

Neichel gives the current HARMONI@ELT design and sensitivity picture, relevant for reionization-era galaxy kinematics. [2607.27149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27149) Boryana Hadzhiyska proposes a shear-kSZ estimator for the matter-electron power spectrum, directly tied to baryonic suppression in weak-lensing cosmology. [2607.26085](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26085) Oem Trivedi frames dark-matter-deficient galaxies as tests of dark-matter separability, dissipation, and halo stability.

## JWST AGN, High-Redshift Black Holes, and LRD Gas

- [2607.26269](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26269) Hiroto Yanagisawa et al. `ATLAS. II. Extremely High Incidence of Balmer Line Absorption with Predominant Blueshifts in LRDs: Statistical Insights through Comparison with Type 1 AGNs`
  - **Question:** how common is Balmer-line absorption in little red dots, and does it trace inflow or outflow?
  - **Data:**
    - Spectroscopy: archival JWST/NIRSpec spectra from the DAWN JWST Archive plus complementary NIRSpec/IFU observations.
    - Sample: `40` LRDs with broad H-alpha and [O III] coverage from medium- or high-resolution gratings.
    - Redshift range: `z~2.5-7.2`.
    - Comparison: low-redshift SDSS type 1 AGNs and `32` additional LRD Balmer absorbers from the literature.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify H-alpha and H-beta absorption in the LRD spectra.
    - Compare incidence rates with low-redshift type 1 AGNs.
    - Combine new and literature detections into a Balmer-absorber census.
    - Compare absorber velocity distributions and test whether incompleteness can explain the difference.
    - Interpret velocities with a radiation-pressure model.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - H-alpha absorption appears in `14/40` LRDs, an incidence of `~35%`.
    - The incidence is about `850` times higher than the `~0.04%` rate in SDSS low-redshift type 1 AGNs.
    - The combined LRD absorber census contains `46` objects.
    - Absorber velocities span `-430` to `+140 km s^-1`, narrower than the `-800` to `+1600 km s^-1` range in SDSS type 1 AGNs.
    - `38/46` absorbers, or `83%`, are blueshifted.
    - Most absorbers with `N_H>=10^24 cm^-2` remain gravitationally bound in the analytic model.
  - **Position:** read first because it connects LRD spectral phenomenology, BLR-scale gas motion, and early AGN demographics with direct JWST spectroscopy.

- [2607.26177](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26177) Sophie Koudmani et al. `How to raise a supermassive black hole: interpreting early JWST AGN with the AESOPICA simulations`
  - **Question:** can early JWST AGN samples be explained by ordinary seed channels plus efficient accretion and selection effects?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: AESOPICA suite of `12` mid-volume cosmological simulations based on the FABLE galaxy-formation model.
    - Volume scale: `L=60 Mpc`.
    - Parameter space: black-hole seed masses of `10^2-10^5 M_sun`, accretion efficiency, super-Eddington bursts, and supernova feedback strength.
    - Mock observations: BALMERSOPICA JWST broad-line survey pipeline.
  - **Method:**
    - Vary seed masses across theoretical formation channels.
    - Forward-model broad-line AGN detectability for JWST grating and exposure choices.
    - Compare intrinsic and selected black-hole scaling relations.
    - Identify observables that preserve information on seed channels after rapid growth.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The bulk of the JWST AGN population can be assembled from any seed mass if accretion efficiency is high.
    - Light seeds require the most favorable accretion conditions in the explored parameter space.
    - Broad-line selection naturally recovers apparently overmassive AGN.
    - Selected AGN lie furthest above the intrinsic `M_BH-M_stellar` relation for inefficient accretion models.
    - Selected AGN lie on the local, weakly evolving `M_BH-sigma_stellar` relation.
    - The low-mass black-hole mass function and host gas-phase metallicities remain the best seed-channel discriminants.
  - **Position:** high-priority early-AGN theory paper because it separates seed physics, efficient growth, and JWST selection bias.

- [2607.26125](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26125) Pedro R. Capelo et al. `Emulating the complex galactic-scale orbital dynamics of LISA massive black hole pairs with normalizing flows`
  - **Question:** can MBH-pair orbital decay in complex merger remnants be emulated accurately enough for population-scale predictions?
  - **Data:**
    - Training set: a large suite of semi-analytical orbital integrations.
    - Galaxy models: multi-component merger remnants with rotating stellar disks and bars.
    - Parameter space: MBH masses, orbital configurations, and bar properties.
    - Application: galaxy populations drawn from a cosmological simulation.
  - **Method:**
    - Train conditional normalizing flows on semi-analytical orbital-decay calculations.
    - Reproduce decay-time distributions for secondary MBHs in realistic galactic potentials.
    - Use host morphology, including barred and non-barred systems, to infer binary-formation times across cosmic time.
    - Compare emulator performance with direct simulation cost.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The emulator reproduces the simulation decay-time distributions.
    - Computational cost drops by orders of magnitude relative to direct integrations.
    - Stellar bars can alter the distribution of MBH binary-formation times.
    - Morphology-aware emulation enables LISA-rate work that is not feasible with brute-force simulations alone.
  - **Position:** useful AGN and gravitational-wave infrastructure paper because it links galaxy morphology to MBH merger-time predictions.

## Cosmic Web, Cluster Environments, and Baryons

- [2607.26255](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26255) Pratik Dabhade et al. `Tracing the Cosmic web across Cosmic time through SKA observations of radio galaxies`
  - **Question:** how can SKA radio galaxies trace and diagnose the cosmic web across redshift?
  - **Data:**
    - Facility: Square Kilometre Array continuum observations.
    - Frequency coverage: `50 MHz-15 GHz`.
    - Sensitivity regime: microJy to sub-microJy radio-source populations.
    - Ancillary data: host identifications, spectroscopic redshifts, optical, infrared, X-ray, Sunyaev-Zel'dovich, and cosmic-web catalogs.
  - **Method:**
    - Use radio-galaxy lobes, magnetic fields, and outflows as environmental diagnostics.
    - Measure source size, morphology, spectral ageing, radio power, polarization, and Faraday rotation.
    - Place radio galaxies in three-dimensional large-scale environments.
    - Connect radio-galaxy populations to clusters, filaments, low-density IGM gas, and protoclusters.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - SKA should detect vast radio-source populations across broad redshift and environment ranges.
    - Radio lobes encode IGM pressure, particle ageing, and magnetization over megaparsec scales.
    - Radio galaxies can trace dense clusters, protoclusters, and lower-density cosmic-web structures.
    - Multiwavelength matching is central to turning radio populations into environment probes.
  - **Position:** high-value survey-method paper because it connects radio AGN feedback to cosmic-web and protocluster mapping.

- [2607.26136](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26136) Roan Haggar et al. `Identifying backsplash galaxies using machine learning`
  - **Question:** can observable galaxy properties separate backsplash galaxies from first-infall and preprocessed systems?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: The Three Hundred suite of galaxy-cluster simulations.
    - Target classes: backsplash galaxies and first-infall galaxies in cluster outskirts.
    - Observational test: Virgo Cluster galaxies with asymmetric H I distributions.
  - **Method:**
    - Train a machine-learning classifier on simulated cluster galaxies.
    - Tune purity and completeness tradeoffs.
    - Test performance using different combinations of observable quantities.
    - Apply the classifier to Virgo galaxies with disturbed H I morphologies.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Backsplash samples can reach purity and completeness up to `~70%`.
    - First-infall samples can reach purity and completeness above `80%`.
    - The model can be tuned to optimize either purity or completeness.
    - Virgo galaxies with asymmetric H I distributions are all likely approaching the cluster for the first time.
    - The result supports fast cold-gas removal after cluster entry.
  - **Position:** useful environment paper because it distinguishes preprocessing, first infall, and post-pericenter histories in cluster outskirts.

- [2607.27149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27149) Boryana Hadzhiyska `Shear-kSZ: A New Estimator for the Matter-Electron Power Spectrum from kSZ Tomography and Weak Lensing`
  - **Question:** can kSZ tomography and weak lensing directly measure the matter-electron power spectrum?
  - **Data:**
    - Fields: kinematic Sunyaev-Zel'dovich temperature, line-of-sight velocity field, and weak-lensing convergence.
    - Forecast maps: Simons Observatory-like CMB temperature map, DESI-like luminous red galaxy velocity reconstruction, and LSST-like high-redshift sources.
    - Target statistic: matter-electron cross-power spectrum `P_me(k)`.
  - **Method:**
    - Correlate the kSZ field with reconstructed velocities and weak-lensing convergence.
    - Factorize the estimator into a calibratable velocity kernel and `P_me(k)`.
    - Validate the analytical expression against simulations.
    - Forecast detectability for current and near-future surveys.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The estimator agrees with simulations at percent level.
    - CMB foreground contributions cancel through the parity structure.
    - A Simons Observatory plus DESI plus LSST-like configuration gives a forecast `16 sigma` measurement over `f_sky=0.2`.
    - That corresponds to a `1%` measurement of baryonic suppression `S(k)`.
    - Early LSST data releases could already yield `~10 sigma` detections.
  - **Position:** high-priority baryon-cosmology method because it targets the electron distribution that drives baryonic systematics in cosmic shear.

- [2607.26085](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26085) Oem Trivedi et al. `Dark Matter Deficient Galaxies as Probes of Dark Matter`
  - **Question:** what can dark-matter-deficient galaxies say about dark-sector interactions, dissipation, and halo stability?
  - **Data:**
    - Systems: galaxies with observed low dark-matter-to-baryon ratios.
    - Framework: dark-matter-baryon separability condition.
    - Physical channels: high-speed collisions, dissipative dark matter, and ultralight fuzzy dark-matter escape.
  - **Method:**
    - Define a separability condition from relative baryon and dark-matter incorporation efficiencies.
    - Apply the condition to high-speed galaxy collisions.
    - Constrain dark-matter-baryon momentum transfer cross section and interacting dark-matter fraction.
    - Bound dissipative-dark-matter abundance, cooling time, and fuzzy-dark-matter escape.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Dark-matter-deficient galaxies can constrain late-time dark-matter interactions.
    - The framework links observed baryon-dark-matter separation to gravitational recapture efficiency.
    - Dissipative and fuzzy-dark-matter scenarios face bounds from the same separability logic.
  - **Position:** context paper for halo physics because it uses extreme galaxy systems as low-redshift tests of nonstandard dark matter.

## Survey Catalogs, Instruments, and Nearby-Galaxy Fossils

- [2607.26537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26537) B. Neichel et al. `HARMONI@ELT: instrument design, performance, and science capabilities`
  - **Question:** what science capabilities remain after the HARMONI rescope for ELT first-light near-infrared integral-field spectroscopy?
  - **Data:**
    - Instrument: HARMONI integral-field spectrograph for the Extremely Large Telescope.
    - Wavelength range: `0.75-2.4 micron`.
    - Adaptive optics modes: SCAO, MCAO via MORFEO, and HCAO.
    - Performance quantities: Strehl ratio, PSF FWHM, sky coverage, sensitivity, and astrometric accuracy.
  - **Method:**
    - Present the updated instrument architecture and subsystem design.
    - Quantify top-level technical specifications.
    - Forecast on-sky performance under the revised design.
    - Connect the design to science cases from exoplanets to reionization-era galaxy kinematics.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - HARMONI remains the ELT workhorse near-infrared integral-field spectrograph.
    - It is designed for diffraction-limited spatially resolved spectroscopy.
    - The rescope defines a new project organization, leadership structure, and delivery path.
    - Reionization-era galaxy internal kinematics remain within the stated science capability envelope.
  - **Position:** important instrument-status paper because ELT IFS capabilities set future limits for resolved high-redshift galaxy dynamics.

- [2607.26558](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26558) Junghyun Hwang et al. `An Enhanced Catalog of Gaia DR3 Galaxy Candidates with Spectroscopic and Machine-Learning Photometric Redshifts`
  - **Question:** can Gaia DR3 galaxy candidates become more useful for extragalactic science once redshift information and outlier scoring are added?
  - **Data:**
    - Base sample: Gaia DR3 Galaxy Candidates table.
    - Spectroscopic matches: DESI, SDSS, 2MRS, and NED.
    - Photometry: Gaia DR3, 2MASS, and unWISE, plus shape parameters, QSO flags, and extinction values.
  - **Method:**
    - Cross-match Gaia candidates with existing spectroscopic redshift catalogs.
    - Train a Multiple-Bin Regression Neural Network on the spectroscopic subsample.
    - Estimate photometric redshifts for sources without spectroscopy.
    - Add an outlier-likelihood score to support downstream filtering.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Spectroscopic redshifts are assigned to `24.5%` of catalog sources.
    - Machine-learning photometric redshifts are provided for the remaining objects.
    - The model improves redshift accuracy and precision relative to Gaia-only photo-z estimates.
    - The catalog turns a lower-purity all-sky extragalactic sample into a more usable resource for large-scale studies.
  - **Position:** useful survey-infrastructure entry because all-sky redshift augmentation can support cross-matching, foreground control, and wide-field environment work.

- [2607.26149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26149) SDSS Collaboration et al. `The Twentieth Data Release of the Sloan Digital Sky Survey: First All-Sky BOSS Spectra, eROSITA-SDSS-V Mapper Coordinated Observations, and a Preview of the Local Volume Mapper`
  - **Question:** what new SDSS-V data products are released in DR20?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: Sloan Digital Sky Survey twentieth data release, third release of SDSS-V.
    - Programs: Milky Way Mapper, Local Volume Mapper, and Black Hole Mapper.
    - Spectra: more than `3 million` spectra of `1.5 million` stars and `0.5 million` galaxies and quasars.
    - LVM products: integral-field spectroscopy maps of `6` targets and `169` tiles.
    - Value-added data: `18` value-added catalogs.
  - **Method:**
    - Release first southern-hemisphere optical BOSS SDSS-V spectra.
    - Add new northern-hemisphere optical MWM and BHM data.
    - Coordinate galactic and extragalactic X-ray targets with eROSITA DR2.
    - Preview LVM maps and visualization products.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - DR20 provides the first all-sky BOSS spectral component of SDSS-V.
    - The release links BHM and MWM spectroscopy with eROSITA DR2 target coordination.
    - LVM contributes early integral-field maps of Galactic H II regions, planetary nebulae, and nearby galaxies.
    - The data release is a major catalog and spectroscopy resource rather than a single science-result paper.
  - **Position:** save as infrastructure because SDSS-V BHM and eROSITA coordination can feed AGN, galaxy, and survey-selection work.

- [2607.27052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27052) Borja Anguiano et al. `J-ATLAS: Javalambre Andromeda and Triangulum Legacy Astrophysical Survey`
  - **Question:** what can dense-filter optical photospectroscopy add to the fossil record of the M31-M33 system?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey concept: Javalambre Andromeda and Triangulum Legacy Astrophysical Survey.
    - Facility: JST/T250 and JPCam.
    - Filter system: dense J-PAS optical filters.
    - Pilot survey: `92` complete pointings centered on the Giant Southern Stream and Andromeda I.
  - **Method:**
    - Map resolved stars, compact stellar systems, emission-line sources, and background objects.
    - Use low-resolution optical SEDs for classification and population measurements.
    - Validate crowded-field photometry, calibration, source classification, and metallicity diagnostics on the pilot field.
    - Extend the method to disks, halos, streams, satellites, and intergalactic regions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - J-ATLAS targets the nearest massive spiral-galaxy environment visible from the northern sky.
    - It is designed to constrain stellar populations, photometric metallicities, carbon-rich evolved stars, star clusters, planetary nebulae, and low-surface-brightness substructure.
    - The pilot survey validates methodology before the full M31-M33 footprint.
  - **Position:** nearby-galaxy context paper because it uses local-group fossils to calibrate hierarchical assembly signatures.

## Galaxy Structure, Star Clusters, and Galactic-Center Dynamics

- [2607.26152](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26152) Maria Luisa Buzzo et al. `The Extended Globular Cluster System of the archetypal "failed galaxy" Dragonfly-44 from deep white-light Hubble Space Telescope imaging`
  - **Question:** does deep HST imaging restore Dragonfly-44 as a globular-cluster-rich failed-galaxy benchmark?
  - **Data:**
    - Imaging: ultra-deep HST WFC3/UVIS F350LP observations.
    - Target: Dragonfly-44 with `M_star=3x10^8 M_sun`.
    - Depth: more than one magnitude below the globular-cluster luminosity-function turnover.
  - **Method:**
    - Count globular-cluster candidates with deep white-light HST imaging.
    - Measure the spatial extent of the globular-cluster system.
    - Infer halo mass from globular-cluster richness.
    - Compare the result with stellar-kinematic halo constraints.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Dragonfly-44 hosts `N_GC=78.3+/-3.7` globular clusters.
    - The globular-cluster half-number radius is `R_gc=1.41(+0.57,-0.25) R_e`.
    - The globular-cluster system contains `~5%` of the total stellar mass.
    - The implied halo mass is `log(M_vir/M_sun)=11.6+/-0.3`.
    - The dark-matter fraction exceeds `99.9%`.
  - **Position:** useful galaxy-formation benchmark because it sharpens the failed-galaxy problem for low-efficiency star formation in massive halos.

- [2607.26330](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26330) Debby Tran et al. `Automated Computer Vision Cluster Identification in the Fireworks Galaxy`
  - **Question:** can computer-vision photometry recover young star-cluster candidates in a crowded nearby star-forming galaxy?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: NGC 6946, the Fireworks Galaxy.
    - Imaging: HST WFC3/UVIS F275W and F336W.
    - Population: young star-cluster candidates with ages `<=25 Myr`.
    - Artificial-source tests: synthetic clusters down to `M_F336W~-6`.
  - **Method:**
    - Build a modified computer-vision algorithm to identify and outline candidate clusters.
    - Test recovery and false-positive behavior with artificial clusters.
    - Measure integrated photometry, radii, and spatial distributions.
    - Compare the candidate luminosity function with a standard power law.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The algorithm recovers `60.7%` of synthetic clusters in the artificial-cluster tests.
    - The conservative false-positive rate is `27.3%` at the tested depth.
    - The full candidate catalog contains `6410` cluster candidates down to `M_F336W~-4`.
    - The candidate luminosity function is consistent with a power-law slope of `~2`.
  - **Position:** method context for resolved stellar-population work, mainly useful as a computer-vision cataloging example.

- [2607.26713](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26713) Arianna Foschi et al. `Effects of a disk structure on stellar motion at the Galactic Center`
  - **Question:** how would disk-like extended mass around Sgr A* bias stellar-orbit tests of the Galactic Center potential?
  - **Data:**
    - Targets: S2, S301, and the clockwise stellar disk.
    - Potential model: thin disk with surface-density profile `Sigma proportional to r^-gamma`.
    - Comparison effects: spherical extended mass, post-Newtonian black-hole terms, and Lense-Thirring precession.
  - **Method:**
    - Derive the acceleration from the disk-like mass distribution.
    - Compute osculating equations and orbital-element variations.
    - Compare in-plane and out-of-plane precession with relativistic terms.
    - Evaluate how disk parameters affect constraints on non-luminous mass.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Disk-like mass induces a secular semi-latus-rectum shift.
    - It also produces extra in-plane and out-of-plane precession.
    - The out-of-plane precession can compete with Lense-Thirring precession for S301 depending on disk mass, radial extent, and orientation.
    - S2 can place upper limits on disk-like non-luminous mass because its Lense-Thirring signal is negligible.
  - **Position:** Galactic Center context paper because extended mass geometry can mimic or obscure black-hole-spin tests.
