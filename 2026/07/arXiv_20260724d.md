## Executive View

The read-first paper is [2607.20606](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20606) Matteo Sapori, which uses `811` JWST/NIRSpec PRISM spectra at `3<z<7` to search for Na I D neutral-gas absorption in the first `2 Gyr`. The detection fraction is low overall but rises sharply in massive and quenched systems, making it directly relevant to early feedback, quenching, and JWST spectral-systematics work.

Two theoretical and early-Universe entries are close behind. [2607.20651](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20651) Hovav Lazare connects bursty JWST-era star formation to the 21-cm Cosmic Dawn power spectrum through Lyman-alpha and X-ray background fluctuations. [2607.20617](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20617) Pau Amaro Seoane offers a dust-free supermassive-star envelope model for Little Red Dots, targeting the same broad-line, red-optical, blue-UV, and X-ray-weak phenomenology now driving many LRD interpretations.

The gas and environment set is practical. [2607.20601](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20601) Scott Lucchini recalibrates Milky Way halo dispersion-measure foregrounds with CGM-refined ENGAWA simulations. [2607.21560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21560) Cameron Lawlor-Forsyth classifies inside-out and outside-in quenching signatures in Hubble Frontier Fields galaxies. [2607.21140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21140) D. T. Mazengo compares Magneticum, IllustrisTNG, and SIMBA against hot-halo gas constraints, highlighting where AGN feedback still fails.

For survey infrastructure, [2607.21122](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21122) C. L. Hale releases MIGHTEE DR1 radio host associations across `7.5 deg^2`, and [2607.20797](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20797) Kendrick Nguyen tests SPHEREx plus DESI Legacy multimodal star-galaxy separation for low-contamination large-scale-structure work.

## High-Redshift Feedback, LRDs, and Cosmic Dawn

- [2607.20606](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20606) Matteo Sapori et al. `Outflows in the Early Universe: Neutral gas Absorption in Galaxies at z > 3 from low-resolution JWST Spectroscopy`
  - **Question:** are neutral-gas outflows already common in massive galaxies during the first `2 Gyr`?
  - **Data:**
    - Spectroscopy: `811` public JWST/NIRSpec PRISM spectra from the DAWN JWST Archive and Mirage or Miracle.
    - Redshift range: secure spectroscopic galaxies at `3<z<7`.
    - Quality cut: continuum `S/N>5` at PRISM resolution `R~100`.
    - Feature: Na I D absorption after stellar-continuum subtraction.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit galaxy physical properties and star-formation histories with Prospector.
    - Subtract the best-fit stellar continuum around Na I D.
    - Search individual spectra for excess absorption.
    - Stack subsamples to test stellar-mass, dust, and quiescence trends.
    - Estimate conservative mass outflow rates for detected systems.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Excess Na I D absorption is detected in `20` galaxies.
    - Most detections are at `3<z<5` and have `M_star>10^10 M_sun`.
    - The overall detection fraction is `~2.5%`.
    - The detection fraction rises to `~11%` among the most massive galaxies and `~43%` among massive quenched systems.
    - Na I D equivalent widths span `4-16 Angstrom`.
    - Conservative mass outflow rates are `4-12 M_sun/yr`; about `30%` of detected galaxies have rates above their current SFR.
  - **Position:** read first because it turns low-resolution JWST spectra into a direct early-feedback and quenching diagnostic, while keeping the kinematic limitation explicit.

- [2607.20617](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20617) Pau Amaro Seoane `A dust-free hierarchically nested supermassive-star model for James Webb Space Telescope Little Red Dots`
  - **Question:** can dust-free supermassive-star envelopes explain Little Red Dot colors, broad lines, and weak X-ray emission?
  - **Data:**
    - Phenomenology: high-redshift Little Red Dots with red optical continua, blue UV continua, broad Balmer lines, and weak or absent X-rays.
    - Model object: a radiation-dominated primary envelope trapping a nuclear star cluster.
    - Accretion target: central seed inside a high-density secondary core.
  - **Method:**
    - Treat plunging stars as drivers of a magnetic dynamo.
    - Inflate an envelope that produces the red optical continuum.
    - Use unobscured infalling stars for the blue UV excess.
    - Thermalize X-rays inside a Compton-thick nested structure.
    - Compare the implied assembly timescale with standard Eddington-limited growth.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The model removes the need for special dust geometry in the dust-poor LRD subset.
    - The nested envelope can hide X-rays while leaving broad H-alpha emission.
    - Accretion at the host-envelope radiation limit shortens seed assembly from hundreds of Myr to tens of Myr.
    - The proposal is a compact physical alternative to dust-obscured AGN interpretations.
  - **Position:** useful LRD theory entry because it gives a falsifiable dust-free channel for the same observables currently attributed to compact obscured accretion.

- [2607.20651](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20651) Hovav Lazare et al. `When galaxies burst II. Implications of enhanced burstiness for the 21-cm Cosmic Dawn signal`
  - **Question:** how does bursty high-redshift star formation alter the 21-cm Cosmic Dawn power spectrum?
  - **Data:**
    - Framework: analytical Zeus21 calculations.
    - Radiation fields: non-local Lyman-alpha and X-ray backgrounds.
    - Star-formation model: stochastic burst amplitude and coherence time.
  - **Method:**
    - Introduce an unequal-time correlation in the star-formation-rate density.
    - Propagate burstiness into Lyman-alpha and X-ray field auto-power.
    - Include burstiness-driven cross-power terms.
    - Evaluate the resulting 21-cm power-spectrum response during Cosmic Dawn.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Burstiness creates a shot-noise-like contribution in Lyman-alpha and X-ray backgrounds.
    - The effect enhances auto- and cross-power spectra of the radiation fields.
    - JWST-inferred burstiness can therefore change the timing and amplitude of the 21-cm signal.
    - The paper links galaxy-scale stochastic star formation to large-scale reionization-era observables.
  - **Position:** important reionization-method paper because it translates JWST galaxy physics into a forecastable 21-cm observable.

## CGM, Gas Reservoirs, and Environment

- [2607.20601](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20601) Scott Lucchini et al. `A New Model for the Milky Way Halo Dispersion Measure with the ENGAWA Simulations: Low DMs and Large Anisotropy`
  - **Question:** how large is the Milky Way halo dispersion-measure foreground for FRB and cosmic-baryon work?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: ENGAWA cosmological zoom-ins of `4` Milky Way-like galaxies.
    - Resolution focus: enhanced CGM resolution.
    - Observable: all-sky halo dispersion measure as seen from solar-like locations.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure halo DM through simulated Milky Way analog CGM.
    - Vary observer position within the simulated galaxies.
    - Compare with earlier halo-DM models.
    - Release a Python API for Galactic-longitude and Galactic-latitude DM estimates.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Median all-sky halo DM spans `19-39 pc cm^-3`.
    - Some polar sightlines reach `<10 pc cm^-3`.
    - Halo DM varies strongly even at fixed feedback strength, halo mass, and `f_CGM`.
    - The simulations predict lower mean halo DM and larger anisotropy than previous models.
  - **Position:** useful CGM foreground paper because Milky Way halo subtraction propagates directly into FRB baryon and extragalactic-gas inference.

- [2607.21560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21560) Cameron Lawlor-Forsyth et al. `Identifying and Distinguishing Quenching Galaxies with Spatially Resolved Star Formation in the Hubble Frontier Fields`
  - **Question:** can resolved star-formation maps distinguish inside-out and outside-in quenching pathways?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `1437` Hubble Frontier Fields galaxies.
    - Cuts: `M_star>=10^8 M_sun` and `SFR>=10^-3 M_sun/yr`.
    - Method input: spatially resolved SED fitting with FAST++.
    - Environments: cluster and field populations.
  - **Method:**
    - Quantify the distribution of star formation with morphology metrics.
    - Train a k-nearest-neighbors classifier for quenching signatures.
    - Separate inside-out and outside-in pathways.
    - Compare fractions against stellar mass, environment, and estimated infall time.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - `129` galaxies are consistent with inside-out quenching.
    - `70` galaxies are consistent with outside-in quenching.
    - Inside-out systems are `0.8 dex` more massive than outside-in systems.
    - Inside-out quenching galaxies are more common in clusters, `106/129`.
    - Outside-in quenching remains `<=10%` of the non-quenched population in both cluster and field samples.
  - **Position:** useful environment paper because it ties resolved quenching morphology to cluster membership and infall context.

- [2607.20626](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20626) Bumhyun Lee et al. `A Naked Dwarf: Molecular Gas in the Completely Stripped HI Tail of VCC 1249`
  - **Question:** can molecular gas be stripped from a dwarf galaxy together with H I during rapid cluster quenching?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: Virgo dwarf galaxy VCC 1249 and its stripped H I tail.
    - Instrument: JCMT deep CO(2-1) observations.
    - Detections: marginal CO in `3` regions of the stripped tail.
  - **Method:**
    - Search for CO emission in the stellar disk and stripped H I tail.
    - Convert marginal CO detections into molecular-gas masses.
    - Interpret the gas distribution against tidal and ram-pressure stripping.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - CO is detected only marginally in the stripped tail, not in the stellar disk.
    - Molecular masses are `~10^5-10^6 M_sun`, comparable to giant molecular clouds.
    - The cold-gas removal likely caused abrupt cessation of star formation.
    - VCC 1249 is a direct local case of environment-driven gas stripping and dwarf quenching.
  - **Position:** useful low-redshift analog because it connects environmental gas removal to a rapid dwarf phase transition.

- [2607.21034](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21034) Qingze Chen et al. `A RELHIC twin candidate near the galaxy M51`
  - **Question:** do compact starless H I clouds near M51 trace reionization-limited dark halos or tidal debris?
  - **Data:**
    - Instrument: FAST FEASTS observations.
    - Objects: Cloud S and Cloud N at projected distances `70-90 kpc` from M51.
    - Optical limit: no counterpart to `g~27.5 mag arcsec^-2`.
  - **Method:**
    - Search for compact H I sources with SoFiA.
    - Remove optical counterparts using DESI Legacy Imaging cross-matches.
    - Model remaining clouds as hydrostatic H I structures in NFW halos.
    - Compare with RELHIC predictions and TNG50 simulations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Each cloud has `M_HI~10^6.5 M_sun`.
    - Velocity dispersion is about `20 km/s`.
    - Stellar luminosity is constrained below `10^5 L_sun`.
    - RELHIC fits imply host halo masses of `3.7+/-0.4 x 10^9 M_sun`.
    - A tidal origin remains possible because M51 is interacting and the FAST clouds are unresolved.
  - **Position:** relevant neutral-gas entry because starless H I candidates probe the low-mass halo boundary set by reionization and environment.

## AGN, Radio Surveys, and Feedback Models

- [2607.21140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21140) D. T. Mazengo et al. `What's Missing in AGN Feedback? Lessons learnt from Magneticum, IllustrisTNG and Simba`
  - **Question:** which AGN feedback ingredients are missing when simulations are tested against galaxy and hot-halo gas constraints together?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: Magneticum, IllustrisTNG, and SIMBA.
    - Observational comparisons: MaNGA spatially resolved galaxy data.
    - Hot-gas constraints: eROSITA and Sunyaev-Zel'dovich measurements of the `f_gas-M_h` relation.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare gas reservoirs, star formation, and feedback outcomes across three simulation models.
    - Test local galaxy properties and hot-halo gas fractions simultaneously.
    - Diagnose which feedback strengths reproduce hot gas while preserving galaxy observables.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Modern simulations still struggle to reproduce galaxy properties and hot-halo gas fractions at the same time.
    - Matching the observed hot-gas relation requires strong AGN feedback.
    - Strong feedback can create tension with other galaxy-scale properties.
    - The missing piece is not only feedback energy, but how and where that energy couples to circumgalactic gas.
  - **Position:** important simulation-audit paper because it evaluates AGN feedback against CGM thermodynamics, not only quenched fractions.

- [2607.21122](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21122) C. L. Hale et al. `MIGHTEE: The Host-Galaxy Associated Catalogue of the Radio Sources in MIGHTEE Continuum Data Release 1`
  - **Question:** can MIGHTEE DR1 radio sources be robustly associated with host galaxies and redshifts?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: MeerKAT International GHz Tiered Extragalactic Exploration continuum DR1.
    - Area: `7.5 deg^2` in fields with strong ancillary data.
    - Output: about `66,000` radio sources after radio-component association.
    - Counterparts: Ks-band selected hosts plus redshifts.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine statistical cross-matching with visual inspection.
    - Merge multiple radio components into single physical sources where needed.
    - Attach multi-wavelength host information and redshift estimates.
    - Package the result as a host-galaxy associated catalogue.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The catalogue provides host associations for a large MeerKAT radio continuum sample.
    - It supports radio-selected SFG and AGN demographic work in well-studied extragalactic fields.
    - The matching framework reduces ambiguity for multi-component radio sources.
  - **Position:** save for survey infrastructure because radio AGN and dust-insensitive SFR samples need host-redshift association before high-redshift interpretation.

- [2607.21029](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21029) Yaqi Chen et al. `Dissecting the multiple-component outflow in NGC 5548 with absorption-line Variability`
  - **Question:** can absorption-line variability measure radial distances for blended AGN outflow components?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: NGC 5548.
    - Spectroscopy: multi-epoch HST/COS data from the 2014 AGN STORM campaign plus 2013 archive data.
    - Components: `6` UV outflow components.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure absorption-line variability detection-rate curves.
    - Use multi-step profiles to separate blended velocity components.
    - Infer recombination timescales for individual components.
    - Convert recombination timescales into radial distances.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Four components are a few parsecs from the central engine.
    - Two components are `30-40 pc` away.
    - The method agrees with prior more secure distance estimates for selected components.
  - **Position:** useful AGN-method paper because outflow distance is the key lever for turning absorption variability into feedback energetics.

## Survey and Inference Methods

- [2607.20797](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20797) Kendrick Nguyen et al. `A Multimodal Approach to Star--Galaxy Separation using SPHEREx Spectrophotometry and DESI Legacy Survey Imaging`
  - **Question:** can SPHEREx low-resolution spectrophotometry improve star-galaxy separation for precision large-scale-structure surveys?
  - **Data:**
    - Inputs: optical broad-band imaging and SPHEREx near-IR low-resolution spectrophotometry.
    - Imaging source: DESI Legacy Survey.
    - Target systematic: sub-percent stellar contamination for local primordial non-Gaussianity constraints.
  - **Method:**
    - Train multimodal models using both image and spectrum information.
    - Use contrastive learning to project image and spectrum embeddings into a shared latent space.
    - Compare classifiers trained on transformed and original representations.
    - Evaluate contamination control for LSS use cases.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Joint SPHEREx plus imaging embeddings outperform single-modality or original-feature classifiers.
    - The method targets stellar contamination rates needed for `sigma(f_NL^loc)~O(1)` analyses.
    - Spectrophotometry adds information that broad-band imaging alone cannot cleanly supply.
  - **Position:** relevant survey-method paper because foreground classification can mimic or erase the largest-scale cosmological signal.

- [2607.20985](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20985) Hilay Shah et al. `Accurate Extragalactic Magnetic Fields from Faraday Rotation with Optimal Dispersion Measure Estimators`
  - **Question:** how can rotation measures be converted into magnetic-field strengths when direct dispersion measures are unavailable?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: high-resolution MHD simulations across galactic environments.
    - Observables: rotation measure, emission measure, and H I column density.
    - Environments: galaxy centers, outskirts, dwarfs, and spirals.
  - **Method:**
    - Create simulated observations of RM and proxy DM estimators.
    - Test estimators based on emission measure and H I column density.
    - Calibrate environment-dependent power-law exponents.
    - Derive best practices for proxy availability regimes.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The best estimator has the form `DM proportional to EM^alpha N_HI^beta`.
    - Typical exponents are `alpha~0.2-0.4` and `beta~0-0.1`.
    - Environment-dependent calibration can recover magnetic fields to within a few tenths of a dex.
    - The method prepares RM data for SKA-era extragalactic magnetic-field work.
  - **Position:** useful gas-physics method because magnetic-field measurements need electron-column calibration before they can constrain feedback or CGM structure.
