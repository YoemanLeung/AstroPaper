## Executive View

The read-first paper is [2607.19938](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19938) Vid Irsic, which gives a direct high-redshift pressure-smoothing measurement from the Lyman-alpha forest at `z=4.2-5.0`. It is the strongest profile match because it connects reionization heating, cosmic-web gas dynamics, and observable small-scale forest power.

The most important high-redshift galaxy result is [2607.19491](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19491) Hyewon Suh, a JWST/NIRSpec plus ALMA discovery of a dual AGN at `z~4.5` with `~1.5 kpc` projected separation. It directly targets obscured black-hole growth during early gas-rich mergers. [2607.19471](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19471) Sergio Martinez-Gonzalez gives a compact theoretical explanation for dust bimodality across `6<z<14`, tying blue JWST galaxies and dusty massive ALMA systems to clustered supernova dust retention and escape.

The IGM and CGM set is also strong. [2607.20371](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20371) Chanasorn Kongprachaya constrains 21-cm forest absorption toward a `z=5.84` quasar and disfavors extremely cold neutral patches below `z=5.84`. [2607.19473](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19473) Derick Flores uses OVI absorption to show that massive star-forming galaxies have much stronger warm-hot inner CGM reservoirs than quiescent galaxies.

Two survey-method papers should be saved for planning. [2607.19504](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19504) Anirban Roy forward-models dust, continuum, and PCA cleaning for optical/near-IR multi-line intensity mapping. [2607.20404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20404) A. Dev simulates CCAT DSS [C II] spectral-cube cleaning and quantifies the power-spectrum transfer function.

## IGM, Reionization, and 21-cm Forest

- [2607.19938](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19938) Vid Irsic et al. `Ringing of the Reionization: A first direct measurement of the intergalactic pressure smoothing scale at redshift z>4.2 as imprinted onto small-scale peculiar velocities in the Lyman-alpha forest`
  - **Question:** can the Lyman-alpha forest directly measure the pressure-smoothing scale left by reionization heating?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: toy models and linear-theory tests of the projected peculiar-velocity-gradient power spectrum.
    - Observations: latest Lyman-alpha forest flux-power measurements at `z=4.2-5.0`.
    - Scale range: small-scale forest power at `k=0.1-1 s/km`.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify an acoustic feature in the projected peculiar-velocity-gradient power spectrum.
    - Test its association with pressure smoothing in simulations.
    - Map the same feature into small-scale Lyman-alpha forest flux power.
    - Apply the simulation-calibrated method to observed high-redshift flux-power data.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The acoustic feature is strongly associated with the pressure-smoothing scale.
    - The inferred pressure-smoothing scale is `lambda_p(z=4.2)>33.78 ckpc` at `1 sigma`.
    - The inferred scale is `lambda_p(z=4.6)=32.36+/-8.35 ckpc`.
    - The inferred scale is `lambda_p(z=5.0)=31.27+/-18.28 ckpc`.
    - Small-scale forest observations can recover reionization thermal history directly, not only through indirect power-spectrum trends.
  - **Position:** read first for IGM work because it turns reionization heating into a measurable cosmic-web gas scale at the redshifts where thermal-memory constraints matter.

- [2607.20371](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20371) Chanasorn Kongprachaya et al. `The burgeoning rise of the 21-cm forest I: constraints on the optical depth of the intergalactic medium at 5.38 < z < 5.84`
  - **Question:** do radio observations of a high-redshift quasar reveal 21-cm forest absorption from residual neutral hydrogen near the end of reionization?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: radio-loud quasar PSO J352.4034-15.3373 at `z=5.84`.
    - Observation: `18 h` integration over `203-222.5 MHz`.
    - Imaging: continuum rms `0.66 mJy/beam`.
    - Spectrum: rms `3.6 mJy/beam` per `390 kHz` channel.
    - Ancillary data: archival fluxes from `150 MHz` to `3.0 GHz`.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit the quasar continuum with a power law plus possible intervening 21-cm absorption.
    - Test an island model for residual cold neutral patches.
    - Test a global model for redshift evolution in neutral fraction and spin temperature.
    - Combine new and archival radio measurements to anchor the continuum.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - No intervening 21-cm absorption is detected at `5.38<z<5.84`.
    - The quasar flux density is `86.8+/-1.1 mJy` at `200 MHz`, with spectral index `-0.88+/-0.02`.
    - The island model gives a `95%` confidence lower limit `T_spin>1.73 K` in residual HI regions.
    - The global model gives `tau_21<0.02` at `95%` confidence.
    - The IGM was heated above the adiabatic cooling limit of `~0.8 K` at `z=5.68`.
  - **Position:** useful 21-cm forest constraint because it links quasar absorption searches to the thermal state of post-overlap neutral gas.

- [2607.20123](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20123) Mohd Kamran et al. `Modeling the HI-Halo Connection: Evolution, Scatter, and a Halo-based Prescription for 21-cm Mock Catalogs`
  - **Question:** how should HI be assigned to dark-matter halos for post-reionization 21-cm intensity-mapping mocks?
  - **Data:**
    - Model: updated GAEA semi-analytic model.
    - Simulations: Millennium-I and Millennium-II halo catalogs.
    - Redshift range: present day to `z~5`.
    - Observable benchmark: HI mass function and HI-halo mass relation.
  - **Method:**
    - Predict the HI mass function across redshift.
    - Measure the median HI-halo mass relation and its scatter.
    - Identify secondary dependences on halo spin, formation time, and concentration.
    - Encode the relation in a prescription using dark-matter halo-catalog quantities.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At `z=0`, the model reproduces the observed HI mass function and its host-halo mass decomposition.
    - The median HI-halo relation peaks near `10^11.7 M_sun`.
    - The total scatter is about `0.5 dex`.
    - Spin plus concentration or formation time explains part of the scatter and leaves about `0.3 dex` intrinsic dispersion.
    - The prescription enables large-volume 21-cm mock catalogs for MeerKAT and SKA-era intensity mapping.
  - **Position:** relevant survey-inference entry because HI assignment is a limiting systematic for interpreting large-scale 21-cm maps.

## High-Redshift Galaxies, Dust, and AGN

- [2607.19491](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19491) Hyewon Suh et al. `Rapid growth in a dual AGN during a gas-rich merger at z~4.5`
  - **Question:** can close dual AGN be confirmed during early gas-rich mergers beyond the local Universe?
  - **Data:**
    - Source: LID-1166 at `z~4.5`.
    - Instrument: JWST/NIRSpec integral-field spectroscopy.
    - Supporting gas tracer: ALMA [C II] `158 um`.
    - Geometry: projected dual-AGN separation `~1.5 kpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Resolve narrow-line emission into two spatially and kinematically distinct components.
    - Identify broad H-alpha emission associated with both components.
    - Compare ionized-gas kinematics with ALMA [C II] cold-gas components.
    - Place the SMBHs against the local black-hole-host mass relation.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - LID-1166 is a dual AGN with projected separation `~1.5 kpc` at `z~4.5`.
    - The two narrow-line components have line-of-sight velocity offset `~-164 km/s`.
    - Broad H-alpha from both components confirms two actively accreting SMBHs.
    - ALMA [C II] shows matching spatially and kinematically distinct cold-gas components.
    - The SMBHs already lie on the local black-hole-host mass relation for massive elliptical galaxies within uncertainties.
  - **Position:** high-priority AGN result because it exposes a hidden, merger-driven SMBH growth phase that JWST/NIRSpec IFU and ALMA can jointly confirm.

- [2607.19471](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19471) Sergio Martinez-Gonzalez et al. `The first dusty galaxies across 6 < z < 14: Blue monsters, red monsters, and the bimodality of dust content in early galaxies`
  - **Question:** why do early galaxies split into extremely blue dust-poor systems and red dusty massive systems?
  - **Data:**
    - Observational regime: JWST galaxies at `z>10` with `beta_UV<-2.4`.
    - Comparison population: JWST and ALMA dusty massive systems with `beta_UV>-1`.
    - Model inputs: clustered supernova dust production, shock processing, radiative losses, and mechanical venting.
  - **Method:**
    - Model cloud-scale mechanical blowout from clustered supernovae.
    - Follow breakout from stratified high-redshift disks.
    - Convert retained shock-processed dust to UV-continuum slope.
    - Evaluate the transition between cloud plus disk breakout and retained dust columns.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Efficient cloud blowout and disk breakout produce blue-monster slopes `beta_UV<-2.4`.
    - Larger stellar masses increase retained dust and produce red-monster slopes `beta_UV>-1.5`.
    - Radiative losses can push dusty systems to `beta_UV~-0.5`.
    - The transition depends on whether clustered-supernova ejecta cross both the natal cloud and the galactic gas layer.
    - The same framework connects dust-poor `z>10` galaxies to dusty massive systems at `z~6-7`.
  - **Position:** useful cosmic-dawn interpretation paper because it makes early dust diversity depend on resolved feedback geometry instead of only stellar mass or dust yield.

- [2607.19478](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19478) Leonardo Trobbiani et al. `A pilot sample of Planck-selected strongly lensed sub-mm galaxies: NOEMA observations and physical characterisation`
  - **Question:** can NOEMA confirm and characterize Planck-selected high-redshift strongly lensed sub-mm galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `4` lensed high-redshift galaxy candidates selected from Planck.
    - Instrument: NOEMA continuum and line imaging in `2 mm` and `3 mm` bands.
    - Resolution: down to `1.2 arcsec` spatial resolution and `40 km/s` spectral resolution.
    - Lines: CO(3-2), CO(4-3), CO(5-4), and [CI](1-0) for one source.
  - **Method:**
    - Image continuum and molecular-line emission.
    - Fit line profiles and construct moment maps.
    - Derive spectroscopic redshifts.
    - Model gravitational lensing for one source.
    - Compare CO luminosity and linewidth against unlensed-galaxy relations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Multiple continuum components in at least two sources support strong lensing.
    - Two CO lines are detected at high signal-to-noise in all four sources.
    - Spectroscopic redshifts span `2.3<z<3.3` with `delta z~10^-4`.
    - All four sources lie above the CO luminosity-linewidth relation for unlensed galaxies.
    - Three sources show broad double-peaked profiles with `FWHM>400 km/s` and velocity gradients.
  - **Position:** useful dusty-galaxy follow-up because lensing turns Planck-selected sub-mm sources into resolved molecular-gas laboratories at cosmic noon.

- [2607.19970](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19970) Henrique Miranda et al. `Nebular continuum in high-redshift galaxies with JWST`
  - **Question:** when does nebular continuum bias stellar-population fitting of high-redshift star-forming galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `54` star-forming galaxies from the DAWN JWST Archive.
    - Redshift range: `1.7<z<3.9`.
    - Tool: FADO optical spectral fitting with stellar and nebular continuum components.
    - Diagnostic threshold: nebular continuum fraction `X_neb=8%`.
  - **Method:**
    - Select spectra meeting quality requirements for population synthesis.
    - Fit rest-frame optical spectra with stellar and nebular continuum emission.
    - Compare `X_neb` with H-alpha and H-beta equivalent widths.
    - Test how galaxies above and below the `8%` threshold differ in physical properties.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - H-alpha and H-beta equivalent widths remain suitable tracers of `X_neb` at high redshift.
    - The `X_neb=8%` threshold remains a useful boundary for significant nebular-continuum bias.
    - Galaxies above the threshold are generally less massive, younger, more actively star-forming, and less dusty.
    - These galaxies are light- and mass-dominated by populations younger than `20 Myr`.
    - Ignoring nebular continuum can bias JWST-era optical spectral fitting at cosmic noon.
  - **Position:** practical JWST analysis paper because stellar masses and ages for young galaxies can be biased if nebular continuum is omitted.

## CGM, Feedback, and Galaxy-Environment Benchmarks

- [2607.19473](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19473) Derick Flores et al. `The GOLIATH Survey: OVI Absorption Reveals CGM Evolution through the Starburst-to-Quiescent Transition in Massive Galaxies`
  - **Question:** how does warm-hot CGM OVI change across the starburst-to-quiescent transition in massive galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: GOLIATH, targeting the multiphase CGM of massive galaxies.
    - Galaxy population: blue starburst and post-starburst systems with mean `log M_star/M_sun~11`.
    - Redshift: mean `z~0.43`.
    - CGM region: inner halo with `R/R_vir<=0.6`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure OVI absorption in the inner CGM.
    - Compare OVI column density with stellar mass and specific SFR.
    - Contrast massive star-forming and quiescent galaxies in the same mass bin.
    - Interpret line widths and columns against feedback-driven radiative-cooling scenarios.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - OVI column density rises by nearly `1 dex` from `log M_star/M_sun~8` to `~11.5`.
    - The strongest star-forming galaxies reach `log N_OVI>15`.
    - In `log M_star/M_sun=[11,12)`, massive star-forming galaxies exceed quiescent systems by a factor of `~3` in OVI column density.
    - CGM OVI mass differs by `~1.5 dex`, with `log M_OVI/M_sun~7.3` versus `~5.8`.
    - Covering fractions are `62.5%` versus `24%` at `log N_OVI>=14`.
    - Cooling times of `~10-100 Myr` require continuous active-feedback replenishment.
  - **Position:** strong low-redshift CGM anchor because it links warm-hot halo gas to active feedback on short cooling timescales.

- [2607.20406](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20406) Cameron Lawlor-Forsyth `Estimating Cluster Galaxy Infall Time from Phase Space in TNG`
  - **Question:** can projected phase space provide usable cluster-galaxy infall-time estimates for observations?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: TNG300 and TNG-Cluster.
    - Host selection: clusters with `M_h>=10^14 M_sun`.
    - Redshift coverage: snapshots with `z<=1`.
    - Outputs: public infall-time data products and tutorial.
  - **Method:**
    - Estimate infall time from cluster phase space in simulations.
    - Compare the estimates with prior literature behavior.
    - Package the resulting products for use when direct orbital histories are unavailable.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Phase-space infall-time estimates are consistent with earlier simulation-based results.
    - The estimator can be applied to observations using only projected phase-space position.
    - The released products provide a baseline for dense-environment orbital-history studies.
  - **Position:** useful environment-method note because infall time is often the hidden axis behind cluster quenching, morphology, and satellite evolution trends.

## Line-Intensity Mapping and Survey Inference

- [2607.19504](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19504) Anirban Roy et al. `Reading Between the Lines: Forward Modeling Dust, Continuum, and Spectral Cleaning for Multi-Line Intensity Mapping`
  - **Question:** how do dust attenuation and continuum cleaning affect optical and near-IR multi-line intensity-mapping observables?
  - **Data:**
    - Mock survey: `~2 deg^2` lightcone from an N-body simulation populated by a physics-based semi-analytic model.
    - Lines: H-alpha, H-beta, [O III] 5007, and [O II] 3727.
    - Components: stellar continuum plus line emission with nebular dust attenuation tied to galaxy properties.
    - Statistics: cross-channel angular power spectra and normalized correlation matrices.
  - **Method:**
    - Build intensity cubes for continuum and strong optical lines.
    - Measure cross-channel correlations between wavelength slices.
    - Isolate same-redshift line ridges in line-only maps.
    - Add continuum and apply PCA-based spectral cleaning.
    - Estimate the line-transfer function from simulation truth.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Line-only maps show same-redshift ridges that multi-line intensity mapping can use to isolate large-scale structure.
    - Dust suppresses line cross-power in a galaxy-, wavelength-, and line-pair-dependent way.
    - A single global amplitude cannot capture the dust effect.
    - Continuum emission dominates raw correlations and hides much of the line-ridge structure.
    - Removing `20` PCA modes gives the best trade-off between line recovery and continuum suppression in the mock maps.
  - **Position:** important survey-method paper because MLIM inference needs transfer functions for dust and spectral cleaning, not just idealized line maps.

- [2607.20404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20404) A. Dev et al. `Spectral Data-cube Cleaning for CCAT Deep Spectroscopic Survey. I. Effect of correlated noise and filtering on the power spectrum`
  - **Question:** how much [C II] power-spectrum signal survives realistic CCAT DSS spectral-cube cleaning?
  - **Data:**
    - Instrument: EoR-Spec on the Fred Young Submillimeter Telescope.
    - Survey: CCAT Deep Spectroscopic Survey.
    - Simulations: end-to-end observations including instrument response, astrophysical emission, atmospheric noise, and observing strategy.
    - Signal: redshifted [C II] emission with CO contamination in the combined power spectrum.
  - **Method:**
    - Simulate atmospheric `1/f` noise and instrumental systematics.
    - Apply a Filter-and-Bin pipeline.
    - Quantify residual correlated noise.
    - Measure the recovered power-spectrum transfer function.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The pipeline suppresses atmospheric `1/f` noise by about `4` orders of magnitude at low temporal frequencies.
    - A single EoR-Spec module at `50%` observing efficiency should detect combined [C II]+CO power on shot-noise-dominated scales with `k>0.1 Mpc^-1`.
    - Two modules at full efficiency can detect the signal across all targeted spatial scales.
    - The transfer function exceeds `80%` at `k>0.5 Mpc^-1`.
    - The transfer function falls below `20%` at `k<0.1 Mpc^-1`, so large-scale clustering needs improved map-making.
  - **Position:** useful LIM readiness paper because it separates feasible shot-noise recovery from the harder large-scale mode-reconstruction problem.

- [2607.19808](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19808) Kana Moriwaki et al. `From Dark Matter to Galaxies: Halo-Free Mock Generation via Conditional Point-Cloud Diffusion`
  - **Question:** can galaxy catalogs be generated directly from dark-matter density fields without halo finding?
  - **Data:**
    - Training set: IllustrisTNG hydrodynamical galaxy catalogs.
    - Input: three-dimensional dark-matter density fields.
    - Output: galaxy point clouds with positions and physical properties including SFR.
    - Benchmark volume: `(151.3 Mpc)^3` for galaxies with `SFR>1 M_sun/yr`.
  - **Method:**
    - Train a conditional point-cloud diffusion model.
    - Generate galaxies directly from density fields.
    - Compare one- and two-dimensional distributions of SFR and stellar mass.
    - Test galaxy auto-power and galaxy-dark-matter cross-power spectra.
    - Optimize the diffusion sampling schedule for rapid mock production.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Generated catalogs reproduce the spatial association of galaxies with dense regions and filaments.
    - The catalogs reproduce SFR and stellar-mass distributions.
    - Galaxy auto-power and galaxy-dark-matter cross-power are accurately recovered.
    - The method populates structures below the input density-field resolution by marginalizing over unresolved small-scale structure.
    - A `(151.3 Mpc)^3` catalog can be generated in about `10 s` on one GPU.
  - **Position:** relevant simulation-inference method because field-level mock generation could support redshift-survey and LIM covariance ensembles without halo-catalog bottlenecks.

## AGN and Spectral Modeling

- [2607.19603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19603) Arjun Suresh et al. `AGN Feedback Models and AGN Demographics II: Comparing Predictions of Radiative and Total Feedback to Observations`
  - **Question:** do major cosmological simulations reproduce observed radiative AGN demographics as a function of host mass and sSFR?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: EAGLE, SIMBA, and TNG100.
    - Observational comparison: intrinsic radiative AGN fraction `F_AGN` for Eddington ratio `lambda>10^-3`.
    - Host axes: stellar mass and specific star-formation rate.
    - Additional comparison: combined radiative plus radio-mode AGN fraction for EAGLE.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare simulated AGN-host relationships to observational constraints.
    - Evaluate `F_AGN(M_star, sSFR)` in star-forming and quiescent populations.
    - Compare EAGLE total feedback predictions with combined radiative and radio-mode measurements.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Observed `F_AGN` declines strongly with stellar mass for quiescent galaxies.
    - Observed `F_AGN` stays approximately constant for star-forming systems.
    - EAGLE, SIMBA, and TNG100 do not reproduce these trends qualitatively.
    - EAGLE's total AGN feedback energy can be broadly consistent with observations for coupling efficiency `epsilon_f=0.15`.
    - EAGLE's predicted Eddington ratios are a factor of `10` higher than observed under the adopted conversions.
  - **Position:** useful simulation-audit paper because it checks feedback prescriptions against host-demographic observables rather than only global quenching success.

- [2607.19640](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19640) Benedict L. Rouse et al. `AGNFormer I: Reconstruction of AGN spectra using a probabilistic transformer model`
  - **Question:** can an uncertainty-aware transformer reconstruct masked AGN broad-line and continuum regions from the rest of the spectrum?
  - **Data:**
    - Training sample: SDSS DR16 Quasar Catalogue spectra.
    - Input: rest-frame spectral fluxes and uncertainties across the observed optical and UV range.
    - Masked regions: major broad lines to `+/-10^4 km/s` and missing spectral halves.
    - Evaluation: full and `S/N>10` spectral test sets.
  - **Method:**
    - Train AGNFormer to predict mean flux and variance in masked spectral regions.
    - Evaluate negative-log likelihood and flux residuals.
    - Compare performance with existing C IV and Lyman-alpha reconstruction methods.
    - Test whether broad-line diversity and Fe II complexes are recovered.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Broad-line reconstruction errors are `10-16%` of flux in the full sample.
    - High-quality spectra reach `4-8%` broad-line reconstruction errors.
    - The error floor is `~2-6%` of flux at `S/N~40`.
    - Larger unseen halves have `12-25%` errors in the full sample and `5-15%` in the high-quality sample.
    - The method recovers broad AGN spectral diversity across quasar main-sequence regimes.
  - **Position:** useful AGN-method paper because spectrum reconstruction affects quasar continuum placement, emission-line inference, and absorption-system measurements.
