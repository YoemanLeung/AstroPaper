## High-Redshift Galaxies, Reionization, and First-Light Searches

- [2607.00084](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00084) Shrriya Kapoor et al. `Little Red Dots at z~2 in EIGER reveal a gentle decline with respect to their peak number density at z~5`
  - **Question:** how common are Little Red Dots at `z~2`, below their reported peak abundance at `z~5`?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: EIGER JWST/NIRCam imaging and wide-field slitless spectroscopy over `140 arcmin^2`.
    - Sample: `19` broad-line sources at spectroscopic redshifts `z=1.55-3.18`.
    - Classification: `5` LRDs and `14` classical AGN, with X-ray detections supporting the AGN class in some objects.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify broad-line sources with an improved blind line-search algorithm.
    - Classify LRDs using spectro-photometric criteria, Balmer-break behavior, and emission-line diagnostics.
    - Measure number densities and compare with higher-redshift photometric LRD samples.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The LRD number density is `~7 x 10^-6 cMpc^-3` at `z=1.9-2.5`.
    - LRDs are `<=3%` of the AGN population at these epochs.
    - The decline from `z~5` is present but gentler than earlier work emphasized.
    - The LRD optical luminosities imply black-hole masses of order `10^6 M_sun` if radiating near the Eddington limit.
  - **Position:** read first for the LRD thread because it anchors the low-redshift tail with spectroscopy instead of only photometric color selection.

- [2607.00165](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00165) Rahul Rana et al. `Far-infrared observations of dust in Lya emitters at z=2-6`
  - **Question:** how much dust emission is present in Lyalpha-selected galaxies, and how does it affect Lyalpha escape?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `~4000` LAEs at `z=2.2-6` from SC4K.
    - Far-infrared data: SCUBA-2, PACS, and SPIRE.
    - Detections: `5` individually detected LAEs, all hosting AGN, with `S_850=3.7-5.5 mJy`.
  - **Method:**
    - Stack individually undetected LAEs in bins of redshift, stellar mass, Lyalpha luminosity, and AGN status.
    - Measure far-infrared fluxes, Lyalpha escape fractions, and IRX.
    - Compare LAEs with typical star-forming galaxies in the IRX-beta plane.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Most stacks are undetected, with an all-undetected stack limit of `S_850<0.09 mJy` at `3 sigma`.
    - Massive and AGN-hosting LAEs are the subsets most often detected in the far-infrared.
    - Detected LAEs and detected stacks have `f_esc(Lyalpha)=1-7%`, while undetected stacks have `f_esc(Lyalpha)>=10%`.
    - Non-zero Lyalpha escape in massive dusty or AGN-hosting systems challenges a simple dusty-galaxies-are-poor-leakers picture.
  - **Position:** useful for reionization-era analog work because it ties Lyalpha escape to dust, AGN, and mass selection rather than only UV colors.

- [2607.00167](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00167) Alessandra Venditti et al. `How can we finally see the first light? Status and perspective in the search for Population III stars`
  - **Question:** which current and near-future routes can constrain Population III stars across cosmic time?
  - **Data:**
    - Scope: theoretical Pop III formation expectations, near-field archaeology, direct high-z searches, and intermediate-redshift hard-ionizing candidates.
    - Observational routes: JWST spectroscopy, time-domain searches, lensing surveys, stellar archaeology, absorption-line systems, and simulations.
    - Context: proceedings from the 2026 CSI: Sesto Pop III session.
  - **Method:**
    - Review the theoretical parameter space for Pop III formation, IMF, feedback, and metal-enrichment transition.
    - Compare direct and indirect observational strategies.
    - Identify where current candidates and improved simulations narrow the allowed space.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - No single technique yet provides a complete Pop III detection path.
    - JWST spectroscopy and lensing surveys are making direct searches more realistic.
    - Stellar archaeology and absorption-line constraints remain necessary complements to high-redshift candidate work.
    - The field is moving from broad non-detections toward a constrained candidate-and-follow-up phase.
  - **Position:** high-value background paper for interpreting Pop III claims in daily reports because it clarifies which observables are decisive and which remain degenerate.

- [2607.01129](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01129) Caio Moreira Goolsby et al. `Tilting at the Turnover: Modeling the Faint-End of the UV Luminosity Function Behind Abell s1063 with JWST`
  - **Question:** does strong-lensing JWST imaging reveal a faint-end turnover in the high-redshift UV luminosity function?
  - **Data:**
    - Field: Abell S1063.
    - Imaging: ultra-deep JWST/NIRCam from GLIMPSE plus HST/ACS from the Hubble Frontier Fields.
    - Targets: faint, highly magnified high-redshift galaxy candidates.
  - **Method:**
    - Remove cluster light and build a photometric catalog.
    - Select lensed high-redshift candidates and estimate their properties with photometric-redshift and SED-fitting tools.
    - Model the UV luminosity function while accounting for lensing magnification.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - GLIMPSE provides a route to the very faint UV regime behind a massive cluster lens.
    - The analysis directly targets the turnover regime that matters for reionization photon-budget estimates.
    - The result is most useful when read with lens-model and completeness assumptions, because those dominate the faint-end inference.
  - **Position:** relevant to reionization demographics because faint galaxies control the ionizing emissivity budget even when individual sources are hard to characterize.

- [2607.00206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00206) A. Della Croce et al. `The evolution of high-z proto-star clusters into local globular clusters`
  - **Question:** can compact stellar clumps observed by JWST at high redshift evolve into present-day globular clusters?
  - **Data:**
    - Observational motivation: massive compact stellar clumps around high-z protogalaxies at `z>0.5`.
    - Models: direct `N`-body simulations of proto-globular-cluster evolution.
    - Initial conditions: high-density stellar systems with evolving stellar mass functions.
  - **Method:**
    - Evolve compact high-z clump analogs dynamically.
    - Track mass loss, expansion, and stellar-mass-function evolution.
    - Compare evolved systems with local old globular-cluster properties.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Some JWST compact clumps can plausibly evolve toward local globular-cluster-like systems.
    - The viability depends on initial density, mass loss, and the long-term survival of the cluster remnant.
    - The work gives a dynamical bridge between high-z clump observations and local fossil systems.
  - **Position:** secondary but useful because JWST clumps now need evolutionary tests rather than only morphology-based analogies.

## AGN, Quasar Pairs, and Black-Hole Feedback

- [2607.00105](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00105) Carolina Andonie et al. `AGN radiative feedback as the main regulator of [O III] outflow activity and obscuration in X-ray AGN`
  - **Question:** how does AGN accretion rate regulate ionized outflows and X-ray obscuration?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: eROSITA Final Equatorial Depth Survey.
    - Sample: `2840` X-ray AGN at `z<0.82` with high-quality SDSS spectra.
    - Diagnostics: optical [O III] kinematics, Eddington ratios, and archival eROSITA X-ray obscuration constraints.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit optical spectra and measure [O III] outflow kinematics.
    - Stack and model X-ray spectra to constrain `N_H`.
    - Match samples in AGN luminosity to separate Eddington-ratio and luminosity effects.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - `35%` of the full sample hosts [O III] outflows with `W_80>600 km/s`.
    - Outflow incidence rises from `15%` at `L_AGN<10^44 erg/s` to `60%` at `L_AGN>10^46 erg/s`.
    - Outflow incidence rises from `29%` at `log lambda_Edd<-2.3` to `50%` at `log lambda_Edd>-1.7`.
    - Sources with `log lambda_Edd>-1.7` are `5x` less obscured than lower-Eddington-ratio AGN.
  - **Position:** important AGN-feedback census because it links radiative output, outflow incidence, and obscuration in a large X-ray-selected sample.

- [2607.00093](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00093) Qihang Chen et al. `Search for Quasar Pairs with Gaia Astrometric Data. III. Discovery of 9 dual and projected quasars`
  - **Question:** how effective are Gaia astrometric selections for finding dual and projected quasars?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `11` quasar-pair candidates from MGQPC and an early version of that catalog.
    - Follow-up: low-resolution long-slit spectroscopy with `5` spectrographs on `3` telescopes.
    - Outcomes: `6` dual quasars and `3` projected quasars.
  - **Method:**
    - Spectroscopically confirm candidate components.
    - Separate dual quasars, projected quasars, and quasar-star projections.
    - Evaluate photometric redshifts and color information for future contamination control.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The follow-up adds `9` dual or projected quasar systems.
    - One dual quasar is at high redshift, `z~3.1`.
    - Several sources can still have a lensing-quasar interpretation that is not completely ruled out.
    - Photometric redshifts help mitigate contamination from projected quasars and quasar-star alignments.
  - **Position:** useful quasar-environment entry because dual-quasar samples trace merger-driven SMBH growth and feed future wide-field follow-up strategies.

- [2607.00209](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00209) M. Signorini et al. `Precision near-IR spectroscopy for understanding AGN physics and shed light on the H0 tension -- SHARP Science Book`
  - **Question:** can ELT/SHARP spectroastrometry and reverberation mapping improve AGN physics and distance measurements?
  - **Data:**
    - Facility concept: SHARP near-infrared spectroscopy.
    - Technique: spectroastrometry and reverberation mapping of broad-line regions.
    - Science target: calibration-independent AGN distance constraints and black-hole accretion physics.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine time-delay measurements with spatially resolved broad-line-region information.
    - Use near-infrared precision spectroscopy to reduce dependence on external distance-ladder calibration.
    - Frame the AGN method as a route to both BLR physics and the Hubble-constant tension.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - SHARP can make AGN broad-line-region geometry a precision observable.
    - The method targets a calibration-independent distance scale.
    - The paper is a science-book contribution rather than a completed survey result.
  - **Position:** lower-priority for immediate science claims but relevant for future quasar and AGN infrastructure.

- [2607.00305](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00305) Yuki Kudoh et al. `Probing the Parsec-Scale Dynamical Structure of Ionized Gas in Radio-Quiet AGN with SKA`
  - **Question:** how can SKA distinguish the radio-emitting components of radio-quiet AGN on parsec scales?
  - **Data:**
    - Systems: radio-quiet AGN.
    - Components: jets, accretion-disk coronae, dusty ionized gas outflows, cold dust, and circumnuclear star formation.
    - Observables: spectral turnovers, spectral indices, polarization, Faraday rotation, and variability.
  - **Method:**
    - Organize expected radio spectral signatures for each component.
    - Define diagnostics based on synchrotron self-absorption, free-free absorption, dust emission, and star-formation emission.
    - Evaluate which signatures SKA can separate observationally.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Radio-quiet AGN are not single-component radio sources.
    - SKA can test whether compact radio emission is dominated by jets, coronae, outflows, dust, or star formation.
    - Component separation is necessary before radio emission can be used as a clean feedback tracer.
  - **Position:** useful planning paper for AGN feedback work because it turns SKA sensitivity into a concrete component-diagnostics framework.

- [2607.00353](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00353) Changshuo Yan et al. `Multiwavelength periodic microlensing signatures of macrolensed supermassive binary black holes`
  - **Question:** can microlensing variability diagnose macrolensed supermassive binary black holes?
  - **Data:**
    - Systems: active supermassive binary black holes in macrolensed quasars.
    - Bands: optical, UV, and X-ray light curves.
    - Parameters: binary mass ratio, separation, accretion-disk structure, and lensing configuration.
  - **Method:**
    - Simulate multiband microlensing signatures of lensed binary accretion disks.
    - Vary binary and disk parameters.
    - Identify periodic or chromatic patterns that distinguish binary systems from single-engine quasars.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Multiwavelength microlensing can encode binary geometry and accretion structure.
    - Optical, UV, and X-ray behavior should be interpreted jointly rather than as independent variability channels.
    - The method is most relevant for future time-domain samples of strongly lensed quasars.
  - **Position:** niche but relevant because lensed quasar monitoring can probe sub-resolution SMBH binaries.

## Galaxy Structure, Environment, and Survey Context

- [2607.00085](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00085) David T. Maltby et al. `The multiwavelength structure of post-starburst galaxies at 0.5 < z < 3 with JWST PRIMER: compact morphologies and residual disturbances`
  - **Question:** what structural signatures remain after rapid quenching in post-starburst galaxies at `0.5<z<3`?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: JWST/NIRCam PRIMER imaging in the Ultra Deep Survey field.
    - Sample: `~120` photometrically selected post-starburst galaxies.
    - Comparison: `~3000` passive and star-forming galaxies.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure effective radii and Sersic indices independently across eight NIRCam bands.
    - Compare wavelength-dependent structures with passive and star-forming control samples.
    - Quantify disturbance indicators including residual flux fraction, asymmetry, and residual asymmetry.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - PSBs show little structural variation with wavelength, implying weak age gradients or dust-obscuration gradients.
    - Massive PSBs at `z>1` are compact spheroids and are more compact than massive passive galaxies.
    - Low-mass PSBs at `0.5<z<1` are compact disc-dominated systems.
    - Massive high-z PSBs show elevated residual asymmetry, consistent with residual disturbances below an otherwise smooth stellar distribution.
  - **Position:** relevant to quenching work because it separates smooth compact remnants from hidden disturbance signatures.

- [2607.00982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00982) Takafumi Tsukui et al. `Bar-driven secular evolution largely complete in a disk galaxy 7.6 billion years ago`
  - **Question:** were mature bar-driven secular-evolution structures already present `7.6` Gyr ago?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: a disk galaxy observed at a lookback time of `7.6` Gyr.
    - Instrument: ALMA kinematics.
    - Structures: bar, gas inflow, nuclear stellar disk, and vertically thickened/X-shaped bulge analogs.
  - **Method:**
    - Use resolved kinematics to infer the dynamical state of the disk and bar.
    - Compare observed structures with secular-evolution expectations.
    - Assess whether the bar-driven sequence had already reached a mature stage.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The galaxy shows evidence for advanced bar-driven evolution at high lookback time.
    - Secular transformation can be largely complete earlier than a simple late-time-only picture suggests.
    - The result adds a structural-evolution constraint complementary to local barred-galaxy archaeology.
  - **Position:** secondary for the profile but useful because it connects high-redshift disk dynamics to present-day Milky-Way-like secular evolution.

- [2607.00347](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00347) Tessa Vernstrom et al. `Cosmic Magnetism Science with the SKA`
  - **Question:** what cosmic-magnetism problems can SKA address across galaxies, clusters, and large-scale structure?
  - **Data:**
    - Facility: SKA.
    - Observables: Faraday rotation, synchrotron emission, polarization, and magnetized plasma tracers.
    - Targets: galaxies, clusters, cosmic web structures, and foregrounds.
  - **Method:**
    - Review SKA measurement capabilities for magnetic-field origin, amplification, and structure-formation roles.
    - Connect angular resolution and sensitivity to science cases across spatial scales.
    - Identify where magnetism interacts with galaxy and IGM/CGM evolution.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - SKA will expand Faraday and polarization samples enough to test magnetic-field growth scenarios.
    - Magnetism becomes a measurable component of structure formation rather than a nuisance parameter.
    - The paper is primarily a science-book roadmap, not a completed observational result.
  - **Position:** lower-priority but relevant for future IGM/CGM and cosmic-web plasma work.

- [2607.01175](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01175) L. E. Bleem et al. `Galaxy Clusters Selected via the Sunyaev-Zel'dovich Effect in 5 year data from the SPT-3G Main Survey`
  - **Question:** what cluster sample does the 5-year SPT-3G Main survey produce for cosmology and environmental studies?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: SPT-3G Main field, `5` years of thermal SZ observations.
    - Sensitivity: `3.2`, `2.5`, and `8.9 uK-arcmin` at `95`, `150`, and `220 GHz`.
    - Catalog: `8892` cluster candidates above the adopted detection threshold.
  - **Method:**
    - Detect galaxy clusters through the thermal Sunyaev-Zel'dovich effect.
    - Build a large candidate catalog from arcminute-resolution maps.
    - Calibrate the sample for cosmological and cluster-population applications.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The catalog is a large SZ-selected cluster resource.
    - It is most immediately useful for cluster cosmology and for selecting environments for multiwavelength follow-up.
    - The cross-list is profile-adjacent rather than a high-z galaxy paper.
  - **Position:** useful context for large-scale structure work, especially for environment-selected follow-up samples.
