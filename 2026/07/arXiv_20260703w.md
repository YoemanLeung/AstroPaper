## Reionization-Era Galaxies, Gas, and Chemical Enrichment

- [2606.31638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31638) Zihao Li et al. `Possible chemical signatures of first-star enrichment in a very metal-poor galaxy overdensity near the end of reionization`
  - **Question:** can metal-poor CGM gas near reionization preserve a detectable Population III enrichment signature?
  - **Data:**
    - System: an overdensity at `z=6.26` traced by `11` galaxies within `600 kpc`.
    - Spectroscopy: a background quasar sightline probing CGM gas near the brightest member.
    - Absorber: chemically primitive gas at `0.022 Z_sun` with strong carbon enhancement.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify the galaxy overdensity and pair it with quasar absorption information.
    - Measure gas metallicity and abundance ratios from the absorber.
    - Compare the chemical pattern with enrichment models for first-star and later stellar populations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The absorber has a low metallicity of `0.022 Z_sun`.
    - The abundance ratio is carbon enhanced, with `[C/O]=+0.57`.
    - The pattern is consistent with chemical enrichment dominated by Population III stars rather than ordinary later stellar populations.
    - The overdensity setting makes the system a high-priority target for linking early galaxies, CGM enrichment, and reionization topology.
  - **Position:** weekly read-first because it links high-z overdensity mapping, quasar absorption, and possible first-star enrichment.

- [2606.30787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30787) Kasper E. Heintz et al. `JWST spectroscopy of galaxies at z>10: Damped Lyalpha absorbers reveal efficient star formation and hidden redshift biases`
  - **Question:** how do neutral gas reservoirs affect the interpretation of luminous JWST galaxies at `z>10`?
  - **Data:**
    - Instrument: JWST/NIRSpec Prism.
    - Sample: `25` galaxies with robust spectroscopic redshifts at `z_spec>=10`.
    - Diagnostics: rest-UV nebular lines, burstiness, DLA-derived HI columns, and redshift inference tests.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure burstiness as `SFR_10Myr/SFR_100Myr`.
    - Compare burstiness and UV-line strength against DLA-derived HI column density.
    - Quantify photometric and Lyalpha-break redshift biases when DLAs are not modeled.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Strong rest-UV lines correlate with burstiness.
    - HI column density does not strongly correlate with `M_UV`, burstiness, or rest-UV line prominence.
    - The most bursty systems require short depletion times, typically `t_dep<=20 Myr`.
    - Omitting DLA absorption biases photometric redshifts by an average `0.39` and Lyalpha-break redshifts by `0.14`.
  - **Position:** important calibration paper because DLA absorption can move both inferred redshifts and gas-depletion interpretation.

- [2606.30802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30802) Mengyuan Xiao et al. `A Census of the 200 Most Massive Galaxies Spectroscopically Observed with JWST at zspec ~3-15`
  - **Question:** what do the most massive spectroscopically confirmed JWST galaxies reveal about early assembly and quenching?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: the `200` most massive galaxies in public JWST prism observations at `z_spec~3-15`.
    - Selection: evolving stellar-mass threshold anchored at `log(Mstar)>10` at `z~5`.
    - Cleaning: LRDs and broad-line AGN contaminants removed from the massive-galaxy sample.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit spectroscopy and photometry jointly to derive physical properties.
    - Classify normal star-forming, dusty star-forming, and quiescent systems.
    - Test UVJ and `(ugi)s` selections against spectroscopic classifications.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Normal star-forming galaxies dominate above `z~6`.
    - Dusty star-forming galaxies and quiescent galaxies become more common toward lower redshift.
    - The census identifies `29` massive quiescent galaxies, including recently quenched systems with star formation falling within `~100 Myr`.
    - Standard UVJ and prior `(ugi)s` selections are inconsistent for the most massive galaxies at `z>3`.
  - **Position:** baseline paper for massive-galaxy work because it moves early quiescence and dust-enriched pathways onto a spectroscopic footing.

- [2607.00165](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00165) Rahul Rana et al. `Far-infrared observations of dust in Lya emitters at z=2-6`
  - **Question:** how much dust emission is present in Lyalpha-selected galaxies, and how does it affect Lyalpha escape?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `~4000` LAEs at `z=2.2-6` from SC4K.
    - Far-infrared data: SCUBA-2, PACS, and SPIRE.
    - Detections: `5` individually detected LAEs, all hosting AGN, with `S_850=3.7-5.5 mJy`.
  - **Method:**
    - Stack undetected LAEs by redshift, stellar mass, Lyalpha luminosity, and AGN status.
    - Measure far-infrared fluxes, Lyalpha escape fractions, and IRX.
    - Compare LAEs with typical star-forming galaxies in the IRX-beta plane.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Most stacks are undetected, with an all-undetected stack limit of `S_850<0.09 mJy` at `3 sigma`.
    - Massive and AGN-hosting LAEs are the subsets most often detected in the far-infrared.
    - Detected LAEs and detected stacks have `f_esc(Lyalpha)=1-7%`, while undetected stacks have `f_esc(Lyalpha)>=10%`.
    - Non-zero Lyalpha escape in massive dusty or AGN-hosting systems challenges a simple dusty-galaxies-are-poor-leakers picture.
  - **Position:** useful reionization analog paper because it ties Lyalpha escape to dust, AGN, and mass selection rather than only UV colors.

## Little Red Dots, Black-Hole Seeds, and Compact Accretion

- [2606.27427](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27427) Lewis R. Prole et al. `SEEDZ: Rapid Galaxy Assembly as a Pathway to Supermassive Stars, Dense Stellar Environments and Massive Black Hole Seeds`
  - **Question:** what early galaxy assembly histories produce the inflow rates needed for supermassive stars and heavy black-hole seeds?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: SEEDZ hydrodynamic simulations of early galaxy assembly.
    - Formation criterion: `>1 M_sun/yr` flowing into `10 pc` regions for heavy seeds.
    - Epoch: heavy-seed formation tracked to `z=10`.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare halos that meet the heavy-seed criterion with halos that never meet it.
    - Track gas flow from virial scales of a few hundred parsecs into the inner parsec.
    - Apply a supermassive-star condition using metallicity, accretion rate, and lifetime thresholds.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Heavy seeds form in halos that grow rapidly relative to non-seed halos.
    - Halos with virial-scale growth rates `>=1 M_sun/yr` sustain `0.1 M_sun/yr` into the inner `1 pc`.
    - Heavy seeds typically form about `100 Myr` after the first stars and stellar-mass black holes.
    - By `z=10`, most heavy seeds form in near-solar-metallicity regions, with a low-metallicity minority at `10^-2 Z_sun`.
  - **Position:** anchors the week because it turns early galaxy assembly into a quantitative black-hole-seed formation channel.

- [2606.30253](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30253) Federica Loiacono et al. `No evolution in the number density of little red dots from cosmic dawn to cosmic noon`
  - **Question:** does the Little Red Dot abundance decline from cosmic dawn to cosmic noon?
  - **Data:**
    - Field: JWST/EIGER J1030 around the `z~6.3` quasar SDSS J1030+0524.
    - Parent catalog: `154` point-like JWST-selected sources.
    - LRD candidates: `5` broad-line emitters with `FWHM>=1000 km/s`, red `F200W-F356W>0`, and X-ray non-detections.
  - **Method:**
    - Select point-like red sources in the JWST catalog.
    - Measure broad-line emitters and derive LRD bolometric luminosity functions at `z=2.4` and `z=4.5`.
    - Compare LRD number densities with pre-JWST AGN samples and recent model predictions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At `z=2.4`, the LRD space density is only a factor of `~2` below all pre-JWST AGN above `L_bol>=3 x 10^44 erg/s`.
    - At `z=4.5`, the inferred density agrees with larger-area LRD studies.
    - The abundance of LRDs above `L_bol>=3 x 10^44 erg/s` shows no significant evolution at `z>2`.
    - The cosmic-noon abundance is `3.4(+5.6/-2.4) x 10^-5 Mpc^-3`, about `350x` larger than recent model predictions.
  - **Position:** important LRD demographics paper because it tests whether LRDs are a short early seed-growth phase or a broader high-accretion state.

- [2607.00084](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00084) Shrriya Kapoor et al. `Little Red Dots at z~2 in EIGER reveal a gentle decline with respect to their peak number density at z~5`
  - **Question:** how common are Little Red Dots at `z~2`, below their reported peak abundance at `z~5`?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: EIGER JWST/NIRCam imaging and wide-field slitless spectroscopy over `140 arcmin^2`.
    - Sample: `19` broad-line sources at spectroscopic redshifts `z=1.55-3.18`.
    - Classification: `5` LRDs and `14` classical AGN, with X-ray detections supporting the AGN class in some objects.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify broad-line sources with an improved blind line-search algorithm.
    - Classify LRDs using spectro-photometric criteria, Balmer-break behavior, and emission-line diagnostics.
    - Measure number densities and compare with higher-redshift photometric LRD samples.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The LRD number density is `~7 x 10^-6 cMpc^-3` at `z=1.9-2.5`.
    - LRDs are `<=3%` of the AGN population at these epochs.
    - The decline from `z~5` is present but gentler than earlier work emphasized.
    - The LRD optical luminosities imply black-hole masses of order `10^6 M_sun` if radiating near the Eddington limit.
  - **Position:** companion to Loiacono because it anchors the low-redshift LRD tail with spectroscopy rather than only photometric selection.

- [2607.01330](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01330) Guozhen Ma et al. `Extreme outflow velocities and weak UV emission lines indicate quasars shedding their dust cocoons`
  - **Question:** what physical state produces LoBAL quasars with extreme outflows and weak rest-UV emission lines?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: six quasars similar to GQ 1309+2904.
    - Redshifts: systemic values `z~2.07-3.28` from detected Halpha emission.
    - Observables: BAL troughs, broad-band `BVR` polarimetry, continuum slopes, and Halpha-based black-hole masses.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure BAL velocities and classify LoBAL systems.
    - Fit reddened quasar continua with SMC-like and steeper extinction laws.
    - Use polarimetry to test inclination and scattering interpretations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - All targets are quasars with highly blueshifted BALs and outflow velocities up to `~0.16c`.
    - Five targets are confidently identified as LoBAL quasars.
    - Black-hole masses are `10^8.7-10^9.4 M_sun`, with Eddington ratios `0.14-0.34`.
    - The objects are consistent with weak-line quasars seen through a dust-processing disc wind.
  - **Position:** useful quasar-evolution entry because it links BAL geometry, dust processing, and weak-line behavior to an emergence phase.

## Galaxy Structure, Environment, and Survey Selection

- [2606.28590](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28590) Kiyoaki C. Omori et al. `Interaction-induced star formation boosts stellar mass assembly in z~5 galaxies`
  - **Question:** how much stellar-mass growth at `z~5` is driven by galaxy-galaxy interactions and the starbursts they trigger?
  - **Data:**
    - Imaging: JWST/NIRCam observations from CANUCS.
    - Sample: `48` spectroscopically selected galaxies at `5.0<z_spec<5.6`.
    - Interaction subset: `21` systems, or `44%`, visually classified as close interactions within `<5 kpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Visually classify multi-component close systems.
    - Fit component star-formation histories with Dense Basis.
    - Compare burst mass growth against a no-burst baseline and assume components eventually merge.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Interacting components undergo brief `~0.2 Gyr` star-formation enhancements.
    - The bursts grow component stellar masses by `2.66+/-0.85x`.
    - The burst phase forms `1.71+/-0.37x` excess mass relative to a no-burst path.
    - Mergers contribute `42(+20/-25)%` of total stellar-mass growth at `z~5`.
  - **Position:** important JWST-resolved assembly result because it assigns a mass-growth budget to interactions rather than only identifying disturbed morphology.

- [2606.31978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31978) Louise Paquereau et al. `When Galaxy Evolution is Driven by More than Halo Mass: Environmental Quenching and Conformity in COSMOS-Web`
  - **Question:** do quiescent and star-forming galaxies cluster differently after controlling for halo mass?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: COSMOS-Web.
    - Tracers: quiescent and star-forming galaxy samples through `z<=2`.
    - Measurements: clustering, halo-mass matching, and environment statistics.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure clustering as a function of galaxy type.
    - Match samples in halo mass to isolate secondary environment effects.
    - Interpret residual clustering as environmental quenching or conformity.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Quiescent galaxies remain more clustered than star-forming galaxies after halo-mass matching.
    - Environment affects quenching beyond the first-order halo-mass trend.
    - COSMOS-Web can test conformity with enough area and depth to reduce small-field ambiguity.
  - **Position:** useful environmental-quenching result because it separates halo mass from local density and neighbor history.

- [2607.02353](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02353) Onnalin Innala et al. `Assessing Ultra-Cool Dwarf Contamination in Photometrically Selected High-Redshift Galaxy Samples`
  - **Question:** how many ultra-cool dwarfs contaminate photometric high-redshift galaxy samples?
  - **Data:**
    - Targets: synthetic T- to early Y-type ultra-cool dwarfs compared with photometric high-z galaxy selections.
    - Surveys: HST COSMOS, Roman deep-tier survey configurations, and JWST-like deep surveys.
    - Models: ELF OWL and BOBCAT ultra-cool-dwarf spectral libraries plus stellar number-density prescriptions.
  - **Method:**
    - Build FC-ENZO, a survey-dependent foreground-contamination estimator.
    - Simulate dwarf-star colors and apply user-defined high-redshift selection functions.
    - Compare contaminant fractions as a function of magnitude, metallicity, spectral library, and survey depth.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The dominant contaminants are T to early Y dwarfs most often misclassified as galaxies near `z~8`.
    - ELF OWL predicts larger contaminant fractions than BOBCAT because it has stronger absorption around `1 micron`.
    - Contamination concentrates near each survey limiting magnitude.
    - At brighter magnitudes, relative contamination is highest for HST COSMOS, then Roman deep, then JWST.
  - **Position:** high-priority survey-design paper because high-z luminosity functions and follow-up programs depend on foreground rejection.

## CGM, HI, Quasar Environments, and Feedback

- [2606.30742](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30742) Jelena Ritter et al. `ALMA visits the QSO MUSEUM: connecting molecular gas and the cool circumgalactic medium around 37 z~3 quasars`
  - **Question:** how are molecular-gas reservoirs in `z~3` quasar hosts connected to extended Lyalpha nebulae and environments?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `37` quasars at `z~3`.
    - Instruments: ALMA CO(4-3) observations plus MUSE Lyalpha nebula maps.
    - Environmental tracers: molecular gas, Lyalpha halos, Eddington ratio, and companion counts.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure host molecular gas and compare with cool CGM Lyalpha emission.
    - Relate gas properties to accretion state and nearby companion galaxies.
    - Test whether host fuel and CGM emission track the same environmental conditions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The dataset links host molecular reservoirs, quasar accretion state, and extended Lyalpha emission.
    - Companion counts provide an environmental axis for interpreting gas-rich quasar fields.
    - The result is a direct bridge between quasar host fuel and cool CGM structure at cosmic noon.
  - **Position:** top quasar-environment paper of the week because it combines host gas, CGM emission, and environment in one sample.

- [2606.28874](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28874) Yu Rong et al. `Stellar Surface Density Modulates MgII Cool-gas Outflow Absorption in DESI Star-forming Galaxies`
  - **Question:** is cool-gas outflow absorption controlled only by stellar mass and SFR, or also by galaxy compactness?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: DESI DR1 star-forming galaxy spectra.
    - Diagnostic: stacked Mg II absorption tracing cool outflows.
    - Galaxy properties: stellar mass, SFR, and stellar surface density.
  - **Method:**
    - Stack spectra in bins of galaxy properties.
    - Measure Mg II absorption strength and outflow signatures.
    - Compare surface-density dependence against mass and SFR trends.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Mg II cool-gas outflow absorption strengthens with stellar surface density.
    - Compactness adds information beyond stellar mass or SFR alone.
    - DESI stacks can turn weak individual outflow signatures into population-level feedback constraints.
  - **Position:** practical feedback paper because it identifies compactness as a control variable for cool outflow interpretation.

- [2606.30088](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30088) Namrata Roy et al. `Ionized Gas Around Radio Galaxies from DESI Quasar Sightlines`
  - **Question:** can background-quasar spectra detect ionized gas preferentially aligned with radio jets?
  - **Data:**
    - Foreground systems: radio galaxies.
    - Background probes: DESI quasar sightlines.
    - Diagnostic: stacked Halpha emission around radio-galaxy environments.
  - **Method:**
    - Stack quasar spectra as a function of projected separation and jet orientation.
    - Search for Halpha emission aligned with radio axes.
    - Interpret the signal as jet-CGM coupling or anisotropic gas excitation.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Stacked DESI sightlines detect localized Halpha emission around radio galaxies.
    - The signal is aligned with radio jets.
    - Jet-CGM coupling can be measured statistically even when individual systems are too faint.
  - **Position:** important survey-scale feedback entry because it uses background spectra to probe anisotropic gas around radio AGN.

- [2607.02162](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02162) M. Ramatsoku et al. `Resolved HI and Environmental Dynamics`
  - **Question:** what will SKA-era resolved HI add to environmental galaxy-evolution studies?
  - **Data:**
    - Precursors: MeerKAT, FAST, and ASKAP resolved HI observations.
    - Environments: Virgo, Fornax, nearby filaments, groups, and isolated galaxies.
    - Future facility: SKA-mid AA4 deep, contiguous HI imaging.
  - **Method:**
    - Review observed HI tails, stripping signatures, accretion signatures, and replenishment events.
    - Compare cluster and low-density environments where ram pressure and tidal effects can mix.
    - Forecast SKA-mid AA4 sensitivity and physical resolution.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Resolved HI already reveals ram pressure at unexpectedly large cluster-centric distances.
    - Fornax and filament systems show mixed tidal and hydrodynamical gas processing.
    - SKA-mid AA4 can reach column-density sensitivities of `10^18-10^19 cm^-2` at `~10` to `1-2 kpc` physical resolution.
    - Wide-area resolved HI samples will connect local environmental physics to statistically representative galaxy evolution.
  - **Position:** useful CGM and environment roadmap because it turns SKA survey design into measurable gas-processing channels.

## Reionization, Radio, and Facility Roadmaps

- [2606.27858](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27858) Anshuman Acharya et al. `Inferring Cosmology and Astrophysics from the High-redshift 21cm Signal with SKA-Low`
  - **Question:** which statistics and inference tools can SKA-Low use to recover Cosmic Dawn and reionization physics from the 21 cm signal?
  - **Data:**
    - Facility: SKA-Low, including the initial AA* configuration.
    - Observable: redshifted 21 cm signal during Cosmic Dawn and the Epoch of Reionization.
    - Statistics: power spectrum, higher-order statistics, distribution moments, morphology, and field-level summaries.
  - **Method:**
    - Review analytical, semi-numerical, numerical, and emulator-based signal models.
    - Compare Bayesian, simulation-based, and field-level inference approaches.
    - Assess complementary statistics for breaking degeneracies among galaxy and IGM parameters.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The non-Gaussian 21 cm signal carries information beyond the power spectrum.
    - Combining morphology, higher-order statistics, and field-level tools is needed to break parameter degeneracies.
    - The initial SKA-Low configuration can already target early constraints on galaxy and IGM properties.
  - **Position:** high-priority reionization infrastructure paper because it specifies how SKA-Low measurements become astrophysical parameters.

- [2607.02366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02366) Jose Afonso et al. `The Road to Identifying the Earliest Radio-Powerful AGN with the SKA`
  - **Question:** how can SKA surveys find the missing population of radio-powerful AGN at `z>6`?
  - **Data:**
    - Population: currently known and predicted radio-powerful AGN during the Epoch of Reionization.
    - Facilities: SKAO and pathfinder radio surveys.
    - Frequency range: SKAO coverage from `50 MHz` to `15 GHz`.
  - **Method:**
    - Synthesize hydrodynamical and semi-analytic predictions with existing observational constraints.
    - Evaluate selection biases tied to sparse radio spectral information.
    - Propose broadband radio SED, spectral-curvature, jet-evolution, and radio-only redshift diagnostics.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Only a few radio-powerful AGN are known at `z>6`, far below many theoretical expectations.
    - Current incompleteness likely reflects both intrinsic rarity and radio-selection limitations.
    - Fine frequency sampling with SKAO can identify high-z AGN without relying only on optical or near-infrared selection.
    - The same population can become a 21 cm absorption probe of early neutral hydrogen.
