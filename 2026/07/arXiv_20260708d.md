## High-Redshift AGN, Filaments, and Early Gas

- [2607.05523](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05523) Giulia Tozzi et al. `BlackTHUNDER Reveals a Massive Filament around a Compact AGN at z ~= 5.23`
  - **Question:** what large-scale environment hosts the compact AGN GN-77652 at `z=5.229`?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: GN-77652, a compact AGN at `z=5.229`.
    - Instrument: JWST/NIRSpec IFU through the BlackTHUNDER Large Program.
    - Imaging: deep multi-band JWST/NIRCam data.
  - **Method:**
    - Map [OIII] `lambda5007` emission and kinematics across the AGN environment.
    - Measure stellar masses, gas-phase metallicities, star-formation rates, and projected separations for neighboring systems.
    - Test direct-collapse and recoiling black-hole interpretations against the local radiation field and group geometry.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - GN-77652 lies near a `12 kpc` filament of multiple sources at `z~5.23`.
    - The filament spans `M_star=0.7-13 x 10^8 M_sun`, `12+log(O/H)=7.6-8.5`, and `SFR=0.4-6 M_sun/yr`.
    - The central group galaxy has `M_star~1.1 x 10^9 M_sun`, `Z~0.6 Z_sun`, and smooth [OIII] velocity structure.
    - The system is expected to coalesce in `150-440 Myr`.
  - **Position:** read first because it directly links a JWST compact AGN to resolved high-redshift filamentary structure and group assembly.

- [2607.05509](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05509) William McClymont et al. `Globular cluster abundance patterns inherited from giant molecular clouds`
  - **Question:** can globular-cluster light-element abundance patterns be inherited from chemically structured birth clouds?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: cosmological radiation-hydrodynamic simulations with standard chemical enrichment.
    - Systems: giant molecular clouds forming after an earlier starburst.
    - Observables: light-element abundance spreads and nitrogen-oxygen anticorrelations.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify dense clouds with internal abundance variation.
    - Track oxygen-rich ejecta colliding with nitrogen-rich galactic gas.
    - Compare cloud abundance structure with globular-cluster chemical signatures.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Some high-density clouds reproduce large light-element spreads and nitrogen-oxygen anticorrelations at nearly constant iron abundance.
    - The abundance patterns arise where earlier ejecta interact with chemically distinct galactic gas.
    - Globular-cluster anomalies need not require extended in-cluster star formation.
  - **Position:** relevant fossil-record paper because globular clusters may preserve high-redshift baryon-cycle chemistry rather than only internal self-enrichment.

- [2607.06315](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06315) Kimberly L. Emig et al. `Low-Frequency Recombination Lines from Galaxies and AGN over Cosmic Time`
  - **Question:** what galaxy and AGN gas phases can low-frequency recombination lines probe over cosmic time?
  - **Data:**
    - Tracers: hydrogen and carbon radio recombination lines below `10 GHz`.
    - Systems: nearby galaxies, absorption-line systems, and radio-bright AGN.
    - Facilities: SKA and pathfinder wide-band spectral-line surveys.
  - **Method:**
    - Review stimulated low-frequency RRL emission and absorption use cases.
    - Connect hydrogen RRLs to ionized gas and carbon RRLs to cold HI and CO-dark molecular gas.
    - Forecast SKA multi-band constraints on gas physical conditions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Low-frequency RRLs can be observable within or against radio-bright sources to `z~6`.
    - Recent detections beyond the local universe at `z~1` demonstrate the method's extragalactic reach.
    - SKA surveys can target baryon conversion into stars, ISM evolution, and gas regulation of AGN activity.
  - **Position:** useful SKA-era infrastructure for gas diagnostics that complement optical, infrared, and 21 cm views of high-redshift systems.

## IGM, CGM, Inflows, and Feedback

- [2607.05494](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05494) Paryag Sharma et al. `Uncertainty-Aware Deep Learning for the Lyalpha Forest: CNN-Based Absorber Detection and Characterization`
  - **Question:** can a CNN replace expensive Voigt-profile fitting for large Lyalpha forest absorber samples?
  - **Data:**
    - Training spectra: synthetic Lyalpha forest spectra generated from IllustrisTNG.
    - Labels: VIPER Voigt-profile fits.
    - Observational test: high-resolution UVES spectra.
  - **Method:**
    - Train a convolutional network to predict absorber presence, `N_HI`, `b_HI`, and line centroid.
    - Compare predicted column-density and Doppler-parameter distributions with VIPER.
    - Use Monte Carlo dropout to quantify predictive uncertainty.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Simulated spectra yield `F1~0.8`, with mean absolute errors of `~0.18 dex` in `log N_HI` and `~0.10 dex` in `log b_HI`.
    - UVES spectra yield lower performance, `F1~0.5`, with errors of `~0.34 dex` in `log N_HI` and `~0.21 dex` in `log b_HI`.
    - Latent-space analysis shows a significant simulation-to-observation domain shift.
    - The method preserves the observed CDDF and `b_HI-N_HI` distributions despite reduced object-level performance.
  - **Position:** important IGM-method paper because scalable absorber measurement is a bottleneck for future quasar-sightline and tomographic surveys.

- [2607.05498](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05498) Benjamin A. Seidel et al. `Non-spherical Cows: Introducing the Asphericity Parameter as a Measure of Accretion Geometry`
  - **Question:** how can simulations quantify non-spherical inflow geometry at halo boundaries?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: Magneticum halos.
    - Validation set: toy models with controlled inflow configurations.
    - Measurement surface: virial boundary of halos and clusters.
  - **Method:**
    - Decompose the inflow field into spherical harmonics.
    - Define linear and logarithmic inflow-asphericity measures.
    - Test sensitivity to total anisotropy, inflow covering fraction, and outflow-dominated regions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Linear asphericity traces the total power in non-spherical inflow and global anisotropy.
    - Logarithmic asphericity traces inflow covering fraction and is sensitive to zero-inflow regions.
    - The metric can separate inflow geometry from regions dominated by feedback-driven outflow.
  - **Position:** useful cosmic-web tool because it turns filamentary accretion geometry into a compact simulation diagnostic.

- [2607.05524](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05524) Chia-Yu Hu and Max Gronke `Survival is not Enough: Dust Sputtering, Growth, and H_2 Formation in Galactic Winds`
  - **Question:** why do some entrained wind clouds become molecular while others remain atomic?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: cloud-crushing calculations with non-equilibrium cooling and chemistry.
    - Physics: dust sputtering, dust growth, and molecular-hydrogen formation.
    - Wind temperatures: cases including `10^6 K` and `>~10^7 K` flows.
  - **Method:**
    - Evolve clouds in hot galactic winds.
    - Track cold-core formation, dust survival, dust-to-gas ratio, and H_2 abundance.
    - Compare runs with and without dust growth.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Surviving clouds develop a cold `~30 K` dense core inside a warm `~10^4 K` envelope.
    - Dust survives well in `10^6 K` winds but is severely depleted by sputtering in hotter winds.
    - Without dust growth, the dust-to-gas ratio declines and H_2 formation is suppressed.
    - High molecular fractions require dust growth and cloud densities `>~10-30` times the critical survival density.
  - **Position:** relevant CGM feedback paper because molecular outflows require chemical growth pathways, not only cloud survival.

## Structure, Clusters, and Survey Context

- [2607.06313](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06313) Naomi Ota et al. `Kinetic structure of the intracluster medium across nearby clusters observed with XRISM`
  - **Question:** what ICM kinetic regimes are resolved by current XRISM/Resolve measurements?
  - **Data:**
    - Instrument: XRISM/Resolve.
    - Sample: `45` measurements in `19` nearby galaxy clusters.
    - Quantities: line-of-sight velocity dispersion, bulk velocity amplitude, `R_v=|v_bulk|/sigma_v`, and non-thermal pressure proxies.
  - **Method:**
    - Place measurements on a common emission-weighted effective line-of-sight scale.
    - Compare cool-core centers, cool-core outer regions, and non-cool-core systems.
    - Contrast observations with forward-modeled TNG-Cluster predictions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Cool-core and disturbed systems do not form a single increasing-turbulence sequence.
    - Mean `R_v` rises from `0.45` in cool-core centers to `1.6` in non-cool-core systems.
    - Bulk coherent motion separates disturbed systems from relaxed central regions more than unresolved line broadening alone.
  - **Position:** relevant cluster-environment benchmark because velocity structure is becoming directly measurable, not only inferred from morphology or hydrostatic bias.

- [2607.06346](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06346) Kohei Kurahara et al. `Unravelling Turbulence and Magnetic Fields in Galaxy Clusters with SKA and XRISM`
  - **Question:** how can SKA and XRISM jointly constrain cluster turbulence and magnetic fields?
  - **Data:**
    - X-ray facility: XRISM velocity measurements with energy resolution `<7 eV` FWHM.
    - Radio facility: SKA-Mid high-resolution rotation-measure grids.
    - Science targets: turbulent ICM, magnetic fields, cosmic rays, and radio halos.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine X-ray kinetic-energy measurements with radio synchrotron and Faraday-rotation constraints.
    - Compare observed turbulent energy and magnetic-field structure with MHD simulations.
    - Use multi-wavelength data to test turbulent dynamo and energy-cascade models.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - XRISM directly measures thermal-gas velocities where radio data trace non-thermal plasma.
    - SKA-Mid can provide RM source densities of `~100-200 deg^-2`.
    - Joint analysis can link turbulence, magnetic-field amplification, and cosmic-ray evolution in clusters.
  - **Position:** infrastructure paper for cluster environments and large-scale structure because it defines a practical XRISM-plus-SKA observable space.

- [2607.05507](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05507) Yiheng Wang et al. `Warped Disk Galaxies: Alignment with the Large-Scale Tidal Field`
  - **Question:** are warped disk galaxies aligned with the reconstructed large-scale tidal field?
  - **Data:**
    - Galaxy sample: warped and non-warped disk galaxies.
    - Environment field: ELUCID large-scale tidal-field reconstruction.
    - Diagnostics: disk orientations relative to tidal-field eigen-directions.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare disk-orientation angles with the `t_1` and `t_3` tidal-field directions.
    - Match warped galaxies to a non-warped control sample.
    - Test mass dependence in the alignment signal.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Disk orientations show a significant alignment signal with the `t_1` direction.
    - Warped galaxies show an excess of intermediate angles, while non-warped galaxies show a deficit of intermediate angles.
    - High-mass warped galaxies drive the intermediate-angle excess.
    - No significant warped-galaxy signal is detected with the `t_3` direction.
  - **Position:** useful low-redshift structure paper because it connects disk morphology to cosmic tidal fields and halo-orientation physics.

- [2607.05510](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05510) David Chemaly et al. `Constraints on the population level distribution of nearby Dark Matter halo shapes with extragalactic streams`
  - **Question:** can images of extragalactic stellar streams constrain the population distribution of halo shapes?
  - **Data:**
    - Stream sample: `32` stellar streams from the STRRINGS catalogue.
    - High-quality subset: `17` gold streams.
    - Observable: projected stream tracks from imaging.
  - **Method:**
    - Forward-model each stream in an axisymmetric halo using only the projected track.
    - Inflate track uncertainties with an added variance term to handle systematics.
    - Combine individual posteriors through importance sampling.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The gold sample favors an oblate halo population with `mu_q~0.72` and `sigma_q~0.34`.
    - Streams dominated by additional model variance yield nearly spherical population constraints.
    - The gold-sample result is broadly consistent with cosmological hydrodynamical simulations.
  - **Position:** relevant survey-method paper because Euclid and Rubin/LSST will turn stream morphology into a statistical halo-shape probe.

## Other Notable Entries

- [2607.05505](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05505) Haonan Zheng et al. combine VVV underdense-region simulations with larger cosmological volumes to fit the `LambdaCDM` halo mass function from `10^-6` to `10^15.5 M_sun` and from `z=30` to today, with typical deviations of `~2-3%` at `z<2` and `~7%` at `z>~5`.
- [2607.06143](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06143) Pranjal Chaturrvedi et al. publish an AstroSat/UVIT FUV catalogue for ELAIS N1 with `1637` sources at `3 sigma`, `458` sources at `5 sigma`, and limiting magnitudes of `25.69` and `25.13` AB.
- [2607.06016](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06016) Lin Zhang et al. use red clump stars and a U-Net inversion to build Galactic-plane dust maps at `10`, `50`, and `100 pc` resolution out to `7 kpc`, deriving a dust disk scale length of `2.90 kpc`.
- [2607.06541](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06541) L. Colzi et al. measure high `12C/13C` ratios toward the CMZ cloud G+0.693-0.027, finding `36.7+/-1.0` for HC3N and `38.8+/-1.5` for HC5N, consistent with bar-driven inflow from the Galactic disk.
- [2607.05502](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05502) Elena Asencio et al. compare Milky Way classical-satellite tidal susceptibility in MOND and cold dark matter; the Local Group dynamics focus makes it peripheral to this profile.
- [2607.05495](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05495) Ishaan Satish et al. model stellar mergers in dense clusters and find that up to roughly half of black holes in Milky Way-like globular-cluster simulations can form from mergers, including `~10%` from significant mergers with `q>0.1`.
- [2607.05899](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05899) Jin-Hong Chen et al. model white-dwarf tidal disruption events by intermediate-mass black holes, predicting accretion rates `10^5-10^9` times typical main-sequence TDEs and possible decihertz gravitational-wave bursts.
- [2607.06154](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06154) M. Bagchi et al. review SKAO pulsar searches in globular clusters, forecasting that SKA-MID AA4 could more than double the current known cluster-pulsar population.
