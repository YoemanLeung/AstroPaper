## Executive View

The Friday listing has two clear high-redshift read-first papers. [2607.08749](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08749) Rebecca L. Larson uses deep JWST/NIRSpec and MIRI/LRS observations to update Maisie's Galaxy to `z=11.408+/-0.005`; its [OII]-based SFR, density, metallicity, and ionization parameter make it look like a typical early galaxy rather than an extreme outlier. [2607.08264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08264) Shunta Shimizu measures Lyalpha escape at `z~6.2` by pairing JWST/NIRCam F470N Halpha with Subaru/HSC NB872 Lyalpha, finding a completeness-weighted median `f_esc^Lyalpha=0.106(+0.066/-0.044)`.

Reionization-era interpretation and Lyalpha methods are strong. [2607.07832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07832) S. Goswami shows that common high-ionization line diagnostics can identify purely Pop III-dominated galaxies but become degenerate once Pop II stars contribute substantially. [2607.08726](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08726) Joshua Kasiri develops a convergence framework for Monte Carlo Lyalpha radiative-transfer force estimators, separating mean accuracy, sampling precision, and variance stability.

AGN and gas diagnostics are broad. [2607.07795](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07795) Z. Igo uses eROSITA plus Legacy Survey DR10 to find `874` X-ray AGN in low-mass galaxies and an AGN fraction of `~1%` above `lambda_SAR>10^-3`. [2607.07793](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07793) Emma Jane Weller tracks black holes from `z=2` to `z=0.5` in ASTRID and TNG300, showing that offsets in the `M_BH-M_star` plane encode mergers, quenching, stripping, and wandering black-hole histories. [2607.08721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08721) Nicholas Battaglia finds a factor-of-two excess tSZ signal in locally bright galaxies hosting co-spatial radio sources, raising a CGM feedback or halo-mass interpretation problem.

## High-Redshift Galaxies, Reionization, and Pop III Diagnostics

- [2607.08749](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08749) Rebecca L. Larson et al. `Deep Spectroscopic Follow-Up of Maisie's Galaxy -- A Typical Galaxy in the Early Universe`
  - **Question:** is Maisie's Galaxy an extreme early object or a representative star-forming galaxy at `z>11`?
  - **Data:**
    - Spectroscopy: two deep JWST/NIRSpec G395M Cycle 3 programs with more than `19 hr` total exposure.
    - Mid-infrared support: MIRI/LRS observations for the same high-redshift source.
    - Diagnostics: [OII], [OIII], [NeIII], SED fits, and rest-frame optical emission-line measurements.
  - **Method:**
    - Update the systemic redshift from deep emission-line spectroscopy.
    - Derive electron density, star-formation rate, metallicity, ionization parameter, and line-ratio diagnostics.
    - Compare the source against other `z>10` galaxies with similar line detections and SED-based properties.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The updated redshift is `z=11.408+/-0.005`.
    - [OII] implies `n_e=108.56(+873.9/-35.37)` and `SFR_[OII]=1.3+/-0.35`.
    - The line ratios give `log(Ne3O2)=-0.219+/-0.145` and `log(O32)=0.724+/-0.191`.
    - The inferred metallicity is `Z/Z_sun=0.17+/-0.05` and `log(U)=-2.26+/-0.13`.
    - The galaxy lies along the star-forming main sequence rather than requiring an extreme interpretation.
  - **Position:** read first because it turns one famous bright `z>10` JWST source into a calibration point for typical early galaxy physics.

- [2607.08264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08264) Shunta Shimizu et al. `Subaru meets JWST: A Direct Measurement of Lyalpha Escape Fraction at z~6.2 with Dual Narrow-Band Imaging`
  - **Question:** what is the direct Lyalpha escape fraction of Halpha-selected galaxies near the end of reionization?
  - **Data:**
    - Field: JWST CEERS.
    - Filters: JWST/NIRCam F470N for Halpha and Subaru/HSC NB872 for Lyalpha.
    - Sample: `84` Halpha emitters at `z~6.2`, with `56` having reliable NB872 photometry and `19` detected in Lyalpha above `2 sigma`.
  - **Method:**
    - Select Halpha emitters from F470N excesses.
    - Combine Lyalpha and Halpha narrow-band measurements to derive direct Lyalpha escape fractions.
    - Stack the sample and test trends with Halpha luminosity, Lyalpha equivalent width, UV slope, size, dust, and optical size.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The completeness-weighted stack gives median `f_esc^Lyalpha=0.106(+0.066/-0.044)` at `z~6.2`.
    - No significant stacked dependence appears over the probed lower limits of Halpha luminosity.
    - Individual `f_esc^Lyalpha` correlates positively with Lyalpha equivalent width and negatively with UV slope and rest-UV size.
    - No significant relation is found with SED-derived `E(B-V)` or rest-frame optical size in the available sample.
  - **Position:** high-priority EoR paper because it directly joins JWST Halpha and ground-based Lyalpha to measure escape, rather than inferring it from UV properties alone.

- [2607.07832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07832) S. Goswami et al. `On the detection of Population III galaxies: Emission Line Diagnostics for Hybrid Stellar Populations`
  - **Question:** can standard emission-line diagnostics identify Pop III stars when galaxies also contain Pop II populations?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: IllustrisTNG galaxies selected by Pop III-to-total stellar mass ratio.
    - Models: self-consistent photoionization calculations using local simulation conditions.
    - Diagnostics: high-ionization lines, including HeII-sensitive diagnostics.
  - **Method:**
    - Build integrated spectra for hybrid Pop III and Pop II stellar populations.
    - Vary the Pop III contribution and galaxy environment.
    - Test how standard line-ratio diagnostics respond as Pop II emission increases.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Diagnostics can identify galaxies dominated almost entirely by Pop III stars.
    - Significant Pop II contribution creates degeneracies that make Pop III signatures hard to isolate.
    - Hybrid populations require additional diagnostics beyond the current high-ionization line set.
  - **Position:** important interpretation paper because JWST Pop III searches must handle mixed stellar populations rather than only idealized metal-free systems.

- [2607.08726](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08726) Joshua Kasiri et al. `Force convergence in Monte Carlo Lyman-alpha radiative transfer`
  - **Question:** how can Lyalpha Monte Carlo radiative-transfer calculations prove convergence for internal force and acceleration fields?
  - **Data:**
    - Testbed: static spherical clouds.
    - Quantities: radial acceleration profiles, integrated force multipliers, photon-packet contribution distributions, and runtime requirements.
    - Estimators: direct event-based scattering, gradient-of-energy-density, and divergence-of-radiation-pressure methods.
  - **Method:**
    - Establish diffusion-limit benchmarks for radiative acceleration and force multipliers.
    - Separate convergence into mean accuracy, finite-sampling uncertainty, and variance-estimate stability.
    - Test the effects of core-skipping prescriptions, source geometry, estimator construction, and spatial resolution.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Correct mean convergence, statistical precision, and stable uncertainty estimates are distinct requirements.
    - Photon number, runtime, and estimator choice control different levels of convergence.
    - Force convergence needs diagnostics beyond emergent spectra alone.
  - **Position:** useful Lyalpha-method paper because radiation-pressure and feedback calculations need internal momentum deposition, not only escaping-line profiles.

## AGN, Black-Hole Growth, and CGM/ICM Feedback

- [2607.07795](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07795) Z. Igo et al. `The incidence of eROSITA X-ray AGN in the local Universe: from dwarf to massive galaxies`
  - **Question:** what does eROSITA reveal about accreting black holes in low-redshift low-mass galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - X-ray survey: four-pass eROSITA All Sky Survey.
    - Parent galaxies: `~5.35 million` Legacy Survey DR10 galaxies with `z`-band fluxes brighter than `20 mag` and `0.03<z<0.2`.
    - Low-mass AGN sample: `874` X-ray AGN in galaxies with `log(M_*/M_sun)<10`.
  - **Method:**
    - Match X-ray detections to a highly complete low-redshift galaxy parent sample.
    - Validate X-ray aperture photometry and host associations.
    - Use a Bayesian framework to infer `p(log lambda_SAR | M_*, z)` from detections and non-detections.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The low-mass AGN detections are mostly newly discovered X-ray emitters.
    - The specific accretion-rate distribution shows mass-dependent structure and a possible high-`lambda_SAR` break.
    - Integrating above `lambda_SAR>10^-3` gives a cumulative AGN fraction of `~1%` for low-mass galaxies.
    - The result sets a firm lower limit on the black-hole occupation fraction in this regime.
  - **Position:** useful low-redshift anchor because dwarf-galaxy occupation and accretion duty cycles calibrate seed and early-growth interpretations.

- [2607.07793](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07793) Emma Jane Weller et al. `Tracing black hole and galaxy growth across environments since cosmic noon`
  - **Question:** how do black holes move through the `M_BH-M_star` plane as hosts merge, quench, strip, or become satellites?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: ASTRID and TNG300.
    - Redshift range: `z=2` to `z=0.5`, spanning `5.3 Gyr`.
    - Populations: central black holes, satellite black holes, and off-nuclear wandering black holes.
  - **Method:**
    - Track black holes themselves rather than only their original host galaxies.
    - Compare trajectories in `M_BH-M_star` and specific BH accretion rate versus specific SFR.
    - Interpret departures from the central relation by environment and dynamical history.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Central black holes evolve along a tight, nearly redshift-invariant relation consistent with local constraints and variable-AGN measurements.
    - High-mass central black holes grow through mergers and quench their hosts through AGN kinetic feedback.
    - Tidal stripping moves satellites to lower `M_star` at nearly fixed `M_BH`, producing overmassive weakly accreting black holes.
    - Wandering black holes in ASTRID are undermassive relative to their new hosts and undergo little accretion or merger growth.
  - **Position:** relevant AGN-environment paper because scaling-relation outliers encode dynamical history rather than simple measurement scatter.

- [2607.08721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08721) Nicholas Battaglia et al. `Thermal Sunyaev-Zel'dovich Measurements of Locally Bright Galaxies with ACT DR6: Radio Source Contamination and Excess Compton-y Signal`
  - **Question:** how do radio sources affect tSZ measurements of locally bright galaxy halos?
  - **Data:**
    - Maps: ACT DR6 component-separated Compton-`y` maps.
    - Sample: Planck Locally Bright Galaxies over about one-third of the sky.
    - Diagnostic: cylindrical Compton-`y` signal as a function of stellar mass and radio-source association.
  - **Method:**
    - Re-measure the stellar-mass to tSZ scaling relation with ACT.
    - Identify residual radio-source contamination at the few percent level.
    - Split LBGs by co-spatial radio-source presence and compare stacked tSZ profiles.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - ACT recovers a power-law scaling between cylindrical Compton-`y` signal and stellar mass.
    - Residual radio-source contamination is present at the few-percent level.
    - LBGs hosting co-spatial radio sources show a factor-of-two excess Compton-`y` signal at fixed stellar mass.
    - The excess persists to radii of at least `6 arcmin` and may trace halo-mass differences or AGN thermal-energy injection into the CGM.
  - **Position:** useful CGM feedback paper because tSZ cross-correlations require explicit radio-source control before interpreting halo gas energetics.

- [2607.07818](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07818) M. I. Arnaudova et al. `The DESI View of the Faint Radio Source Population in LoTSS DR2`
  - **Question:** how can DESI spectroscopy classify faint LoTSS radio sources into star-forming and AGN populations?
  - **Data:**
    - Radio survey: LoTSS DR2.
    - Spectroscopy: DESI DR1.
    - Sample: `251,413` radio sources classified probabilistically.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine radio excess, BPT, modified MEx, and [OIII] equivalent-width diagnostics.
    - Use Monte Carlo methods to estimate class probabilities.
    - Separate SFGs, radio-quiet AGN, LERGs, and HERGs at selectable probability thresholds.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At `90%` probability threshold the sample includes `68,820` SFGs, `32,288` RQ AGN, `35,210` LERGs, and `3,085` HERGs.
    - LERGs typically accrete below `1%` of Eddington and HERGs above it.
    - A subset of high-accreting LERGs shows stacked star-forming spectral features, complicating accretion interpretation.
  - **Position:** important survey-infrastructure paper because SKA-era radio demographics need spectroscopic labels at this scale.

## Gas Physics, Lensing, and Survey Infrastructure

- [2607.07973](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07973) J. Eduardo Mendez-Delgado et al. `There is no single density: star-forming regions and galaxies hold more dense ionized gas than long assumed`
  - **Question:** why do different density-sensitive emission lines return inconsistent electron densities in the same gas?
  - **Data:**
    - Systems: individual HII regions and integrated galaxies across cosmic time.
    - Diagnostics: multiple density-sensitive ionized-gas emission lines.
    - Physical model: broad gas density distributions rather than a single representative density.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare each diagnostic's returned density with the density range where that line is most sensitive.
    - Test whether discrepancies require atomic-data errors or ionization-zone stratification.
    - Generalize the relation from local nebulae to whole galaxies.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Each diagnostic responds to the part of a broad density distribution where it is most sensitive.
    - The disagreement among diagnostics is not mainly an atomic-data or ionization-stratification failure.
    - Single-density assumptions can bias inferred masses, pressures, abundances, and energetics.
  - **Position:** useful line-diagnostic paper because high-redshift nebular analyses often rely on sparse density tracers and single-zone assumptions.

- [2607.08576](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08576) Tian Li et al. `Disentangling the dark and stellar mass through precise lens modelling of the JWST observation of lensed quasar WFI2033--4723`
  - **Question:** can JWST time-delay lens imaging separate stellar and dark matter in a quadruply imaged quasar lens?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: lensed quasar WFI2033--4723.
    - Imaging: high-resolution JWST/NIRCam.
    - External information: measured time delays and two cosmological priors, Planck+DESI and Pantheon+SH0ES.
  - **Method:**
    - Build an elliptical power-law baseline model.
    - Replace the total mass profile with a composite stellar plus generalized NFW dark-matter model.
    - Use time delays to constrain mass-sheet freedom in the stellar-dark decomposition.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Fermat-potential differences match previous HST- and JWST-based analyses.
    - The stellar normalization lies between Chabrier and Salpeter IMF expectations.
    - A radial mass-to-light gradient is only mildly preferred.
    - The dark halo has an inner slope `gamma_in~1.3`, steeper than standard NFW, with conclusions insensitive to the cosmological prior.
  - **Position:** relevant lensing method paper because JWST image quality and time delays can constrain baryon-dark matter decompositions in quasar lenses.

- [2607.08657](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08657) Alejandro Mus et al. `VAPOLA -- A multi-year, multi-band polarization survey of AGN and Sgr A* at mm wavelengths with ALMA I. Survey Overview and Science-Ready Archival Products`
  - **Question:** can ALMA VLBI-mode polarization data be turned into ready-to-use AGN and Sgr A* products?
  - **Data:**
    - Facility: ALMA Phasing System observations during global VLBI campaigns.
    - Products: calibrated interferometric visibilities, full-Stokes images, polarization maps, spectral-index maps, and tabulated polarization parameters.
    - Targets: AGN and Sgr A*.
  - **Method:**
    - Build an automated pipeline for fully calibrated ALMA QA2 data.
    - Generate image-domain and visibility-domain polarization products.
    - Serve products through an online portal for non-expert users.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - VAPOLA provides the first online multi-epoch, multi-band repository of high-level ALMA VLBI-mode products.
    - The data support studies of magnetic fields in accretion flows, relativistic jets, dusty or molecular tori, and Galactic-center absorption.
    - Source-integrated parameters and calibration metadata help refine VLBI calibration and imaging workflows.
  - **Position:** useful AGN infrastructure because curated mm polarization products reduce the barrier to jet and accretion-flow science.

- [2607.07750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07750) Ramananda Santra et al. `Multi-Wavelength Signatures of a Giant Cometary Radio Halo in MACSJ0417-1154`
  - **Question:** what powers the giant cometary radio halo in the galaxy cluster MACSJ0417-1154?
  - **Data:**
    - Cluster: MACSJ0417 at `z=0.445`.
    - Radio data: uGMRT `300-850 MHz` and MeerKAT `900-1670 MHz`.
    - X-ray data: archival XMM-Newton.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure integrated and resolved radio spectra for the halo and relic candidates.
    - Compare radio surface brightness edges with X-ray discontinuities and temperature structure.
    - Test turbulent re-acceleration and pure hadronic scenarios.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The halo extends to `~1.75 Mpc` at `400 MHz`, with candidate relics at `2.9 Mpc`.
    - Integrated spectral indices are `alpha~-1.3` for the halo and `alpha~-1.6` for relics.
    - Radio and X-ray surface brightness correlate with coefficient `~0.85`.
    - A pure hadronic model is excluded by unrealistic cosmic-ray proton energy requirements.
    - The cluster likely experienced a minor off-axis dissociative merger with mass ratio `~6:1`.
  - **Position:** useful ICM environment entry because it connects merger geometry, turbulence, and non-thermal plasma in a massive cluster.
