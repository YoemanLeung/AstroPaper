## Executive View

The Thursday listing has one clear high-redshift read-first paper. [2607.07698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07698) Adarsh Kuruvanthodi uses JWST/NIRCam photometry from CEERS, JADES, FRESCO, and PRIMER to track Balmer-break strength over `z=3.5-10`. The median break strength rises from `1.1` near cosmic dawn to `1.5` toward cosmic noon, and extremely strong Balmer breaks, `BB>3.0`, select dusty or old systems and Little Red Dots at `z=3.5-4` and `z=7-10`.

CGM and feedback are the strongest physical theme. [2607.06744](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06744) Matthias Weber shows that cosmic-ray transport changes the cool and warm CGM phases that dominate low- and intermediate-ion absorption, with efficient transport producing stronger MgII and SiII absorption. [2607.06844](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06844) Yerong Xu reports a nuclear ultra-fast outflow in the long-quenched galaxy KUG 1208+386, with `v_out~-0.07c` and kinetic power `(0.8-6.5) x 10^43 erg/s`, supporting a wind-maintenance mode of AGN feedback.

Survey infrastructure and environment diagnostics are broad. [2607.07296](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07296) Euclid Collaboration: M. Giulietti identifies `166` radio-selected Euclid-dark galaxies in EDF-N, including `88` isolated sources, with photometric redshifts spanning `0.4<=z_ph<=5.0` and AGN evidence in about `40%`. [2607.07441](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07441) Filip Janak estimates HI deficiency from optical properties and measures a `0.15 dex` median environmental contribution after controlling for internal factors. [2607.06662](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06662) A. Hernan-Caballero releases J-PLUS DR4 classifications and photometric redshifts for `79.2 million` sources to `r=22`, with WISE reducing quasar catastrophic outliers from `~40%` to `~23%`.

The cross-list [2607.06828](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06828) Pablo Marchant is useful context for JWST high-redshift abundance anomalies. Very massive stars above `100 M_sun` in the Tarantula Nebula show nitrogen enrichment by factors of `>~5-10`; standard mass loss, binary fraction, and star-formation-history variations do not reproduce the trend.

## High-Redshift Stellar Populations and Enrichment

- [2607.07698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07698) Adarsh Kuruvanthodi et al. `Tracing the Evolution of the Balmer Break from Cosmic Dawn to Cosmic Noon with JWST`
  - **Question:** how does Balmer-break strength evolve across the galaxy population from cosmic dawn to cosmic noon?
  - **Data:**
    - Imaging: JWST/NIRCam photometry from CEERS, JADES, FRESCO, and PRIMER.
    - Redshift range: photometric galaxy samples across `z=3.5-10`.
    - Spectral feature: Balmer-break strength measured with adjacent broad-band filters chosen to avoid strong line contamination.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure Balmer-break flux ratios in several redshift windows.
    - Use CIGALE for SED fitting and mock-galaxy simulations.
    - Test correlations between break strength and galaxy physical parameters.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The median Balmer-break strength rises from `1.1` to `1.5` from cosmic dawn toward cosmic noon.
    - The rise is primarily driven by older stellar populations.
    - Extremely strong breaks, `BB>3.0`, appear at `z=3.5-4` and `z=7-10`.
    - The extreme-break population points to strong extinction plus old stars and Little Red Dots at `z=3.5-4`, and mainly Little Red Dots at `z=7-10`.
  - **Position:** read first because it connects JWST photometric selection, LRD contamination or physics, and early stellar-population ages in one diagnostic.

- [2607.06828](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06828) Pablo Marchant et al. `Nitrogen rises to the top: evidence of enhanced mixing in very massive stars`
  - **Question:** can very massive stars explain rapid nitrogen enrichment observed in young high-redshift galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Stellar sample: `122` Tarantula Nebula stars in the Large Magellanic Cloud.
    - Mass range: representative sample with `M>~30 M_sun`, including stars above `100 M_sun`.
    - Diagnostic: surface nitrogen enrichment as a function of stellar mass.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare the observed nitrogen-rich fraction with stellar evolution models.
    - Vary binary fraction, star-formation history, and mass-loss assumptions.
    - Test whether enhanced core overshooting can reproduce the observed high-mass enrichment trend.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Nearly all stars above `100 M_sun` show nitrogen enrichment by factors of `>~5-10`.
    - Standard mass loss, binary fraction, and star-formation-history variations do not reproduce the trend.
    - Enhanced overshooting with `alpha_ov>~1` can enrich the stars but produces quasi-homogeneous evolution inconsistent with the observed HR diagram.
    - Efficient early mixing during or soon after very massive-star formation is required by the data.
  - **Position:** useful high-z interpretation paper because nitrogen-rich JWST galaxies may require stellar mixing physics missing from standard tracks.

- [2607.07010](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07010) Nolan Dickson et al. `Fast Dynamical Modelling of Milky Way Globular Clusters -- I. Implications for Initial Cluster Densities`
  - **Question:** what initial densities are required to evolve into the present Milky Way globular-cluster population?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `40` Milky Way globular clusters.
    - Observables: radial number-density profiles, proper motions, line-of-sight velocities, and stellar mass functions.
    - Validation: grid of star-by-star Monte Carlo cluster models.
  - **Method:**
    - Couple rapid cluster evolution models to multimass equilibrium models.
    - Fit present-day observations to recover initial and current cluster properties.
    - Infer initial mass, half-mass radius, half-mass density, and black-hole mass fraction.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The inferred initial half-mass density distribution has median `rho_h,0=10^6.4 M_sun pc^-3` with `1 sigma` width `0.9 dex`.
    - The densities exceed those of many young massive clusters in the local Universe and are consistent with young clusters at high redshift.
    - Present-day black-hole mass fractions are small, `<=1.5%`.
    - The inferred initial mass functions are bottom-light relative to canonical prescriptions.
  - **Position:** relevant fossil-record result because proto-globular clusters remain a candidate high-redshift compact star-formation channel.

## CGM, Feedback, and Gas Cycling

- [2607.06744](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06744) Matthias Weber et al. `CRexit observed: probing cosmic ray transport in the circumgalactic medium with absorption line spectra`
  - **Question:** can absorption-line spectra distinguish cosmic-ray transport regimes in CR-pressure-dominated halos?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: high-resolution galaxy-halo simulations with different cosmic-ray transport prescriptions.
    - Mock observations: synthetic absorption spectra over large sightline ensembles.
    - Diagnostics: column densities, equivalent widths, covering fractions, velocity widths, abundance ratios, and stacked profiles.
  - **Method:**
    - Generate spectra for cool, warm, and hot CGM ions.
    - Compare slow and efficient CR transport models.
    - Track how MgII, SiII, CIV, and OVI respond to changes in multiphase gas structure.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Efficient CR transport enhances cool `T~10^4 K` and warm `T~10^5 K` CGM gas.
    - The two-moment CR transport model produces the strongest MgII and SiII absorption and reaches MgII covering fractions consistent with star-forming-galaxy inferences.
    - Slow CR transport underproduces cool low-ionization gas and yields weaker absorption.
    - CIV shifts from extended warm halo gas in slow-transport cases to mixing layers around cool clouds in efficient-transport cases.
  - **Position:** important CGM-method paper because metal absorption can become a direct constraint on cosmic-ray transport physics.

- [2607.06844](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06844) Yerong Xu et al. `First detection of ultra-fast outflows in a quiescent galaxy`
  - **Question:** can powerful AGN winds operate in gas-poor quiescent galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: nearby quiescent galaxy KUG 1208+386.
    - Nuclear diagnostic: X-ray ultra-fast outflow.
    - Host diagnostics: quiescent stellar population, [OIII] galactic outflow, and obscuring column densities.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure the nuclear UFO velocity and kinetic power.
    - Compare nuclear wind power with galactic-scale [OIII] outflow power.
    - Model host quenching history and obscuring columns.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The nuclear UFO has `v_out~-0.07c`.
    - The kinetic power is `(0.8-6.5) x 10^43 erg/s`, sufficient for effective AGN feedback at `(1-8)%` of Eddington.
    - The [OIII] outflow power is only `~10^40 erg/s`.
    - The host has `sSFR~3 x 10^-12 yr^-1` and was quenched `~9 Gyr` ago.
    - The line-of-sight column is `log N_H~23`, while circumnuclear scattering material is Compton-thick with `log N_H=24.7`.
  - **Position:** useful AGN-feedback result because it supports episodic wind maintenance in quiescent galaxies outside the classical radio-jet mode.

- [2607.06914](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06914) Ren Matsusaka et al. `A CO detection in the off-plane region of the edge-on galaxy NGC 4565 with the Nobeyama 45-m telescope`
  - **Question:** does molecular gas above a disk trace internal cycling or external inflow?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: edge-on Milky Way-like galaxy NGC 4565.
    - Instrument: Nobeyama 45-m `12CO(J=1-0)` observations at `14 arcsec`, or `~0.8 kpc`, resolution.
    - Supporting context: a prominent dust filament above the galactic plane.
  - **Method:**
    - Observe multiple off-plane positions and test beam-pattern contamination.
    - Derive per-beam molecular gas masses and line widths.
    - Compare off-plane CO intensity with geometrically thin-disk models.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Significant off-plane CO is detected at three positions.
    - Per-beam molecular masses are `(2.1-4.3) x 10^7 M_sun`.
    - Off-plane effective line widths are `83-115 km/s`, broader than disk spectra.
    - A high-velocity component has `M_mol~10^7 M_sun`, is offset by `~100 km/s`, and has `E_kin~10^54 erg`.
    - The energy requirement is difficult to explain with disk feedback in a Milky Way-like star-forming disk, making external inflow plausible.
  - **Position:** relevant gas-cycling paper because molecular extraplanar material can trace accretion as well as feedback.

## Survey Infrastructure, Environment, and Obscured Populations

- [2607.07296](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07296) Euclid Collaboration: M. Giulietti et al. `Euclid Quick Data Release (Q1). Searching for radio-selected Euclid-dark galaxies in the EDF-N`
  - **Question:** what population is selected by radio detections that remain dark in Euclid Q1 optical and near-infrared imaging?
  - **Data:**
    - Field: Euclid Deep Field-North.
    - Radio selection: LOFAR HBA observations at `144 MHz`.
    - Sample: `166` Euclid-dark galaxies, with an isolated subsample of `88`.
    - Ancillary data: Spitzer/IRAC, far-infrared data, and sub-arcsecond International LOFAR Telescope imaging.
  - **Method:**
    - Select radio sources with no Euclid Q1 counterpart above `3 sigma`.
    - Fit UV-to-radio SEDs to infer physical properties.
    - Use brightness temperatures and radio excess relative to the infrared/radio correlation to flag AGN activity.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The photometric-redshift distribution spans `0.4<=z_ph<=5.0`.
    - About `40%` of the sources show evidence for AGN activity.
    - Far-infrared detected systems are consistent with heavily obscured, massive star-forming galaxies with high SFRs.
    - The sources likely capture a phase where intense star formation and AGN activity coexist.
  - **Position:** strong survey-infrastructure entry because Euclid plus LOFAR can find obscured galaxy-black-hole growth missed by optical catalogues.

- [2607.06662](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06662) A. Hernan-Caballero et al. `J-PLUS: Spectral classification and photometric redshifts for 79 million sources in the fourth data release`
  - **Question:** how well can J-PLUS DR4 classify sources and estimate photometric redshifts over a wide area?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: J-PLUS Data Release 4.
    - Catalogue size: `79.2 million` sources to `r=22`.
    - Photometry: the `12`-band J-PLUS filter system plus WISE infrared photometry for some classifications.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare LePhare template fitting with the LeMoNNADE machine-learning pipeline.
    - Use spectral mixing augmentation to address limited training sets.
    - Apply Expectation-Maximization Bayesian calibration to recover unbiased class probabilities.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - LeMoNNADE outperforms template fitting in precision, robust scatter, and outlier rate.
    - Adding WISE reduces the catastrophic outlier rate for quasars from `~40%` to `~23%`.
    - Systemic redshift bias stays below `1%` up to `z=4`.
    - Extragalactic counts agree with literature down to the `r~20.5` completeness limit.
  - **Position:** useful catalogue paper because wide-area calibrated classifications support quasar, galaxy, and environment pre-selection.

- [2607.07441](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07441) Filip Janak et al. `A machine learning approach to estimating HI deficiency in galaxies`
  - **Question:** can optical properties predict the expected HI content needed to measure environmental gas removal?
  - **Data:**
    - Training sample: `6982` isolated ALFALFA galaxies with SDSS optical photometry.
    - Application sample: non-isolated ALFALFA galaxies.
    - Target metric: HI deficiency relative to the expected unaltered HI content.
  - **Method:**
    - Train a random-forest model to predict original HI content from optical properties.
    - Compare performance with the traditional optical-size relation.
    - Control for internal factors such as stellar mass and AGN presence before isolating environment.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The ML model reaches `RMSE~0.22 dex` and `R^2~0.80`.
    - The traditional method gives `RMSE~0.26 dex` and `R^2~0.70`.
    - Environmental effects increase binned median HI deficiency by `0.15 dex`.
    - The method also models temporal evolution after gas-removal events through stellar-population changes.
  - **Position:** practical environment paper because HI deficiency becomes a scalable gas-removal metric for groups and clusters.

- [2607.07329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07329) J. A. Alcolea et al. `Beyond traditional emission-line diagnostics: using autoencoders to uncover active galactic nuclei in DESI spectra`
  - **Question:** can full-spectrum representation learning recover AGN missed by line-ratio diagnostics?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: DESI Main Survey spectra from the Guadalupe dataset, released in DR1.
    - Training set: `50,222` spectra at `z<=0.5`.
    - Labels: FastSpecFit emission-line classes including AGN, broad-line AGN, composite, star-forming, passive, retired, and Other.
  - **Method:**
    - Compress DESI spectra with the SPENDER autoencoder.
    - Classify sources in latent space with a k-d tree nearest-neighbor search.
    - Benchmark against traditional emission-line diagnostics.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Classification accuracy is `0.952` for AGN and `0.965` for broad-line AGN.
    - The method identifies AGN in low-S/N spectra and recovers sources missed by single-diagnostic methods.
    - The latent space correlates with stellar mass and star-formation rate.
    - The framework is scalable to current and future spectroscopic surveys.
  - **Position:** useful survey-method paper because AGN completeness controls demographic and environment studies.

## Structure, Clusters, and Lensing Context

- [2607.06891](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06891) Renhao Ye et al. `From DESI to Euclid: A Generative Bridge to Unbiased Galaxy Structures`
  - **Question:** can ground-based DESI imaging be translated into Euclid-like structural measurements?
  - **Data:**
    - Input imaging: DESI Bright Galaxy Survey targets.
    - Target domain: Euclid VIS-like image structure.
    - Validation metric: Fourier-domain recovery and structural-parameter bias.
  - **Method:**
    - Train a generative model to translate DESI images into Euclid VIS-like images.
    - Test recovered spatial structure against the DESI `r`-band seeing baseline.
    - Measure size and Sersic-parameter biases before and after translation.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The method recovers structure down to `0.37 arcsec`, compared with the `1.41 arcsec` DESI `r`-band baseline.
    - Petrosian-radius bias improves to `+0.075 arcsec` from `-0.870 arcsec`.
    - Sersic-radius bias improves to `-0.018 arcsec` from `-0.322 arcsec`.
    - Sersic-index bias improves to `+0.093` from `+0.262`.
  - **Position:** relevant morphology infrastructure because large-area ground imaging can be de-biased before full space-based coverage exists.

- [2607.06977](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06977) Kazunori Suda et al. `XRISM Reveals a Kinematically Coherent Core System of the Nearby Cool-Core Cluster Abell 2199`
  - **Question:** what gas velocity structure does XRISM resolve in the Abell 2199 cool core?
  - **Data:**
    - Instrument: XRISM/Resolve.
    - Exposure: `251 ks`.
    - Region: central `3 arcmin x 3 arcmin`, or `104 x 104 kpc^2`, field of view.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit the integrated Resolve spectrum.
    - Compare ICM and brightest-cluster-galaxy redshifts.
    - Measure velocity dispersion, Mach number, and non-thermal pressure fraction.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The ICM and BCG form a kinematically coherent core offset by `~200 km/s` from the mean cluster redshift.
    - The velocity dispersion is `~100 km/s`.
    - The inferred `M_3D=0.16`.
    - The non-thermal pressure fraction is `1.4+/-0.2%`.
    - Turbulent dissipation could supply `Q_turb/Q_cool~0.2` for large-scale sloshing drivers.
  - **Position:** useful cluster-environment benchmark because XRISM is turning ICM turbulence into a direct observable.

- [2607.07268](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07268) Yingxiao Song et al. `Quantifying Environmental Effects on Galaxy Properties using Non-spherical Voids Identified from SDSS DR7`
  - **Question:** how do non-spherical void environments affect galaxy color and specific star formation?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: SDSS DR7.
    - Environment finder: non-spherical voids from Voronoi tessellation and watershed segmentation.
    - Galaxy metrics: stellar mass, luminosity, color, and sSFR.
  - **Method:**
    - Classify void galaxies by local volume.
    - Compare void and non-void galaxies at fixed stellar-mass bins.
    - Measure blue-to-red and star-forming-to-quiescent ratios in each environment.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Void galaxies are systematically less massive, fainter, bluer, and higher in sSFR than non-void galaxies.
    - The ratio of void to non-void blue/red and star-forming/quiescent metrics declines with stellar mass.
    - The stronger environmental effect occurs in lower-mass systems over `log(M_*/M_sun)=9.4-10.4`.
  - **Position:** useful low-redshift environment paper because it quantifies underdense-environment effects with non-spherical void geometry.
