## High-Redshift Galaxies, Structure, and Early Enrichment

- [2607.09647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09647) Yuzo Ishikawa et al. `Spatial decomposition of Little Red Dots with JWST/NIRSpec IFU into broad-line red cores and narrow-line blue host galaxies`
  - **Question:** do Little Red Dot spectra come from a single compact component or a central engine embedded in a host galaxy?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRSpec: IFU spectroscopy of five broad-Halpha LRDs at `z~5`.
    - Spectral setup: prism and high-resolution G395H grating observations.
    - Diagnostics: broad Balmer lines, narrow emission lines, continuum color, and [OIII] equivalent-width maps.
  - **Method:**
    - Decompose continuum and emission-line components spatially and spectrally.
    - Compare the morphology of red continuum, blue continuum, broad lines, and narrow lines.
    - Test whether the components align with central-engine and host-galaxy interpretations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The red continuum is compact and co-spatial with broad Balmer emission and absorption.
    - The blue continuum is co-spatial with the narrow-line region.
    - Central [OIII] equivalent width drops sharply.
    - The data support a red central engine embedded in a blue host galaxy.
  - **Position:** weekly read-first because it changes LRD interpretation from pure SED classification to spatially resolved component separation.

- [2607.11182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11182) Ronaldo Laishram et al. `A Compact Proto-group at z~5: A Massive Galaxy Caught in Formation`
  - **Question:** what does group-scale assembly look like before a massive high-redshift galaxy coalesces?
  - **Data:**
    - Field: MACS0416 SAPPHIRES Early Data Release.
    - Members: six Halpha-confirmed galaxies at `4.96<=z_spec<=4.98`.
    - Scale: projected diameter `~16 pkpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Confirm compact member galaxies spectroscopically with Halpha.
    - Fit SEDs and derive spatially resolved star-formation properties.
    - Compare with analogous systems in EAGLE.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Total stellar mass is `log(M*/Msun)=10.07+/-0.04`.
    - The projected mass estimator gives `log(M_PM/Msun)~12.30`.
    - Three members lie on or above the `z~5` star-forming main sequence.
    - EAGLE analogs predict coalescence by `z~3-4`.
  - **Position:** important protocluster entry because it catches early group assembly in a compact, spectroscopically confirmed phase.

- [2607.12016](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12016) Luca Costantin et al. `The onset of stellar bars at Cosmic Noon`
  - **Question:** how can an early stellar bar affect quenching, disc growth, and AGN feedback at Cosmic Noon?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: EGS-24154, a barred spiral at `z=1.17`.
    - Instrument: JWST/NIRSpec medium-resolution IFU spectroscopy.
    - Diagnostics: resolved stellar populations, emission-line ratios, dynamics, and outflow signatures.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit spatially resolved ages, metallicities, and star-formation histories.
    - Diagnose ionization mechanisms across the galaxy.
    - Model galaxy dynamics and a biconical AGN outflow.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - More than `90%` of the stellar mass formed when the Universe was `~2 Gyr` old.
    - The bar population began forming near `z~5`.
    - The bar region shows a star-formation desert.
    - AGN feedback may have limited central mass buildup and supported bar growth.
  - **Position:** high-value JWST structure paper because it resolves bar-driven evolution before the familiar local-Universe regime.

- [2607.14355](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14355) Yoshihisa Asada et al. `VENUS: an ultra-faint galaxy hosting the metal-poor type II supernova at z=5.13`
  - **Question:** what can a lensed high-redshift supernova host reveal about ultra-faint early galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: SN Eos and its multiply imaged host at `z=5.13`.
    - Lensing: total magnification `mu~53`.
    - Host diagnostics: rest-UV magnitude, Lyman-alpha, Halpha, and [OIII]/Hbeta.
  - **Method:**
    - Use lensing to localize the supernova within the host.
    - Infer local metallicity from weak oxygen emission and the metal-poor SN context.
    - Compare detectability with unlensed NIRCam survey limits.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The host has `M_UV=-14.4+/-0.3`.
    - The host is a high-equivalent-width Lyman-alpha emitter.
    - [OIII] 5007 is weak, with `[OIII]5007/Hbeta<0.7`.
    - The environment plausibly has metallicity below `1% Z_sun`.
    - The host would be undetectable in blank-field NIRCam surveys without lensing.
  - **Position:** important because transients and lensing expose the ultra-faint galaxy population that direct imaging often misses.

- [2607.13269](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13269) Vahid Asadi et al. `COSMOS2025: A Machine Learning Census of Massive Quiescent Galaxies at 2.5 < z < 5`
  - **Question:** how incomplete are standard SED-fitting quiescent selections at `2.5<z<5`?
  - **Data:**
    - Catalog: COSMOS2025.
    - Training source: Santa Cruz semi-analytic model mock photometry.
    - Target regime: `log(M*/Msun)>9.5` at `2.5<z<5`.
  - **Method:**
    - Train a CatBoost classifier on mock JWST/NIRCam-like photometry.
    - Validate against model sSFR-defined quiescence.
    - Apply the classifier to COSMOS2025 and compare with SED-fitting labels.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Mock-validation recall and purity are `78%` and `82%`.
    - The SED-fitting baseline recall is `53%`.
    - The classifier identifies `1111` COSMOS2025 quiescent candidates.
    - The catalog SED-fitting setup identifies `427` candidates.
  - **Position:** key high-z census paper because quenching demographics depend directly on selection completeness.

## Cosmic Star Formation, Dust, and Transient Constraints

- [2607.12018](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12018) Christa DeCoursey et al. `The JADES Transient Survey II: Volumetric Supernova Rates out to z~5`
  - **Question:** what direct supernova-rate constraints can JWST provide at `z~2-5`?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: JADES Transient Survey in the JADES Deep Field.
    - Area: `~25 arcmin^2`.
    - Sample: `83` supernova candidates.
  - **Method:**
    - Classify candidates from single-epoch SEDs when light curves or spectra are unavailable.
    - Simulate `~23,000` mock core-collapse and Type Ia supernova SEDs.
    - Derive redshift-binned volumetric rates.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Full-sample core-collapse rates are `6.2(+2.2/-1.7)x10^-4 yr^-1 Mpc^-3` at `2.06<=z<2.78`.
    - The rate is `4.1(+1.5/-1.1)x10^-4 yr^-1 Mpc^-3` at `2.78<=z<=5.06`.
    - The rates broadly match galaxy-luminosity-based star-formation histories.
    - The sample tentatively shows the expected decline beyond cosmic noon.
  - **Position:** important independent check on galaxy-based star-formation histories at high redshift.

- [2607.12028](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12028) Christian Vassallo et al. `The JADES Transient Survey III: Linking Core-Collapse Supernova Rates to Cosmic Star Formation`
  - **Question:** can core-collapse supernova rates reconstruct the cosmic star-formation history consistently?
  - **Data:**
    - Inputs: JADES high-redshift rates and published CCSN rates.
    - Redshift range: `0<=z<=5`.
    - Model factors: IMF, progenitor mass ranges, production efficiency, and dust-obscuration corrections.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit CCSN production efficiency.
    - Reconstruct SFRD directly from observed CCSN rates.
    - Test missed-supernova fractions under different dust-obscured SFRD scenarios.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Observed CCSN rates are consistent with dust-corrected UV+IR SFRDs.
    - The reconstructed star-formation history peaks near `z~2`.
    - IMF evolution has modest impact when treated consistently.
    - High dusty-millimeter SFRDs require substantial missed obscured supernovae.
  - **Position:** important companion to UV, IR, and millimeter SFRD work because it tests stellar death directly.

- [2607.08824](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08824) Enrico Garaldi et al. `The Thesan-Zoom project: bursty star formation is incompatible with prolonged dust survival`
  - **Question:** can bursty high-redshift galaxies keep large dust reservoirs for long periods?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: Thesan-Zoom radiation-hydrodynamic zoom-ins.
    - Physics: dust formation, growth, destruction, and radiative-transfer coupling.
    - Redshift regime: `z>=3`.
  - **Method:**
    - Model dust self-consistently in a bursty multiphase ISM.
    - Compare dust-to-gas ratios, dust-to-metal ratios, temperatures, and UV-IR offsets with observations.
    - Track the duration of dust-rich phases.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - IR-bright phases are short, with median duration `20.3(+2.3/-2.4) Myr`.
    - Predicted `1500 A` attenuation remains low compared with observations.
    - Long-lived reservoirs with `M_dust/M_star>=10^-3` are difficult to maintain.
    - Bursty star formation naturally creates short dust-rich windows.
  - **Position:** useful for JWST and ALMA interpretation because dust duty cycle controls observed high-redshift galaxy demographics.

- [2607.14758](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14758) Aaron Myszka et al. `The Mysterious Case of Iron in XMPs`
  - **Question:** can local XMPs identify enrichment channels relevant to early galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Targets: HSCJ1631+4426 and SDSSJ0811+4730.
    - Instrument: KCWI IFU spectroscopy.
    - Wavelength coverage: `3545-5529 A`.
  - **Method:**
    - Derive integrated oxygen and iron abundances.
    - Compare Fe/O with chemical-evolution model expectations.
    - Evaluate rare energetic supernova channels.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Oxygen abundances are near `~2%` solar.
    - Fe/O ratios approach or exceed solar.
    - Core-collapse and delayed Type Ia enrichment alone are insufficient.
    - Bright hypernovae and/or pair-instability supernovae are plausible.
  - **Position:** useful local laboratory for interpreting metal-poor systems and first-enrichment signatures.

## CGM, Gas Supply, Environment, and the Cosmic Web

- [2607.14090](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14090) Zhiyuan Yao et al. `Cold Stream Penetration of Virial Shocks`
  - **Question:** when do cold streams survive virial-shock penetration and continue feeding galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: idealized three-dimensional streams entering hot CGM.
    - Parameters: stream radius, Mach number, pressure contrast, and density contrast.
    - Regimes: fragmentation, coagulation, disruption, and survival.
  - **Method:**
    - Vary stream and CGM parameters.
    - Track cold mass, interface area, and streamwise momentum.
    - Compare post-shock cooling time with virial crossing time.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Modest pressure-contrast survival depends on shear versus radiative cooling.
    - Large pressure contrasts can create bow shocks.
    - Survival then depends on post-shock cooling time relative to crossing time.
    - High-sigma density peaks at `z>2` should favor stream survival.
  - **Position:** central CGM theory paper because it links cosmic-web gas supply to multiphase halo structure.

- [2607.14359](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14359) Glenn G. Kacprzak et al. `The chemodynamical signature of coherent metal-poor inflow and enriched recycled accretion in the cool circumgalactic medium`
  - **Question:** can CGM metallicity and kinematics separate inflow from recycled accretion?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `21` galaxies from the Multiphase Galaxy Halos Survey.
    - Measurements: cloud-level ionization, metallicity, density, temperature, H I, and line widths.
    - Context: absorber azimuthal angle and galaxy rotation.
  - **Method:**
    - Model ionization cloud by cloud.
    - Compare major-axis co-rotating clouds with larger-azimuth clouds.
    - Separate low- and higher-ionization phases.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Major-axis co-rotating low-ionization clouds are about `0.5 dex` more metal poor.
    - They also have higher H I column density, higher density, and less non-thermal broadening.
    - Higher-ionization gas lacks the same azimuthal metallicity dependence.
    - The pattern supports cold metal-poor planar inflow plus enriched recycled gas at larger azimuth.
  - **Position:** read first for CGM interpretation because geometry, angular momentum, and metallicity are all used together.

- [2607.12361](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12361) Runyu Zhu et al. `Environmental Imprints on the Assembly of the Cool Gas around Bright Cluster Galaxies`
  - **Question:** how does cluster environment reshape cool gas around brightest cluster galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Targets: BCGs at `z~0.55`.
    - Tracer: Mg II absorption in stacked DESI quasar spectra.
    - Radial scale: `40 kpc` to `15 Mpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Stack more than one million background quasar sightlines.
    - Compare BCG profiles with matched field galaxies.
    - Separate inner-CGM suppression from larger-scale excess.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Mg II is suppressed inside `200 kpc` around BCGs.
    - Mg II is enhanced from `200 kpc` to `10 Mpc`.
    - Cluster cores strip or deplete cool gas while outskirts accumulate it.
  - **Position:** key environment paper because absorption stacks directly trace cool gas across cluster scales.

- [2607.14785](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14785) Ankit Singh et al. `Enabling Cosmic Web Analysis at Gigaparsec Scales`
  - **Question:** how can cosmic-web topology be reconstructed in gigaparsec simulations?
  - **Data:**
    - Validation: `300 h^-1 Mpc` MDPL2 subvolume.
    - Application: `(1 h^-1 Gpc)^3` MDPL2 box with `92 million` halos.
    - Output: connectivity for `22,900` halos.
  - **Method:**
    - Split the volume into overlapping blocks.
    - Use a circumsphere criterion to retain globally valid tetrahedra.
    - Stitch, deduplicate, and core-filter the tiled filament network.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The method recovers `99.6%` of total filament length.
    - Density extrema recovery is `100%`.
    - Individual filament matching reaches `94.7%`.
    - The full catalogue confirms the predicted halo mass-connectivity relation.
  - **Position:** important infrastructure because profile-relevant protocluster and filament studies need reproducible cosmic-web catalogues at survey scale.

## Black Holes, AGN, and Modeling Infrastructure

- [2607.09853](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09853) Emma Jane Weller et al. `The Black Hole Occupation Fraction as a Fossil Record of Seeding, Dynamics, and Galaxy Assembly`
  - **Question:** what information is hidden inside black-hole occupation when central and wandering black holes are separated?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: ASTRID.
    - Redshift range: `z=5` to `z=0`.
    - Stellar-mass range: `10^7-10^12 M_sun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure total, central, and wandering black-hole occupation fractions.
    - Separate active selections from intrinsic occupation.
    - Compare trends by primary/satellite status and star-forming state.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Total occupation stays near unity across much of the mass range.
    - Central occupation declines at low mass toward low redshift.
    - Wandering occupation rises as mergers displace black holes.
    - Active-BH selections undercount true occupation.
  - **Position:** useful seed-demographics paper because observed AGN fractions trace duty cycle as much as intrinsic occupation.

- [2607.13151](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13151) Francisco Maion et al. `Evaluating the flexibility of the MillenniumTNG galaxy formation model with multi-zoom re-simulations`
  - **Question:** can feedback parameters fit both galaxy stellar-mass functions and halo gas fractions?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: MillenniumTNG multi-zoom re-simulations.
    - Observables: GSMF and gas fractions in large dark-matter halos.
    - Models: Gaussian-process emulators.
  - **Method:**
    - Vary star-formation and AGN-feedback parameters.
    - Train emulators on simulation outputs.
    - Fit recent GSMF and halo gas-fraction measurements simultaneously.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - GSMF predictions reach `~0.1 dex` precision.
    - Halo gas-fraction predictions reach `~10%` precision.
    - A parameter combination can qualitatively fit both observables.
    - The shift favors less energetic stellar feedback and rarer, more energetic kinetic AGN feedback.
  - **Position:** useful model-calibration paper because gas fractions and galaxy statistics constrain feedback together.

- [2607.14901](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14901) Andrea Gebek et al. `The COLIBRE-SKIRT pipeline`
  - **Question:** can cosmological simulations with live dust generate synthetic observables without calibration-heavy dust postprocessing?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation suite: COLIBRE.
    - Radiative transfer: SKIRT.
    - Observables: FUV-to-FIR spectra and local CSED comparison.
  - **Method:**
    - Use simulated dust masses and species fractions directly.
    - Add no birth-cloud attenuation model.
    - Map simulated dust distributions into continuous grain-size distributions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The pipeline reproduces the local CSED without a priori postprocessing calibration.
    - Residual mid-infrared tension is `~0.2 dex`.
    - The likely issues are hot-dust heating and PAH emission carriers.
    - The framework is ready for low- and high-redshift synthetic spectra and images.
  - **Position:** important survey infrastructure because dust assumptions shape comparisons between simulations and JWST/ALMA observables.
