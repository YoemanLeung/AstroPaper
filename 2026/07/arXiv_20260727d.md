## Executive View

The read-first paper is [2607.21719](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21719) Lucas Tortora, which uses `z~6` protocluster zoom simulations to connect super-Eddington quasar growth, Compton-thick obscuration, cold neutral CGM clumps, escape channels, and extended H-alpha nebulae. It sits directly on the high-redshift quasar feedback and JWST-CGM axis.

[2607.21749](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21749) Shanquan Gui is the strongest observational environment entry. It uses DESI DR1 quasars and PAC measurements at `0.8<z<1.0` to constrain quasar host halos and the central-versus-satellite occupation probability. [2607.21869](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21869) Rahma Alfarsy is also worth saving because DESI stacked reverberation mapping could open broad-line-region constraints for quasars out to `z=5.2`.

The gas and environment set is broad. [2607.21717](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21717) Qingzheng Yu reports an ultra-deep FAST detection of a `~1 Mpc` H I structure around HCG 100. [2607.21709](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21709) Thinh Huu Nguyen and [2607.21710](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21710) Thinh Huu Nguyen use the AGORA code-comparison suite to show how stellar-feedback prescriptions alter starbursts, compaction, and disc rebuilding during a `z~4.5` major merger.

Several lower-redshift papers are useful context rather than immediate reads. [2607.21818](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21818) Guangwen Chen links compact-galaxy abundance and quenching to overdensity. [2607.21696](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21696) F. La Barbera shows that a low-redshift compact massive galaxy can be intermediate-age rather than a simple ancient relic. [2607.21841](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21841) Cosima Eibensteiner releases resolved Local Group H I and multiwavelength radial profiles that can anchor nearby gas-supply comparisons.

## High-Redshift Quasars, AGN, and Feedback

- [2607.21719](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21719) Lucas Tortora et al. `Outflows in super-Eddington quasars drive clumpy circumgalactic medium and extended H-alpha nebulae at z >= 6`
  - **Question:** how does rapid quasar growth reshape the ISM and CGM of massive systems at `z~6`?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: zoom-in simulations of a massive protocluster at `z~6`.
    - Physics variants: the fiducial FABLE model plus modified runs with earlier black-hole seeding and mildly super-Eddington accretion.
    - Observable connection: JWST-accessible ISM and CGM phases around `z>6` quasars.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare fiducial and enhanced early-growth quasar histories.
    - Track obscuration, ionizing escape channels, and metal-enriched outflows.
    - Follow the formation of cold neutral CGM clumps after feedback blow-out.
    - Use radiative-transfer calculations to predict extended H-alpha nebulae.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The central quasar remains Compton-thick for much of its early evolution.
    - Strong feedback opens escape channels and leaves the central engine unobscured.
    - Metal-enriched outflows generate cold, fast, neutral CGM clumps.
    - Neutral-hydrogen covering fractions increase after the blow-out episode.
    - H-alpha nebulae become larger and brighter when quasar feedback is stronger and obscuration is weaker.
  - **Position:** read first because it links JWST high-redshift quasar observables to a concrete CGM feedback pathway.

- [2607.21749](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21749) Shanquan Gui et al. `Where Do Quasars Live? DESI DR1 Constraints from PAC Measurements`
  - **Question:** are quasars at fixed halo accretion mass equally likely to live in central halos and satellite subhalos?
  - **Data:**
    - Spectroscopy: DESI Data Release 1 quasars.
    - Imaging: DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys DR9 photometric galaxies.
    - Redshift interval: `0.8<z<1.0`.
    - Scale range: projected separations `0.1<r_p/(h^-1 Mpc)<15`.
  - **Method:**
    - Apply the Photometric Objects Around Cosmic Webs method.
    - Measure excess projected galaxy surface density around quasars.
    - Jointly model quasar environments, quasar autocorrelation, LRG autocorrelation, and quasar-LRG cross-correlation.
    - Interpret the measurements with an N-body simulation, a stellar-to-halo mass relation, an incompleteness model, and Gaussian quasar occupation.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The quasar occupation peak is `log10(M_acc/h^-1 M_sun)=12.88`.
    - The Gaussian occupation width is `sigma_q=0.51`.
    - The relative satellite-hosting parameter is `B=1.01`.
    - Quasars are consistent with equal central and satellite hosting probability at fixed halo accretion mass in the adopted model.
  - **Position:** important environment paper because it measures quasar-halo connection with small-scale DESI-era clustering information.

- [2607.21869](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21869) Rahma Alfarsy et al. `Stacked Reverberation Mapping of High Redshift Quasars in DESI. I. Feasibility Analysis`
  - **Question:** can DESI cadence and quasar samples support stacked reverberation mapping at high redshift?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: DESI quasar spectroscopy.
    - Mock redshift range: `1.48<z<5.2`.
    - Mock luminosity range: `44.68<=log L_1350 lambda/(erg s^-1)<=47.79`.
    - Observable: continuum and broad-emission-line variability.
  - **Method:**
    - Build mock quasar light curves with DESI-like sampling.
    - Stack ensembles to recover average continuum-line lags.
    - Test whether stacked mapping can reduce small-sample biases.
    - Evaluate feasibility across redshift and luminosity bins.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Stacked reverberation mapping is positioned as a practical alternative to object-by-object campaigns.
    - DESI's quasar sample enables ensemble lag recovery experiments at high redshift.
    - The method could extend broad-line-region size and black-hole-mass calibration beyond the regime reached by traditional monitoring.
  - **Position:** useful AGN survey-method paper because it converts a large spectroscopic survey into time-domain black-hole growth infrastructure.

- [2607.21956](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21956) Yue Xu et al. `The Distinctive Evolution and Spectral Energy Distribution of Binary Massive Black Hole Accretion`
  - **Question:** what SED signatures should distinguish accreting binary massive black holes from single-accretor systems?
  - **Data:**
    - Model system: binary massive black holes with total mass `10^8 M_sun`.
    - Accretion state: Eddington ratio `0.1`.
    - Mass-ratio range: `10^-4<=q<=0.5`.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine recent accretion-theory updates with mass-transfer rates between the two black holes.
    - Model two mini-disks plus circumbinary emission.
    - Track how mass ratio controls luminosity and spectral structure.
    - Compare predicted SED features with expectations for bright massive-black-hole binaries.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Binary accretion can produce SEDs that differ from single-black-hole disks.
    - The mass ratio is a key control parameter for the relative contribution of the two mini-disks.
    - Bright binaries remain promising electromagnetic counterparts for massive-black-hole merger searches.
  - **Position:** relevant AGN-method entry because binary-black-hole SEDs affect how dual or inspiraling SMBHs are selected.

## Gas Reservoirs, Environment, and Galaxy Evolution

- [2607.21717](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21717) Qingzheng Yu et al. `Megaparsec-Scale Neutral Hydrogen Flows in the Neighborhood of Hickson Compact Group 100`
  - **Question:** can deep H I emission observations reveal group-scale gas exchange around compact galaxy groups?
  - **Data:**
    - Instrument: ultra-deep FAST neutral-hydrogen observations.
    - Target: Hickson Compact Group 100 and neighboring galaxies.
    - Sensitivity: integrated column densities down to `~8.6 x 10^17 cm^-2`.
  - **Method:**
    - Map diffuse H I emission on megaparsec scales.
    - Measure integrated neutral-gas mass and velocity structure.
    - Compare the coherent envelope with tidal-debris and cosmic-web-accretion interpretations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Diffuse H I extends over `~1 Mpc` around HCG 100.
    - The gas forms a coherent envelope connecting the compact group to neighboring galaxies.
    - A large-scale velocity gradient extends across `~0.6 Mpc`.
    - The diffuse component contains `~1.4 x 10^10 M_sun` of neutral gas.
    - Up to `~40-50%` of the H I may reside outside galaxies.
  - **Position:** high-value gas-reservoir entry because it shows that low-column neutral gas can persist around dense environments on cosmic-web scales.

- [2607.21709](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21709) Thinh Huu Nguyen et al. `The AGORA High-resolution Galaxy Simulations Comparison Project. IX - Part 1: Effects of a Major Galaxy Merger on Star Formation of a Milky Way-mass Galaxy Progenitor`
  - **Question:** how sensitive is merger-driven star formation to stellar-feedback implementation?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation suite: AGORA CosmoRun cosmological zoom-ins.
    - Event: a major merger of a Milky Way-mass galaxy progenitor at `z~4.5`.
    - Numerical comparison: `9` calibrated simulation codes with different stellar-feedback schemes.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare star-formation histories through first passage, apoapsis, and coalescence.
    - Separate kinetic, thermal, delayed-cooling, radiation-pressure, and superbubble feedback behavior.
    - Track gas-particle inflow in particle-based codes.
    - Relate gas flow to burst fraction and pre-merger gas fraction.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Kinetic feedback produces an earlier and stronger merger-induced starburst.
    - Thermal feedback without kinetic feedback allows prolonged SFR growth after coalescence.
    - Delayed cooling and radiation pressure produce highly fluctuating star-formation histories.
    - Burst fraction anti-correlates with pre-merger gas fraction independent of feedback model.
  - **Position:** useful high-redshift simulation audit because merger starbursts become feedback-prescription tests rather than simple merger outcomes.

- [2607.21710](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21710) Thinh Huu Nguyen et al. `The AGORA High-resolution Galaxy Simulations Comparison Project. IX - Part 2: Effects of a Major Galaxy Merger on the Stellar Morphology of a Milky Way-mass Galaxy Progenitor`
  - **Question:** how do feedback prescriptions alter merger remnant morphology and disc rebuilding at high redshift?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation suite: the same `z~4.5` AGORA major-merger comparison.
    - Numerical comparison: `9` hydrodynamic codes.
    - Benchmark: Santa Cruz semi-analytic model comparison.
  - **Method:**
    - Track stellar morphology through the merger.
    - Compare compaction, disc formation, and remnant orientation across feedback implementations.
    - Measure stellar mass, half-mass radius, dark-matter fraction, and velocity dispersion differences.
    - Compare remnant properties with semi-analytic predictions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Thermal-feedback codes form compact disc remnants mainly during and after coalescence.
    - Kinetic-feedback codes begin disc formation and compaction near first periapsis.
    - Strong delayed-cooling or superbubble feedback suppresses disc formation and leaves more extended remnants.
    - The remnant disc angular momentum aligns with the interaction orbital angular momentum across codes.
  - **Position:** useful companion to the star-formation paper because morphology carries the same feedback sensitivity into observable structure.

- [2607.21818](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21818) Guangwen Chen et al. `From relics to stripped systems: the environmental origin of compact galaxies`
  - **Question:** do compact galaxies follow one evolutionary pathway or environment-dependent channels?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: nearby galaxies at `z<0.05`.
    - Stellar-mass range: `8.2<log(M_star/M_sun)<10.5`.
    - Classification: compact galaxies compared with control populations as a function of local overdensity.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure compact-galaxy abundance across overdensity.
    - Define transition and critical overdensity regimes.
    - Compare red-galaxy fractions and mass dependence between compact and control samples.
    - Interpret low-mass and high-mass compact systems separately.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Compact-galaxy environmental dependence is non-monotonic.
    - Compact galaxies are relatively abundant below the transition overdensity.
    - Their relative abundance drops between the transition and critical overdensities.
    - Compact galaxies become overabundant again above the critical overdensity.
    - Low-mass compact galaxies show the strongest deficit at intermediate overdensity.
  - **Position:** useful low-redshift analog because it separates compact-galaxy formation into mass- and environment-dependent channels.

- [2607.21696](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21696) F. La Barbera et al. `Different paths, same appearance: an intermediate-age red nugget at z~0.13`
  - **Question:** can a nearby compact massive galaxy resemble high-redshift red nuggets without being an old relic?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: G79071, an ultra-compact early-type galaxy at `z~0.13`.
    - Environment: low-mass group.
    - Spectroscopy: deep VLT/X-Shooter long-slit data.
    - Radial reach: stellar kinematics out to `~4 R_e`.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit stellar kinematics and stellar-population gradients.
    - Combine full spectral fitting, full-index fitting, and index fitting.
    - Infer the low-mass end of the stellar IMF.
    - Fit Jeans anisotropic models with an NFW halo.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - G79071 is massive and compact, with `M_star~10^11 M_sun` and `R_e<2 kpc`.
    - The stellar population is intermediate-age, about `3-4 Gyr`.
    - There is no evidence for a dominant old component older than `5 Gyr`.
    - The IMF remains bottom-heavy out to `~2 R_e`.
    - The dark-matter fraction within one effective radius is `f_DM(<R_e)=0.18+/-0.07`.
  - **Position:** useful cautionary relic paper because compact morphology alone does not uniquely imply ancient high-redshift survival.

- [2607.21841](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21841) Cosima Eibensteiner et al. `A Multiwavelength Inventory for the Local Group L-band Survey I: Atlas and Radial Profiles of Local Group Galaxies`
  - **Question:** how do atomic gas, stars, and recent star formation compare across nearby Local Group disks?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: Local Group L-Band Survey first data release.
    - Instrument: VLA H I data at `120 pc` physical resolution.
    - Ancillary maps: infrared, ultraviolet, H-alpha, and CO.
    - Targets: M31, M33, IC 10, IC 1613, NGC 6822, and WLM.
  - **Method:**
    - Build matched multiwavelength maps.
    - Measure radial profiles of gas, stars, and recent star formation.
    - Compare H I extents across dwarfs and spirals.
    - Package atlas products for resolved nearby-galaxy work.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Atomic-gas disks are the most extended component in the sample.
    - Dwarf H I radii span `2.8-5.8 kpc`.
    - M33 and M31 H I radii span `13-26 kpc`.
    - The atlas provides a resolved benchmark for linking gas supply to star formation.
  - **Position:** useful local anchor because resolved neutral-gas profiles calibrate baryon-cycling intuition used at higher redshift.

## Survey, Inference, and Instrument Methods

- [2607.21702](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21702) Maximilian Dax et al. `An Introduction to Bayesian and Frequentist Simulation-Based Inference with Machine Learning`
  - **Question:** how should machine-learning simulation-based inference be validated across Bayesian and frequentist use cases?
  - **Data:**
    - Scope: inverse problems in science and engineering.
    - Methods: neural posterior estimation, neural likelihood estimation, Empirical Bayes, and unfolding.
    - Astronomy relevance: parameter inference in cosmology and galaxy modeling.
  - **Method:**
    - Review Bayesian and frequentist statistical frameworks.
    - Map machine-learning SBI methods onto parameter estimation.
    - Discuss validation strategies and method limitations.
    - Compare use cases where posterior, likelihood, or unfolding targets differ.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - SBI is now a general tool for likelihood-free or simulator-heavy inference.
    - Validation must be matched to the statistical target, not only to prediction accuracy.
    - The same neural methods can support distinct scientific inference tasks when their assumptions are explicit.
  - **Position:** useful methods reference because profile-relevant survey and simulation work increasingly depends on simulator-calibrated inference.

- [2607.22411](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22411) Andrea Bianco et al. `HRMOS: a very high-resolution, multi-object spectrograph for the ESO VLT`
  - **Question:** what niche would a very high-resolution multi-object spectrograph fill on the VLT?
  - **Data:**
    - Instrument concept: HRMOS for the ESO VLT Beyond 2030 call.
    - Resolving power: `R=80,000`.
    - Multiplex: `50-60` simultaneous targets.
    - Radial-velocity precision: `10 m/s`.
    - Spectral windows: `385-421 nm`, `480-522 nm`, and a third redder window.
  - **Method:**
    - Define the instrument capability relative to the existing VLT landscape.
    - Tie spectral resolution and multiplex to stellar, Galactic, and extragalactic science cases.
    - Specify simultaneous-object and wavelength-coverage requirements.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - HRMOS would occupy a currently missing combination of very high resolution and multiplex.
    - The concept targets precision abundance, dynamics, and survey follow-up problems.
    - The design would support future high-resolution samples rather than single-object spectroscopy only.
  - **Position:** relevant infrastructure note because high-resolution multi-object spectroscopy can support chemical and dynamical environment work.

- [2607.22499](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22499) Pierre Thibodeaux et al. `Payne4GAIN: NLTE Corrections for Red Giants in Milky Way Mapper using H-Band Neural Network Emulators`
  - **Question:** how large are NLTE abundance corrections for luminous red giants in SDSS-V Milky Way Mapper?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: SDSS-V Milky Way Mapper DR19.
    - Spectra: APOGEE H-band spectra.
    - Sample size: `360,000` red giant stars.
    - Elements: Na, Mg, Si, Al, Ca, Ti, Mn, and Ni.
  - **Method:**
    - Generate NLTE spectra from departure-coefficient grids.
    - Train neural-network emulators for LTE and NLTE H-band spectra.
    - Fit spectra within the training-data parameter range.
    - Provide LTE, NLTE, and polynomial-corrected ASPCAP abundances.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - NLTE effects are of order `0.1 dex` for Al, Mn, and Ti.
    - Effects are smaller for Si and Ni.
    - The resulting catalog makes abundance corrections available at Milky Way Mapper scale.
  - **Position:** lower-priority but useful because precise stellar abundances feed Galactic archaeology and chemical-evolution baselines.
