## Executive View

The strongest high-redshift entry is [2607.09647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09647) Yuzo Ishikawa, which uses JWST/NIRSpec IFU spectroscopy to decompose five broad-Halpha Little Red Dots at `z~5` into compact red broad-line cores plus blue narrow-line host galaxies. It directly supports the central-engine plus host-galaxy interpretation of LRD spectra. [2607.08820](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08820) Devontae C. Baxter tests JWST/NIRCam massive-galaxy color selections in TNG300 and warns that current selections may miss many simulated massive `z~5` galaxies while not cleanly tracing the most massive `z=0` descendants.

Early-galaxy modeling centers on feedback and dust. [2607.08846](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08846) Cheonsu Kang shows that a sink-based star-formation model in a `10^10 M_sun` halo at `z=6` produces stronger burstiness, lower final stellar mass by `~3x`, and a Lyman-continuum escape fraction higher by `~10x` than a Schmidt-type model. [2607.08824](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08824) Enrico Garaldi finds in Thesan-Zoom that bursty star formation makes long-lived large dust reservoirs difficult, even while matching several observed dust trends.

The rest of the listing is useful for lower-redshift calibration and infrastructure. [2607.08812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08812) Rahul Ramesh compares supernova, SMBH, and primordial magnetic seeding in AREPO simulations, with SMBH-driven injection helping satisfy present-day IGM constraints. [2607.09209](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09209) Aidan P. Cotter updates non-parametric merger criteria and finds precisions of `69.5%` and `72.3%` in mock HSC images. [2607.09036](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09036) D. Grupe reports a third giant X-ray outburst of IC 3599, strengthening the case for radiation-pressure instability in an extreme changing-look AGN.

## High-Redshift Galaxies, Dust, and Bursty Formation

- [2607.09647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09647) Yuzo Ishikawa et al. `Spatial decomposition of Little Red Dots with JWST/NIRSpec IFU into broad-line red cores and narrow-line blue host galaxies`
  - **Question:** do Little Red Dot spectra come from one compact component or from a central engine embedded in a host galaxy?
  - **Data:**
    - JWST/NIRSpec: IFU spectroscopy of five broad-Halpha-selected LRDs at `z~5`.
    - Spectral coverage: prism plus high-resolution G395H grating data.
    - Diagnostics: continuum shape, broad and narrow emission lines, Balmer absorption, and [OIII] equivalent-width maps.
  - **Method:**
    - Spectrally decompose continuum, narrow lines, broad lines, and absorption.
    - Map each component spatially across the IFU field.
    - Compare red-core, blue-host, and emission-line morphologies.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The blue continuum is co-spatial with the narrow emission-line region.
    - The red continuum is compact and co-spatial with broad Balmer emission and absorption.
    - Central [OIII] equivalent width drops sharply.
    - The data support two physical components: a red central engine embedded in a blue host galaxy.
  - **Position:** read first because it turns the LRD debate into a spatial-decomposition problem rather than only an SED-fitting problem.

- [2607.08820](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08820) Devontae C. Baxter et al. `What becomes of JWST/NIRCam-selected high-redshift massive galaxies?`
  - **Question:** do current JWST/NIRCam color selections reliably identify massive high-redshift galaxies and their descendants?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: TNG300 with synthetic dust-attenuated photometry.
    - Selection tests: five observational criteria for high-redshift massive galaxy candidates.
    - Descendant tracking: selected massive galaxies at `z=7`, `z=4`, and `z=2`.
  - **Method:**
    - Apply observational color selections to simulated galaxies.
    - Evaluate completeness and contamination against true stellar masses.
    - Track simulated descendants to `z=0`.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The Perez-Gonzalez et al. selection is the most reliable and inclusive among the five tested criteria.
    - Only `1/18` simulated `z~5` galaxies with `M_star>=10^11 M_sun` satisfies that selection.
    - The missed simulated massive galaxies are on average `~0.5 mag` bluer than the adopted color cut.
    - NIRCam-selected high-z massive galaxies rarely become the most massive `z=0` galaxies unless they undergo major late-time merger growth.
  - **Position:** important selection-function paper because the massive-high-z tension depends on which galaxy populations NIRCam color cuts actually recover.

- [2607.08846](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08846) Cheonsu Kang et al. `mitigating the overcooling problem with sink-based bursty star formation in a high-z dwarf galaxy`
  - **Question:** can sink-based bursty star formation regulate early dwarf galaxies more realistically than a Schmidt-type prescription?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: cosmological zoom-in radiation-hydrodynamics of a halo reaching `10^10 M_sun` at `z=6`.
    - Models: Schmidt-type star formation and sink-based star formation.
    - Diagnostics: stellar mass, LyC escape fraction, metallicity, size, and outflows.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare star formation tied to local gravo-thermo-turbulent conditions with star formation governed by local gas inflows.
    - Track radiation feedback before the first supernovae.
    - Measure outflow strength and galaxy properties at `z=6`.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The sink model produces burstier star formation through rapid accretion onto young sink particles.
    - Photoionization disperses dense clumps before supernovae explode, allowing stronger supernova momentum coupling.
    - The final stellar mass is lower by `~3x` than in the Schmidt-type run.
    - The LyC escape fraction is higher by `~10x` by `z=6`.
  - **Position:** high-priority simulation paper because burstiness and escape fraction feed directly into reionization-era galaxy interpretation.

- [2607.08824](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08824) Enrico Garaldi et al. `The Thesan-Zoom project: bursty star formation is incompatible with prolonged dust survival`
  - **Question:** can bursty high-redshift galaxies retain large dust reservoirs for long periods?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: Thesan-Zoom radiation-hydrodynamic zoom-ins.
    - Physics: dust formation, growth, destruction, and radiative-transfer coupling.
    - Redshift range: galaxies at `z>=3`.
  - **Method:**
    - Model dust self-consistently in a multi-phase ISM with bursty star-formation histories.
    - Compare dust-to-gas ratios, dust-to-metal ratios, dust temperatures, and UV-IR offsets with observations.
    - Track the duration of dust-rich and dust-poor phases.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Simulated galaxies reproduce several observed dust trends but show a dust deficit at high specific SFR.
    - IR-bright phases are short, with median duration `20.3(+2.3/-2.4) Myr`.
    - Predicted attenuation at `1500 A` remains low compared to observations.
    - Long-lived large dust reservoirs with `M_dust/M_star>=10^-3` are difficult to maintain during bursty evolution.
  - **Position:** important companion to JWST bright-galaxy debates because dust survival and UV attenuation set the observable high-redshift census.

## CGM/IGM Magnetization, Dwarfs, and Local Structure

- [2607.08812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08812) Rahul Ramesh et al. `Magnetogenesis by galactic processes: impact on circumgalactic and intergalactic fields`
  - **Question:** can magnetic fields injected by supernovae or SMBH feedback explain CGM and IGM magnetization?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: large-volume `25 Mpc/h` AREPO cosmological MHD runs.
    - Galaxy model: IllustrisTNG plus additional magnetogenesis prescriptions.
    - Seeding channels: supernova injection, SMBH feedback injection, and uniform primordial seeds.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare halo and IGM magnetic fields across seeding models.
    - Measure magnetic-field topology and coherence length.
    - Test consistency with gamma-ray cascade constraints at `z=0` and `z~3`.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Halo magnetic field strengths at `z=0` are broadly similar across seeding models after dynamo amplification.
    - SMBH-driven models have systematically smaller coherence lengths.
    - Feedback injection accelerates dynamo growth and improves resolution convergence in low-mass halos.
    - Supernova-only injection underproduces IGM fields relative to cascade lower limits, while the tested SMBH prescription satisfies present-day constraints but remains mildly tense at high redshift.
  - **Position:** useful CGM/IGM theory paper because it connects feedback physics to magnetic-field observables beyond galaxy disks.

- [2607.08818](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08818) Jorge Sarrato-Alos et al. `Dark matter density profiles of the Milky Way satellite population: reconciling simulations and observations`
  - **Question:** does the dwarf-galaxy cusp-core tension persist when simulations and observations are compared at matched radii?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: NIHAO and FIRE-2 hydrodynamical suites over `M_star~10^3-10^11 M_sun`.
    - Observations: dynamical inferences for `16` Milky Way satellites from four methods.
    - Diagnostic: inner dark-matter slope evaluated within observational reliability limits.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare simulated and observed profiles at matched stellar mass and accessible radii.
    - Avoid evaluating slopes at inconsistent physical scales.
    - Quantify scatter among observational models and simulations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Both simulations and observations show mass-dependent core formation.
    - Inner slopes move from cuspy `~ -1.5` near `M_star~10^5 M_sun` to core-like `0` to `-0.4` near `M_star~10^8 M_sun`.
    - Simulated profiles agree with most observed systems within `<=1 sigma`.
    - Willman 1 remains an unresolved outlier.
  - **Position:** relevant low-redshift anchor because dwarf feedback and dark-matter inference calibrate small-scale galaxy formation models.

- [2607.09308](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09308) Matthew G. Walker et al. `Structural Diversity Among the Milky Way's Dwarf Spheroidal Satellites`
  - **Question:** how much structure is hidden by standard Plummer or Sersic descriptions of Milky Way dwarf spheroidals?
  - **Data:**
    - Targets: known Milky Way dwarf spheroidal satellites.
    - Models: flexible double-power-law stellar-density profiles.
    - Quantities: inner and outer stellar-density slopes, half-light radii, and stellar masses.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit alpha-beta-gamma profiles to observed stellar density fields.
    - Compare model selection against Plummer, Sersic, and exponential alternatives.
    - Propagate profile flexibility into structural and dynamical uncertainties.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Flexible alpha-beta-gamma profiles are favored where model selection is decisive.
    - Massive dwarf spheroidals can have steep outer profiles with `beta>=8`, while systems such as Sextans and Bootes I fade more slowly with `beta<=4`.
    - Dwarfs with `M_star>=10^5 M_sun` are consistent with inner stellar-density cores.
    - Half-light radii can become larger by up to an order of magnitude when profile flexibility is allowed.
  - **Position:** useful satellite-structure paper because stellar-profile systematics can dominate dwarf mass modeling.

- [2607.08831](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08831) Priyanka Verma et al. `Testing the validity of backward orbit reconstruction for outer Local Group dwarf galaxies in IllustrisTNG-100`
  - **Question:** can analytic backward orbit reconstruction recover the histories of outer Local Group dwarf galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: IllustrisTNG-100 Local Group analogs, both dark-matter-only and hydrodynamical.
    - Systems: dwarf analogs near the Local Group turnaround radius, roughly `1 Mpc`, outside the Milky Way and M31 virial radii.
    - Comparison: simulated tracks and analytic Local Group potential reconstructions.
  - **Method:**
    - Track dwarf orbits in simulated Local Group analogs.
    - Reconstruct orbits in analytic Milky Way plus M31 potentials.
    - Compare accuracy as a function of lookback time.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Reconstructed and simulated orbits agree within `<=10%` over the last `6 Gyr`.
    - Scatter rises to `~40%` by `6 Gyr` lookback.
    - Outer satellite orbits are not strongly affected by excess outer Local Group mass or the surrounding tidal field in these analogs.
    - Residuals likely come from mass accretion, non-spherical potentials, and dynamical-friction prescriptions.
  - **Position:** useful Local Group dynamics systematics paper, peripheral to high-redshift work but relevant to dwarf-environment interpretation.

## AGN, Binary Black Holes, and Morphology Infrastructure

- [2607.09036](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09036) D. Grupe et al. `In the Eye of the Storm: The Third Giant X-ray Outburst of the Extreme Changing-look AGN IC 3599`
  - **Question:** what powers repeated giant X-ray outbursts in the changing-look AGN IC 3599?
  - **Data:**
    - Monitoring: long-term Swift observations.
    - Follow-up: rapid multiwavelength observations after the third outburst.
    - Diagnostics: Swift spectra, XMM-Newton short-term variability, and optical high-state spectra.
  - **Method:**
    - Detect and follow the third giant outburst after earlier events in `1990` and `2010`.
    - Compare X-ray spectral state, optical coronal-line response, and short-term variability.
    - Test repeat-tidal-stripping, disk-impact, and disk-instability interpretations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The new outburst is a factor `>100` X-ray event.
    - Swift spectra are supersoft, with almost no photons above `2.5 keV`.
    - XMM-Newton shows candidate QPO-like variability on hour timescales.
    - The observations favor accretion-disk radiation-pressure instability over strictly periodic scenarios.
  - **Position:** high-value AGN time-domain paper because recurrent changing-look behavior probes accretion physics near the Eddington limit.

- [2607.08832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08832) Debojyoti Garain et al. `Massive Perturbers and Transient Pickup Discs in Disc-Crossing Encounters in OJ 287-like Supermassive Black Hole Binaries`
  - **Question:** what accretion signatures should disc-crossing supermassive black-hole binaries produce?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation target: OJ 287-like inclined, eccentric secondary black-hole passages through an accretion disk.
    - Method: three-dimensional smoothed-particle hydrodynamics.
    - Variables: mass ratio, eccentricity, disk thickness, and viscosity.
  - **Method:**
    - Simulate secondary black-hole impacts through the primary accretion disk.
    - Track accretion onto both black holes after disk crossing.
    - Identify conditions for primary spikes and secondary pickup disks.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Large primary accretion spikes require a massive perturber with `q>=0.1`.
    - Spikes are delayed by roughly a free-fall time, about months for OJ 287.
    - Higher eccentricity produces larger and less delayed gas delivery.
    - A massive secondary can carry a pickup disk of `10^2-10^3 M_sun`, potentially powering near-Eddington UV/EUV emission.
  - **Position:** useful binary-SMBH theory paper because it links time-domain flare morphology to hydrodynamic disk response.

- [2607.09209](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09209) Aidan P. Cotter et al. `Performance of morphological classifiers for galaxy mergers compared to current machine learning methods`
  - **Question:** can non-parametric morphology still compete with machine-learning merger classifiers?
  - **Data:**
    - Images: mock HSC-SSP images from IllustrisTNG and Horizon-AGN plus HSC-SSP observations.
    - Statistics: Gini coefficient, M20, and concentration.
    - Comparison: state-of-the-art machine-learning merger classifiers.
  - **Method:**
    - Use MCMC to find merger/non-merger boundaries in two-dimensional morphology spaces.
    - Test criteria on unseen IllustrisTNG mock HSC images.
    - Measure redshift robustness relative to CNN-based methods.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Updated criteria classify mergers with precisions of `69.5%` and `72.3%`.
    - Performance is consistent with current ML methods.
    - The classifiers mainly select pre-mergers; post-mergers are hard to distinguish from non-mergers with these statistics.
    - Morphological classifiers remain robust to `z~0.52` and outperform CNN robustness at `0.52<z<1`.
  - **Position:** relevant infrastructure because transparent morphology cuts remain useful when merger labels feed environmental or evolutionary analyses.

- [2607.09460](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09460) Erik Zackrisson et al. `Project Hephaistos -- IV. James Webb Space Telescope Observations of Two Dyson Sphere Candidates`
  - **Question:** what are the mid-infrared excess sources previously selected as Dyson-sphere candidates?
  - **Data:**
    - Follow-up: JWST/MIRI imaging and spectroscopy.
    - Targets: Project Hephaistos candidates D and E, selected from WISE mid-infrared excess around M dwarfs.
    - Diagnostics: projected separations, redshifts, and infrared spectral shapes.
  - **Method:**
    - Resolve the M-dwarf candidates with JWST/MIRI.
    - Identify whether the mid-infrared excess is associated with the stars or projected background sources.
    - Classify the contaminants from spectra and morphology.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The mid-infrared excesses do not arise from megastructures or other stellar-neighborhood emission.
    - Candidate D is confused by a background galaxy at `z~0.9` with a Hot DOG-like mid-infrared spectrum.
    - Candidate E is confused by an extended dusty starburst at `z~0.4`.
    - WISE photometry can blend rare-object candidates with extragalactic interlopers within `~1 arcsec`.
  - **Position:** useful cautionary paper because galaxy contaminants can dominate rare-object and technosignature color selections.

## Stellar Systems and Other Peripheral Entries

- [2607.08834](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08834) M. Cadelano et al. `Binary Stars as Dynamical Tracers in Globular Clusters .I. First Observations of Bimodal Spatial Distributions`
  - **Question:** do globular-cluster binary fractions decline monotonically with radius?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: six Galactic globular clusters spanning a range of dynamical ages.
    - Imaging: deep optical HST observations plus wide-field ground-based data.
    - Tracer: radial binary fraction and blue-straggler dynamical-age proxy.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure binary fractions across the full cluster extent.
    - Compare radial minima with relaxation time and the blue-straggler `A+` parameter.
    - Link observations to the companion simulation paper.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Binary fractions show a central peak, an intermediate-radius minimum, and an outer rise.
    - The minimum sits near `1-2` half-light radii.
    - The minimum location correlates with cluster relaxation time and `A+`.
    - Bimodality likely traces long-term mass segregation and binary disruption.
  - **Position:** peripheral for this profile, but useful as a stellar-dynamics tracer paper.

- [2607.08836](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08836) J. Bruce et al. `Binary stars as dynamical tracers in globular clusters .II. Evolution of the radial distribution of the binary star fraction`
  - **Question:** what dynamical process preserves bimodal binary-fraction profiles in globular clusters?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: Monte Carlo cluster-evolution models.
    - Cluster types: single-population and multiple-population globular clusters.
    - Observable: binary-fraction radial profile and migration of the minimum.
  - **Method:**
    - Follow binary segregation and disruption over cluster lifetimes.
    - Compare single-population and multiple-population cluster structures.
    - Track the binary-fraction minimum as a dynamical-age indicator.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Mass segregation and binary disruption jointly produce bimodal profiles.
    - Single-population clusters can make a weak transient version of the feature.
    - Multiple-population structure strengthens and preserves the bimodality.
    - The minimum radius tends to move outward with dynamical age.
  - **Position:** companion context for the observational globular-cluster binary paper.

- [2607.08917](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08917) Annapurni Subramaniam et al. `Star Clusters in the Ultraviolet`
  - **Question:** what has ultraviolet observing added to star-cluster astrophysics?
  - **Data:**
    - Scope: review of UV studies of Galactic, Magellanic Cloud, and nearby-galaxy clusters.
    - Facilities: historical space missions, wide-field surveys, HST, and UVIT-like data.
    - Populations: blue stragglers, hot subdwarfs, horizontal-branch stars, stripped stars, binaries, and compact remnants.
  - **Method:**
    - Synthesize five decades of UV observations.
    - Organize results by Galactic open clusters, globular clusters, Magellanic Clouds, and extragalactic clusters.
    - Connect UV-sensitive populations to cluster evolution.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - UV data expose hot and evolved stellar populations that optical/IR studies miss.
    - UV observations constrain mass transfer, multiple populations, helium enrichment, and horizontal-branch morphology.
    - Wide-field UV surveys extend cluster studies beyond HST pointings.
  - **Position:** background review, mostly outside the extragalactic high-redshift focus.

- [2607.09464](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09464) Long-Fei Ding et al. `Characterising the Kinematics and Evolution of Young Stellar Groups within 1 kpc of the Sun Using Gaia DR3`
  - **Question:** what kinematic structure do nearby young stellar groups inherit from their parent molecular clouds?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: Gaia DR3.
    - Sample: `145` young stellar groups containing `5713` stars within `1 kpc`.
    - Diagnostic: velocity dispersion-size relation across age bins up to `20 Myr`.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify groups with HDBSCAN clustering in Gaia phase space.
    - Measure group kinematics and spatial scales.
    - Test whether Larson's relation changes with age.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The derived relation is `sigma_v=(1.10+/-0.13) r^(0.38+/-0.03)`.
    - The slope is stable across ages up to `20 Myr`.
    - The inherited turbulent structure persists without strong disruption over that period.
  - **Position:** local star-formation context, not a primary profile target.

- [2607.09364](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09364) T. Vorsselmans et al. `Effect of Ice Charging on the Astrochemistry of Interstellar Sulfur Bearing Species on Amorphous Solid Water`
  - **Question:** how does negative ice charging affect sulfur-bearing species on amorphous solid water?
  - **Data:**
    - Molecules: S, H2S, SO2, and OCS.
    - Substrate: neutral and negatively charged amorphous solid water clusters.
    - Calculations: DFT protocols benchmarked against coupled-cluster reference energies.
  - **Method:**
    - Sample adsorption sites on five independent amorphous ice clusters.
    - Correct binding energies for basis-set superposition error and zero-point energy.
    - Compare neutral and charged ice surfaces.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Neutral amorphous ice gives broad site-dependent binding-energy distributions.
    - Negative charge always increases binding for S and SO2 through electron transfer.
    - H2S binding increases slightly without electron transfer.
    - OCS binding changes only when electron transfer occurs under specific conditions.
  - **Position:** astrochemistry entry included for completeness; low priority for the galaxy-evolution profile.
