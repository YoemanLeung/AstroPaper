## High-Redshift AGN and Reionization-Era Galaxies

- [2607.02666](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02666) Gene C. K. Leung et al. `The MIRI Early Obscured-AGN Wide Survey (MEOW): A Population of Hidden AGN at z >= 5 Revealed by JWST/MIRI Imaging`
  - **Question:** how many obscured AGN at `z>=5` are missed by rest-UV and broad-line selection?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: JWST/MIRI MEOW imaging in GOODS-N and GOODS-S.
    - Area and filters: `43` pointings over `95 arcmin^2` with F1000W and F2100W.
    - Ancillary data: HST, JWST/NIRCam, and SCUBA-2 photometry.
  - **Method:**
    - Select MIRI-bright high-redshift candidates.
    - Fit SEDs with mid-infrared constraints.
    - Compare MIRI-selected AGN with broad-line AGN and Little Red Dot populations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The sample contains `16` MIRI-selected AGN at `z=4.5-7.2`, including `12` spectroscopically confirmed objects.
    - Twelve of the `16` AGN are newly identified.
    - At least `5` narrow-line AGN represent an obscured population missed by broad-line searches.
    - The MIRI-selected AGN luminosity function at `z=4.5-6` has number densities comparable to broad-line AGN and LRDs.
  - **Position:** read first because a complete early SMBH census requires mid-infrared selection, not only rest-UV or broad-line discovery.

- [2607.03432](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03432) D. Yang et al. `Euclid: Discovery of 31 new quasars at 6.6 < z < 7.8`
  - **Question:** how rapidly can Euclid expand the known faint quasar population during reionization?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: first `1.5` years of the Euclid Wide Survey.
    - Area: about `3000 deg^2`.
    - Follow-up: Keck, Magellan, and Large Binocular Telescope spectroscopy.
  - **Method:**
    - Select candidates with machine-learning and probabilistic methods using Euclid `I_E`, `Y_E`, `J_E`, and `H_E` imaging.
    - Add ancillary `z`-band data where available.
    - Spectroscopically confirm the highest-priority candidates.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Euclid identifies `31` new quasars at `6.6<z<7.8`.
    - The sample includes `12` quasars at `z>=7`, more than doubling the previously known count in that range.
    - The new quasars span `21.2<J_E<23.2` and `-25.5<M_1450<-23.6`.
    - EUCL J172902.75+641018.1 at `z~7.77` is reported as the most distant quasar known.
  - **Position:** high-priority survey result because Euclid is moving reionization quasar work into the faint-luminosity and large-area regime.

- [2607.03430](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03430) S. Belladitta et al. `Euclid: A UV-faint quasar in a highly luminous star-forming host galaxy at z ~ 7.7`
  - **Question:** what kind of host galaxy contains a UV-faint Euclid quasar at `z~7.7`?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: EUCL J125308.55+705432.3.
    - Facility: IRAM/NOEMA observations.
    - Lines and continuum: [CII] `158 micron` and cold dust emission.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure [CII] systemic redshift and luminosity.
    - Infer far-infrared luminosity, dust mass, dynamical mass, and star-formation rate.
    - Compare the host with previously known UV-bright quasars at similar redshift.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The systemic redshift is `z=7.6980+/-0.0004`.
    - The host has `L_FIR=3.6 x 10^12 L_sun`, `L_[CII]=2 x 10^9 L_sun`, and dust mass `1.4 x 10^8 M_sun`.
    - The star-formation rate is `>250 M_sun/yr`.
    - Despite `M_1450=-24.06`, the source has the brightest [CII] emission among comparable `z~7.5` quasars.
  - **Position:** important companion to the Euclid discovery paper because submillimeter follow-up reveals host growth hidden by UV-faint quasar selection.

- [2607.02650](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02650) L. Y. Aaron Yung et al. `Investigating the star formation histories of galaxies from Cosmic Dawn to the Epoch of Reionization with the Santa Cruz SAM`
  - **Question:** how diverse are galaxy star-formation histories from `z~14` to `z~6` in semi-analytic models?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation input: GUREFT dark-matter-only halo merger trees.
    - Model: Santa Cruz semi-analytic model for galaxy formation.
    - Redshift range: cosmic dawn through the end of reionization, `14<z<6`.
  - **Method:**
    - Generate model galaxy SFHs across masses and redshifts.
    - Compare median and individual SFH shapes.
    - Track `t_50` and `t_90`, the lookback times for forming the youngest `50%` and `90%` of stellar mass.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Median SFHs rise rapidly over time at all masses.
    - Individual galaxies show bursts and mini-quenching episodes even at fixed terminal mass or redshift.
    - At `z>12`, typical `t_50<30 Myr` and `t_90<70 Myr`.
    - Young-star-dominated populations make synthetic photometry sensitive to the modeling of very recent star formation.
  - **Position:** useful for JWST interpretation because inferred stellar masses, ages, and colors at the highest redshifts depend on bursty recent SFHs.

- [2607.05087](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05087) Daniil Smirnov et al. `Lyman-alpha haloes in the aftermath of reionisation`
  - **Question:** how do Lyalpha haloes around low-luminosity LAEs change from `z~3` to `z>=6`?
  - **Data:**
    - Instrument: MUSE at the ESO VLT.
    - High-z sample: `18` LAEs at `z>=6` in the MUSE eXtremely Deep Field.
    - Comparison sample: LAEs at `z~3` matched in Lyalpha luminosity and host stellar mass.
  - **Method:**
    - Match intrinsic surface-brightness sensitivity after accounting for cosmological dimming.
    - Detect individual extended Lyalpha haloes.
    - Stack non-detections at high and lower redshift.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Extended Lyalpha emission is detected around `6/18` high-z LAEs.
    - Typical high-z exponential scale lengths are `3x` smaller than those at `z~3`.
    - Non-detected `z~3` LAEs show a significant stacked halo, while the `z>=6` stack shows no extended Lyalpha signal.
    - High-z Lyalpha line widths are about `2.5x` smaller than in the lower-redshift comparison sample.
  - **Position:** important CGM/IGM paper because it links reionization-era neutral gas and host evolution to observable Lyalpha halo suppression.

- [2607.03536](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03536) Benedetta Mestichelli et al. `Pebbles to Gems: Intermediate-mass black holes in the first star clusters`
  - **Question:** can Population III star clusters bridge light- and heavy-seed pathways for early SMBH formation?
  - **Data:**
    - Systems: Pop III star clusters with `M_cl~10^3-4 x 10^5 M_sun`.
    - Hosts: isolated dark-matter minihalos.
    - Method input: direct `N`-body simulations with multiple stellar-evolution and binary prescriptions.
  - **Method:**
    - Simulate dense Pop III cluster dynamics.
    - Vary binary orbital distributions, stellar radii, and cluster structure.
    - Estimate IMBH masses and number densities by `z~19`.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The IMBH mass function peaks near `200 M_sun` by `z~19`.
    - Dense clusters can form IMBHs with masses `>10^3 M_sun`.
    - The most massive modeled IMBH reaches `~6200 M_sun` through repeated stellar collisions.
    - Retention fractions are `>=88%`, making massive dense clusters efficient seed incubators.
  - **Position:** relevant to early quasar growth because it provides a cluster-dynamical seed channel between ordinary Pop III remnants and direct-collapse scenarios.

## Environment, Feedback, and Gas Diagnostics

- [2607.02654](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02654) David Valles-Perez et al. `The youth of the intracluster medium. I. A non-parametric characterisation of the gas and electron number density profiles of z ~= 2 protoclusters`
  - **Question:** what are the gas and electron-density structures of proto-ICM environments at `z=2`?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: Magneticum.
    - Sample: more than `3800` regions around protocluster cores.
    - Mass threshold: `M_500c>10^13 M_sun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Extract density profiles, temperature structures, ionization degrees, and electron number densities.
    - Split protoclusters by mass, dynamical state, and central activity.
    - Follow profiles from central halos into the surrounding protocluster environment.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Protoclusters show moderate departures from self-similar density profiles.
    - High-mass and strongly accreting systems show a prominent double-beta structure.
    - Hot ionized gas dominates mainly at intermediate radii, `r>~0.1-0.5 R_500c`.
    - The results provide calibration targets for proto-ICM observables sensitive to density and ionization.
  - **Position:** useful protocluster infrastructure because high-z environment studies need gas profiles, not only galaxy overdensity maps.

- [2607.02655](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02655) V. Rodriguez Morales et al. `The largest sample of AGN outflows in dwarf galaxies using DESI DR1`
  - **Question:** how common are AGN-driven ionized outflows in dwarf galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: DESI DR1.
    - Parent sample: about `7` million galaxies at `z<0.45`.
    - Diagnostic line: broad [OIII] `lambda5007` emission.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify broad [OIII] components as ionized outflow signatures.
    - Separate dwarf and massive galaxies.
    - Classify star-forming and AGN systems with emission-line diagnostic diagrams.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The catalog contains `1,502` AGN dwarf galaxies with outflow signatures.
    - Among these, AGN are the likely outflow drivers for `~83%` of objects with `W_80>250 km/s`.
    - Massive galaxies require a higher threshold, `W_80>350 km/s`, for AGN dominance.
    - Dwarf-galaxy outflows are more likely to escape their dark-matter halos than massive-galaxy outflows.
  - **Position:** relevant feedback reference because low-mass AGN can redistribute gas beyond their hosts and affect dwarf-galaxy regulation.

- [2607.02801](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02801) Yijun Wang et al. `Satellite quenching by radio jets of central galaxies in galaxy groups`
  - **Question:** can radio AGN in central group galaxies quench their satellites?
  - **Data:**
    - Group framework: Yang et al. 2007 galaxy groups.
    - Population: radio AGN centrals and comparison normal centrals.
    - Observable: satellite quiescent fraction.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare satellite quenching around radio AGN and normal central galaxies.
    - Control for multiple physical parameters that affect satellite star formation.
    - Test dependence on radio-lobe size.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Satellites around radio AGN have higher quiescent fractions than satellites around normal galaxies.
    - The enhancement is strongest around AGN with large radio lobes.
    - Kinetic AGN feedback can affect galaxies beyond the central host.
  - **Position:** useful environmental-quenching result because it gives a direct route from central radio activity to group-scale conformity.

- [2607.03913](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03913) C. E. Mentzer et al. `Widespread Detection of Aromatic and Aliphatic Emission in the Dual Quasar J0749+2255 at Cosmic Noon`
  - **Question:** can PAH-related aromatic and aliphatic emission survive around dual quasars at cosmic noon?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: SDSS J074922.96+225511.7, a dual quasar at `z~2.17`.
    - Instrument: JWST/MIRI integral-field spectroscopy.
    - Features: rest-frame `3.3 micron` aromatic and `3.4 micron` aliphatic emission.
  - **Method:**
    - Map the emission-band strengths across the dual-quasar system.
    - Estimate aliphatic fractions of PAHs from the `3.3` and `3.4 micron` intensities.
    - Compare the emission morphology with the two quasar nuclei and starburst regions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Both aromatic and aliphatic features are detected across the system.
    - The detection is reported as the most distant object with both features observed.
    - The highest aliphatic fractions lie near the two quasar nuclei.
    - Small PAHs and attached aliphatic sidegroups can survive intense UV radiation from extreme starburst and quasar activity.
  - **Position:** useful cosmic-noon merger paper because JWST/MIRI resolves dust chemistry in an active dual-quasar environment.

- [2607.05326](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05326) Chuan-Peng Zhang et al. `Weak Evolution of Cosmic Atomic Hydrogen over the Past 4.5 Billion Years`
  - **Question:** did the decline in late-time cosmic star formation come mainly from rapid depletion of atomic hydrogen?
  - **Data:**
    - Radio data: FAST HI spectra.
    - Optical data: DESI spectroscopy.
    - Sample: about `2.5` million galaxies over `~12,000 deg^2`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure cosmic HI density, `Omega_HI`, over `0<z<0.41`.
    - Apply forward-model systematic corrections.
    - Compare HI evolution with the cosmic star-formation-rate density and molecular gas evolution.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Raw `Omega_HI` decreases by a factor of `1.35+/-0.10` over the past `4.5 Gyr`.
    - After conservative corrections, the decline is only `1.12+/-0.10`.
    - The cosmic star-formation-rate density declines by a larger factor of `2.46`.
    - Rapid HI depletion is not the primary driver of the late-time star-formation decline.
  - **Position:** useful gas-regulation benchmark because models must decouple the slow evolution of atomic gas from the faster decline in star formation.

## Survey Infrastructure, Radio AGN, and Structure

- [2607.03271](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03271) Jacco Th. van Loon et al. `Advancing extragalactic spectral line studies with the Square Kilometre Array Observatory`
  - **Question:** what extragalactic spectral-line science beyond 21 cm becomes possible with SKAO?
  - **Data:**
    - Facility: SKAO and possible AA4/high-frequency extensions.
    - Tracers: masers, kilomasers, megamasers, radio recombination lines, molecular lines, and absorption systems.
    - Redshift reach: systematic molecular gas inventories to `z=5` and possible first-molecule searches.
  - **Method:**
    - Synthesize SKAO Science Book prospects.
    - Identify spectral-line cases enabled by sensitivity, bandwidth, and frequency coverage.
    - Evaluate gains from higher-frequency coverage up to `24 GHz`.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - SKAO can extend extragalactic line work beyond neutral hydrogen.
    - AA4-level capability would strongly improve maser, recombination-line, and molecular-gas surveys.
    - Higher-frequency coverage would enable water-maser and ammonia surveys in nearby galaxies and molecular inventories at high redshift.
  - **Position:** infrastructure paper for planning SKA follow-up of gas, star formation, and black-hole environments.

- [2607.04022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04022) Nelson Padilla et al. `Assembly bias from nuisance to probe I: the relation between galactic conformity and the linear matter clustering`
  - [2607.04022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04022) links two-halo galactic conformity in IllustrisTNG300-1 to the linear matter correlation function over `~2-40 h^-1 Mpc`. Fixed-mass shuffling suppresses the signal, implying that assembly-dependent occupancy, not halo-mass bias alone, dominates the conformity amplitude.
- [2607.03071](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03071) Mayur B. Shende et al. simulate jet-ISM coupling in 3C 326 N-like gaseous disks; small-scale clouds with `l_c,max=50 pc` produce the largest velocity dispersions and kinetic energies, while mixed clouds produce asymmetric lobes.
- [2607.03729](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03729) Sabyasachi Pal reviews how SKAO can test whether arm-length asymmetries in extragalactic radio sources are driven by jet interactions with clumpy interstellar and intergalactic media.
- [2607.02665](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02665) Sarah N. Bradbury detects quasi-periodic Faraday-rotation oscillations in the Corkscrew Galaxy with ASKAP/POSSUM, with an RM spatial period of `342+/-101 arcsec`, linking helical radio morphology to jet or local ICM Faraday screens.
- [2607.03147](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03147) Eric Emsellem uses PHANGS-JWST `3` and `7.7 micron` imaging of `57` star-forming disks to identify a bar-driven ISM-structuring transition near `10^10 M_sun`.
- [2607.03543](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03543) Charlotte L. Jackson derives a MIGHTEE COSMOS SFR-radio relation with a Bayesian mixture model that avoids hard AGN cuts and finds intrinsic scatter of `0.178 dex`.
- [2607.04248](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04248) Satoko Sawada-Satoh reviews SKA measurements of circumnuclear magnetic fields using HI Zeeman measurements, megamasers, and broadband Faraday rotation.
- [2607.02704](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02704) C. Mendes de Oliveira introduces the S-PLUS SCALE catalog of `83` nearby clusters and groups, with a pilot Abell 4038 environment analysis out to `10 h^-1 Mpc`.
- [2607.04942](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04942) O. C. Jones uses JWST/MIRI imaging of Centaurus A to associate `928` red point sources with recent disc star formation and finds no strong evidence for radio-jet-triggered star formation.
- [2607.05295](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05295) Eric Habjan combines SITELLE and MUSE lines to reduce density-inhomogeneity bias in `T_e` oxygen abundances for HII regions in five nearby galaxies.
