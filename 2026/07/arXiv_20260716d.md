## Executive View

The strongest high-redshift galaxy entry is [2607.13269](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13269) Vahid Asadi, which applies a CatBoost classifier trained on mock JWST/NIRCam photometry from Santa Cruz semi-analytic models to COSMOS2025 and identifies `1111` massive quiescent candidates at `2.5<z<5`. The result is method-dependent, but it is directly relevant to quenching completeness in JWST-era high-redshift catalogs.

Gas supply and feedback set the center of the day. [2607.14090](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14090) Zhiyuan Yao uses idealized 3D simulations to map when cold streams penetrating virial shocks fragment, coagulate, or disrupt in hot CGM. [2607.13961](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13961) Ke Xu proposes "cosmic siphoning" as a multi-scale environmental channel that redirects cool gas toward deeper potential wells, connecting isolated centrals, group centrals, satellites, and massive neighboring halos. [2607.13132](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13132) Isaac Rosenberg tests AGN jet prescriptions in zoom-in cluster simulations and finds low-density gas outside filaments is a sensitive feedback diagnostic.

The best secondary set links observables to model systematics. [2607.13151](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13151) Francisco Maion builds MillenniumTNG multi-zoom emulators for the stellar-mass function and halo gas fractions. [2607.13868](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13868) Ruonan Guo translates kiloparsec-scale attenuation-curve variation into effective dust composition and dust-mass trends. [2607.13971](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13971) Jun-Sung Moon connects galaxy size and morphology information to primordial spin-factor statistics through a universal density-potential relation.

## High-Redshift Galaxies, Quenching, and Structure

- [2607.13269](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13269) Vahid Asadi et al. `COSMOS2025: A Machine Learning Census of Massive Quiescent Galaxies at 2.5 < z < 5`
  - **Question:** how incomplete are standard SED-fitting selections of massive quiescent galaxies at `2.5<z<5`?
  - **Data:**
    - Catalog: COSMOS2025.
    - Training source: mock photometry from Santa Cruz semi-analytic models.
    - Bands: key JWST/NIRCam filters with realistic noise.
    - Target population: massive galaxies with `log(M*/Msun)>9.5` at `2.5<z<5`.
  - **Method:**
    - Train a CatBoost classifier on mock photometry.
    - Transfer the model quiescent label, defined by specific star-formation rate, into observed color space.
    - Validate the classifier against the semi-analytic-model ground truth.
    - Apply the trained model to COSMOS2025 and compare with catalog SED-fitting classifications.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The classifier reaches `78%` recall and `82%` purity on the mock validation set.
    - The SED-fitting baseline reaches `53%` recall in the same validation comparison.
    - Application to COSMOS2025 identifies `1111` quiescent candidates.
    - The catalog SED-fitting configuration identifies `427` candidates.
    - The result depends on how well the semi-analytic quiescence definition transfers to real galaxies.
  - **Position:** read first for high-redshift quenching because it tests whether simplified SED histories miss transitional quiescent systems in JWST-era catalogs.

- [2607.13868](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13868) Ruonan Guo et al. `Mapping Dust Attenuation at Kiloparsec Scales. IV. A Dust-model Interpretation of Attenuation Curves in Nearby Galaxies`
  - **Question:** can empirical attenuation-curve variation be translated into effective dust-grain properties?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `2487` high-continuum-S/N spaxels in `91` SwiM v4.2 galaxies.
    - Measurements: normalized attenuation-curve shapes and attenuation amplitudes.
    - Model ingredients: astronomical silicate and graphite grains with MRN-like size distributions.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit attenuation-curve shape with a uniform-screen dust model.
    - Use attenuation amplitude to estimate model-dependent dust mass surface density.
    - Compare star-forming and non-star-forming regions.
    - Map curve-shape trends onto effective small-grain fractions and composition.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The `2175 A` bump sequence maps mainly onto the effective fraction of small graphitic grains.
    - The NUV-slope sequence maps onto the effective small-silicate grain fraction and total silicate mass fraction.
    - Non-star-forming regions have higher dust mass surface densities but lower dust-to-stellar mass ratios.
    - Regions with larger specific Halpha surface brightness have larger dust-to-stellar mass ratios and lower inferred small-grain fractions.
  - **Position:** useful for high-redshift SED interpretation because attenuation-law variation can mimic or bias age, dust, and quenching in unresolved galaxies.

- [2607.13747](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13747) Nina Mackensen et al. `Adaptive optics imaging of the nuclei of Seyfert 2 galaxies: An unexpectedly high incidence of nuclear stellar rings`
  - **Question:** do near-infrared adaptive-optics images reveal nuclear rings in Seyfert 2 hosts more often than earlier optical studies suggested?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `18` Seyfert 2 galaxies.
    - Imaging: Ks-band adaptive-optics observations from the Large Binocular Telescope.
    - Comparison: archival HST V- or H-band images.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare nuclear morphology across near-infrared and shorter-wavelength images.
    - Fit 2D light distributions with GALFIT.
    - Apply unsharp masking as a complementary ring-detection method.
    - Compare detected ring sizes and masses with non-active-galaxy ring samples.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Nuclear stellar rings are identified in `8/18` galaxies.
    - The inferred incidence is `44+/-12%`.
    - The fraction is about twice that reported in a complete nuclear-ring atlas.
    - Ring radii, sizes, and masses are similar to those in non-active galaxies.
    - Low-luminosity AGN activity does not appear to strongly suppress nuclear-ring formation.
  - **Position:** relevant structure paper because central morphology, bars, rings, and AGN fueling are coupled ingredients in galaxy-evolution models.

## CGM, Gas Supply, and Environment

- [2607.14090](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14090) Zhiyuan Yao et al. `Cold Stream Penetration of Virial Shocks: Fragmentation, Coagulation, and Disruption in the Hot Circumgalactic Medium`
  - **Question:** when can cold cosmic-web streams survive virial-shock penetration and continue feeding galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: idealized three-dimensional cylindrical streams entering a hot CGM.
    - Parameters: stream radius, Mach number, pressure contrast, and three density contrasts.
    - Physical regimes: coagulation, fragmentation, disruption, and marginal survival.
  - **Method:**
    - Vary stream and CGM parameters while controlling post-equilibration stream properties.
    - Track cold-gas mass, interfacial area, and streamwise momentum.
    - Separate transient pressure-restoration effects from later shear-dominated evolution.
    - Compare post-shock cooling time with virial crossing time.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At modest pressure contrast, survival is set mainly by velocity shear versus radiative cooling.
    - Large pressure contrasts can steepen oblique shocks into bow shocks.
    - Survival then depends on the ratio of post-shock cooling time to virial crossing time.
    - In survival cases, turbulent radiative entrainment increases cold-gas mass flux while approximately conserving mean streamwise momentum flux.
    - Cold streams in high-sigma density peaks at `z>2` are expected to survive and may fragment into multiphase CGM structures.
    - At `z<0.5`, bow shocks and longer cooling times suppress penetration.
  - **Position:** read first for CGM fueling because it links cosmic-web accretion to multiphase gas survival across redshift.

- [2607.13961](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13961) Ke Xu and Tao Wang `Even central galaxies feel their environment: cosmic siphoning of cool gas accretion`
  - **Question:** how can external environment regulate cool gas in central galaxies after internal black-hole scaling is considered?
  - **Data:**
    - Observable relation: cool gas fraction `mu_HI = M_HI/M*` versus black-hole mass.
    - Galaxy class: central galaxies, including isolated gas-poor centrals and group centrals.
    - Environmental markers: satellites and massive neighboring halos.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify galaxies that strongly deviate from the `mu_HI-M_BH` relation.
    - Compare gas-poor isolated centrals with centrals that retain substantial cool gas.
    - Examine black-hole-to-stellar-mass ratios, bulge prominence, satellite context, and large-scale neighbors.
    - Interpret the trends with a multi-scale gas-competition model.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Strong outliers from the `mu_HI-M_BH` relation are mainly isolated central galaxies without substantial satellites.
    - Gas-poor isolated centrals have higher black-hole-to-stellar-mass ratios and more prominent bulges.
    - They are preferentially located near massive neighboring halos.
    - Massive halos can intercept cold filamentary streams and starve lower-mass neighbors.
    - Within groups and clusters, centrals can drain gas from satellites through tidal interaction and accretion of satellite CGM.
  - **Position:** useful environment paper because it frames cool-gas supply as a competition between internal AGN regulation and large-scale potential wells.

- [2607.13132](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13132) Isaac Rosenberg et al. `Here, There and Everywhere: How AGN jets affect galaxy cluster environments`
  - **Question:** which AGN jet parameters leave observable signatures in cluster, filament, and under-dense gas?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: zoom-in hydrodynamic simulations of `3` galaxy clusters from The Three Hundred.
    - Model: SIMBA-C with varied AGN jet parameters.
    - Observables: tSZ signal, brightest cluster galaxy properties, black-hole-halo mass relations, and baryonic mass fractions.
  - **Method:**
    - Vary jet velocity, orientation, and delayed hydrodynamic coupling.
    - Use filament finding and oriented stacking to separate cluster, filament, and outside-filament regions.
    - Compare simulated galaxy and gas properties with X-ray and other survey constraints.
    - Track redshift-dependent thermodynamic response.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Jet velocity is the dominant tested parameter for heating low-density environments at `z>1`.
    - At lower redshift, higher-velocity jets quench star formation, expel baryons, and prevent black-hole growth.
    - Central cluster and filament tSZ signals change by only `~10%`.
    - Under-dense gas outside filaments shows a tSZ enhancement of `~100%`.
    - Hot gas in low-density regimes is a sensitive probe of AGN feedback.
  - **Position:** important feedback-systematics paper because large-scale gas outside clusters can discriminate jet prescriptions that look similar in cluster cores.

## Simulations, Black Holes, and Model Systematics

- [2607.13151](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13151) Francisco Maion et al. `Evaluating the flexibility of the MillenniumTNG galaxy formation model with multi-zoom re-simulations`
  - **Question:** can MillenniumTNG feedback parameters be adjusted to fit both galaxy stellar-mass functions and halo gas fractions?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: multi-zoom re-simulations of halos selected from MillenniumTNG.
    - Observables: galaxy stellar-mass function and gas fractions in large dark-matter halos.
    - Emulator targets: GSMF and halo gas-fraction response to feedback parameters.
  - **Method:**
    - Run a multi-zoom campaign that re-simulates several sub-regions of a larger volume at higher resolution.
    - Vary parameters controlling star formation and AGN feedback.
    - Train Gaussian-process emulators on GSMF and gas-fraction outputs.
    - Fit recent measurements of both observables simultaneously.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The emulators predict the GSMF with `~0.1 dex` precision.
    - They predict halo gas fractions with `~10%` precision.
    - A parameter combination can qualitatively fit both GSMF and the lower gas fractions observed in massive clusters.
    - The preferred shift makes stellar feedback less energetic and kinetic AGN feedback more energetic and rarer.
    - The modified parameters still require checks against other MillenniumTNG successes.
  - **Position:** useful calibration paper because it tests whether galaxy-formation models can absorb cluster gas-fraction constraints without losing galaxy statistics.

- [2607.13786](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13786) Ruby J. Wright et al. `RABBITS IV: Stellar feedback and SMBH merging time-scales in the sub-Milky Way mass regime`
  - **Question:** how does stellar feedback change SMBH binary merger delays in low- and intermediate-mass galaxy mergers?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: `16` equal-mass galaxy mergers run with KETJU.
    - Galaxy regime: stellar masses below `Mstar~10^10 Msun`.
    - Parameter variation: supernova outflow velocity changed by a factor of `~4`.
  - **Method:**
    - Simulate galaxy coalescence and SMBH binary hardening.
    - Measure post-hardening merger time-scales.
    - Compare binary evolution with stellar-dynamical hardening models.
    - Infer whether nuclear stellar properties can predict merger delays.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Post-hardening SMBH merger times span `~30-500 Myr`.
    - Stronger stellar feedback produces longer merger delays.
    - The delay changes through the central stellar density of the merger remnants.
    - Stellar-feedback uncertainty alone can shift SMBH merger delays by more than an order of magnitude.
    - Merger times can be recovered with useful accuracy from nuclear properties extrapolated from scales up to `~100 R_infl`.
  - **Position:** relevant black-hole demographics paper because low-mass SMBH merger timing affects LISA-rate predictions and feedback-dependent occupation models.

- [2607.13233](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13233) Jaymin Ding `When X-ray Features Hurt: Label Noise and Luminosity Overlap in Machine Learning Classification of AGN and Star-forming Galaxies`
  - **Question:** why can adding X-ray flux reduce machine-learning separation of optical AGN and star-forming galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: optically selected SDSS galaxies with BPT-derived labels.
    - Classifier: random forest using optical and X-ray features.
    - Diagnostic: X-ray luminosity overlap across the canonical `10^42 erg/s` AGN threshold.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare cross-validation errors with and without X-ray flux.
    - Measure misclassified-object distances from the BPT demarcation curve.
    - Test whether label uncertainty is concentrated near the classification boundary.
    - Evaluate permutation importance of the X-ray feature.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Adding X-ray flux reduced reported classifier accuracy from `97.51%` to `89.26%`.
    - AGN and star-forming galaxies overlap substantially in X-ray luminosity for this optically selected sample.
    - Misclassified objects cluster near the BPT boundary.
    - Correctly classified objects have median boundary distance `0.748 dex`, while misclassified objects have `0.130 dex`.
    - The KS test gives `D=0.701` and `p=3.93x10^-9`.
  - **Position:** useful survey-methods warning because multiwavelength features can degrade labels when the training truth is boundary-noisy.

- [2607.13971](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13971) Jun-Sung Moon et al. `A Universal Relation Between Primordial Density-Potential Cross-correlation Coefficient and Spin Factor Distribution`
  - **Question:** can observable galaxy-size information constrain primordial density-potential correlations through halo spin statistics?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation source: Multiverse simulations in flat LambdaCDM and wCDM cosmologies.
    - Quantities: primordial spin factor `tau` and density-potential cross-correlation coefficient `q`.
    - Observable bridge: prior reconstruction of the `tau` distribution from galaxy size distribution.
  - **Method:**
    - Develop analytic expressions for the mean and variance of `tau` as functions of `q`.
    - Test the relation across cosmologies and smoothing scales.
    - Compare the analytic model with simulated spin-factor distributions.
    - Discuss inference of `q` from the reconstructed `tau` distribution.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The mean and variance of `tau` follow a universal relation with `q` in the tested simulations.
    - The relation is insensitive to the smoothing scales considered.
    - It holds for both LambdaCDM and wCDM simulation sets.
    - Galaxy size distributions may provide a route to constraining early-universe physics through spin statistics.
  - **Position:** relevant large-scale-structure methods paper because it connects galaxy morphology and size distributions to initial-condition diagnostics.
