## High-Redshift Quasars, AGN, and Early Galaxies

- [2607.03432](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03432) D. Yang et al. `Euclid: Discovery of 31 new quasars at 6.6 < z < 7.8`
  - **Question:** how quickly can Euclid expand the faint reionization-era quasar sample?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: first `1.5` years of Euclid Wide Survey data.
    - Area: about `3000 deg^2`.
    - Follow-up: Keck, Magellan, and Large Binocular Telescope spectroscopy.
  - **Method:**
    - Select candidates with machine-learning and probabilistic methods from Euclid optical and near-infrared imaging.
    - Add ancillary `z`-band data when available.
    - Spectroscopically confirm prioritized sources.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The survey confirms `31` quasars at `6.6<z<7.8`.
    - The sample includes `12` quasars at `z>=7`, more than doubling the known count.
    - The luminosities span `-25.5<M_1450<-23.6`.
    - EUCL J172902.75+641018.1 at `z~7.77` is reported as the most distant known quasar.
  - **Position:** weekly read-first because Euclid is changing high-z quasar work from rare-object follow-up into large-area demographics.

- [2607.03430](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03430) S. Belladitta et al. `Euclid: A UV-faint quasar in a highly luminous star-forming host galaxy at z ~ 7.7`
  - **Question:** what host galaxy accompanies a UV-faint Euclid quasar at `z~7.7`?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: EUCL J125308.55+705432.3.
    - Facility: IRAM/NOEMA.
    - Measurements: [CII] `158 micron`, cold dust continuum, far-infrared luminosity, dust mass, and dynamical context.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure the [CII] systemic redshift and line luminosity.
    - Derive far-infrared luminosity, dust mass, and star-formation rate.
    - Compare the UV-faint quasar host with UV-bright high-redshift quasar hosts.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The systemic redshift is `z=7.6980+/-0.0004`.
    - The host has `L_FIR=3.6 x 10^12 L_sun`, `L_[CII]=2 x 10^9 L_sun`, and dust mass `1.4 x 10^8 M_sun`.
    - The star-formation rate is `>250 M_sun/yr`.
    - The source has the brightest [CII] emission among comparable `z~7.5` quasars despite `M_1450=-24.06`.
  - **Position:** key companion to Euclid discovery because UV-faint quasar selection still finds massive dusty hosts.

- [2607.02666](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02666) Gene C. K. Leung et al. `The MIRI Early Obscured-AGN Wide Survey (MEOW): A Population of Hidden AGN at z >= 5 Revealed by JWST/MIRI Imaging`
  - **Question:** what obscured AGN population is missed by rest-UV and broad-line selection at `z>=5`?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: JWST/MIRI MEOW imaging in GOODS-N and GOODS-S.
    - Area: `95 arcmin^2` in `43` pointings.
    - Ancillary data: HST, JWST/NIRCam, and SCUBA-2.
  - **Method:**
    - Select high-redshift MIRI-bright candidates.
    - Fit SEDs with mid-infrared constraints.
    - Compare MIRI-selected AGN with broad-line AGN and Little Red Dot populations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The sample contains `16` MIRI-selected AGN at `z=4.5-7.2`, with `12` spectroscopically confirmed.
    - Twelve of the `16` are newly identified AGN.
    - At least `5` narrow-line AGN represent an obscured high-redshift population.
    - The luminosity function at `z=4.5-6` has number densities comparable to broad-line AGN and LRDs.
  - **Position:** important early SMBH census paper because mid-infrared selection closes a major obscuration gap.

- [2607.08749](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08749) Rebecca L. Larson et al. `Deep Spectroscopic Follow-Up of Maisie's Galaxy -- A Typical Galaxy in the Early Universe`
  - **Question:** what does deep spectroscopy reveal about one of the best-known `z>10` JWST galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Spectroscopy: more than `19 hr` of JWST/NIRSpec G395M exposure.
    - Mid-infrared support: MIRI/LRS observations.
    - Diagnostics: [OII], [OIII], [NeIII], SED fits, and rest-frame optical emission lines.
  - **Method:**
    - Update the systemic redshift.
    - Measure density, SFR, metallicity, ionization parameter, and rest-optical line ratios.
    - Compare the source with other `z>10` galaxies and star-forming main-sequence expectations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The redshift is `z=11.408+/-0.005`.
    - [OII] gives `n_e=108.56(+873.9/-35.37)` and `SFR_[OII]=1.3+/-0.35`.
    - The inferred metallicity is `Z/Z_sun=0.17+/-0.05` with `log(U)=-2.26+/-0.13`.
    - Maisie's Galaxy lies along the star-forming main sequence and looks typical rather than extreme.
  - **Position:** useful calibration source because it moderates the interpretation of bright early JWST galaxies.

- [2607.07698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07698) Adarsh Kuruvanthodi et al. `Tracing the Evolution of the Balmer Break from Cosmic Dawn to Cosmic Noon with JWST`
  - **Question:** how does Balmer-break strength evolve in galaxies from `z=10` to `z=3.5`?
  - **Data:**
    - Imaging: JWST/NIRCam photometry from CEERS, JADES, FRESCO, and PRIMER.
    - Redshift range: `z=3.5-10`.
    - Diagnostic: broad-band Balmer-break strength.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure break strengths in redshift windows chosen to avoid strong line contamination.
    - Fit SEDs with CIGALE and run mock-galaxy tests.
    - Relate break strength to age, dust, and Little Red Dot-like colors.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Median Balmer-break strength rises from `1.1` near cosmic dawn to `1.5` toward cosmic noon.
    - Older stellar populations are the main driver of the median increase.
    - Extremely strong breaks, `BB>3.0`, appear at `z=3.5-4` and `z=7-10`.
    - The extreme population points to dusty or old galaxies and LRDs at lower redshift, and mainly LRDs at `z=7-10`.
  - **Position:** important JWST photometric-calibration paper because Balmer-break selection intersects stellar ages, dust, and compact accretors.

## Reionization Gas, Lyalpha, and Pop III Searches

- [2607.08264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08264) Shunta Shimizu et al. `Subaru meets JWST: A Direct Measurement of Lyalpha Escape Fraction at z~6.2 with Dual Narrow-Band Imaging`
  - **Question:** what Lyalpha escape fraction is measured directly for Halpha emitters at `z~6.2`?
  - **Data:**
    - Field: CEERS.
    - Imaging: JWST/NIRCam F470N Halpha plus Subaru/HSC NB872 Lyalpha.
    - Sample: `84` Halpha emitters, `56` with reliable Lyalpha photometry, and `19` Lyalpha detections above `2 sigma`.
  - **Method:**
    - Select Halpha emitters from F470N excesses.
    - Measure Lyalpha and Halpha fluxes with dual narrow-band data.
    - Stack the sample and compare escape fraction with luminosity, equivalent width, UV slope, size, and dust indicators.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The completeness-weighted median is `f_esc^Lyalpha=0.106(+0.066/-0.044)`.
    - No significant stacked dependence appears on the lower Halpha luminosity limit.
    - Individual Lyalpha escape tracks Lyalpha equivalent width positively and UV slope plus UV size negatively.
  - **Position:** weekly read-first for EoR gas because it measures escape directly with matched line fluxes rather than only inferred selection effects.

- [2607.05087](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05087) Daniil Smirnov et al. `Lyman-alpha haloes in the aftermath of reionisation`
  - **Question:** how do Lyalpha haloes around low-luminosity LAEs evolve from `z~3` to `z>=6`?
  - **Data:**
    - Instrument: MUSE.
    - High-z sample: `18` LAEs at `z>=6` in the MUSE eXtremely Deep Field.
    - Comparison: matched lower-redshift LAEs at `z~3`.
  - **Method:**
    - Match intrinsic surface-brightness sensitivity after cosmological dimming.
    - Detect individual haloes and stack non-detections.
    - Compare scale lengths and line widths.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Extended Lyalpha emission is detected around `6/18` high-z LAEs.
    - High-z exponential scale lengths are about `3x` smaller than at `z~3`.
    - The stack of high-z non-detections shows no extended Lyalpha signal.
    - High-z Lyalpha line widths are about `2.5x` smaller than in the lower-redshift comparison.
  - **Position:** important because compact or absent Lyalpha haloes are a direct environmental and IGM constraint near reionization.

- [2607.07832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07832) S. Goswami et al. `On the detection of Population III galaxies: Emission Line Diagnostics for Hybrid Stellar Populations`
  - **Question:** can Pop III galaxies be recognized when metal-enriched Pop II stars contribute to the spectrum?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation source: IllustrisTNG galaxies selected by Pop III contribution.
    - Spectral modeling: self-consistent photoionization models.
    - Diagnostics: high-ionization emission lines and Pop III-sensitive line ratios.
  - **Method:**
    - Compute integrated spectra for hybrid Pop III and Pop II stellar populations.
    - Vary the Pop III-to-total stellar mass ratio.
    - Test diagnostic separation between pure and mixed systems.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Standard diagnostics can identify galaxies dominated almost entirely by Pop III stars.
    - Significant Pop II emission creates degeneracies and hides Pop III signatures.
    - Additional methods are needed to detect Pop III stars during and after reionization.
  - **Position:** important Pop III paper because JWST line searches must account for realistic hybrid populations.

- [2607.08726](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08726) Joshua Kasiri et al. `Force convergence in Monte Carlo Lyman-alpha radiative transfer`
  - **Question:** what convergence tests are needed for Lyalpha radiative acceleration and force multipliers?
  - **Data:**
    - Test geometry: static spherical clouds.
    - Benchmarks: diffusion-limit acceleration profiles and integrated force multipliers.
    - Estimators: event-based scattering, gradient-of-energy-density, and divergence-of-radiation-pressure.
  - **Method:**
    - Separate zeroth-order mean convergence, first-order sampling precision, and second-order variance stability.
    - Quantify photon-number and runtime requirements.
    - Test core-skipping, geometry, estimator construction, and resolution.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Emergent spectra alone are insufficient for internal force convergence.
    - Accuracy, statistical cost, and variance reliability must be checked separately.
    - The framework gives practical diagnostics for Lyalpha momentum-transfer calculations.
  - **Position:** useful theory-method paper because Lyalpha feedback predictions require robust internal momentum deposition.

- [2607.05494](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05494) Paryag Sharma et al. `Uncertainty-Aware Deep Learning for the Lyalpha Forest: CNN-Based Absorber Detection and Characterization`
  - **Question:** can deep learning scale Lyalpha forest absorber fitting for large survey samples?
  - **Data:**
    - Training: IllustrisTNG synthetic spectra.
    - Labels: VIPER Voigt-profile fits.
    - Observational test: UVES spectra.
  - **Method:**
    - Train a CNN to infer absorber presence, `N_HI`, Doppler parameter, and centroid.
    - Use Monte Carlo dropout for uncertainty estimates.
    - Compare distributions against classical fits and observed spectra.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Synthetic tests reach `F1~0.8` with `~0.18 dex` mean absolute error in `log N_HI`.
    - UVES tests fall to `F1~0.5`, showing simulation-to-observation domain shift.
    - The method preserves CDDF and `b_HI-N_HI` distributions better than object-level performance alone suggests.
  - **Position:** useful IGM infrastructure because automated absorber catalogs need realistic uncertainty and domain-shift control.

## Environments, CGM, and Feedback

- [2607.05523](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05523) Giulia Tozzi et al. `BlackTHUNDER Reveals a Massive Filament around a Compact AGN at z ~= 5.23`
  - **Question:** what environment hosts the compact AGN GN-77652 at `z=5.229`?
  - **Data:**
    - Instrument: JWST/NIRSpec IFU.
    - Imaging: deep JWST/NIRCam.
    - System: GN-77652 plus neighboring sources at `z~5.23`.
  - **Method:**
    - Map [OIII] emission and kinematics.
    - Measure stellar masses, gas metallicities, star-formation rates, and projected separations.
    - Test formation scenarios for the compact AGN and its neighbors.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The compact AGN lies near a `12 kpc` galaxy filament.
    - Filament members span `M_star=0.7-13 x 10^8 M_sun`, `12+log(O/H)=7.6-8.5`, and `SFR=0.4-6 M_sun/yr`.
    - The central group galaxy has `M_star~1.1 x 10^9 M_sun` and `Z~0.6 Z_sun`.
    - The system is expected to coalesce in `150-440 Myr`.
  - **Position:** important high-z environment result because it resolves compact AGN growth inside group-scale filamentary assembly.

- [2607.06744](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06744) Matthias Weber et al. `CRexit observed: probing cosmic ray transport in the circumgalactic medium with absorption line spectra`
  - **Question:** can absorption-line spectra distinguish cosmic-ray transport regimes in the CGM?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: high-resolution galaxy-halo runs with different cosmic-ray transport prescriptions.
    - Mock spectra: large sightline ensembles through multiphase CGM gas.
    - Ions: MgII, SiII, CIV, OVI, and related metal absorption diagnostics.
  - **Method:**
    - Generate synthetic spectra for cool, warm, and hot phases.
    - Compare slow and efficient cosmic-ray transport models.
    - Measure column densities, equivalent widths, covering fractions, velocity widths, and stacked profiles.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Efficient cosmic-ray transport enhances cool `T~10^4 K` and warm `T~10^5 K` CGM gas.
    - Two-moment transport produces the strongest MgII and SiII absorption.
    - Slow transport underproduces cool low-ion gas.
    - CIV shifts from extended warm gas in slow-transport cases to mixing layers around cool clouds in efficient-transport cases.
  - **Position:** important CGM theory paper because metal absorption can constrain cosmic-ray transport physics.

- [2607.08721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08721) Nicholas Battaglia et al. `Thermal Sunyaev-Zel'dovich Measurements of Locally Bright Galaxies with ACT DR6: Radio Source Contamination and Excess Compton-y Signal`
  - **Question:** how should radio-source contamination and AGN heating be handled in galaxy tSZ stacks?
  - **Data:**
    - Maps: ACT DR6 Compton-`y` maps.
    - Sample: Planck Locally Bright Galaxies.
    - Split: galaxies with and without co-spatial radio sources.
  - **Method:**
    - Re-measure the stellar-mass to tSZ scaling relation.
    - Quantify residual radio-source contamination.
    - Compare radial Compton-`y` profiles at fixed stellar mass.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The ACT stacks recover a power-law tSZ-stellar-mass relation.
    - Radio-source contamination remains at the few-percent level.
    - LBGs with co-spatial radio sources show a factor-of-two excess Compton-`y` signal at fixed stellar mass.
    - The excess persists to at least `6 arcmin`.
  - **Position:** useful CGM feedback paper because tSZ analyses must separate contamination, halo mass, and thermal energy injection.

- [2607.02654](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02654) David Valles-Perez et al. `The youth of the intracluster medium. I. A non-parametric characterisation of the gas and electron number density profiles of z ~= 2 protoclusters`
  - **Question:** what gas-density and electron-density profiles characterize `z=2` protoclusters?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: Magneticum.
    - Sample: more than `3800` protocluster-core regions.
    - Mass cut: `M_500c>10^13 M_sun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Extract gas-density, temperature, ionization, and electron-density profiles.
    - Split by mass, dynamical state, and central activity.
    - Follow profiles from central halos into protocluster outskirts.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Density profiles show moderate self-similarity departures.
    - High-mass and strongly accreting systems show a double-beta structure.
    - Hot ionized gas dominates mainly beyond `r~0.1-0.5 R_500c`.
  - **Position:** useful protocluster calibration because future high-z environment measurements need gas observables, not only galaxy counts.

- [2607.07296](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07296) Euclid Collaboration: M. Giulietti et al. `Euclid Quick Data Release (Q1). Searching for radio-selected Euclid-dark galaxies in the EDF-N`
  - **Question:** what population is selected by LOFAR radio detections with no Euclid Q1 counterpart?
  - **Data:**
    - Field: Euclid Deep Field-North.
    - Radio data: LOFAR HBA at `144 MHz`.
    - Sample: `166` Euclid-dark galaxies, including `88` isolated sources.
  - **Method:**
    - Select radio sources without Euclid Q1 detections above `3 sigma`.
    - Fit UV-to-radio SEDs with ancillary infrared and sub-arcsecond LOFAR data.
    - Use brightness temperatures and radio excess to flag AGN.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Photometric redshifts span `0.4<=z_ph<=5.0`.
    - About `40%` of sources show AGN evidence.
    - Far-infrared detected systems are consistent with heavily obscured massive star-forming galaxies.
  - **Position:** strong survey-infrastructure entry because Euclid plus LOFAR finds obscured galaxy and black-hole growth missed by optical selection.

## Survey Infrastructure and Lower-Redshift Anchors

- [2607.07795](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07795) Z. Igo et al. `The incidence of eROSITA X-ray AGN in the local Universe: from dwarf to massive galaxies`
  - **Question:** how common are X-ray-selected AGN in low-mass galaxies at low redshift?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: eROSITA four-pass all-sky X-ray data.
    - Parent sample: `~5.35 million` Legacy Survey DR10 galaxies at `0.03<z<0.2`.
    - Low-mass detections: `874` X-ray AGN in galaxies with `log(M_*/M_sun)<10`.
  - **Method:**
    - Build a complete galaxy parent sample and validate X-ray associations.
    - Model `p(log lambda_SAR | M_*, z)` using detections and non-detections.
    - Integrate the accretion-rate distribution above chosen thresholds.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Most low-mass X-ray AGN are newly discovered.
    - The cumulative AGN fraction above `lambda_SAR>10^-3` is `~1%`.
    - The result gives a lower limit on the black-hole occupation fraction in low-mass galaxies.
  - **Position:** useful anchor because dwarf black-hole occupation informs high-z seed and growth models.

- [2607.07818](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07818) M. I. Arnaudova et al. `The DESI View of the Faint Radio Source Population in LoTSS DR2`
  - **Question:** how can faint LoTSS radio sources be separated into star-forming and AGN populations?
  - **Data:**
    - Radio survey: LoTSS DR2.
    - Spectroscopy: DESI DR1.
    - Sample: `251,413` radio sources.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine radio excess, BPT, modified MEx, and [OIII] equivalent-width diagnostics.
    - Estimate class probabilities with Monte Carlo methods.
    - Select high-confidence SFG, RQ AGN, LERG, and HERG samples.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - At `90%` probability threshold the catalog contains `68,820` SFGs, `32,288` RQ AGN, `35,210` LERGs, and `3,085` HERGs.
    - LERGs typically accrete below `1%` of Eddington, while HERGs accrete above it.
    - High-accreting LERGs can show star-forming spectral features, complicating classification.
  - **Position:** important for SKA-era demographics because radio-galaxy physics needs spectroscopic labels and probabilistic classes.

- [2607.06662](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06662) A. Hernan-Caballero et al. `J-PLUS: Spectral classification and photometric redshifts for 79 million sources in the fourth data release`
  - **Question:** how well can J-PLUS DR4 classify sources and estimate photo-z values at wide-field scale?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: J-PLUS DR4.
    - Catalogue: `79.2 million` sources to `r=22`.
    - Photometry: `12` J-PLUS bands plus WISE for selected classifications.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare LePhare template fitting with LeMoNNADE machine learning.
    - Use spectral-mixing augmentation and Bayesian calibration.
    - Validate redshift precision and source-class probabilities.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - LeMoNNADE outperforms template fitting in precision, robust scatter, and outlier rate.
    - WISE reduces quasar catastrophic outliers from `~40%` to `~23%`.
    - Systemic redshift bias stays below `1%` up to `z=4`.
  - **Position:** useful catalogue infrastructure for galaxy, quasar, and environment pre-selection.

- [2607.06313](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06313) Naomi Ota et al. `Kinetic structure of the intracluster medium across nearby clusters observed with XRISM`
  - **Question:** what ICM kinetic regimes are emerging from XRISM/Resolve?
  - **Data:**
    - Instrument: XRISM/Resolve.
    - Sample: `45` measurements in `19` nearby clusters.
    - Quantities: velocity dispersion, bulk velocity, and non-thermal pressure proxies.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare measurements on a common effective line-of-sight scale.
    - Separate cool-core centers, cool-core outer regions, and non-cool-core systems.
    - Contrast observations with forward-modeled TNG-Cluster predictions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Cool-core and disturbed systems do not form a single turbulence sequence.
    - Mean `R_v=|v_bulk|/sigma_v` rises from `0.45` in cool-core centers to `1.6` in non-cool-core systems.
    - Coherent bulk motion, more than unresolved broadening alone, distinguishes disturbed systems.
  - **Position:** important cluster benchmark because velocity structure is now directly measured by X-ray spectroscopy.
