## Executive View

The read-first paper is [2607.16404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16404) Anna Durrant, which uses a `100 cMpc` COLIBRE simulation variant to test whether density-dependent top-heavy IMFs can reduce JWST bright-galaxy tensions. The model boosts early far-UV output by up to `~4x`, reaches `M_UV~-20` at `z=15`, and still reproduces `z=5` stellar-mass and optical-luminosity functions at the level of the fiducial run.

JWST also drives the strongest observational entry. [2607.17424](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17424) Ken-ichi Tadaki combines JWST/MIRI, JWST/NIRCam, and ALMA to show that massive dusty star-forming galaxies at `z~2` can already host compact rest-frame K-band bulges, with F770W and `870 um` profiles closely matched out to `8 kpc` in the stack. This is directly useful for interpreting the route from dusty starbursts to compact quiescent systems.

The gas and AGN side is broad. [2607.17567](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17567) Xiaoxia Zhang measures an intrinsic TNG anti-correlation between black-hole mass and cold CGM gas after controlling for stellar and halo mass. [2607.17729](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17729) Konstantinos Kouroumpatzakis reports a dual AGN in a Green Pea system, making a low-mass compact starburst analog relevant to early SMBH growth. [2607.17448](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17448) Jun-Rong Liu gives an optically thick supercritical-outflow model for Little Red Dots.

For environment and survey infrastructure, [2607.16417](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16417) Anthony M. Englert validates the LoVoCCS LSST-pipeline products on a `~16 deg^2` Hercules Supercluster field, [2607.16467](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16467) R. Ianjamasimanana quantifies H I disk truncation across environments, and [2607.17792](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17792) G. Martin tests when intracluster light can trace dark-matter halo shape.

## High-Redshift Galaxies, Dust, and Early SMBH Growth

- [2607.16404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16404) Anna Durrant et al. `Cosmological simulations of the high-redshift galaxy population adopting a variable stellar initial mass function`
  - **Question:** can a density-dependent stellar IMF alleviate the overabundance gap between JWST high-redshift galaxies and conventional galaxy-formation models?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: COLIBRE variant in a `L=100 cMpc` cosmological volume evolved to `z=5`.
    - Physics: density-dependent top-heavy IMF for stars born from dense gas.
    - Emission model: UV/optical stellar and nebular emission with dust attenuation.
  - **Method:**
    - Adjust metal yields, dust yields, and supernova feedback energetics self-consistently with the changing IMF.
    - Compare the variable-IMF run with the fiducial COLIBRE L100m6 Chabrier-IMF run.
    - Track UV luminosity, optical luminosity, stellar-mass functions, dust effects, and UV continuum slopes.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Early far-UV luminosities rise by up to `~4x` relative to the fiducial simulation.
    - The variable-IMF run reaches observed brightnesses up to `M_UV~-20` at `z=15`, compared with `M_UV~-18.5` in the fiducial run.
    - The UV boost is later partly offset by stronger dust attenuation from enhanced supernova dust injection and grain growth.
    - The model remains broadly consistent with the `z=5` stellar-mass function, rest-frame optical luminosity function, and JWST UV-slope constraints.
  - **Position:** read first because it gives a physically explicit route for easing JWST bright-galaxy tensions without treating luminosity offsets as only a selection or calibration problem.

- [2607.17424](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17424) Ken-ichi Tadaki et al. `A Rest-frame K-band View of Bulge Growth in Massive Dusty Star-forming Galaxies at z~2`
  - **Question:** are compact bulges already present in massive dusty star-forming galaxies at `z~2`?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `16` massive dusty star-forming galaxies at median `z=2.18`.
    - Imaging: JWST/MIRI F770W, JWST/NIRCam eight-band imaging, and ALMA `870 um` data.
    - Rest-frame coverage: F770W probes rest-frame `2.4 um`, close to K band.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure F770W effective radii and compare them with ALMA dust-emission radii and NIRCam F444W radii.
    - Stack the sample at matched resolution.
    - Fit a bulge-plus-disk model to the F770W stack.
    - Fit radial-bin SEDs with CIGALE.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - F770W effective radii match `870 um` dust radii with median ratio `0.95`.
    - F770W radii are typically `23%` smaller than F444W radii.
    - The stacked `870 um` and F770W profiles agree out to `8 kpc`.
    - The F770W stack gives a bulge-to-total ratio of `0.50`.
    - Central attenuation declines from `A_V=2.6 mag` to `1.5 mag` beyond `r~7 kpc`, while the specific-SFR profile is flat.
    - Two systems have `870 um` emission `3-6x` more compact than the F770W light, marking a possible active compaction phase.
  - **Position:** high-priority JWST/MIRI result because rest-frame K-band structure is a cleaner stellar-mass tracer for dusty progenitors of compact quiescent galaxies.

- [2607.17729](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17729) Konstantinos Kouroumpatzakis et al. `Two Peas in a Pod: The First Confirmed Dual Active Galactic Nucleus within a Green Pea Galaxy System`
  - **Question:** can compact, intensely star-forming low-mass galaxies host simultaneous SMBH growth in multiple nuclei?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: Green Pea system SDSS J162209.41+352107.5.
    - X-ray imaging: Chandra resolves two hard X-ray sources.
    - Optical follow-up: Keck spectroscopy of both optical nuclei.
  - **Method:**
    - Use hard X-ray imaging to identify spatially distinct accreting nuclei.
    - Confirm common redshift from optical spectra.
    - Check each nucleus for broad Balmer emission and high-ionization AGN lines.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The two luminous hard X-ray sources are separated by `8.4 kpc` in projection.
    - Both optical nuclei share a common redshift.
    - Both nuclei independently show broad Balmer emission and high-ionization AGN lines.
    - The system demonstrates simultaneous SMBH accretion in a compact low-mass starburst environment.
  - **Position:** important analog entry because Green Pea galaxies connect local observability to compact, low-metallicity, high-ionization systems common in early galaxy assembly.

- [2607.17448](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17448) Jun-Rong Liu et al. `Optically Thick Outflow Driven by Supercritical Accretion May Explain Little Red Dots`
  - **Question:** can optically thick outflows from supercritical black-hole accretion explain Little Red Dot continua and line widths?
  - **Data:**
    - Target population: JWST Little Red Dots.
    - Model regime: black holes with `M~10^5-10^7 M_sun` and dimensionless accretion rates `m_dot~1500-5000`.
    - Photoionization component: Cloudy simulations for gas beyond the photosphere.
  - **Method:**
    - Build an analytic radiative-hydrodynamic outflow model.
    - Relate photosphere luminosity and temperature to black-hole mass and accretion rate.
    - Include kinematic broadening and scattering effects when predicting line FWHM.
    - Compare Balmer-break behavior with photoionization outputs.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The model produces photosphere luminosities of `10^43-10^45 erg s^-1`.
    - The predicted photosphere temperatures are `~3000-6000 K`, matching red optical continua.
    - Predicted emission-line FWHM values agree within a factor of `1.5` for more than `80%` of objects.
    - Partially ionized gas beyond the photosphere can produce Balmer breaks broadly consistent with observations.
  - **Position:** high-priority theory entry for LRD interpretation because it makes testable links among continuum color, line width, black-hole mass, and accretion rate.

## Gas, CGM, and AGN Host Diagnostics

- [2607.17567](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17567) Xiaoxia Zhang et al. `The Intrinsic Multiphase Gas--Black Hole Connection across Scales in IllustrisTNG`
  - **Question:** what intrinsic connection remains between SMBHs and multiphase circumgalactic gas after host-galaxy and halo-mass effects are removed?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: IllustrisTNG100.
    - Sample: `5089` central galaxies at `z=0`.
    - Gas phases: cold `T<10^4 K`, cool `10^4<=T<10^5 K`, warm `10^5<=T<10^6 K`, and hot `T>=10^6 K`.
    - Apertures: `0.03R_200`, `0.15R_200`, and `R_200`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure partial correlations between black-hole mass and gas mass.
    - Control for stellar mass and dark-matter halo mass.
    - Compare trends across environments and galaxy classes.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Black-hole mass has a significant negative partial correlation with cold gas within `R_200` and `0.15R_200`.
    - The cold-gas correlation reaches `rho~-0.37`.
    - Warm and hot gas show no comparable intrinsic correlation.
    - Over-massive black holes are associated with systematically reduced cold gas.
    - The trend persists across environments and varies with star-forming, green-valley, and quenched classifications.
  - **Position:** useful CGM/feedback diagnostic because it separates the black-hole signal from the stellar-mass and halo-mass correlations that often dominate galaxy-gas comparisons.

- [2607.16434](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16434) S. Satheesh-Sheeba et al. `VAR-PZnn: A machine-learning framework for AGN photometric redshifts using color and variability-based features`
  - **Question:** can single-band variability features improve AGN photometric redshifts when multi-band light curves are unavailable?
  - **Data:**
    - Training and test sample: `72,728` spectroscopically confirmed AGNs and QSOs.
    - Redshift and magnitude range: `0.01<z<4.5`, `17<g<21.5`.
    - Inputs: `26` ZTF g-band variability features, Pan-STARRS1 optical photometry, CatWISE MIR photometry, and UKIDSS NIR photometry for a subsample.
  - **Method:**
    - Train a fully connected mixture-density network.
    - Test ablations for color, MIR, NIR, and variability information.
    - Benchmark against Low-Resolution Templates SED fitting and the VAR-PZ framework.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The main sample reaches `sigma_NMAD=0.058` and outlier fraction `eta=8.2%`.
    - Removing the highest-uncertainty `10%` of sources lowers the outlier fraction to `5.4%`.
    - MIR photometry is the dominant accuracy driver; variability is a secondary refiner.
    - With UKIDSS as a Euclid/Roman proxy, the outlier fraction is `13.3%` without MIR and `4.6%` with MIR.
    - Low-Resolution Templates have `eta=28.7%`; applying single-band VAR-PZ priors worsens the result to `39.4%`.
  - **Position:** relevant for LSST/Roman-era AGN demographics because photo-z errors propagate directly into quasar environment and luminosity-function work.

- [2607.17752](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17752) Xiaotong Feng et al. `Far-Infrared Star Formation Rates of Quasar Host Galaxies from Multiwavelength Spectral Energy Distribution Decomposition`
  - **Question:** how robust are FIR star-formation rates for quasar hosts under different SED models and submillimeter coverage?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `202` SDSS and PG quasars at `0.02<z<~0.8`.
    - SFR range: `log(SFR_FIR/M_sun yr^-1)~-0.45` to `2.76`.
    - Photometry: optical through FIR plus JCMT/SCUBA-2 `450 um` and `850 um` for `58` quasars.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit multiwavelength SEDs with CIGALE and AGNfitter.
    - Test multiple cold-dust templates.
    - Quantify the effect of adding SCUBA-2 measurements.
    - Compare FIR SFRs with main-sequence offsets and accretion proxies.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The median model-dependent scatter among five FIR-SFR estimates is `0.14 dex`.
    - AGNfitter gives FIR SFRs lower than the mean CIGALE estimate by median `0.09 dex`.
    - Adding SCUBA-2 changes the adopted FIR SFR by only `~0.01 dex` on average but matters for individual sources with limited Herschel coverage or radio-loud emission.
    - Main-sequence offset is not clearly correlated with direct Eddington ratio.
    - Main-sequence offset is positively related to an IR-based `L_tor/L_Edd` proxy.
  - **Position:** useful calibration paper because quasar-host SFR systematics affect black-hole-galaxy coevolution claims and AGN environment comparisons.

- [2607.17788](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17788) Xiaotong Feng et al. `Testing [O II] lambda3727 as a Star Formation Rate Tracer in Quasar Host Galaxies`
  - **Question:** can [O II] remain a statistical star-formation-rate tracer in type I quasar hosts after AGN contamination and dust/metallicity effects are handled?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: the same `202` SDSS and PG quasars used for FIR SFR calibration.
    - Lines: [O II], [O III], and narrow Balmer emission from spectral fitting.
    - Reference: FIR SFRs from multiwavelength SED decomposition.
  - **Method:**
    - Correct [O II] luminosities for dust extinction and metallicity.
    - Estimate and subtract AGN narrow-line contribution using [O III].
    - Compare [O II] SFRs with FIR SFRs for the full and high-S/N samples.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - After AGN subtraction, the median [O II]-FIR offset is `-0.20+/-0.72 dex` for the full sample.
    - For sources with S/N `>5` in both [O II] and [O III], the offset is `-0.17+/-0.69 dex`.
    - Without AGN subtraction, the corresponding offsets are `0.00+/-0.69 dex` and `0.12+/-0.66 dex`.
    - [O II] is useful statistically, but individual sources still need checks for AGN contamination, extinction, metallicity, aperture effects, and redshift systematics.
  - **Position:** practical companion to the FIR-SFR paper because line-based SFRs are common in quasar samples where FIR constraints are sparse.

- [2607.16423](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16423) L. Hernandez-Garcia et al. `Simultaneous radio, optical and X-ray monitoring of hard X-ray selected AGN: a variability study`
  - **Question:** how tightly are radio, optical, and X-ray AGN variations coupled in hard-X-ray-selected systems?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `14` hard-X-ray-selected AGN from the INTEGRAL/IBIS catalog.
    - Monitoring: AMI-LA `15 GHz`, ZTF g/r, and Swift/XRT over 2018-2020.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure normalized excess variance and fractional variability amplitudes.
    - Apply Mexican Hat filtering at `70` and `200 day` timescales.
    - Compare time-averaged and strictly simultaneous positions on the Fundamental Plane of black-hole activity.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Significant variability is detected in `86%` of the sample.
    - Median fractional rms amplitudes are `30%` in X-rays, `19%` in optical g, `8.5%` in optical r, and `10%` in radio.
    - Stochastic variability contributes only `~3%` of the total Fundamental Plane scatter.
    - The `15 GHz` emission is consistent with a core-dominated compact jet base or magnetized corona.
  - **Position:** useful AGN-physics cross-list because simultaneous variability calibrates how accretion, coronae, and compact jets enter black-hole scaling relations.

## Environment, H I, and Low-Surface-Brightness Structure

- [2607.16417](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16417) Anthony M. Englert et al. `LoVoCCS. III. Third Generation Pipeline & The Hercules Supercluster`
  - **Question:** what data quality and mass-mapping performance does the latest LoVoCCS pipeline deliver for nearby cluster complexes?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: Local Volume Complete Cluster Survey of more than `100` southern X-ray-luminous clusters.
    - Redshift and luminosity range: `0.03<z<0.12`, `L_500>10^44 erg s^-1`.
    - Test field: `~16 deg^2` around Abell 2147, 2151, 2152, and related Hercules Supercluster structures.
  - **Method:**
    - Reduce observations with the third-generation LSST Science Pipelines.
    - Validate data products using recent Hercules Supercluster imaging.
    - Conduct multi-plane weak-lensing analysis of the complex.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - LoVoCCS observations reach Rubin LSST Year 1-2 depth with seeing `<=1 arcsec`.
    - The dynamical mass of Abell 2147 is biased by its disturbed state.
    - Abell 2147 is consistent with being `~0.2-0.4 Gyr` out of periapsis.
    - The estimated total supercluster mass is `8.9(+1.7,-1.4)x10^14 M_sun`.
  - **Position:** useful LSS infrastructure because deep homogeneous low-z cluster imaging and weak-lensing products provide calibration baselines for environment analyses.

- [2607.16467](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16467) R. Ianjamasimanana et al. `The size of the HI disk across different environments: isolated, compact groups, clusters, and pairs`
  - **Question:** how does H I disk size respond to compact-group and cluster-like environments after removing optical-size bias?
  - **Data:**
    - Samples: Hickson Compact Groups and AMIGA isolated galaxies.
    - Observable: H I diameter at the `1 M_sun pc^-2` iso-density contour.
    - Comparison sets: loose groups, compact groups, cluster infall/field environments, and Virgo.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit H I diameters directly with ellipses.
    - Establish an isolated-galaxy `D_HI-D_25` baseline from AMIGA using Bayesian analysis.
    - Quantify truncation as a residual from the baseline instead of using the biased `D_HI/D_25` ratio.
    - Include H I nondetections as upper limits.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - HCG galaxies lie below the isolated-galaxy baseline in the `D_HI-D_25` plane.
    - Including nondetections, HCG H I disks are at least `~71%` smaller than expected at fixed optical diameter.
    - Truncation increases monotonically from HCG Phase 1 to Phase 3.
    - HCGs and Virgo occupy the most strongly truncated end of the environmental comparison.
  - **Position:** relevant gas-environment paper because it gives a cleaner H I truncation metric for linking environment, stripping, and gas supply.

- [2607.17792](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17792) G. Martin et al. `Intracluster light and dark matter halo shapes reflect common assembly, not mutual coupling: shape correspondence in CDM but not SIDM`
  - **Question:** does intracluster light trace dark-matter halo shape because of direct coupling or shared assembly history?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: matched CDM and SIDM hydrodynamical clusters from TheThreeHundred project.
    - SIDM cases: `sigma_DM/m=0.1` and `0.5 cm^2 g^-1`.
    - Observables: 3D axis ratios and major-axis orientations for dark matter and stellar BCG+ICL distributions.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure radial shape profiles and orientations.
    - Quantify ICL-DM correspondence with the Weighted Overlap Coefficient.
    - Test robustness across baryonic models and resolution levels.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - In SIDM, dark-matter halos become rounder in their inner regions as cross-section increases.
    - The stellar BCG+ICL distribution keeps the same elongated morphology in CDM and SIDM.
    - The decoupling is detected at `>5 sigma` for `sigma_DM/m=0.5 cm^2 g^-1`.
    - ICL-DM shape correspondence in CDM reflects shared accretion geometry rather than unavoidable mutual coupling.
  - **Position:** useful for cluster-environment work because it clarifies when ICL morphology can and cannot be treated as a dark-matter shape proxy.

- [2607.16664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16664) Kyle R. Adams et al. `Tidal Structures Around Edge-On Galaxies in Deep Imaging Surveys`
  - **Question:** how common are low-surface-brightness tidal structures around edge-on disk galaxies in large imaging surveys?
  - **Data:**
    - Main sample: `5606` EGIS edge-on galaxies with DESI Legacy Imaging, HSC-SSP support, and selected APO follow-up.
    - Validation sample: `14,237` EGIPS galaxies from Pan-STARRS.
    - Typical depth: DESI r-band surface brightness `28.6 mag arcsec^-2`.
  - **Method:**
    - Process images homogeneously for faint diffuse structure detection.
    - Visually classify tidal structures into established morphological categories.
    - Control for imaging artifacts and Galactic cirrus contamination.
    - Compare completeness-limited incidence rates with observations and simulations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Tidal features are detected in `324` EGIS galaxies and `690` EGIPS galaxies.
    - Raw incidence rates are `5.8%` and `4.8%`.
    - Completeness-limited rates are `6.4%` and `6.2%`.
    - The measured rates match previous observational studies but sit below many cosmological simulation predictions.
    - Recent high-resolution simulations move closer to the observed incidence rates.
  - **Position:** useful low-z morphology baseline because LSB feature incidence constrains merger histories and the observational forward-modeling needed for galaxy-assembly comparisons.

- [2607.16700](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16700) Aditya Manuwal et al. `Asymmetries in spatially unresolved 21-cm emission line profiles of isolated galaxies`
  - **Question:** what drives unresolved H I profile asymmetry in isolated galaxies when external and secular channels are hard to separate?
  - **Data:**
    - Samples: UNAM-KIAS and AMIGA isolated galaxies.
    - H I sources: H I-MaNGA, NIBLES, KLUN, ALFALFA, and xGASS single-dish spectra.
    - Ancillary quantities: galaxy and halo properties released with the H I measurements.
  - **Method:**
    - Compile isolated systems with near-complete beam coverage.
    - Measure robust unresolved H I line asymmetry.
    - Compare sSFR, H I mass, bar frequency, merger fractions, and stellar-mass dependence.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The isolated galaxies are late-type star-forming centrals with lower sSFR and higher H I content than expected.
    - Higher H I content is linked to lower star-formation efficiency, weaker outflows, and stronger gas accretion below `M_star~10^10.3 M_sun`.
    - AMIGA has systematically higher H I masses than UNAM-KIAS.
    - Bar frequencies resemble normal spirals, supporting an internal origin for many bars.
    - Asymmetric galaxies below `M_star~10^10.3 M_sun` have higher sSFRs, consistent with EAGLE central-galaxy predictions.
  - **Position:** relevant as a control-sample gas paper because isolated H I asymmetries set a baseline for interpreting environment-driven asymmetry and accretion signatures.
