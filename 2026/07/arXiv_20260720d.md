## Executive View

The read-first paper is [2607.15515](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15515) Hiroya Umeda, which uses `405` JWST/NIRSpec spectra at `z=4.5-10.1` to push metallicity and nitrogen-abundance work into the low-mass, lensed-galaxy regime. The sample reaches `M_star~10^5.7 M_sun`, derives a `z~6` mass-metallicity relation down to `M_star~10^6.6 M_sun`, and flags a localized highly ionized N-rich component that may connect early galaxies to globular-cluster-scale enrichment.

Cosmic-dawn modeling is the other strong axis. [2607.15344](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15344) Sergio Martin-Alvarez and [2607.15357](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15357) Michelle S. Park use the Azahar simulations to argue that radiative transfer, cosmic rays, and MHD can produce bursty, compact, self-regulated galaxies from `z~14` to `z~3`. These entries matter because they address JWST-era bright-galaxy tensions with explicit non-thermal feedback physics rather than only retuning supernova feedback.

The day also has useful infrastructure for the profile. [2607.15971](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15971) Sai Zhai links LOFAR radio AGN to HETDEX Ly-alpha emitters at `1.88<z<3.52`. [2607.15595](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15595) Wenkai Hu extends the FAST blind H I absorber sample and searches for OH absorption. [2607.15573](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15573) Paula S. Ferreira measures morphology-dependent galaxy bias with DES and DESI Legacy Imaging. [2607.15638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15638) Cameron Lawlor-Forsyth tests whether CASTOR and Roman-like imaging can identify quenching with spatially resolved star-formation histories.

## High-Redshift Galaxies, Metals, and Cosmic-Dawn Physics

- [2607.15515](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15515) Hiroya Umeda et al. `Gas-Phase Metallicity and Nitrogen Abundances in Low-Mass Galaxies Down to M_star~10^5.7 M_sun at z~4.5-10.1 from JWST Lensing Cluster Surveys`
  - **Question:** how chemically enriched are intrinsically faint, low-mass galaxies during the first billion years?
  - **Data:**
    - Spectroscopy: `405` deep JWST/NIRSpec spectra from DREAMS and other lensing-cluster surveys.
    - Redshift and luminosity: galaxies at `z=4.5-10.1` with `M_UV~-12` to `-22`.
    - Stellar mass: sample reaches `M_star~10^5.7 M_sun`; half of the `M_UV>-17.5` sources have magnification `mu>3`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure rest-frame UV and optical emission lines in individual spectra and stellar-mass-binned stacks.
    - Derive gas-phase metallicity, nitrogen abundance, and carbon abundance.
    - Build empirical strong-line metallicity calibrations from direct-method stacked measurements.
    - Compare UV and optical nitrogen diagnostics in the same mass bins.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The `z~6` mass-metallicity relation is traced down to `M_star~10^6.6 M_sun`.
    - The relation reaches `12+log(O/H)~7.2` at the low-mass end.
    - The low-mass slope is slightly steeper than the local relation and broadly agrees with hydrodynamical simulations.
    - In the `M_star~10^7.7 M_sun` stack, N/O from N IV] exceeds N/O from [N II] by `~1.4 dex`.
    - The He II 4686/Hbeta ratio is `~0.03`, above BPASS predictions, making WR stars a plausible source of prompt nitrogen and hard ionizing photons.
  - **Position:** read first because it gives low-mass JWST metallicity constraints directly relevant to early enrichment, faint galaxies, and abundance-ratio anomalies.

- [2607.15344](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15344) Sergio Martin-Alvarez et al. `The Azahar Project: Non-Thermal Physics Drives Star Formation Burstiness and the Evolution of the UV Luminosity Density at Cosmic Dawn`
  - **Question:** can non-thermal feedback physics resolve JWST-era tensions in the first galaxy populations?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation suite: Azahar, with `10` high-resolution cosmological zoom simulations.
    - Resolution and volume: `~20 pc` resolution while following thousands of galaxies.
    - Physics variants: standard baryonic physics plus progressive radiative transfer, cosmic rays, and MHD.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare standard, radiative-transfer, cosmic-ray, and combined non-thermal physics models.
    - Track UV luminosity functions, stellar mass functions, the galaxy main sequence, gas metallicities, outflow loading, and star-formation variability.
    - Evaluate the same models from `z~14` to `z~3`.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The Non-Thermal model reproduces observed UV luminosity functions from `z~14` to `z~3`.
    - It also matches stellar mass functions, the main sequence, and observed gas metallicities.
    - Star formation becomes burstier on both `>~50 Myr` and `<~10 Myr` timescales.
    - Outflow mass-loading factors transition from high to low as cosmic-ray pressure builds in the ISM.
    - Boosted thermal supernova feedback either overproduces stellar mass or drives overly explosive outflows.
  - **Position:** important theory entry because it gives a physically specific route from feedback microphysics to JWST cosmic-dawn demographics.

- [2607.15357](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15357) Michelle S. Park et al. `Non-Thermal Physics Drives Compact, Self-Regulated Galaxy Morphologies at Cosmic Dawn`
  - **Question:** what galaxy sizes and surface-brightness distributions emerge when cosmic-dawn simulations include full non-thermal physics?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: Azahar suite with synthetic JWST observations.
    - Redshift range: `z=12` to `z=3`.
    - Mass regime: includes predictions for `M_star<10^8 M_sun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Generate synthetic JWST views of several physics models.
    - Compare compactness, surface-brightness distributions, and bursty star formation.
    - Contrast Full-Physics, standard hydrodynamics, and calibrated supernova-feedback models.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Full-Physics models produce compact, self-regulated galaxies consistent with current observations.
    - The bright end is dominated by compact sizes and bursty star formation.
    - This compact starburst scenario naturally explains bright `z>10` detections in flux-limited surveys.
    - Standard hydrodynamics gives systems that are too small and too concentrated.
    - Calibrated supernova feedback produces systems almost twice as large as observed.
  - **Position:** companion to the Azahar UV-luminosity paper and directly useful for interpreting JWST size, compactness, and selection effects.

- [2607.15638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15638) Cameron Lawlor-Forsyth et al. `Identifying and Distinguishing Quenching Galaxies with Spatially Resolved Star Formation in Mock CASTOR and NGRST Observations`
  - **Question:** can future wide-field space imaging separate quenching galaxies by their spatially resolved star-formation histories?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: IllustrisTNG galaxies in quenching phases.
    - Synthetic observatories: CASTOR and Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope imaging.
    - Modeling tools: GALAXEV stellar populations and FAST++ annular SED fitting.
  - **Method:**
    - Generate dust- and noise-aware mock images.
    - Fit binned circular annuli with flexible star-formation histories.
    - Recover radial star-formation histories and compare quenching classes.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Mock CASTOR and Roman data can recover spatially resolved star-formation histories well enough to identify quenching behavior.
    - Radial information helps distinguish quenching systems from otherwise similar star-forming galaxies.
    - The method is survey-planning relevant because it tests quenching classifications on images rather than idealized intrinsic histories.
  - **Position:** relevant for Roman-era galaxy-evolution work because high-z quenching and morphology need scalable resolved-photometry diagnostics.

## Gas, Absorption, and AGN Diagnostics

- [2607.15971](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15971) Sai Zhai et al. `Radio-detected Lya emitters at 1.88 < z < 3.52: AGN fraction and Lya emission`
  - **Question:** how often do Ly-alpha emitters host radio AGN, and how often do radio sources show Ly-alpha emission at cosmic noon?
  - **Data:**
    - Surveys: HETDEX and LOFAR.
    - Matched sample: `928` sources detected in both surveys.
    - Redshift range: `1.88<z<3.52`.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify radio-emitting AGN among LAEs.
    - Measure the fraction of LAEs with radio AGN and the fraction of radio sources with Ly-alpha emission.
    - Use the joint sample to connect radio activity with Ly-alpha emission and extended Ly-alpha mechanisms.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The matched HETDEX-LOFAR sample provides a direct radio-AGN view of LAEs across the profile's cosmic-noon-to-high-z transition regime.
    - The paper links AGN incidence to Ly-alpha selection, which is central for interpreting LAE samples used as density and ionization tracers.
  - **Position:** read for AGN-LAE coupling because radio selection can expose active nuclei missed by optical-only Ly-alpha samples.

- [2607.15595](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15595) Wenkai Hu et al. `The FAST HI 21-cm Absorption Blind Survey. III. OH Absorption Search in the 21-cm Absorber Sample and Continued HI Absorption Search`
  - **Question:** can blind FAST surveys find OH absorption in H I 21-cm absorber systems?
  - **Data:**
    - Telescope: FAST.
    - Survey inputs: 2024 and part of 2025 CRAFTS and FASHI data.
    - Exposure and area: `394.4 hr` and `1622.1 deg^2`, plus FATHOMER observations.
    - Absorber sample: previous `34` H I systems extended to `41`.
  - **Method:**
    - Continue the blind H I absorption search.
    - Search for OH absorption in `19` H I absorbers where the redshifted OH frequency falls in the FAST band.
    - Apply survival analysis to upper limits in the N(OH)-N(H I) comparison.
    - Stack spectra for associated, intervening, and combined samples.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Three known and four new H I absorbers extend the FAST absorber sample to `41` systems.
    - The known OH absorber toward PKS 1413+135 is re-detected.
    - No new OH absorbers are identified.
    - The analysis finds no statistically significant N(OH)-N(H I) correlation or N(OH)/N(H I) redshift evolution.
    - Stacked `3 sigma` OH column-density upper limits are `4.93`, `1.64`, and `1.72 T_ex/c_f,OH x 10^12 cm^-2 K^-1` for associated, intervening, and combined samples.
  - **Position:** useful gas-survey paper because radio absorption gives a dust-insensitive view of cool gas reservoirs complementary to optical/UV absorption work.

- [2607.15564](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15564) Litao Zhu et al. `22 Newly Identified Repeating Changing-look AGNs and Evidence for Extreme Broad-line Region Breathing`
  - **Question:** do repeating changing-look AGNs reveal the physical thresholds for broad-line visibility?
  - **Data:**
    - Candidate search: known changing-look AGN samples with long-term multi-band light curves.
    - Follow-up target set: `34` candidates.
    - Confirmed systems: `25` repeating changing-look AGNs, including `22` new cases.
  - **Method:**
    - Select candidates from repeated photometric state changes.
    - Obtain spectroscopic follow-up to confirm repeating broad-line transitions.
    - Compare on/off spectral states, Eddington ratios, and photometric histories.
    - Separate Hbeta-only transitions from transitions involving both Halpha and Hbeta.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Second-transition rest-frame intervals are mostly `3-4 yr`.
    - Some optical changes imply transition timescales of several months.
    - On/off states track higher/lower Eddington ratios.
    - Hbeta-only transitions occur at higher Eddington ratios than Halpha+Hbeta transitions.
    - The results support BLR breathing across line-dependent visibility thresholds.
  - **Position:** relevant AGN-demographics entry because state changes can bias broad-line AGN selection and black-hole accretion census work.

- [2607.16104](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16104) Hendrik Mueller et al. `Dynamics and geometry of the inner sub-parsec-scale jet in 3C 279 observed with the Event Horizon Telescope`
  - **Question:** what sub-parsec jet geometry is resolved by EHT observations of the blazar 3C 279?
  - **Data:**
    - Observations: 2021 Event Horizon Telescope imaging of 3C 279.
    - Frequency context: morphology recurs from `22-230 GHz`.
    - Target scale: innermost jet region.
  - **Method:**
    - Reconstruct EHT images across epochs.
    - Fit geometric components to the compact core and inner jet.
    - Compare morphology with large-scale jet orientation and brightness-temperature constraints.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The compact core is elongated nearly orthogonal to the large-scale jet axis.
    - The morphology is recurrent across epochs and frequencies, so it is unlikely to be an imaging artifact.
    - Components show apparent speeds up to `10c`.
    - Bulk Lorentz factors must exceed `10.3`.
    - Viewing angles are constrained to smaller than `1 deg`.
  - **Position:** useful AGN-jet paper for nuclear geometry and beaming systematics, though less central than the high-z and absorber entries.

## Environment, Morphology, and Survey Infrastructure

- [2607.15573](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15573) Paula S. Ferreira et al. `Morphological Bias: How Ellipticals and Spirals Trace the Cosmic Web Differently`
  - **Question:** do elliptical and spiral galaxies trace the cosmic web with different scale-dependent bias?
  - **Data:**
    - Surveys: DES and DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys DR8.
    - Tracers: morphologically selected elliptical and spiral galaxies.
    - Observable: angular auto- and cross-power spectra.
  - **Method:**
    - Define a cross-tracer clustering ratio from auto- to cross-power spectra.
    - Measure relative clustering as a function of angular multipole.
    - Test covariance choices, contamination, luminosity splits, and N-body mock comparisons.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Ellipticals are more strongly clustered than spirals in both surveys.
    - DESI provides the cleanest constraints because redshift distributions are better matched.
    - Relative bias is close to unity at `ell<~50`.
    - The separation rises toward `ell~150-200`, reaching an average `2.6 sigma`.
    - Ellipticals are positively biased relative to matter, while spirals are consistent with weak bias or anti-bias.
  - **Position:** read for LSS work because morphology-dependent bias affects galaxy-environment inference and future imaging-survey HOD tests.

- [2607.15891](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15891) Erik Vinicius Rodrigues de Lima et al. `The S-PLUS Fifth Data-Release: Over 4500 square degrees of the Southern Sky and a multicolor view of the Hydra and Antlia galaxy clusters`
  - **Question:** what new photometric and value-added products does S-PLUS DR5 provide for southern-sky galaxy studies?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: S-PLUS with the T80-South telescope.
    - Area: `4592 deg^2` across `2491` fields.
    - Photometry: Javalambre 12-filter system, with `5` broad and `7` narrow bands.
    - Catalog scale: more than `113 million` detections.
  - **Method:**
    - Release FITS images and extensive flux, magnitude, and shape catalogs.
    - Provide value-added photometric redshift, classification, mask, and extinction products.
    - Add a multi-color view of the Hydra and Antlia galaxy clusters.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - DR5 substantially expands southern-sky multi-band imaging for low- and intermediate-redshift galaxy work.
    - The value-added products make the release useful for cluster membership, galaxy classification, and photometric-redshift selection.
    - The Hydra and Antlia focus makes it a practical environment dataset, even if not a primary high-z survey.
  - **Position:** useful survey-infrastructure entry because wide-field photometric products support environment baselines and target selection.

- [2607.16170](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16170) Joy Bhattacharyya et al. `Morphologies of SAGAbg low-mass galaxies in Legacy Survey multi-band imaging: dependence on stellar masses, star-formation rates and low-redshift evolution`
  - **Question:** how do low-mass galaxy morphologies depend on stellar mass, star formation, and low-redshift evolution?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `6211` low-mass star-forming galaxies from SAGAbg.
    - Mass and redshift: `7<~log(M_star/M_sun)<~10` and `z<0.1`.
    - Imaging: Legacy Surveys `griz` maps.
  - **Method:**
    - Apply STATMORPH to measure Gini, M20, and CAS morphology parameters.
    - Test how resolution and signal-to-noise affect morphology recovery.
    - Relate morphology to mass, star-formation rate, and redshift.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The sample gives a high-completeness low-redshift baseline for low-mass galaxy structure.
    - Bulge-strength measurements are sensitive to resolution and signal-to-noise.
    - The morphology catalog can anchor comparisons between dwarf-galaxy assembly and simulations.
  - **Position:** relevant as a local baseline for low-mass morphology and feedback, but lower priority than high-z JWST or LSS entries.

- [2607.15860](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15860) Takafumi Tsukui `Compressing radio interferometric visibility data into a probabilistic model using sparse Gaussian processes`
  - **Question:** can radio interferometric visibilities be compressed without discarding information needed for later imaging?
  - **Data:**
    - Test case: simulated ALMA observations.
    - Target facilities: SKA-Mid and other next-generation radio interferometers.
    - Data type: visibility measurements in uv space.
  - **Method:**
    - Model visibilities with a sparse Gaussian process.
    - Store inferred mean visibility and uncertainty rather than raw measurements.
    - Predict visibilities at arbitrary uv coordinates for later imaging.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Sparse-GP compression can recover images with high fidelity in the simulation tests.
    - Estimated compression factors for an 8-hour SKA-Mid observation are `10^3-10^5`.
    - The probabilistic model can boost signal-to-noise relative to independent grid averaging.
    - The approach may support incremental updates and outlier filtering.
  - **Position:** useful methods paper for ALMA/SKA-scale survey logistics and future high-z gas/dust data products.
