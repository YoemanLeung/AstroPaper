## Executive View

The Wednesday listing is unusually strong for the high-redshift galaxy and gas program. The top entry is [2606.31638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31638) Zihao Li, which reports a very metal-poor overdensity at `z=6.26` with a carbon-enhanced CGM absorber and positions it as a possible Population III enrichment signature. [2606.30787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30787) Kasper E. Heintz uses `25` JWST/NIRSpec Prism galaxies at `z_spec>=10` to connect burstiness, strong rest-UV lines, DLA absorption, and redshift biases. [2606.30802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30802) Mengyuan Xiao gives a spectroscopic census of the `200` most massive JWST-observed galaxies at `z_spec~3-15`.

Quasar environments and CGM work are also central. [2606.30742](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30742) Jelena Ritter links ALMA CO(4-3), MUSE Lyalpha nebulae, molecular gas, Eddington ratio, and companion counts around `37` `z~3` quasars. [2606.31917](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31917) Susanna Bisogni lays out how ELT/SHARP-VESPER could map `z>9` Lyalpha structures from `~150 pc` to `100 kpc`.

The LRD and early black-hole thread remains active. [2606.30711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30711) Rohan P. Naidu argues that LRD spectra may be produced by dense winds around intermediate-mass, super-Eddington engines, reducing typical engine masses to below `10^5 M_sun`. [2606.31515](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31515) G. P. Nikopoulos shows that metallicity assumptions can produce order-of-magnitude shifts in inferred LRD black-hole masses and accretion rates. [2606.30715](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30715) Urvi Thakurdesai searches CEERS NIRSpec 2D spectra for spatially offset high-ionization peaks in `3<z<8` galaxies.

Large-scale structure and environment have a useful COSMOS-Web entry. [2606.31978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31978) Louise Paquereau finds that quiescent galaxies remain more clustered than star-forming galaxies even after halo-mass matching, pointing to environmental quenching and conformity at `z<=2`. Several SHARP and SKA science-book papers are lower-priority as standalone science results, but they map future observational routes for cosmic-noon quenching, radio-jet feedback, high-z Lyalpha nebulae, and cold gas in AGN.

## Reionization-Era Galaxies, Gas, and Chemical Enrichment

- [2606.31638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31638) Zihao Li et al. `Possible chemical signatures of first-star enrichment in a very metal-poor galaxy overdensity near the end of reionization`
  - **Question:** can metal-poor CGM gas near reionization preserve a detectable Population III enrichment signature?
  - **Data:**
    - System: an overdensity at `z=6.26` traced by `11` galaxies within `600 kpc`.
    - Spectroscopy: a background quasar sightline probing CGM gas near the brightest member.
    - Absorber: chemically primitive gas at `0.022 Z_sun` with strong carbon enhancement.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify the galaxy overdensity and pair it with quasar absorption information.
    - Measure gas metallicity and abundance ratios from the absorber.
    - Compare the chemical pattern with enrichment models for first-star and later stellar populations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The absorber has a low metallicity of `0.022 Z_sun`.
    - The abundance ratio is carbon enhanced, with `[C/O]=+0.57`.
    - The pattern is consistent with chemical enrichment dominated by Population III stars rather than ordinary later stellar populations.
    - The overdensity setting makes the system a high-priority target for linking early galaxies, CGM enrichment, and reionization topology.
  - **Position:** read first because it directly connects high-z overdensity mapping, quasar absorption, and possible first-star enrichment.

- [2606.30787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30787) Kasper E. Heintz et al. `JWST spectroscopy of galaxies at z>10: Damped Lyalpha absorbers reveal efficient star formation and hidden redshift biases`
  - **Question:** how do neutral gas reservoirs affect the interpretation of luminous JWST galaxies at `z>10`?
  - **Data:**
    - Instrument: JWST/NIRSpec Prism.
    - Sample: `25` galaxies with robust spectroscopic redshifts at `z_spec>=10`.
    - Diagnostics: rest-UV nebular lines, burstiness, DLA-derived HI columns, and redshift inference tests.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure burstiness as `SFR_10Myr/SFR_100Myr`.
    - Compare burstiness and UV-line strength against DLA-derived HI column density.
    - Quantify photometric and Lyalpha-break redshift biases when DLAs are not modeled.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Strong rest-UV lines correlate with burstiness.
    - HI column density does not strongly correlate with `M_UV`, burstiness, or rest-UV line prominence.
    - The most bursty systems require short depletion times, typically `t_dep<=20 Myr`.
    - Omitting DLA absorption biases photometric redshifts by an average `0.39` and Lyalpha-break redshifts by `0.14`.
  - **Position:** important JWST high-z calibration paper because DLA absorption can bias redshifts while bursty star formation drives the observed line strengths.

- [2606.30802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30802) Mengyuan Xiao et al. `A Census of the 200 Most Massive Galaxies Spectroscopically Observed with JWST at zspec ~3-15`
  - **Question:** what do the most massive spectroscopically confirmed JWST galaxies reveal about early assembly and quenching?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: the `200` most massive galaxies in public JWST prism observations at `z_spec~3-15`.
    - Selection: evolving stellar-mass threshold anchored at `log(Mstar)>10` at `z~5`.
    - Cleaning: LRDs and broad-line AGN contaminants removed from the massive-galaxy sample.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit spectroscopy and photometry jointly to derive physical properties.
    - Classify normal star-forming, dusty star-forming, and quiescent systems.
    - Test UVJ and `(ugi)s` selections against spectroscopic classifications.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Normal star-forming galaxies dominate above `z~6`.
    - Dusty star-forming galaxies and quiescent galaxies become more common toward lower redshift.
    - The census identifies `29` massive quiescent galaxies, including recently quenched systems with star formation falling within `~100 Myr`.
    - Standard UVJ and prior `(ugi)s` selections are inconsistent for the most massive galaxies at `z>3`.
  - **Position:** useful baseline for massive-galaxy work because it moves early quiescence and dust-enriched pathways onto a spectroscopic footing.

- [2606.30750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30750) Patricia Iglesias-Navarro et al. `A Pixel-by-Pixel Path to Population III Discovery with JWST`
  - **Question:** can spatially resolved JWST imaging recover Population III clumps that integrated photometry misses?
  - **Data:**
    - Models: Yggdrasil primordial stellar-population models.
    - Mock observations: JWST/JADES-like filter coverage and noise.
    - Scenarios: Pop III clumps embedded in or projected near enriched Pop I/II host galaxies.
  - **Method:**
    - Forward-model Pop III and Pop I/II configurations under several IMF, nebular, and Lyalpha-transmission assumptions.
    - Use simulation-based inference for model comparison.
    - Compare integrated-source recovery against pixel-level resolved recovery.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Integrated analyses are strongly limited by host-galaxy contamination.
    - Pixel-level model comparison improves detectability for spatially offset Pop III clumps.
    - Favorable configurations reach `~90%` recovery for young massive clumps in nearly quenched hosts at larger projected separations.
    - A compact blue companion in a literature candidate is preferentially described by Pop III-like models, while the host is better fit by Pop I/II models.
  - **Position:** methodologically useful because JWST Pop III searches need resolved contamination control before spectroscopy can be decisive.

## Quasar Environments, CGM, and Early Black Holes

- [2606.30742](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30742) Jelena Ritter et al. `ALMA visits the QSO MUSEUM: connecting molecular gas and the cool circumgalactic medium around 37 z~3 quasars`
  - **Question:** how are molecular-gas reservoirs in `z~3` quasar hosts connected to extended Lyalpha nebulae and environments?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `37` quasars at `z~3` from QSO MUSEUM.
    - Instruments: ALMA CO(4-3) and existing VLT/MUSE Lyalpha maps.
    - Companion search: CO-emitting galaxies in quasar fields.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure CO(4-3) detections, molecular gas masses, and gas fractions.
    - Compare gas reservoirs with Lyalpha nebula brightness and quasar Eddington ratio.
    - Search for high-fidelity CO companions and derive quasar-galaxy clustering.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - CO(4-3) is detected in `21/37` quasars.
    - Molecular gas masses span `3-40 x 10^9 M_sun`.
    - Massive molecular reservoirs are associated with centrally dim Lyalpha nebulae, while centrally bright Lyalpha nebulae are generally CO-undetected.
    - Strongly accreting quasars with `lambda_Edd~0.9` likely deplete gas through quasar-driven outflows.
    - The fields contain `14` high-fidelity companions and a quasar-galaxy cross-correlation length of `9.81 h^-1 cMpc`.
  - **Position:** read first for quasar-environment work because it connects host molecular gas, cool CGM emission, accretion state, and overdensity in one sample.

- [2606.30711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30711) Rohan P. Naidu et al. `Little Red Dots as Intermediate Mass, Super-Eddington Engines: Insights from Type IIn Supernovae and The 1837-1856 Great Eruption of eta Carinae`
  - **Question:** can the unusual spectra of Little Red Dots be explained by dense winds rather than standard overmassive black-hole broad-line regions?
  - **Data:**
    - Population: JWST Little Red Dots.
    - Analogs: Type IIn supernovae and the Great Eruption of eta Carinae.
    - Observables: blackbody-like continua, broad wings, P-Cygni cores, low variability, and low photospheric surface gravity.
  - **Method:**
    - Interpret LRD continua and line profiles through outflows trapped by dense circumsource gas.
    - Use an escape-velocity argument to constrain central-engine mass.
    - Compare wind photospheres and shock-powered emission with stellar-transient analogs.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Wind-like physics can mimic a virial broad-line region.
    - Typical engine masses can be constrained to `M<10^5 M_sun`.
    - The systems may host intermediate-mass engines of `10^3-10^6 M_sun`.
    - Their luminosities require super-Eddington operation, with `L_bol/L_Edd>=5`.
  - **Position:** important LRD interpretation paper because it changes the inferred black-hole mass scale and accretion mode.

- [2606.31515](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31515) G. P. Nikopoulos et al. `Why Does Metallicity Matter in Little Red Dots?`
  - **Question:** how much do metallicity assumptions change the physical interpretation of LRD spectra?
  - **Data:**
    - Models: emission-line spectra of LRDs under different metallicity assumptions.
    - Key cases: solar and `3x` solar metallicity.
    - Derived quantities: black-hole mass, accretion rate, and ionization parameter.
  - **Method:**
    - Refit LRD emission-line spectra with metallicity treated as a variable.
    - Track how inferred black-hole and gas parameters shift.
    - Test whether high-metallicity solutions ease tensions in LRD interpretation.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Increasing metallicity from solar to `3 Z_sun` reduces inferred black-hole mass by `~1 dex`.
    - The inferred accretion rate drops by `~2 dex`.
    - The ionization parameter also shifts strongly.
    - Metal-rich gas can make intermediate-mass, highly accreting black holes more viable for LRDs.
  - **Position:** read with Naidu because both papers show that LRD masses are model-dependent, not direct virial facts.

- [2606.30715](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30715) Urvi Thakurdesai et al. `Hunting Wandering 3<z<8 Black Holes: via Spatial Offsets in Ionization Ratio and Continuum Emission`
  - **Question:** can spatial offsets between high-ionization gas and stellar continuum identify wandering black holes at high redshift?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: JWST/NIRSpec PRISM spectroscopy from CEERS.
    - Sample: `90` galaxies at `3<z<8`, including two known broad-line AGN.
    - Lines: Halpha, Hbeta, [OIII]5007, [NeIII]3868, and [OII]3727,3729.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure spatial flux-ratio profiles in 2D spectra.
    - Use [OIII]/Hbeta as the main high-ionization diagnostic.
    - Compare the peak ionization-ratio position with the stellar-continuum center.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - `26` galaxies, about `30%` of the sample, show localized [OIII]/Hbeta peaks.
    - `12/26`, or about `46%`, have significant offsets between the ionization-ratio peak and the stellar center.
    - Six sources show the largest offsets, above `1.5` pixels.
    - Spatially offset ionization can probe early SMBH growth, but follow-up is needed to separate AGN from stellar and shock excitation.
  - **Position:** useful observational test because it turns NIRSpec spatial information into an early black-hole search axis.

- [2606.30734](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30734) Jarrett Johnson et al. `The Lifetimes of High-redshift Quasars Suggest Magnetic Disk Support`
  - **Question:** do inferred high-redshift quasar lifetimes require magnetic support in AGN disks?
  - **Data:**
    - Observational anchor: quasar lifetimes inferred from photoionized proximity zones.
    - Theory: maximum sustained accretion timescales with and without magnetic support.
    - Regime: luminous high-redshift quasars and rapid SMBH growth.
  - **Method:**
    - Derive accretion-maintenance timescales for gas-pressure and magnetic-pressure disk support.
    - Compare model timescales with proximity-zone lifetime constraints.
    - Interpret the absence of very long inferred lifetimes.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Some short inferred lifetimes are consistent with pure gas pressure.
    - Lifetimes above `10^4 yr` likely require extra magnetic support.
    - Magnetic pressure can be up to `100x` the gas pressure in the longest-lived systems.
    - The lack of lifetimes definitively above `10^6 yr` is consistent with gas plus advected magnetic fields setting the support budget.
  - **Position:** relevant for early SMBH growth because disk microphysics affects how long luminous accretion can be sustained.

## Environment, Quenching, and Survey Infrastructure

- [2606.31978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31978) Louise Paquereau et al. `COSMOS-Web: does halo mass alone shape the clustering of star-forming and quiescent galaxies?`
  - **Question:** after controlling for halo mass, do environment and secondary halo properties still affect galaxy star-formation activity?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: COSMOS-Web.
    - Redshift range: from `z=5` to the present day.
    - Populations: star-forming and quiescent galaxies with auto- and cross-correlation measurements.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure clustering for star-forming and quiescent samples.
    - Match halo-mass distributions using UniverseMachine.
    - Test one-halo conformity and low-mass environmental quenching signals.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Quiescent galaxies remain more clustered than star-forming galaxies by at least `0.5-1 dex` at all redshifts after halo-mass matching.
    - At `z<=2`, the excess is strongest for low-mass quiescent galaxies with `log(M_*/M_sun)<=9.5`.
    - Cross-correlations show one-halo conformity up to `z~2`.
    - Environmental quenching and conformity appear to vanish between `z~5` and `z~2`.
  - **Position:** high-value COSMOS-Web result because it challenges halo-mass-only interpretations of galaxy clustering.

- [2606.31549](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31549) Shihong Liu et al. `Electron Densities of Typical Low-Mass Galaxies at z~2-7 from Stacked JWST/NIRSpec Spectra`
  - **Question:** how does the average ISM electron density of low-mass galaxies evolve from `z~2` to `z~7`?
  - **Data:**
    - Archive: public JWST/NIRSpec medium-resolution spectra from the DAWN JWST Archive.
    - Sample: low-mass galaxies at `2<z<7`.
    - Diagnostic: stacked [SII] density-sensitive doublets.
  - **Method:**
    - Stack spectra to recover average [SII] doublet ratios below individual-detection limits.
    - Fit electron densities in redshift bins.
    - Compare stacked results with individually measurable high-S/N galaxies.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Stacks give `n_e~100-150 cm^-3` at `2<z<5`.
    - At `5<z<7`, the density rises to `381^{+104}_{-89} cm^-3`.
    - The redshift evolution follows `alpha=1.88^{+0.60}_{-0.64}` in the adopted power-law form.
    - Individually measurable [SII] sources have higher normalization, showing that individual detections select a denser subset.
  - **Position:** useful calibration result because stacked JWST spectra recover typical ISM conditions rather than only the densest emitters.

- [2606.31671](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31671) M. Polletta et al. `Tracing Galaxy Transformations in Cosmic Web Nodes with SHARP`
  - **Question:** what ELT/SHARP observations are needed to resolve galaxy transformations inside dense cosmic-web nodes?
  - **Data:**
    - Targets: five clusters at `1.5<z<1.7` and five protoclusters at `2<z<2.5`.
    - Planned sample: about `60` massive core members.
    - Facility: SHARP/VESPER with `80 hr` of observing time.
  - **Method:**
    - Target galaxies in the densest cluster and protocluster regions.
    - Map gas and stellar kinematics, star formation, companions, inflows, outflows, shocks, and AGN activity.
    - Compare cluster and protocluster phases from maximal growth to rapid decline.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - VESPER can deliver the spatially resolved diagnostics needed for environmental transformation studies.
    - The proposed sample spans the transition from protoclusters to virialized clusters.
    - The program is designed to identify which environmental processes drive rapid galaxy changes in cosmic-web nodes.
  - **Position:** infrastructure-heavy but relevant because it defines a future route for high-z environment physics at subgalactic resolution.

- [2606.31673](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31673) M. Polletta et al. `AGN Feedback: The impact of galactic-scale radio jets on the interstellar medium in starbursting obscured AGN`
  - **Question:** can SHARP observe radio-jet feedback at the onset of quenching in dusty cosmic-noon AGN hosts?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `11` luminous obscured AGN at `1.5<z<2.5`.
    - Radio structure: resolved jets on `~1-15 kpc` scales.
    - Facility plan: SHARP/VESPER multi-IFU observations.
  - **Method:**
    - Map stellar ages, star-formation-rate density, gas density, ionization state, and kinematics.
    - Compare resolved gas and stellar properties with radio structures.
    - Use multiplexing to characterize at least `110` galaxies in the environments.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The selected systems probe a short-lived phase below `10^5 yr`.
    - Recent starbursts, newly triggered radio jets, and embedded AGN coexist in the same objects.
    - `55 hr` of integration can provide resolved continuum and emission-line maps for the AGN and their environments.
  - **Position:** useful planning paper because it targets the phase where jet feedback, dusty star formation, and quenching are coeval.
