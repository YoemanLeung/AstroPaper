## Executive View

The strongest high-redshift entry is [2607.11182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11182) Ronaldo Laishram, which reports a compact six-member proto-group at `z=4.97` in MACS0416 with spectroscopic Halpha confirmation, a projected diameter of `~16 pkpc`, total stellar mass `log(M*/Msun)=10.07`, and a projected mass estimate near `log(M_PM/Msun)=12.30`. It is directly relevant to early brightest-group or brightest-cluster galaxy assembly. [2607.11448](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11448) J. Nadolny uses JWST/NIRCam in the Spiderweb protocluster at `z=2.16` to connect mass-size evolution, bulge-disc structure, and a density-driven passive fraction. [2607.11049](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11049) Zhiyu Yan combines JWST and SMA for a strongly lensed DSFG at `z=2.236`, with magnifications up to `mu~40` and an elevated SFR of `103 Msun/yr`.

Cosmic star-formation and survey modeling are also strong. [2607.09848](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09848) Sahil Hegde reconstructs the star-forming main sequence from mass functions across `z=0.1-9`, warning that common concordance relations can misrepresent uncertainties. [2607.10419](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10419) Georgios Zacharegkas introduces Diffhalos, a JAX-based generative lightcone model for halos, subhalos, and mass-assembly histories. [2607.08453](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08453) Jeongin Moon turns HETDEX PDR1 [OII] galaxies into dense low-redshift clustering samples over `540 deg^2`.

The CGM/AGN side is broad rather than dominated by one paper. [2607.11865](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11865) Carlos J. Vargas outlines Ardua, a UV plus X-ray mission concept for emission mapping of the multiphase CGM. [2607.09853](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09853) Emma Jane Weller uses ASTRID to decompose black-hole occupation into central and wandering components from `z=5` to `z=0`. [2607.10359](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10359) Huibo Fei finds that MAD accretion can account for jet powers in `289` low-accretion FR I radio galaxies selected from LoTSS DR1.

## High-Redshift Galaxies and Protoclusters

- [2607.11182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11182) Ronaldo Laishram et al. `A Compact Proto-group at z~5: A Massive Galaxy Caught in Formation`
  - **Question:** what does a compact spectroscopically confirmed galaxy group look like during early massive-galaxy assembly?
  - **Data:**
    - Field: MACS0416, using the SAPPHIRES Early Data Release.
    - Members: six Halpha-confirmed galaxies at `4.96<=z_spec<=4.98`.
    - Scale: projected diameter `~16 pkpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify compact candidate members in the SAPPHIRES release.
    - Confirm membership through Halpha spectroscopy.
    - Fit SEDs and perform pixel-by-pixel radial star-formation analysis.
    - Compare with structurally similar groups in EAGLE.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The group has total stellar mass `log(M*/Msun)=10.07+/-0.04`.
    - Three of six members lie on or above the `z~5` star-forming main sequence by up to `0.5 dex`.
    - The velocity dispersion is `375(+55/-195) km/s`.
    - The projected mass estimator gives `log(M_PM/Msun)~12.30(+0.30/-0.25)`.
    - EAGLE analogs predict coalescence by `z~3-4` and a remnant reaching `log(M*/Msun)>11` by `z~1`.
  - **Position:** read first for early group-scale assembly because it resolves a rare pre-coalescence phase rather than inferring one from integrated photometry.

- [2607.11448](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11448) J. Nadolny et al. `Mass-size evolution and the emerging passive-density relation revealed by JWST/NIRCam in the Spiderweb protocluster`
  - **Question:** how does the `z=2.16` Spiderweb protocluster environment affect galaxy structure and quenching?
  - **Data:**
    - Imaging: JWST/NIRCam F115W, F182M, and F410M.
    - Rest-frame coverage: `~3500 A` to `1.4 micron`.
    - Sample: `103` Spiderweb members within the JWST field, out to `~2 R200`.
  - **Method:**
    - Perform homogeneous multi-wavelength single-Sersic modeling.
    - Fit bulge-disc decompositions.
    - Compare mass-size relations, wavelength-dependent sizes, local density, and clustercentric distance.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Star-forming galaxies broadly follow the field mass-size relation.
    - Passive galaxies have a flatter mass-size relation and sizes between coeval field and cluster passive populations.
    - Passive fraction resembles the field at `Sigma<=100-200 gal/Mpc^2` and rises to `~60%` at `Sigma>=1000 gal/Mpc^2`.
    - Local density correlates weakly but significantly with Sersic index, strongest in F410M.
    - Quenching and structural transformation are advanced while size growth remains ongoing.
  - **Position:** high-value protocluster structure paper because it separates local-density quenching from clustercentric-radius trends.

- [2607.11049](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11049) Zhiyu Yan et al. `Gravitationally Lensed View of DSFG-1 in PLCK G165.7+67.0`
  - **Question:** what does resolved stellar and dust structure reveal about a strongly lensed Cosmic Noon dusty star-forming galaxy?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: PLCK G165.7+67.0 DSFG-1 at `z=2.236`.
    - Imaging: JWST/NIRCam.
    - Millimeter data: SMA continuum at `225 GHz` and `273 GHz`.
    - Lens geometry: image 1a with `mu~5` and image 1bc with `mu~40`.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine NIRCam photometry with SMA dust-continuum detections.
    - Fit integrated and spatially resolved stellar populations.
    - Compare morphology and size-mass placement with star-forming and compact systems.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - SMA flux densities are `(1.19+/-0.38) mJy` for image 1a and `(10.02+/-0.85) mJy` for image 1bc.
    - The lensing-corrected stellar mass is `(1.2+/-0.4)x10^10 Msun`.
    - The SFR is `(103+/-14) Msun/yr`, about `4x` above the main sequence.
    - Spatial variations in age and dust attenuation support a non-uniform star-formation history and a merger-like interpretation.
  - **Position:** useful Cosmic Noon analog because strong lensing turns a dusty starburst into a resolved stellar-population and dust-geometry test.

## Cosmic Star Formation, Structure, and Survey Modeling

- [2607.09848](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09848) Sahil Hegde et al. `Do we understand the star formation history of the universe?`
  - **Question:** what star-forming main sequence is required for stellar mass functions to build a self-consistent galaxy growth history?
  - **Data:**
    - Compilation: ground- and space-based star-forming and quiescent stellar mass functions.
    - Redshift range: `z=0.1-9`.
    - Stellar-mass range: `10^8<=M*/Msun<=10^11`.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit mass functions across redshift.
    - Trace galaxy growth histories through those mass functions.
    - Compare the inferred main sequence with spectroscopic SFR measurements, SED-fitting analyses, and theoretical models.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The inferred main sequence agrees with independent spectroscopic SFR measurements at `z~2-7`.
    - It is consistent with SED-fitting photometric samples at `z<~3`.
    - It differs from commonly used concordance relations.
    - Applications that use those compilations need explicit uncertainty treatment.
  - **Position:** important calibration paper because JWST-era high-z SFR and stellar-mass tensions depend on the assumed main-sequence prior.

- [2607.10419](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10419) Georgios Zacharegkas et al. `Diffhalos: A Generative Model of Cosmological Lightcones of Dark Matter Halos`
  - **Question:** can halo lightcones and mass-assembly histories be generated quickly enough for differentiable cosmological and galaxy-halo modeling?
  - **Data:**
    - Model inputs: halo mass functions, conditional subhalo mass functions, and merger-tree-trained normalizing flows.
    - Implementation: JAX-based Halox plus Diffhalos.
    - Target products: halos, subhalos, and mass assembly histories in lightcones.
  - **Method:**
    - Draw Monte Carlo halo samples from a lightcone halo model.
    - Generate subhalos from a conditional subhalo mass function.
    - Generate mass assembly histories using a normalizing flow trained on N-body merger trees.
    - Demonstrate gradients of halo and subhalo mass functions with respect to cosmological parameters.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Diffhalos approximates simulated lightcone populations statistically.
    - The framework can support galaxy-halo connection predictions and mock galaxy catalogs.
    - Differentiable lightcone generation opens a route to gradient-based survey inference.
  - **Position:** useful infrastructure for LSS and galaxy-formation forecasts where many mock catalogs and parameter gradients are needed.

- [2607.08453](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08453) Jeongin Moon et al. `HETDEX [OII] galaxies at z<=0.48`
  - **Question:** how well do volume-limited HETDEX [OII] galaxy samples trace low-redshift large-scale structure?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: HETDEX Public Data Release 1.
    - Area: `540 deg^2`.
    - Sample: half a million emission-line-selected [OII] galaxies at `z<=0.48`.
    - Volume-limited bins: `11,354` to `64,794` galaxies each.
  - **Method:**
    - Construct luminosity-binned volume-limited samples in the Spring and Fall fields.
    - Measure monopole and quadrupole power spectra.
    - Compare with Uchuu-based mock spectra in flat LambdaCDM.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Number densities are `~(2-5)x10^-3 h^3 Mpc^-3`, five to ten times higher than typical ELG spectroscopic surveys.
    - Power spectra agree with mocks over `0.01<k<0.7 h/Mpc`.
    - The characteristic halo mass is `log(M0/[h^-1 Msun])~11.9-12.3`.
    - The halo mass-luminosity slope is `a=0.37+/-0.10`.
    - About `13%` of the [OII] galaxies reside in subhalos in the best-fit mock.
  - **Position:** strong low-redshift LSS entry because dense [OII] tracers can support RSD, galaxy-halo, and cross-correlation work.

- [2607.09846](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09846) Hector Afonso G. Cruz et al. `The Rise and Fall of Acoustic Oscillations at Cosmic Dawn`
  - **Question:** how should 21-cm standard-ruler analyses separate BAO and velocity-induced acoustic oscillations during cosmic dawn?
  - **Data:**
    - Signal: cosmic-dawn 21-cm power spectrum.
    - Physics: matter overdensity and baryon-CDM streaming-velocity modulation of early star formation.
    - Forecast context: SKA sensitivity.
  - **Method:**
    - Decompose the 21-cm power spectrum into BAO and VAO acoustic components.
    - Track the relative prominence of the two features across cosmic dawn.
    - Forecast standard-ruler recovery with joint BAO-VAO modeling.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - BAO and VAO shapes have a percent-level offset.
    - Ignoring the offset would bias inferred `H(z)` by `~2%`.
    - The changing BAO-VAO balance is sensitive to early-galaxy and first-star physics.
    - Joint BAO-VAO modeling is needed for robust 21-cm acoustic inference.
  - **Position:** relevant cosmic-dawn theory entry because first-galaxy astrophysics enters directly into future standard-ruler cosmology.

## AGN, Black Holes, and Emission-Line Infrastructure

- [2607.10282](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10282) Minjin Kim et al. `A UV-to-Near-infrared QSO Composite Spectrum from the SPHEREx All-Sky Survey`
  - **Question:** what QSO spectral template does SPHEREx provide from UV through near-infrared wavelengths?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `~61,000` type 1 SDSS QSOs.
    - Median redshift: `z~1.26`.
    - SPHEREx coverage: `0.75-5.0 micron` in `102` near-infrared channels.
    - Rest-frame composite coverage: `0.14-4.5 micron`.
  - **Method:**
    - Construct a composite spectrum from SPHEREx spectrophotometry.
    - Fit UV/optical and near-infrared continuum power laws.
    - Test luminosity dependence, line ratios, equivalent widths, and host-galaxy contamination.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The UV/optical continuum has `alpha_nu=-0.10`.
    - The near-infrared continuum has spectral index `-1.46`.
    - More luminous QSOs have flatter UV/optical and steeper near-infrared continua.
    - IR-to-optical flux ratio decreases with AGN luminosity, consistent with a receding torus.
    - Host-galaxy contamination affects the composite shape.
  - **Position:** useful AGN template paper for survey photometry, QSO selection, and host-contamination corrections.

- [2607.09853](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09853) Emma Jane Weller et al. `The Black Hole Occupation Fraction as a Fossil Record of Seeding, Dynamics, and Galaxy Assembly`
  - **Question:** what physical information is hidden inside the black-hole occupation fraction when central and wandering black holes are separated?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: ASTRID cosmological hydrodynamical simulation.
    - Redshift range: `z=5` to `z=0`.
    - Stellar-mass range: `10^7-10^12 Msun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure total, central, and wandering black-hole occupation fractions.
    - Separate heavy-seed descendants from the full population.
    - Compare intrinsic occupation with active-BH-selected occupation.
    - Split trends by primary/satellite status and star-forming state.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Total occupation remains near unity across most stellar masses and redshifts.
    - Central occupation declines toward low redshift at low stellar mass.
    - Wandering occupation rises as mergers redistribute black holes away from centers.
    - Active-BH selections strongly undercount true late-time occupation.
    - At `z=0`, star-forming low-mass galaxies preferentially host wandering rather than central black holes.
  - **Position:** valuable seeding-context paper because it warns that AGN-selected occupation mostly traces duty cycle rather than intrinsic black-hole demographics.

- [2607.10359](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10359) Huibo Fei et al. `Magnetically Arrested Discs Powering Jets in a Large Sample of Low-Accretion FR I Radio Galaxies`
  - **Question:** what accretion state powers jets in low-accretion FR I radio galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `289` FR I radio galaxies from LoTSS DR1.
    - Ancillary data: SDSS DR17 optical photometry and spectroscopy.
    - Accretion range: `-6.84<log mdot<-0.87`, median `~-2.84`.
  - **Method:**
    - Select edge-darkened FR I radio morphologies.
    - Derive Eddington-scaled accretion rates.
    - Compare observed jet powers from `151 MHz` luminosity with Blandford-Znajek predictions.
    - Test standard ADAF and MAD interpretations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Most sources lie below `mdot=0.01`, in the ADAF regime.
    - Standard ADAF Blandford-Znajek power is too low for `~70%` of the sample even with spin `a=0.95`.
    - MAD accretion can account for the observed jet powers.
    - The data are consistent with slow black-hole spins, `a<0.5`, within the MAD framework.
  - **Position:** useful radio-AGN demographics paper because it extends MAD requirements from bright 3CR systems to a large LoTSS-selected FR I sample.

- [2607.09961](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09961) Anand Utsav Kapoor et al. `Predicting ionised gas emission in 3D with SKIRT. I. Framework and validation`
  - **Question:** can SKIRT predict 3D ionized-gas emission for synthetic observations of simulated galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Code: SKIRT with a new photoionization module.
    - Validation: `60` spherical shell Cloudy models.
    - 3D comparison: a Milky Way-analog galaxy against COLT.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine Cloudy tables for gas temperature and opacity with inline ion-fraction and emissivity solving.
    - Characterize the local ionizing field from `1-6 Ryd` using `log U` and four spectral-shape ratios.
    - Compare line luminosities and pixel-level maps against Cloudy and COLT.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Hydrogen recombination lines agree with Cloudy to within a few percent; Halpha median ratio is `0.97`.
    - Most forbidden lines agree to within `~5%`, except [SII] 6717 at `1.23`.
    - In 3D, integrated hydrogen lines agree with COLT within `18%`, and [NII] within `2%`.
    - [OIII] and [SII] are elevated by `~70%` and `~80%`.
    - Pixel-by-pixel correlations reach `r>=0.92`.
  - **Position:** practical methods paper because synthetic IFU line diagnostics are needed to compare simulations with MaNGA, MUSE, and JWST/NIRSpec.

- [2607.09865](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09865) Shanquan Gui `Six-Class BPT Galaxy Classification for Survey-Scale AGN Candidate Prioritization`
  - **Question:** can survey-scale models recover six-class BPT labels when line measurements are incomplete?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `1.47 million` galaxies.
    - Features: `27` raw measurements, derived ratios, and missingness indicators.
    - Models: five deep tabular architectures plus boosted-tree and classical baselines.
  - **Method:**
    - Train multi-class BPT classifiers.
    - Evaluate hard labels, precision-recall curves, top-k retrieval, ablations, and feature interpretation.
    - Compare deep tabular models with boosted trees.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - CNN-Transformer reaches accuracy `0.8266`.
    - Star-forming versus AGN Class 1/Class 4 ROC AUC reaches `0.9998`.
    - Missingness indicators add substantial predictive information.
    - `log([NeIII]/[OII])` plus stellar mass or sSFR helps separate star-forming galaxies from AGN hosts.
    - The models are best used for AGN candidate ranking rather than replacing traditional BPT diagnostics.
  - **Position:** useful survey-infrastructure paper for large spectroscopic samples where emission-line completeness is uneven.

## CGM, Environmental Processing, and Feedback

- [2607.11865](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11865) Carlos J. Vargas et al. `Ardua: Unveiling the Baryon Cycle from Stars to the Cosmic Web`
  - **Question:** what mission capability would map the multiphase CGM in emission rather than through pencil-beam absorption alone?
  - **Data:**
    - Concept: NASA ASTRA Initiative mission submission.
    - Instruments: wide-field far-ultraviolet spectroscopy plus a LEM-derived X-ray microcalorimeter.
    - Program scale: more than `50` nearby galaxies.
  - **Method:**
    - Map cool neutral gas, ionized warm-hot gas, and hot coronae in UV and X-ray emission.
    - Target spatial structure, kinematics, and phase interactions in the CGM.
    - Extend baryon-cycle mapping to the IGM and adjacent community science.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Ardua would test competing galaxy-formation models with spatially resolved multiphase gas maps.
    - The design targets feedback-driven outflows and gas flows across the full CGM temperature range.
    - No planned or approved mission has the same combined UV/X-ray survey capability.
  - **Position:** high-level mission concept, but important for CGM strategy because it targets the main missing observable: emission mapping of multiphase gas.

- [2607.11870](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11870) Andrew Weldon et al. `Electron Density of Ionized Gas Outflows: Insights from the MaNGA Survey`
  - **Question:** how do ionized outflow electron densities connect to local star-formation conditions and redshift evolution?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: final MaNGA Data Release.
    - Sample: nearby star-forming galaxies.
    - Detections: broad Halpha, [NII], and [SII] components in `115` galaxies, `~3%` of star-forming MaNGA systems.
  - **Method:**
    - Define physically motivated outflow apertures.
    - Fit additional broad emission-line components.
    - Compare outflow electron density, mass-loss rate, and mass loading with local and global galaxy properties.
    - Combine with literature measurements to `z~1.9`.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Lower SFR surface-density regions tend to drive denser ionized gas outflows.
    - Mass-loading correlations depend on whether individual electron densities or a fixed median density are used.
    - Outflow electron density shows no significant redshift evolution to `z~1.9`.
    - Ionized outflows are denser than the ISM by `~8.5x` at `z~0` and `~1.6x` at `z~1.9`.
  - **Position:** useful feedback-calibration paper because density assumptions directly affect outflow masses and loading factors.

- [2607.11426](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11426) Alessandro Ignesti et al. `An HI+radio continuum study of local cluster galaxies`
  - **Question:** can HI plus radio-continuum selection catch cluster galaxies at the start of environmental processing?
  - **Data:**
    - Facility: MeerKAT.
    - Clusters: IIZW108, A4059, and A3558 at `z~0.04`.
    - Cluster masses: `M200~2-9x10^14 Msun`.
    - Sample: `61` galaxies detected in both HI and radio continuum plus `677` optically selected cluster members for stacking.
  - **Method:**
    - Trace neutral gas with HI 21 cm and nonthermal ISM with `1.4 GHz` radio continuum.
    - Compare galaxy properties with field controls.
    - Stack optically selected members to measure HI deficiency trends.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The HI plus radio-continuum selected cluster galaxies are `~2.7x` more star-forming than field galaxies matched in stellar and HI mass.
    - The selection catches galaxies before gas removal but after pressure-enhanced star formation and radio luminosity.
    - Stacked cluster members have steadily increasing HI deficiency toward cluster centers.
    - Central cluster galaxies have on average `~3x` less HI mass than field controls.
  - **Position:** useful local environmental-processing analog for interpreting gas stripping and triggered star formation in denser environments.

- [2607.11675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11675) Harrison J. Souchereau et al. `Up, Up, and Away? Quantifying ISM Fallback using Ram Pressure Stripping Simulations`
  - **Question:** when can stripped cold gas fall back onto a satellite galaxy rather than being permanently removed?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: hydrodynamical wind tunnels.
    - Galaxy: intermediate-mass disk with `M*=10^9.7 Msun`.
    - Environment: Coma-like host.
    - Variable: disk-wind angle.
  - **Method:**
    - Track cold ISM outflow and fallback during ram-pressure stripping.
    - Identify the geometry of fallback as a function of wind angle.
    - Use idealized tests to separate offset rotation, disk shadowing, and cloud growth.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Cold gas can cycle through outflow and inflow even when the final outcome is complete gas removal.
    - Fallback is elevated for wind angles closer to edge-on.
    - Most inflow stays within an inner tail extending to `~20 kpc`.
    - Offset rotational motion is a major fallback driver for highly inclined disks.
  - **Position:** simulation context for cluster stripping because gas fallback complicates simple one-way removal pictures.
