## JWST High-Redshift Galaxies and Early Enrichment

- [2607.15515](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15515) Hiroya Umeda et al. `Gas-Phase Metallicity and Nitrogen Abundances in Low-Mass Galaxies Down to M_star~10^5.7 M_sun at z~4.5-10.1 from JWST Lensing Cluster Surveys`
  - **Question:** how chemically enriched are intrinsically faint low-mass galaxies during the first billion years?
  - **Data:**
    - Spectroscopy: `405` JWST/NIRSpec spectra from DREAMS and other lensing-cluster surveys.
    - Redshift range: `z=4.5-10.1`.
    - Stellar mass reach: `M_star~10^5.7 M_sun`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure rest-frame UV and optical emission lines in individual spectra and mass-binned stacks.
    - Derive gas-phase metallicity and abundance ratios.
    - Build direct-method strong-line calibrations for low-mass high-redshift systems.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The `z~6` mass-metallicity relation is traced down to `M_star~10^6.6 M_sun`.
    - The low-mass relation reaches `12+log(O/H)~7.2`.
    - One stack shows N/O from N IV] exceeding N/O from [N II] by `~1.4 dex`.
    - He II 4686/Hbeta of `~0.03` makes prompt nitrogen and hard-ionizing sources a live issue.
  - **Position:** weekly read-first because it connects faint-galaxy JWST spectroscopy to early enrichment and abundance-ratio anomalies.

- [2607.16404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16404) Anna Durrant et al. `Cosmological simulations of the high-redshift galaxy population adopting a variable stellar initial mass function`
  - **Question:** can a density-dependent IMF reduce the tension between JWST bright galaxies and standard simulations?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: COLIBRE `L=100 cMpc` variant evolved to `z=5`.
    - Physics: density-dependent top-heavy IMF for stars born in dense gas.
    - Emission: UV and optical stellar plus nebular light with dust attenuation.
  - **Method:**
    - Adjust yields and feedback energetics consistently with the changing IMF.
    - Compare against the fiducial Chabrier-IMF COLIBRE run.
    - Test UV luminosity functions, stellar-mass functions, optical luminosities, and UV slopes.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Early far-UV luminosities rise by up to `~4x`.
    - The model reaches `M_UV~-20` at `z=15`, compared with `M_UV~-18.5` in the fiducial run.
    - Later dust attenuation offsets part of the UV boost.
    - The run remains broadly consistent with `z=5` stellar-mass and optical-luminosity constraints.
  - **Position:** central theory entry because it tests a physically explicit solution to JWST bright-galaxy demographics.

- [2607.20606](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20606) Matteo Sapori et al. `Outflows in the Early Universe: Neutral gas Absorption in Galaxies at z > 3 from low-resolution JWST Spectroscopy`
  - **Question:** do massive galaxies already drive neutral outflows at `3<z<7`?
  - **Data:**
    - Spectroscopy: `811` JWST/NIRSpec PRISM spectra.
    - Redshift range: `3<z<7`.
    - Feature: Na I D absorption after stellar-continuum subtraction.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit physical properties and star-formation histories.
    - Search individual spectra and stacks for Na I D excess absorption.
    - Compare detection fraction with stellar mass, dust, and quiescence.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Excess Na I D absorption is detected in `20` galaxies.
    - The overall detection fraction is `~2.5%`.
    - The fraction rises to `~11%` among the most massive galaxies and `~43%` among massive quenched systems.
    - Conservative mass outflow rates are `4-12 M_sun/yr`.
  - **Position:** important observational feedback paper because it extracts outflow demographics from existing low-resolution JWST spectra.

- [2607.19471](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19471) Sergio Martinez-Gonzalez et al. `The first dusty galaxies across 6 < z < 14: Blue monsters, red monsters, and the bimodality of dust content in early galaxies`
  - **Question:** why do early galaxies split into extremely blue dust-poor systems and red dusty massive systems?
  - **Data:**
    - Observational regimes: JWST blue galaxies at `z>10` and dusty massive systems at `z~6-7`.
    - Model ingredients: clustered supernova dust production, shock processing, and disk breakout.
  - **Method:**
    - Model cloud-scale mechanical blowout and disk breakout.
    - Convert retained dust columns into UV-continuum slopes.
    - Test the transition between blue and red early-galaxy populations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Efficient blowout produces blue slopes `beta_UV<-2.4`.
    - Larger stellar masses retain more dust and produce red slopes `beta_UV>-1.5`.
    - Radiative losses can push dusty systems to `beta_UV~-0.5`.
  - **Position:** useful cosmic-dawn interpretation paper because dust bimodality is tied to resolved feedback geometry.

## Little Red Dots, AGN, and Black-Hole Growth

- [2607.19057](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19057) Ignas Juodzbalis et al. `A JWST/NIRSpec Census of Balmer Absorption in Type 1 AGN`
  - **Question:** how common is Balmer absorption in JWST Type 1 AGN and Little Red Dots?
  - **Data:**
    - Instrument: JWST/NIRSpec spectra.
    - Population: Type 1 AGN including LRD-like sources.
    - Diagnostic: H-alpha and Balmer absorption kinematics.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify Balmer absorption signatures.
    - Estimate occurrence fractions across the Type 1 AGN and LRD subsets.
    - Interpret line kinematics as outflow signatures where supported.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - A large fraction of LRDs show H-alpha absorption, quoted in the daily report as `44_-6^+21%`.
    - The absorption provides a direct probe of dense, high-velocity gas close to compact accretors.
    - LRD spectral diversity likely includes geometry and outflow effects, not only stellar-population color.
  - **Position:** key LRD diagnostic because Balmer absorption is a direct spectroscopic constraint on compact obscured-accretion models.

- [2607.19491](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19491) Hyewon Suh et al. `Rapid growth in a dual AGN during a gas-rich merger at z~4.5`
  - **Question:** can close dual AGN be confirmed during early gas-rich mergers?
  - **Data:**
    - Source: LID-1166 at `z~4.5`.
    - Instruments: JWST/NIRSpec IFU and ALMA [C II] `158 um`.
    - Geometry: projected separation `~1.5 kpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Resolve narrow-line components spatially and kinematically.
    - Identify broad H-alpha emission in both components.
    - Compare ionized-gas and [C II] cold-gas kinematics.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - LID-1166 is a dual AGN at `z~4.5`.
    - The two components have velocity offset `~-164 km/s`.
    - ALMA [C II] confirms distinct cold-gas components.
    - Both SMBHs are already consistent with the local black-hole-host relation within uncertainties.
  - **Position:** high-priority AGN result because it reveals an early hidden merger-driven growth phase.

- [2607.20617](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20617) Pau Amaro Seoane `A dust-free hierarchically nested supermassive-star model for James Webb Space Telescope Little Red Dots`
  - **Question:** can LRD observables arise from nested supermassive-star envelopes rather than dust geometry?
  - **Data:**
    - Observables: red optical continua, blue UV continua, broad Balmer lines, and weak X-rays.
    - Model: radiation-dominated envelope trapping a nuclear star cluster and secondary core.
  - **Method:**
    - Use plunging stars to inflate a magnetized envelope.
    - Let infalling stars contribute blue UV emission.
    - Thermalize X-rays in the Compton-thick host.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The model can produce red optical light and broad H-alpha without invoking dust.
    - Seed growth can occur at the global radiation limit of the envelope.
    - Assembly times fall from hundreds of Myr to tens of Myr.
  - **Position:** useful because it gives an alternative physical channel for LRDs that can be tested against dust-obscured AGN models.

- [2607.17448](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17448) Jun-Rong Liu et al. `Optically Thick Outflow Driven by Supercritical Accretion May Explain Little Red Dots`
  - **Question:** can optically thick supercritical outflows explain Little Red Dot continua and line signatures?
  - **Data:**
    - Target class: JWST Little Red Dots.
    - Physical ingredients: supercritical accretion, thick outflows, compact AGN emission.
  - **Method:**
    - Model envelope emission and outflow geometry.
    - Compare with the observed red continuum and broad-line requirements.
    - Link LRD abundance to early black-hole fueling.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Supercritical outflow envelopes remain a viable explanation for compact red sources.
    - The model complements the week's other LRD papers by tying spectral shape to accretion flow geometry.
  - **Position:** useful context paper because LRD interpretation is now a competition between dust, outflow, and stellar-envelope models.

## IGM, CGM, Molecular Gas, and 21-cm Physics

- [2607.18974](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18974) Pasquier Noterdaeme et al. `Molecular gas hidden in plain sight in the early Universe`
  - **Question:** can strong H2 absorption reveal hidden molecular gas at high redshift?
  - **Data:**
    - Absorber: `z_abs=4.1` toward a `z=4.7` quasar.
    - Spectroscopy: archival data plus VLT/X-shooter.
    - Dust and metals: about one-fifth solar metallicity and `A_V~0.06 mag`.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify H2 absorption missed in prior data.
    - Derive atomic and molecular hydrogen column densities directly.
    - Compare molecular fraction and velocity width with known absorbers.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The absorber has `f_H2~60%`.
    - This is the highest directly measured molecular fraction at `z>0`.
    - Low-ionization metals span `Delta v~500 km/s`.
    - High-redshift molecular gas may be undercounted in quasar-absorber samples.
  - **Position:** weekly read-first for gas work because it exposes a selection bias in the early molecular-gas census.

- [2607.19938](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19938) Vid Irsic et al. `Ringing of the Reionization: A first direct measurement of the intergalactic pressure smoothing scale at redshift z>4.2 as imprinted onto small-scale peculiar velocities in the Lyman-alpha forest`
  - **Question:** can the Lyman-alpha forest directly measure the pressure-smoothing scale left by reionization heating?
  - **Data:**
    - Observations: Lyman-alpha forest flux-power measurements at `z=4.2-5.0`.
    - Scale range: `k=0.1-1 s/km`.
    - Simulations: toy models and linear-theory tests.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify an acoustic feature in the peculiar-velocity-gradient power spectrum.
    - Calibrate its relationship to pressure smoothing.
    - Apply the method to small-scale forest flux power.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The method directly measures the pressure-smoothing scale at `z>4.2`.
    - The inferred values include `lambda_p(z=4.6)=32.36+/-8.35 ckpc`.
    - The forest can recover reionization thermal history through small-scale velocity structure.
  - **Position:** key IGM result because pressure smoothing is a fossil of reionization heating.

- [2607.20371](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20371) Chanasorn Kongprachaya et al. `The burgeoning rise of the 21-cm forest I: constraints on the optical depth of the intergalactic medium at 5.38 < z < 5.84`
  - **Question:** does a high-redshift radio quasar reveal 21-cm forest absorption near the end of reionization?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: PSO J352.4034-15.3373 at `z=5.84`.
    - Observation: `18 h` over `203-222.5 MHz`.
    - Spectrum: rms `3.6 mJy/beam` per `390 kHz` channel.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit the continuum plus possible intervening absorption.
    - Test island and global IGM models.
    - Combine archival radio fluxes from `150 MHz` to `3.0 GHz`.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - No intervening 21-cm absorption is detected at `5.38<z<5.84`.
    - The global model gives `tau_21<0.02` at `95%` confidence.
    - The IGM was heated above the adiabatic cooling limit of `~0.8 K` at `z=5.68`.
  - **Position:** important 21-cm constraint because it ties radio quasar absorption searches to the post-overlap thermal state.

- [2607.20651](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20651) Hovav Lazare et al. `When galaxies burst II. Implications of enhanced burstiness for the 21-cm Cosmic Dawn signal`
  - **Question:** how does bursty high-redshift star formation affect the 21-cm signal?
  - **Data:**
    - Framework: Zeus21 analytical calculations.
    - Backgrounds: Lyman-alpha and X-ray radiation fields.
    - Star-formation model: stochastic burst amplitude and coherence time.
  - **Method:**
    - Introduce unequal-time correlations in star-formation-rate density.
    - Propagate burstiness into radiation-field power spectra.
    - Evaluate the resulting 21-cm power spectrum.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Burstiness adds a shot-noise-like component to Lyman-alpha and X-ray fields.
    - Auto- and cross-power spectra are enhanced.
    - JWST-scale star-formation stochasticity can change Cosmic Dawn observables.
  - **Position:** high-value bridge between JWST galaxy physics and upcoming 21-cm inference.

- [2607.18385](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18385) Huanian Zhang et al. `Star formation powers optical line emission from the CGM`
  - **Question:** what powers optical emission lines in the inner CGM of normal galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `72` low-redshift galaxies.
    - Tracers: H-alpha, [O II], and [O III] radial profiles.
    - Radius: `5-10 R_e`, or `0.1-0.25 r_vir`.
  - **Method:**
    - Build emission-line maps.
    - Compare radial line fluxes with SFR and stellar mass.
    - Use [O III]/[O II] behavior to infer ionizing-photon escape.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Large-radius line flux correlates with SFR, not stellar mass.
    - Escaping ionizing photons can power the inner-CGM emission.
    - Current models disagree on cool-CGM dependence on central SFR.
  - **Position:** useful low-redshift CGM anchor for feedback and photon-leakage interpretation.

  - **Question:** why do state-of-the-art simulations still struggle with galaxy and hot-halo gas properties together?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: Magneticum, IllustrisTNG, and SIMBA.
    - Observations: MaNGA, eROSITA, and SZ hot-gas constraints.

    - The remaining problem is feedback coupling to halo gas, not only feedback availability.
  - **Position:** important simulation benchmark because CGM thermodynamics are now a first-order feedback constraint.
