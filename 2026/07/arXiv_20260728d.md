## Executive View

The read-first paper is [2607.22834](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22834) Emma Giovinazzo, which uses `1428` JWST/NIRSpec spectra at `5<z<10` to infer galaxy ionizing escape and build an observational ionizing photon budget. It directly connects early-galaxy spectra, escape fraction, ionizing luminosity functions, and the reionization timeline.

[2607.22966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22966) Zheng Ma is the strongest AGN entry. It presents a red quasar at `z=3.711` with JWST, HST, MUSE, Chandra, ALMA, and VLA evidence for a partially exposed nucleus embedded in dense gas and dust. [2607.22835](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22835) Michele Ginolfi is the strongest JWST selection-method entry because it uses manifold learning on `~242,000` ASTRODEEP-JWST sources to recover Little Red Dot regions without explicit color cuts.

The gas and environment set is unusually useful. [2607.24071](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24071) Xuanyi Wu measures azimuthally anisotropic MgII absorption around DESI LRGs. [2607.24461](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24461) Ellis R. Owen models cosmic-ray heating of cold streams at cosmic noon. [2607.24412](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24412) Minal Chhabra combines uGMRT and CHIME constraints on the `z~1` H I distribution. [2607.22838](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22838) Daniela Palma ties large-scale conformity to filaments in TNG300 and SAG.

Several lower-priority entries are worth keeping as methods or context. [2607.23020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23020) Yongda Zhu turns NIRCam imaging into a photometric IFU for feedback mapping. [2607.22812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22812) Alex Ostridge accelerates quadruply lensed quasar mass modeling. [2607.23339](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23339) Lurdes Ondaro-Mallea clarifies when stacked kSZ measurements trace gas optical depth versus nonlinear velocity effects.

## Reionization, JWST Populations, and High-Redshift AGN

- [2607.22834](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22834) Emma Giovinazzo et al. `Reionization driven by the few: the ionizing budget of galaxies at z=5-10 from JWST/NIRSpec`
  - **Question:** which galaxies dominate the ionizing photon budget during reionization?
  - **Data:**
    - Spectroscopy: JWST/NIRSpec spectra from the DAWN JWST Archive.
    - Sample: `1428` galaxies at `5<z<10`.
    - Population link: UV luminosity functions extended to `z=15`.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit spectra with a picket-fence escape model.
    - Infer posteriors for ionizing escape fraction and escaping ionizing output.
    - Combine object-level outputs with UV luminosity functions.
    - Derive hydrogen-ionizing luminosity functions and an ionizing photon budget.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The budget integrated down to `M_UV=-13` gives a neutral-fraction evolution consistent with independent probes under the adopted assumptions.
    - Reionization ends near `z~5.8` in the standard scenario.
    - Bright sources around `M_UV~-20` dominate at `z=5-6`.
    - Fainter galaxies with `M_UV>-18` contribute roughly equally at `z>6`.
    - About `20%` of sources, those with `f_esc>10%`, produce about `87%` of the ionizing photons.
  - **Position:** read first because it converts JWST spectra into a direct reionization-budget measurement rather than only a galaxy-property census.

- [2607.22966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22966) Zheng Ma et al. `A quasar hatching from a buried red phase at z = 3.7`
  - **Question:** what does a partially exposed red quasar reveal about the transition from buried to unobscured accretion?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: JADES-GS 209777, also CANDELS J033238.02-274626.2, at `z=3.711`.
    - Facilities: JWST/NIRSpec, NIRCam, MIRI, HST, MUSE, Chandra, ALMA, and VLA.
    - Gas tracers: broad emission lines, X-rays, H-alpha and He I absorption, O I, Mg II, Na D, [O III], and Lyman-alpha.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine multiwavelength data to separate visible nuclear activity from embedded gas and dust.
    - Use line diagnostics to identify dense gas near the nucleus.
    - Use kinematic disturbance and extended emission to trace outflowing multiphase material.
    - Use ALMA dust emission to place the system in a high-infrared-luminosity regime.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Broad lines and strong X-rays show that the nucleus is at least partially exposed.
    - H-alpha and He I absorption indicate dense gas close to the nucleus.
    - Disturbed low-ionization and ionized features indicate multiphase acceleration into the host environment.
    - Extended Lyman-alpha emission reaches more than `20 kpc`.
    - The infrared luminosity places the system in the ULIRG regime.
  - **Position:** high-priority AGN paper because it gives a detailed JWST-era view of feedback clearing a dusty high-redshift quasar.

- [2607.22835](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22835) Michele Ginolfi et al. `Unsupervised selection and characterisation of Little Red Dots in JWST surveys with manifold learning`
  - **Question:** can Little Red Dots be selected from JWST photometry without fixed color cuts?
  - **Data:**
    - Photometry: ASTRODEEP-JWST catalog.
    - Sample: `~242,000` isolated, well-measured sources.
    - Validation: spectroscopically confirmed Little Red Dots and archival NIRSpec spectra from the DJA.
  - **Method:**
    - Map broadband colors, morphology, and photometric redshifts with UMAP.
    - Anchor LRD regions using spectroscopic labels.
    - Compare data-driven regions with published color selections.
    - Test whether the manifold also recovers brown dwarfs, broad-line AGN, and rare outliers.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Spectroscopic LRDs occupy two well-defined manifold regions.
    - The main region reaches purity `~0.78` and completeness `~0.82` on the spectroscopic subset.
    - The selection yields `~100` additional candidates.
    - The same map recovers brown dwarfs and broad-line AGN without explicit criteria.
  - **Position:** useful selection-method paper because it provides a common JWST photometric space for LRDs, AGN contaminants, and rare-source discovery.

- [2607.23020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23020) Yongda Zhu et al. `PhotoIFU: NIRCam as a Photometric Integral Field Unit for Mapping Feedback in Galaxies`
  - **Question:** can deep NIRCam imaging map resolved feedback-related galaxy structure when spectroscopic IFU data are unavailable?
  - **Data:**
    - Imaging: PSF-matched JWST/NIRCam imaging.
    - Targets: three JADES galaxies at `z=1.3-3.7`.
    - Systems: two galaxies with extended ionized emission and one post-starburst galaxy with a strong neutral outflow.
  - **Method:**
    - Treat each spatial pixel as a coarse SED element.
    - Fit pixel SEDs with Prospector.
    - Map stellar-mass surface density, specific star-formation rate, dust attenuation, metallicity, and recent star-formation history.
    - Compare feedback-selected regions with the full host distribution.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Extended-emission regions occupy different resolved SED-property space than the full host.
    - Ionized-emission regions are generally less dusty.
    - The neutral-outflow system shows enhanced recent star formation in selected regions.
    - Imaging-based PhotoIFU can scale resolved feedback mapping to samples lacking spectroscopic IFU coverage.
  - **Position:** useful JWST methods paper because it turns deep imaging into a spatial diagnostic for outflows and extended emission.

- [2607.23062](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23062) Nicole Firestone et al. `HETDEX: Star Formation Stochasticity Diagram of Lyman Alpha Emitting Galaxies at Cosmic Noon Confirms Three Archetypes`
  - **Question:** how variable are recent star-formation histories in cosmic-noon Lyman-alpha emitters?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: HETDEX LAEs in the COSMOS field.
    - Sample: `270` LAEs with rest-UV through NIR photometry from CANDELS.
    - Comparison: ODIN LAE star-formation archetypes from Firestone et al. 2025.
  - **Method:**
    - Reconstruct non-parametric star-formation histories with Dense Basis.
    - Compare `SFR_10` and `SFR_100` behavior.
    - Build a Star Formation Stochasticity Diagram.
    - Use Gaussian-mixture decomposition after removing redshift-evolution effects.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The HETDEX sample follows the First Burst, Dominant Burst, and Nondominant Burst archetypes.
    - First Burst LAEs are not well represented in traditional `SFR_100-M_*` space.
    - `SFR_10-M_*` better captures rapidly rising recent star formation.
    - The stochasticity diagram recovers three populations consistent with empirical LAE archetypes.
  - **Position:** useful LAE paper because it gives a compact diagnostic for burst timing in cosmic-noon emission-line samples.

## Gas, CGM, and Large-Scale Structure

- [2607.24071](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24071) Xuanyi Wu et al. `Evidence for Azimuthally Anisotropic MgII Absorption around DESI Luminous Red Galaxies`
  - **Question:** is cool or warm CGM gas around massive quiescent galaxies azimuthally anisotropic?
  - **Data:**
    - Foregrounds: DESI DR1 luminous red galaxies.
    - Backgrounds: quasar spectra behind the LRGs.
    - Redshift bins: `0.4<z_LRG<0.75` and `0.75<z_LRG<1.1`.
    - Radius range: projected separations `0.01-1.0` proper Mpc.
  - **Method:**
    - Assign an MgII doublet-window equivalent width to every LRG-quasar pair.
    - Subtract a redshift-matched random-control signal from the same normalized quasar spectra.
    - Measure equivalent width by radius and by azimuth relative to the projected major axis.
    - Test major-axis versus minor-axis differences with position-angle randomization.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The all-angle MgII profile declines smoothly across the measured radius range.
    - Inner absorption is stronger at `0.4<z_LRG<0.75` than at `0.75<z_LRG<1.1`.
    - Major-axis sightlines show enhanced absorption at `r_p~30-80 kpc`.
    - The fiducial major-minus-minor difference over `0.03-0.077 Mpc` is `0.184+/-0.075 Angstrom`.
    - The position-angle randomization probability is `p=0.013`.
  - **Position:** high-value CGM entry because it extends azimuthal absorption mapping to massive quiescent halos using DESI statistics.

- [2607.24461](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24461) Ellis R. Owen et al. `Cosmic ray heating of cold streams: Implications for the gas supply and growth of massive galaxies`
  - **Question:** can cosmic rays from cosmic-web filaments heat or disrupt cold streams feeding massive galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Model target: magnetized cold streams in galaxy halos during cosmic noon.
    - External energy source: cosmic rays observed or expected in cosmic-web filaments.
    - Gas phases: dense stream cores, diffuse streams, and mixed stream-CGM interfaces.
  - **Method:**
    - Couple spectrally resolved cosmic-ray transport to a redshift-dependent analytic cold-stream model.
    - Compute cosmic-ray energy deposition in stream material.
    - Compare heating against radiative cooling in cores and interfaces.
    - Identify the halo-mass, radius, and density regimes where stream structure changes.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Dense stream cores are resilient and heat by less than a factor of `10`.
    - Diffuse streams and mixed interface gas can be heated to or above the mixing-layer temperature.
    - Complete evaporation requires extreme conditions.
    - Cold streams are most vulnerable at larger galactocentric radii, higher halo mass, and lower density.
    - Surviving cold-flow components may become thinner and more sharply confined.
  - **Position:** useful gas-supply theory because it links cosmic-web nonthermal physics to the selectivity of cold accretion.

- [2607.24412](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24412) Minal Chhabra et al. `Constraining the z approx 1 neutral hydrogen (HI) distribution`
  - **Question:** what H I halo-mass relation is consistent with existing `z~1` 21-cm measurements?
  - **Data:**
    - Emission constraint: uGMRT stacked 21-cm measurement of `Omega_HI` from blue star-forming galaxies.
    - Clustering constraint: CHIME 21-cm autocorrelation power spectrum.
    - Simulation input: dark-matter halos populated with H I through a three-parameter H I mass-halo mass relation.
  - **Method:**
    - Assign H I masses to simulated halos.
    - Compare simulated `Omega_HI` and 21-cm power spectrum with observations.
    - Infer the H I mass-halo mass posterior with joint Bayesian modeling.
    - Predict the `z~1` H I mass function.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The H I mass function is nearly constant below `~3.6 x 10^9 M_sun`.
    - It declines rapidly at larger H I masses.
    - About `90%` of total H I lies in `2 x 10^9` to `4 x 10^11 M_sun`.
    - The inferred abundance of high-H I-mass galaxies is larger than in earlier observations and simulations.
  - **Position:** useful baryon census paper because it connects intensity mapping and stacked emission to the gas reservoirs feeding galaxy growth.

- [2607.22838](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22838) Daniela Palma et al. `The role of filaments in the large-scale conformity signal`
  - **Question:** which large-scale environments drive conformity around low-mass central galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Models: IllustrisTNG300 and the MDPL2-SAG semi-analytic model.
    - Environment finder: DisPerSE.
    - Regions: group outskirts, cluster outskirts, filaments, filament outskirts, voids, and walls.
  - **Method:**
    - Exclude galaxies that were satellites in the past.
    - Measure conformity signals across environment classes.
    - Vary filament thickness and overlap definitions.
    - Compare dependence on filament linear density, length, and mass.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Filament galaxies dominate the conformity signal in SAG.
    - TNG300 shows a coherent but weaker filament contribution.
    - The signal is not strongly sensitive to filament-thickness definitions.
    - High-linear-density, short, and high-mass filaments enhance the signal.
    - Massive nearby systems amplify conformity out to `4 Mpc/h`.
  - **Position:** relevant LSS entry because it places galaxy conformity in a filament-specific environment framework.

- [2607.23339](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23339) Lurdes Ondaro-Mallea et al. `Interpreting the stacked kinetic SZ effect I: velocity reconstruction and non-linear velocity effects`
  - **Question:** when does a stacked kSZ signal trace mean gas optical depth rather than nonlinear velocity structure?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: FLAMINGO hydrodynamical simulations.
    - Mock tracers: DESI-like LRG, BGS, and ELG galaxy samples.
    - Observable: stacked velocity-weighted projected gas momentum.
  - **Method:**
    - Decompose the stacked kSZ signal into bulk-flow and nonlinear gas-momentum terms.
    - Compare ideal linear real-space velocity reconstruction with practical estimators.
    - Test redshift-space distortions and retained small-scale velocity information.
    - Quantify modeling accuracy relative to a `10%` target.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Ideal linear real-space velocities recover mean optical depth to within a few percent.
    - Practical velocities with nonlinear information or redshift-space distortions suppress the signal by `10-20%`.
    - The effect is `1-2 sigma` for current data and should matter for upcoming surveys.
    - High-S/N velocity estimators require more simulation-based modeling.
  - **Position:** useful methods entry because kSZ gas-fraction inference is becoming a CGM and feedback probe for DESI-era galaxy samples.

## Survey, Inference, and Context Entries

- [2607.22812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22812) Alex Ostridge et al. `Speeding up Gravitational Lens Mass Models with Machine Learning: Applications in X-ray Astronomy`
  - **Question:** can machine learning make quadruply lensed quasar mass modeling fast enough for large samples?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: grids of quadruply lensed sources with singular isothermal ellipsoid lenses.
    - Training observables: projected positions of four lensed images.
    - Application: seven quadruply lensed quasars with archival Chandra observations.
  - **Method:**
    - Train two fully connected neural networks to predict lens mass and ellipticity.
    - Use neural predictions to initialize mass-model optimization.
    - Compare optimized models with Gaia DR3 lensed-image positions.
    - Apply the caustic method to localize X-ray and optical emission regions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Neural initialization accelerates mass modeling by several orders of magnitude.
    - Many systems converge within minutes.
    - Source positions are recovered at the `<0.005 arcsec` level over broad lens-parameter ranges.
    - The method can improve effective Chandra angular resolution for high-redshift lensed quasars by up to two orders of magnitude.
  - **Position:** useful quasar-method paper because it links lens modeling throughput to spatially resolved accretion-region constraints.

- [2607.24289](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24289) Matteo Bonato et al. `Local luminosity functions of galaxies at (sub)millimeter wavelengths from Planck surveys`
  - **Question:** how do updated Planck compact-source catalogs revise local submillimeter luminosity functions?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: Second Planck Catalog of Compact Sources.
    - Frequencies: `857`, `545`, `353`, and `217 GHz`.
    - Derived functions: total IR luminosity function and dust mass function.
  - **Method:**
    - Use all-sky nearby-galaxy catalogs with redshift-independent distances where needed.
    - Compute luminosity functions with `1/V_max`.
    - Apply nonparametric kernel-density estimation with `1/V_max` weighting.
    - Compare against earlier estimates based on the Planck ERCSC.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The newer Planck catalog changes local submillimeter luminosity-function estimates.
    - The method improves treatment of binning and survey selection.
    - The output updates dust and IR baselines used for galaxy-evolution comparisons.
  - **Position:** useful reference because high-redshift dust and submillimeter studies still rely on local calibration functions.

- [2607.22814](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22814) Tom Wagg et al. `Charting the Galactic Underworld I: Comprehensive simulations of the kinematics, rates, and demographics of Milky Way black holes`
  - **Question:** what Milky Way black-hole population should Gaia, Roman, and spectroscopic surveys expect?
  - **Data:**
    - Model: cogsworth Milky Way black-hole population synthesis.
    - Physics variations: `32` binary-evolution, supernova, and Galactic-potential variants.
    - Observational context: future Roman, Gaia DR4, and spectroscopic constraints.
  - **Method:**
    - Simulate binary evolution and trajectories through the Galactic potential.
    - Track isolated, escaped, binary, and luminous-companion black holes.
    - Compare sensitivity to remnant-mass prescriptions and natal kicks.
    - Evaluate the impact of time-dependent Galactic potentials.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The fiducial Milky Way forms about `1.7 x 10^8` black holes, with an order-of-magnitude model span.
    - About `91%` are isolated and about `3%` escape the Galaxy.
    - About `10^5` black holes retain luminous stellar companions.
    - Time-dependent Galactic potentials more than double the escape fraction and increase the bound-population scale height by about `20%`.
  - **Position:** lower-priority but relevant because Roman and Gaia demographics affect compact-object foregrounds and survey interpretation.
