## Executive View

The read-first paper is [2607.25801](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25801) Cheng Cheng, which uses JWST/NIRCam H-alpha selection plus VLT/MUSE coverage to measure Lyman-alpha escape in typical `z~5.5` star-forming galaxies. It is directly relevant to reionization-era visibility, nebular-line-selected samples, and environmental modulation of Lyman-alpha transmission.

[2607.24952](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24952) Raul Jimenez is the strongest cosmic-dawn stellar-population entry. It compares lensed star clusters in the Cosmic Gems arc at `z=9.625` with the Sparkler at `z=1.378`, placing the high-redshift clusters near a metal-poor floor. [2607.25696](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25696) Angel Rodriguez Barbosa is the main JWST AGN-demographics entry, using SMILES and JADES coverage in GOODS-S to trace obscured AGN from `0<z<6`.

The gas and feedback set is strong. [2607.24911](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24911) Satoshi Yamada uses XRISM spectroscopy of H1821+643 to infer unusually strong quasar-mode turbulence in a cluster core. [2607.25035](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25035) James H. Matthews turns C IV blueshifts into a constraint on quasar-wind mass loading and clumpiness. [2607.24929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24929) Cheqiu Lyu reports anisotropic metallicity excess around DESI galaxy pairs, while [2607.25800](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25800) Cheng Cheng uses FAST stacking to show H I depletion out to `5R200` around nearby clusters.

The survey-method papers are worth saving for future work. [2607.24917](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24917) Anirban Roy reframes intensity-mapping interlopers as extra large-scale-structure tracers. [2607.25722](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25722) Yuxiang Qin tests whether high-redshift Lyman-alpha forest constraints on reionization survive different galaxy-source prescriptions. [2607.26058](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26058) Aswin P. Vijayan provides COLIBRE dust scaling relations over `0<=z<=15`. [2607.24903](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24903) Akanksha Bij forecasts CHORD H I galaxy yields at both low and high H I masses.

## Reionization, High-Redshift Galaxies, and JWST AGN

- [2607.25801](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25801) Cheng Cheng et al. `Lyalpha Escape in JWST/NIRCam F430M-Selected Halpha Emitters at z~5.5`
  - **Question:** how much Lyman-alpha escapes from H-alpha-selected star-forming galaxies near the end of reionization?
  - **Data:**
    - Selection: JWST/NIRCam F430M-excess H-alpha emitters at `z~5.5`.
    - Spectroscopy: archival VLT/MUSE coverage for the Lyman-alpha measurements.
    - Sample: `12` galaxies with star-formation rates above `~0.1 M_sun yr^-1`.
  - **Method:**
    - Use H-alpha emission to anchor the intrinsic Lyman-alpha production.
    - Search for Lyman-alpha emission in the MUSE data.
    - Combine detections and upper limits to infer a population average.
    - Compare escape behavior with dust, star-formation rate, and local environment.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Lyman-alpha is detected in `3` of `12` galaxies.
    - The population-averaged Lyman-alpha escape fraction has a conservative upper bound of `<0.32`.
    - Detections preferentially occur in nearly dust-free systems.
    - Three of four Lyman-alpha-detected galaxies lie in a known overdense structure.
  - **Position:** read first because it connects JWST nebular-line selection to Lyman-alpha visibility in typical post-reionization galaxies.

- [2607.24952](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24952) Raul Jimenez et al. `Reaching the Metallicity Floor at z~10: Lensed Star Clusters at Cosmic Dawn and Cosmic Noon`
  - **Question:** do lensed star clusters at cosmic dawn already occupy the metal-poor floor seen in old globular-cluster systems?
  - **Data:**
    - Cosmic-dawn system: Cosmic Gems arc at `z=9.625`.
    - Cosmic-noon comparison: the Sparkler at `z=1.378`.
    - Photometry: STARRED deconvolution photometry for `10` unique, doubly imaged cluster candidates in the Cosmic Gems arc.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit both cluster populations with a homogeneous Bayesian stellar-population analysis.
    - Compare formation redshifts and metallicities on a common cosmological timeline.
    - Test stellar-population models with and without binary evolution.
    - Use closed-box and gas-regulator calculations to interpret enrichment.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The Cosmic Gems clusters formed at `z_form~10-11`, with median `z_form~10.2`.
    - Individual cluster metallicities are consistent with `[Z/H]<-1.2`.
    - The data exclude `[Z/H]>=-0.5` for the Cosmic Gems clusters.
    - Binary-evolution models give similarly low values, `[Z/H]~-2.2` to `-2.7`.
    - The Sparkler clusters formed later, at `z_form~2-3.5`, and are more enriched around `[Z/H]~-0.5`.
  - **Position:** high-priority cosmic-dawn entry because it links lensed cluster photometry to early galaxy assembly and enrichment.

- [2607.25696](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25696) Angel Rodriguez Barbosa et al. `Tracing Obscured AGN Contribution and Number Fraction Across 0 < z < 6 with JWST`
  - **Question:** how does obscured AGN prevalence evolve across cosmic time when mid-infrared JWST coverage is included?
  - **Data:**
    - Imaging: JWST SMILES and JADES in GOODS-S.
    - Wavelength baseline: continuous optical-to-mid-infrared coverage from `0.4` to `25 micron`.
    - Sample: `278` AGN across `0<z<6`.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit multiwavelength SEDs with CIGALE.
    - Use MIRI coverage to recover infrared AGN emission missed by ultraviolet and optical selection.
    - Compare AGN infrared contribution and AGN number fraction as functions of redshift and total infrared luminosity.
    - Benchmark the enlarged SMILES footprint against earlier CEERS-based samples.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The sample is about `7` times larger than earlier CEERS-based analyses using similar mid-infrared logic.
    - AGN fractions rise from `<=5%` at `z<2` to about `30%` at higher redshift.
    - The median AGN infrared contribution increases by up to `~0.15`.
    - Over `log(L/L_sun)~8-12`, the AGN contribution and number fraction are nearly static with total infrared luminosity.
  - **Position:** important JWST AGN-demographics paper because it tracks dust-obscured accretion with the wavelength range most sensitive to reprocessed emission.

- [2607.25378](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25378) K. E. Gabanyi et al. `From compact jets to extended lobes: radio morphologies of distant quasars at z > 4`
  - **Question:** what radio structures surround high-redshift quasars that appear compact in milliarcsecond-scale VLBI data?
  - **Data:**
    - Targets: three radio quasars at `z>4`.
    - Radio data: multi-frequency interferometric observations spanning milliarcsecond to arcsecond angular scales.
    - Facilities: international LOFAR and e-MERLIN, with complementary lower-resolution radio measurements.
  - **Method:**
    - Combine compact and extended radio-imaging constraints.
    - Measure radio morphology across angular-resolution regimes.
    - Use spectral information to separate compact cores, jets, and extended lobes.
    - Interpret missing flux between VLBI and arcsecond scales as evidence for resolved structures.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - High-redshift radio quasars can contain compact VLBI components plus extended radio emission.
    - Multi-resolution imaging is needed to recover jet and lobe power that the highest-resolution data resolve out.
    - The systems provide direct tests of AGN-environment coupling early in black-hole growth.
  - **Position:** useful high-redshift AGN entry because it links early radio-loud quasars to jet feedback and environment diagnostics.

## Quasar Feedback, Metal Transport, and Gas Reservoirs

- [2607.24911](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24911) Satoshi Yamada et al. `Vigorous turbulence driven by quasar-mode feedback in a cluster core`
  - **Question:** can a central quasar drive energetically significant turbulence beyond galactic scales?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: H1821+643, the nearest galaxy cluster with a central quasar.
    - Redshift: `z=0.297`.
    - Instrument: XRISM high-resolution X-ray spectroscopy.
    - Tracer: Fe XXV emission from the intracluster medium.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure broadening of intracluster-medium emission lines.
    - Localize the dominant line emission in projected cluster-core radii.
    - Interpret the velocity dispersion as turbulence from a quasar-driven shock.
    - Estimate the feedback energy relative to quasar radiative output.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Fe XXV lines imply velocity dispersion of about `300 km s^-1`.
    - The broadened emission originates mainly at radii of `20-100 kpc`.
    - The inferred energy injected beyond `>20 kpc` is `>=1-10%` of the quasar radiative energy.
    - This efficiency is far above previous multiwavelength estimates of `<=0.01%` for this object class.
  - **Position:** high-value feedback entry because it gives a direct X-ray turbulence measurement on cluster-core scales.

- [2607.25035](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25035) James H. Matthews `How massive and clumpy must a quasar wind be to create emission line blueshifts?`
  - **Question:** what mass flux is required if C IV emission-line blueshifts form in quasar winds?
  - **Data:**
    - Observable: blue asymmetries in luminous-quasar C IV `1550 Angstrom` emission.
    - Model inputs: line-forming region density, ionization state, volume filling factor, and terminal velocity.
    - Wind comparison: broad absorption line-like outflows with `v_infinity~10000 km s^-1`.
  - **Method:**
    - Build analytic one-dimensional and numerical two-dimensional wind models.
    - Estimate the mass required to maintain the line-forming gas.
    - Compare smooth-wind, clumpy-wind, and swept-up ambient-gas scenarios.
    - Relate terminal velocity to feedback power.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - C IV blueshifts require wind mass outflow rates of `~50 f_V` times the accretion rate.
    - Smooth disk winds are disfavored under the adopted assumptions.
    - Clumpy winds or swept-up ambient gas reduce the mass-budget tension.
    - If the wind is also the BAL outflow, its kinetic power can be feedback-relevant.
  - **Position:** useful quasar-feedback theory paper because it translates a common ultraviolet-line signature into a mass-loading constraint.

- [2607.24929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24929) Cheqiu Lyu et al. `Observational Evidence for Anisotropic Metal Excess around Galaxies`
  - **Question:** can neighboring galaxies carry a measurable chemical-enrichment signature from anisotropic outflows?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: DESI galaxy pairs.
    - Sample: `1433` pairs.
    - Geometry: neighboring galaxies aligned with the minor axis of massive primaries.
    - Separation range: projected distances of `15-60 kpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure gas-phase metallicity in neighboring galaxies as a function of azimuthal alignment.
    - Compare minor-axis and other orientations around massive primaries.
    - Test distance dependence in `15-30 kpc` and `30-60 kpc` bins.
    - Compare the qualitative trend with IllustrisTNG predictions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Minor-axis neighbors show metallicity excess of `14.6%+/-3.7%` to `24.2%+/-2.6%`.
    - The `15-30 kpc` bin gives a marginal detection above `92%` confidence.
    - The `30-60 kpc` bin gives a stronger signal above `98%` confidence.
    - The geometry is consistent with enrichment by galactic outflows.
  - **Position:** high-value baryon-cycle entry because it gives an observational chemical counterpart to anisotropic wind escape.

- [2607.25800](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25800) Cheng Cheng et al. `HI Depletion Begins Well Beyond the Virial Radius: A FAST Stacking Study of 36 Galaxy Clusters to 5R200`
  - **Question:** how far from cluster centers does neutral-gas depletion begin?
  - **Data:**
    - Radio data: FAST All Sky H I survey stacking.
    - Spectroscopy: DESI-heavy galaxy redshift catalog.
    - Systems: `36` local clusters at `z<0.07`.
    - Radial reach: projected distances out to `5R200`.
  - **Method:**
    - Stack 21-cm spectra for cluster members.
    - Measure average H I mass and `M_HI/M_*`.
    - Compare cluster members with field galaxies at fixed color.
    - Interpret radial trends as stripping, preprocessing, and large-scale-environment effects.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - H I mass and `M_HI/M_*` decline toward cluster centers by about `0.5 dex`.
    - Galaxies remain H I-poor relative to the field even at `5R200`.
    - Blue cluster members still show about `0.5 dex` lower `M_HI/M_*` than field galaxies of similar color.
    - Gas depletion begins before full optical transformation.
  - **Position:** important environment paper because it moves gas quenching into the infall-region and filament-preprocessing regime.

## Survey Methods, Reionization Inference, and Cosmic Structure

- [2607.24917](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24917) Anirban Roy et al. `Interlopers as Signal in Line Intensity Mapping`
  - **Question:** when should line-intensity-mapping analyses keep interlopers rather than remove them?
  - **Data:**
    - Forecasts: SPHEREx H-alpha maps with [O III], H-beta, and [O II] interlopers.
    - Forecasts: FYST [C II] maps with CO interlopers.
    - Observable: projected line emission from multiple redshift slices in one observed-frequency channel.
  - **Method:**
    - Treat possible line identities and redshifts as part of the cosmological model.
    - Formulate interloper handling as a Fisher decision problem.
    - Require both precision improvement and parameter bias below a chosen tolerance.
    - Compare interloper removal with joint target-plus-interloper modeling.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Interlopers can act as additional large-scale-structure tracers.
    - Removing them is not always optimal for cosmological inference.
    - The best strategy depends on precision gains and tolerable bias.
    - Multi-line forecasts can exploit projection information instead of treating it only as contamination.
  - **Position:** high-priority survey-method entry because it changes how SPHEREx-like and far-infrared intensity maps may be analyzed.

- [2607.25722](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25722) Yuxiang Qin et al. `Robust Lyalpha forest constraints on reionization from semi-analytic galaxy formation models`
  - **Question:** how sensitive are Lyman-alpha forest reionization histories to galaxy-source modeling choices?
  - **Data:**
    - Model: Meraxes semi-analytic galaxy formation and reionization model.
    - Volume: `(210 h^-1 cMpc)^3`.
    - Resolution extension: merger trees augmented down to the atomic-cooling threshold.
    - Source variants: escape fraction tied to halo mass, stellar mass, star-formation rate, specific star-formation rate, or star-forming disk surface density.
  - **Method:**
    - Build multiple ionizing-source prescriptions.
    - Include variants with accreting black holes and dense CGM attenuation.
    - Calibrate each model to relevant high-redshift observables.
    - Compare inferred reionization histories against Lyman-alpha forest constraints.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Lyman-alpha forest inference can be stress-tested against physically different source models.
    - Escape-fraction parameterization is a central systematic in translating galaxies into ionizing backgrounds.
    - Black-hole contribution and CGM attenuation are explicit reionization-model degrees of freedom.
  - **Position:** useful reionization-inference paper because it tests whether forest constraints survive source-model flexibility.

- [2607.26058](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26058) Aswin P. Vijayan et al. `The evolution of galaxy dust scaling relations in the COLIBRE simulations`
  - **Question:** how do dust scaling relations evolve when cosmological simulations track dust growth, destruction, and grain sizes?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: COLIBRE cosmological volumes up to `(400 cMpc)^3`.
    - Resolution range: baryonic mass resolution from `10^5` to `10^7 M_sun`.
    - Redshift range: `0<=z<=15`.
    - Tracked quantities: dust mass function, cosmic dust density, dust-to-gas ratio, dust-to-metal ratio, grain species, and grain sizes.
  - **Method:**
    - Evolve dust production, growth, destruction, and grain-size distributions in a multiphase ISM.
    - Compare scaling relations with observations across redshift.
    - Test resolution sensitivity and gas-phase selection effects.
    - Examine extreme submillimeter galaxies as a stress test.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Silicates dominate the dust mass at `>=70%` across epochs.
    - Large grains dominate at `z>=5` but decline toward parity with small grains by `z=0`.
    - At `z<1`, dust mass functions match observations well, while cosmic dust density is high by `<=0.3 dex`.
    - The simulations underpredict the most dust-rich bright submillimeter galaxies at `z>=2`.
  - **Position:** useful galaxy-formation-method entry because dust prescriptions directly affect high-redshift SED interpretation and obscured-galaxy inference.

- [2607.24903](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24903) Akanksha Bij et al. `CHORD HI-Galaxy Survey Forecasts: Searching for nearby dark galaxies and high redshift giants`
  - **Question:** what H I galaxy population can CHORD recover in blind drift-scan surveys?
  - **Data:**
    - Facility: Canadian Hydrogen Observatory and Radio-transient Detector.
    - Survey geometry: untargeted drift scans at `+20 deg < dec < +80 deg`.
    - Forecasts: fiducial `1`-year and `5`-year survey realizations.
    - Input: known H I mass function over `10^5 < M_HI/M_sun < 10^11`.
  - **Method:**
    - Draw survey realizations from the H I mass function.
    - Forecast source counts for multiple survey durations.
    - Estimate the impact of radio-frequency interference and spectroscopic source confusion.
    - Identify low-mass and high-mass regimes where CHORD changes current census limits.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - A `5`-year survey should exceed current H I catalogs by about an order of magnitude in source counts.
    - CHORD can push the low-mass H I census to `M_HI~10^5.5 M_sun`.
    - It should detect about `10^3` gas-rich giants with `M_HI>=10^10.5 M_sun` at `0.3<z<0.5`.
    - RFI and source confusion remain important forecast limitations.
  - **Position:** useful survey-feasibility paper because it maps the neutral-gas discovery space opened by next-generation radio surveys.

- [2607.26009](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26009) Qinglin Ma et al. `Anisotropic Secondary Bias of Dark Matter Haloes in a LCDM Universe`
  - **Question:** how does halo secondary bias vary with direction relative to the halo major axis?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: `z=0` TNG300-1-Dark.
    - Bias measures: ordinary orientation-averaged secondary bias and anisotropic secondary bias.
    - Environmental descriptors: halo-environment alignment, outer matter anisotropy, and tidal anisotropy.
  - **Method:**
    - Match halo samples by tidal anisotropy, halo-environment alignment, or outer matter anisotropy.
    - Compare orientation-averaged and anisotropic secondary bias.
    - Test dependence on formation time, concentration, triaxiality, spin, and axis ratio.
    - Interpret directional bias through filamentary alignment.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Matching tidal anisotropy suppresses much of ordinary secondary bias.
    - Anisotropic secondary bias remains distinct from the orientation-averaged effect.
    - Spin definitions and minor-to-major axis ratio show strong anisotropic behavior.
    - Slowly rotating and more elongated halos align more strongly with filamentary structure.
  - **Position:** useful LSS theory entry because it connects halo orientation, secondary bias, and filament alignment in a way that can matter for environment statistics.
