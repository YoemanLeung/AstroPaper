## Executive View

The strongest high-redshift galaxy entry is [2607.12016](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12016) Luca Costantin, which uses JWST/NIRSpec IFU spectroscopy of the `z=1.17` barred spiral EGS-24154 to connect an early stellar bar, spatially resolved stellar populations, quenching in the bar region, and AGN-driven outflows. [2607.12129](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12129) Homer Davila Gutierrez reports a JWST/NIRCam gravitational-arc candidate behind MACS J0308.9+2645 at `z_phot=4.4`, with lens-model support and estimated magnification `|mu|=7`.

The best cosmic star-formation and high-redshift rate pair is the JADES Transient Survey sequence. [2607.12018](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12018) Christa DeCoursey measures volumetric supernova rates from `83` JWST candidates out to `z~5`. [2607.12028](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12028) Christian Vassallo uses those rates to reconstruct the cosmic star-formation history and to test how dust-obscured star formation affects missed core-collapse supernova fractions.

Gas, environment, and feedback dominate the rest of the relevant list. [2607.12361](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12361) Runyu Zhu stacks DESI quasar sightlines to map Mg II around brightest cluster galaxies at `z~0.55`, finding suppressed cool CGM inside `200 kpc` and excess gas out to `10 Mpc`. [2607.12024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12024) A. Le Reste uses `7641` SDSS mergers to show that merger progression enhances `O32`, linking low-redshift merger physics to ionizing-photon escape analogs. [2607.12952](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12952) Rushikesh Bhutkar uses SMA observations of the compact steep-spectrum radio source 3C 303.1 to constrain cold-gas depletion under young radio-jet feedback.

## High-Redshift Galaxies, JWST Structure, and Cosmic Star Formation

- [2607.12016](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12016) Luca Costantin et al. `The onset of stellar bars at Cosmic Noon. Bar-driven quenching and AGN co-evolution in a mature disc galaxy`
  - **Question:** how can an early stellar bar affect disc assembly, quenching, and AGN feedback at Cosmic Noon?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: EGS-24154, a barred spiral galaxy at `z=1.17`.
    - Instrument: JWST/NIRSpec medium-resolution IFU spectroscopy.
    - Diagnostics: spatially resolved stellar populations, emission lines, dynamics, and outflow constraints.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit the IFU spectra to derive light- and mass-weighted ages and metallicities.
    - Reconstruct the spatially resolved star-formation history.
    - Diagnose ionization mechanisms from emission-line ratios.
    - Model the galaxy dynamics and a biconical AGN outflow.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - EGS-24154 is a baryon-dominated, gas-rich disc.
    - More than `90%` of its stellar mass formed when the Universe was `~2 Gyr` old.
    - The bar stellar population began forming near `z~5`, close to the time of disc assembly.
    - The bar region shows a star-formation desert, implying quenching over several Gyr.
    - AGN feedback may have limited central mass buildup and allowed the bar to grow.
  - **Position:** read first because it gives a rare IFU-based view of bar-driven structural evolution before the local-Universe regime.

- [2607.12129](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12129) Homer Davila Gutierrez `Discovery of a gravitational arc candidate at photometric redshift 4.4 in MACS J0308.9+2645 from a catalogue-based search of JWST imaging`
  - **Question:** can public JWST imaging reveal previously missed high-redshift lensed arcs in known cluster fields?
  - **Data:**
    - Lens: RELICS cluster MACS J0308.9+2645 at `z_l=0.356`.
    - Imaging: public JWST/NIRCam data from program GO-5293.
    - Candidate: tangential arc A1 with six-band photometry from `1.15-4.10 micron`.
  - **Method:**
    - Search the catalog for elongated tangential sources near the cluster critical region.
    - Fit the photometric redshift of the candidate arc.
    - Compare the position with an independent Zitrin-LTM-Gauss lens model.
    - Check against published multiple-image inventories and archival databases.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The candidate has `z_phot=4.4(+0.1/-0.3)` and `P(z>3)=0.99`.
    - The axis ratio is `a/b=6.5` and the catalogued length is `3.9 arcsec`.
    - The median projected separation from the tangential critical curve is `4.6 arcsec`.
    - The median absolute magnification estimate is `|mu|=7`.
    - Spectroscopic confirmation and dedicated lens modeling are still needed.
  - **Position:** useful high-redshift lensing target because it is a concrete follow-up candidate from existing JWST cluster imaging.

- [2607.12018](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12018) Christa DeCoursey et al. `The JADES Transient Survey II: Volumetric Supernova Rates out to z~5`
  - **Question:** what direct supernova-rate constraints can JWST provide at `z~2-5`?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: JADES Transient Survey in the JADES Deep Field.
    - Area: `~25 arcmin^2`.
    - Imaging: deep multi-band, multi-epoch JWST/NIRCam coverage.
    - Sample: `83` supernova candidates.
  - **Method:**
    - Classify candidates using single-epoch SEDs where light curves or spectra are unavailable.
    - Simulate `~23,000` core-collapse and Type Ia supernova mock SEDs over `0.7<=z<=5`.
    - Derive rates for a full sample and a restricted gold sample.
    - Compare rates with cosmic star-formation-rate-density expectations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Full-sample core-collapse rates are `6.2(+2.2/-1.7)x10^-4 yr^-1 Mpc^-3` at `2.06<=z<2.78`.
    - The corresponding rate is `4.1(+1.5/-1.1)x10^-4 yr^-1 Mpc^-3` at `2.78<=z<=5.06`.
    - The rates broadly match galaxy-luminosity-based star-formation history expectations.
    - The sample tentatively shows the predicted decline beyond cosmic noon.
    - The full-sample Type Ia rate is `0.3(+0.3/-0.2)x10^-4 yr^-1 Mpc^-3` at `1.92<=z<3.60`.
  - **Position:** high-value transient paper because it gives direct stellar-death constraints in the redshift range where galaxy-based SFR estimates are still uncertain.

- [2607.12028](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12028) Christian Vassallo et al. `The JADES Transient Survey III: Linking Core-Collapse Supernova Rates to Cosmic Star Formation`
  - **Question:** how consistently do core-collapse supernova rates trace the cosmic star-formation history out to `z=5`?
  - **Data:**
    - Rate inputs: new JADES Transient Survey high-redshift rates plus published CCSN rates.
    - Redshift range: `0<=z<=5`.
    - Model ingredients: progenitor mass ranges, CCSN production efficiency, stellar initial mass function, and dust-obscuration corrections.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit the CCSN production efficiency that maps SFRD to CCSN rates.
    - Test dependence on IMF and progenitor mass limits.
    - Reconstruct the cosmic star-formation history directly from observed CCSN rates.
    - Compare UV+IR SFRD estimates with more dust-obscured scenarios.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Observed CCSN rates are consistent with dust-corrected UV+IR SFRDs for plausible progenitor masses.
    - The CCSN-rate-based reconstruction recovers a star-formation peak near `z~2`.
    - IMF evolution has modest impact when SFRD estimates are treated consistently.
    - High SFRDs that include faint dusty millimeter populations require many missed obscured CCSNe.
    - The missed-supernova fraction rises toward `z~2` and remains substantial toward `z~5`.
  - **Position:** important companion to galaxy-count SFRD work because it tests the same cosmic history with an independent transient observable.

- [2607.12929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12929) Federica Mauro et al. `The MAGPI survey: Stellar populations radial trends and mass assembly in star-forming galaxies at z~0.3`
  - **Question:** how do inner and outer stellar populations assemble during the transition from cosmic noon to the local Universe?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: MAGPI MUSE integral-field spectroscopy.
    - Sample: `34` galaxies at `0.28<z<0.35`.
    - Diagnostics: stellar ages, metallicities, Halpha equivalent widths, and cumulative mass assembly.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit stellar populations with FADO and Starlight.
    - Build radial profiles from isophotal annuli.
    - Reconstruct star-formation histories for inner and outer regions.
    - Compare mass assembly inside and outside `1 R_eff`.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Galaxies show negative radial age gradients and negative to flat metallicity gradients.
    - Inner regions are older and more metal rich than outskirts.
    - Age differences reach `3-4 Gyr` in the most massive systems.
    - Galaxy cores formed `80%` of their stellar mass within the first `2-3 Gyr` of cosmic time.
    - Outer regions assembled more gradually and sustained star formation longer.
  - **Position:** useful low-redshift bridge because it links resolved stellar-population gradients to inside-out growth after the cosmic star-formation peak.

## CGM, Environmental Processing, and Gas Surveys

- [2607.12361](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12361) Runyu Zhu et al. `Environmental Imprints on the Assembly of the Cool Gas around Bright Cluster Galaxies`
  - **Question:** how does the cluster environment reshape the cool gas around brightest cluster galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Targets: massive brightest cluster galaxies at `z~0.55`.
    - Absorption tracer: Mg II in stacked background-quasar spectra.
    - Survey: DESI, using more than one million background quasar sightlines.
    - Radial scale: `40 kpc` to `15 Mpc`.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare BCG absorption profiles with a matched field-galaxy sample.
    - Stack sightlines to recover the mean cool-gas profile.
    - Separate inner CGM and large-scale cluster-outskirts regimes.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Cool CGM around BCGs is suppressed within `200 kpc` compared with field galaxies.
    - Mg II absorption shows an excess from `200 kpc` to `10 Mpc`.
    - The profile changes from core suppression to large-scale environmental enhancement.
    - Cluster environments may strip gas in the core while accumulating cool gas in the outskirts.
  - **Position:** read first for CGM/environment work because it maps cluster-scale cool gas with a statistical absorption experiment.

- [2607.12024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12024) A. Le Reste and K. B. Mantha `Galaxy mergers drive enhancements in ionization states`
  - **Question:** do galaxy mergers systematically raise ionization conditions across the merger sequence?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: SDSS DR17.
    - Sample: `7641` galaxy mergers with robust emission-line fluxes.
    - Classifications: spectroscopic pair analysis and visual labels from Cosmic Disco.
    - Diagnostic: `O32` as an ionization-state tracer.
  - **Method:**
    - Track `O32` across projected pair separation and visual merger stage.
    - Compare mergers with isolated controls.
    - Identify high-`O32` merger subsets and examine mass, SFR, and metallicity.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - `O32` increases continuously with merger progression.
    - Median and 90th-percentile `O32` values peak after coalescence.
    - Mergers after first pericenter have enhanced `O32` relative to isolated controls.
    - Systems with `O32>3` are mainly low-mass mergers near coalescence.
    - High `O32` is driven by higher specific SFR and lower metallicity.
  - **Position:** useful low-redshift analog paper because merger-driven ionization may connect to escape-fraction pathways in early galaxies.

- [2607.12377](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12377) Paris E. Gordon-Hall et al. `A FLASH HI absorption search in compact sources in the pilot ASKAP interplanetary scintillation field`
  - **Question:** can ASKAP interplanetary scintillation identify compact radio sources that are efficient HI absorption backlights?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: FLASH pilot data.
    - Ancillary compactness: ASKAP IPS measurements at `820 MHz`.
    - Sample: `157` bright radio sources.
    - Redshift search range: `0.4<z<1`.
  - **Method:**
    - Use the normalized scintillation index to estimate sub-arcsecond compactness.
    - Select high-compactness sources as high-covering-factor backlights.
    - Search for redshifted HI 21 cm absorption in the FLASH band.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - `18+/-5%` of ASKAP sources above `150 mJy` are highly compact with `NSI>=0.8`.
    - About half of the compact subset also has peaked radio SEDs.
    - FLASH detects two new HI absorption lines in the pilot `~30 deg^2` field.
    - One associated absorber lies at `z=0.9540` toward MRC 2125-237.
    - One likely intervening absorber lies at `z=0.4632` toward MRC 2131-241.
  - **Position:** valuable gas-survey methods paper because compactness pre-selection can improve future large HI absorption samples.

- [2607.12434](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12434) Sai Wagh et al. `Bridging the mass gap: Diffuse radio emission in GAMA galaxy groups using EMU and DINGO survey data`
  - **Question:** how common is diffuse non-thermal radio emission in galaxy groups below cluster mass?
  - **Data:**
    - Surveys: EMU `943 MHz` radio continuum and DINGO `1.37 GHz` data.
    - Parent sample: `400` GAMA galaxy groups at `z<0.1`.
    - Measurement scale: integrated emission within group radii.
  - **Method:**
    - Apply multi-resolution filtering to suppress compact radio sources.
    - Measure extended low-surface-brightness group emission.
    - Quantify backgrounds using random control regions.
    - Compare results with cluster scaling relations and MHD simulations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - `46/400` groups show candidate diffuse emission.
    - Candidate radio powers span `10^19-10^24 W Hz^-1`.
    - Stacks show a weak positive radio-power trend with halo mass.
    - The emission levels exceed simple extrapolations of cluster scaling relations.
    - Shock acceleration alone cannot explain the observed signal.
  - **Position:** relevant environment paper because group-scale non-thermal processes affect baryon-cycle and feedback interpretation outside rich clusters.

- [2607.12607](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12607) Francesco Guidi et al. `The Three Hundred project: Low Gas Fraction Clusters properties and their environment`
  - **Question:** what cluster populations are missed when X-ray or SZ selection favors gas-rich systems?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation: The Three Hundred hydrodynamical cluster simulations using Gadget-X.
    - Sample: `9858` simulated objects.
    - Redshift range: `0<=z<=0.817`.
    - Selection: outliers in the `f_g,500-M_500` relation.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify low-gas-fraction clusters statistically.
    - Compare radial gas density and temperature profiles against normal systems.
    - Test scaling relations involving temperature, entropy, and spherical Compton parameter.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Low-gas-fraction clusters are preferentially low-mass systems.
    - Their abundance rises toward low redshift.
    - Their cores show lower gas concentration and higher temperature.
    - They are extreme positive outliers in entropy scaling.
    - Their `Y_sph,500` values do not strongly deviate from the general simulated population.
  - **Position:** useful cluster-selection systematics paper because environmental and mass incompleteness can bias gas-based cluster samples.

## AGN Feedback, Black Holes, Radio Systems, and Survey Methods

- [2607.12952](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12952) Rushikesh Bhutkar et al. `SMA Observations of the Archetypal Compact Steep-spectrum Radio Source 3C 303.1`
  - **Question:** what cold-gas constraints test radio-jet feedback in the compact steep-spectrum source 3C 303.1?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: compact steep-spectrum radio source 3C 303.1.
    - Instrument: Submillimeter Array.
    - Lines and continuum: continuum at `221.1` and `271.2 GHz`, plus CO(J=3-2).
  - **Method:**
    - Measure millimeter continuum emission.
    - Search for the CO(J=3-2) line.
    - Infer gas-to-dust ratio, molecular-gas upper limits, and star-formation state.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Continuum emission is detected at both observed frequencies.
    - CO(J=3-2) is not detected.
    - The molecular-gas mass upper limit is `~2.3x10^9 Msun`.
    - The gas-to-dust ratio is at the low end of typical Galactic values.
    - Moderate but declining star formation plus low gas content is consistent with shock heating or gas removal by the AGN.
  - **Position:** useful young-radio-source paper because compact jets may be caught during their strongest coupling phase with host cold gas.

- [2607.12964](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12964) T. M. O. Franzen et al. `Dynamical modelling of giant radio quasars in the HETDEX Spring Field`
  - **Question:** what do giant radio quasar lobe properties imply about jet power, age, and ambient medium density?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `15` giant radio quasars in the HETDEX Spring Field.
    - Radio frequencies: `54 MHz` to `4.85/15.5 GHz`.
    - Comparison: compiled Fanaroff-Riley type II radio-source sample.
  - **Method:**
    - Model radio morphology and spectra.
    - Fit dynamical evolution models to infer lobe ages, jet powers, and ambient densities.
    - Compare giant radio quasars with smaller radio quasars.
    - Use partial-rank tests to separate age, size, and jet-power trends.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Giant radio quasars evolve in lower-density environments than smaller radio quasars with similar jet powers.
    - Inferred central core densities are sensitive to the assumed ambient-density profile.
    - Jet power anti-correlates with lobe age with `R=-0.67`.
    - Jet power also anti-correlates more weakly with linear size, with `R=-0.32`.
    - A fundamental relation links jet power, lobe age, and lobe size.
  - **Position:** useful radio-AGN environment paper because it connects jet energetics to large-scale source growth.

- [2607.12708](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12708) Julian Chan et al. `Perturber-Driven Dynamics of Supermassive Black Hole Binaries in Galaxy Merger`
  - **Question:** can massive perturbers in galaxy mergers create genuine scatter in supermassive-black-hole-binary eccentricities?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulation source: a major merger from IllustrisTNG100-1.
    - Resimulation code: Griffin `N`-body.
    - Perturbers: two suites with `10^7 Msun` and `10^8 Msun` perturbers plus a no-perturber control.
  - **Method:**
    - Redistribute `f_target=0.1` of the primary bulge mass into equal-mass perturbers.
    - Run four realizations per perturber scenario.
    - Measure binary eccentricity scatter and event-aligned energy and angular-momentum residuals.
    - Compare with binary-single scattering expectations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The control case gives eccentricity scatter `sigma_e~0.11`.
    - The `10^7 Msun` perturber case gives `sigma_e~0.115`, indistinguishable from the control.
    - The `10^8 Msun` case gives `sigma_e~0.26`, about `2.4x` above the control floor.
    - The excess coincides with stronger near-impulsive torques.
    - Expected perturber populations in massive ellipticals likely fall mostly below the regime that changes gravitational-wave-background-relevant binaries.
  - **Position:** relevant SMBH-binary paper because it tests whether merger substructure changes PTA-source population predictions.

- [2607.13005](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13005) Hanneke C. Woudenberg et al. `Alignment of the Milky Way and M31 with their cosmic environment. New insights from constrained Local Group simulations`
  - **Question:** how does the Local Sheet influence the halo and disk orientations of Milky Way and M31 analogs?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: constrained Local Group simulations reproducing the two main halos and the Local Sheet.
    - Targets: Milky Way and M31 analogs.
    - Diagnostics: halo shapes, spins, disk angular momenta, merger history, and sheet orientation.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure halo-shape and spin alignment relative to Local Sheet analogs.
    - Track the effect of massive mergers over the last `8-10 Gyr`.
    - Compare disk angular momentum with halo minor axes and spins.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Simulated MW/M31 analog halos are slightly rounder than similar random-environment halos in the literature.
    - The sheet normal preferentially aligns with halo minor axes but not with halo spins.
    - Present-day disk angular momenta align closely with halo minor axes, with median `15(+15/-8)` degrees at `R_vir`.
    - Disk orientation is often set by one of the two largest recent mergers.
    - The Milky Way outer-halo orientation may reflect Magellanic Clouds or Sagittarius accretion from within the Local Sheet.
  - **Position:** useful Local Group environment paper because it ties galaxy orientation to constrained cosmic-web context.

- [2607.12396](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12396) Dan Miao and Guang-Xing Li `Demystifying image-recovery from radio interferometers: toward a multiscale predictive model`
  - **Question:** can interferometric missing-flux losses be predicted in the image domain without expensive mock observations?
  - **Data:**
    - Input: simulated ALMA observations of the Perseus molecular cloud across multiple array configurations.
    - Framework: Constrained Diffusion Decomposition scale-space components.
    - Target systematic: missing short-spacing response and scale-dependent flux recovery.
  - **Method:**
    - Decompose input images into continuous multiscale components.
    - Fit the spatial-filtering response with a one-dimensional error-function form.
    - Reconstruct a predicted interferometric image by applying the response to each scale component.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Compact structures are recovered efficiently while extended emission declines toward the maximum recoverable scale.
    - The CDD-erf model predicts spatially filtered interferometric images directly in the image domain.
    - The method provides an analytic bridge between true sky brightness and interferometric observations.
  - **Position:** practical survey-methods paper because missing-flux corrections affect gas masses, star-formation efficiencies, and extended-emission comparisons.
