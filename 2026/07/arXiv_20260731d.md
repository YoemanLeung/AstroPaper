It is the strongest match to AGN environments, merger-driven triggering, high-redshift black-hole growth, and future LISA progenitor work.

## Quasars, AGN Demographics, and Black-Hole Growth

- [2607.27390](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27390) Ekaterine Dadiani et al. `Cosmic Pairs: A DESI Census of Dual and Offset AGN as Precursors to Massive Black Hole Binaries`
  - **Question:** how large is the DESI spectroscopic population of dual and offset AGN candidates, and what does it imply for massive black-hole-binary progenitors?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: DESI DR1 spectroscopy.
    - Sample: more than `7000` dual AGN and `27000` galaxy pairs containing one AGN.
    - Redshift range: `0<z<3.6`.
    - Simulation comparison: ASTRID massive-black-hole merger predictions.
  - **Method:**
    - Select kpc-scale dual and offset AGN candidates from DESI spectra.
    - Correct the sample for observational systematics.
    - Compare host star-formation behavior as a function of projected separation.
    - Use ASTRID to estimate later black-hole merger and LISA-detectable fractions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The sample expands known dual AGN counts by `~1-2` orders of magnitude at `0.2<z<0.4`.
    - It includes about `50` dwarf dual AGN candidates.
    - It triples the census at `z>2`.
    - Dual AGN prefer small separations, consistent with merger-driven triggering.
    - The predicted fraction whose central black holes merge by `z~0` reaches `~76%` by `z~2`.
    - The LISA-detectable fraction peaks near `~37%` at `z~0.9`.
  - **Position:** read first because it turns DESI spectroscopy into a direct bridge between galaxy mergers, AGN triggering, and massive-black-hole-binary demographics.

- [2607.27329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27329) Shir Aviram et al. `An Exploratory Analysis of New Large Gaia-informed Quasar Samples in SDSS-V`
  - **Question:** can Gaia- and WISE-informed targeting fill gaps in all-sky quasar selection for SDSS-V?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: SDSS-V dual-hemisphere spectroscopy.
    - Selection methods: GUA and Skewt-QSO using mainly WISE and Gaia information.
    - Sample: more than `250000` spectroscopically confirmed quasars.
    - Redshift reach: `z~5`.
  - **Method:**
    - Apply Gaia-informed and optical-IR color selection to SDSS-V targets.
    - Measure confirmation purity and contaminants.
    - Decompose spectra to estimate luminosities, black-hole masses, and Eddington ratios.
    - Compare the physical distributions with previous SDSS quasar catalogs.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The sample includes tens of thousands of newly identified southern quasars.
    - The selection purity is well above `80%`.
    - The main contaminants are M-type stars.
    - Bolometric luminosities span `10^44-10^48 erg s^-1`.
    - Black-hole masses span `10^6-10^10 M_sun`.
    - Accretion rates span `L/L_Edd~0.01-1`.
  - **Position:** high-value infrastructure paper because it improves all-sky quasar samples for AGN demographics, environments, and future southern surveys.

- [2607.27887](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27887) W. Roster et al. `The eROSITA X-ray luminosity function of active galactic nuclei`
  - **Question:** how does the eROSITA AGN sample reshape X-ray luminosity function constraints across luminosity and redshift?
  - **Data:**
    - Facility: SRG/eROSITA.
    - Observable: soft X-ray AGN luminosity function.
    - Dynamic range: about `8` luminosity decades.
    - Redshift reach: `z~6`.
  - **Method:**
    - Build an eROSITA AGN sample with redshift information.
    - Fit a redshift-dependent smoothly broken power-law XLF.
    - Allow all XLF model parameters to evolve continuously with redshift.
    - Infer SMBH accretion history and the optical/UV-missed AGN contribution.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - eROSITA provides rare luminous AGN statistics that pencil-beam surveys cannot supply alone.
    - The XLF supports luminosity-dependent evolution across a broad luminosity-redshift plane.
    - The sample can constrain the SMBH accretion history and missed AGN populations.
  - **Position:** important survey-demographics entry because X-ray selection anchors obscuration-corrected black-hole growth.

- [2607.27675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27675) P. Arevalo et al. `The X-ray-Weak Tail of the Quasar Population: Physical Properties and Redshift Evolution of Variability-Selected AGN in eFEDS`
  - **Question:** what fraction of optically variable AGN candidates are weak or undetected in eROSITA X-rays?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: eROSITA Final Equatorial-Depth Survey.
    - Selection: optical variability-selected AGN candidates.
    - Spectroscopy: DESI public data products for matched samples.
  - **Method:**
    - Cross-match variability-selected AGN with eFEDS X-ray AGN.
    - Separate X-ray detected and X-ray undetected subsets.
    - Compare physical properties and redshift evolution.
    - Use spectroscopic classifications to assess selection purity.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The variability-selected candidates have `98%` broad-line AGN/QSO confirmation purity.
    - About `25%` lack eFEDS X-ray counterparts.
    - X-ray detected and undetected subsets differ in physical properties.
    - Optical variability finds AGN missed by current X-ray depth.
  - **Position:** useful AGN-selection paper because X-ray weakness affects completeness in SMBH growth censuses.

- [2607.27860](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27860) Wen-Qiang Liang et al. `Revealing the Physical Driver of the Baldwin Effect: Gas Density in the Broad-Line Region`
  - **Question:** what physical parameter drives the MgII Baldwin effect in radio-quiet and radio-loud quasars?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `41159` radio quasars from SDSS and LOFAR.
    - Observable: MgII broad-emission equivalent width and continuum luminosity.
    - Populations: radio-quiet and radio-loud quasars.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure the Baldwin-effect slope as a function of Eddington ratio.
    - Compare radio-loud and radio-quiet populations at fixed accretion state.
    - Run photoionization simulations for BLR gas conditions.
    - Test whether gas density controls the slope.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The Baldwin-effect slope correlates positively with Eddington ratio.
    - Radio-loud quasars show steeper slopes than radio-quiet quasars at fixed Eddington ratio.
    - Photoionization modeling identifies BLR gas density as the primary driver.
  - **Position:** useful broad-line-region paper because it connects a long-standing quasar scaling relation to gas-density physics.

- [2607.28501](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28501) Isaiah S. Cox et al. `X-ray Absorption Variability in NGC 1142: Another Constraint on the Nature of the Torus/Broad-Line Region in Active Galactic Nuclei`
  - **Question:** what does multi-epoch X-ray column-density variability reveal about AGN obscuring clouds?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: NGC 1142.
    - Observations: `9` X-ray epochs over `~20` years.
    - New campaign: `5` NuSTAR plus XMM-Newton observations in `2024-2025`.
    - Spectral models: UXCLUMPY, XSKIRTor, and RXTorusD.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit physical torus models to each X-ray epoch.
    - Measure line-of-sight `N_H` variability.
    - Compare observed variability amplitudes with timescale trends.
    - Test simulated obscuration curves for different cloud locations and distributions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - NGC 1142 follows the known trend that `N_H` variability is easier to detect with more observations.
    - Variability amplitude depends on epoch separation.
    - The multi-year record constrains the location and distribution of obscuring clouds.
  - **Position:** lower-redshift AGN context paper because torus and BLR cloud structure controls obscured accretion diagnostics.

## Gas, Feedback, and Large-Scale Environments

- [2607.27319](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27319) Jonathan J. Davies et al. `The influence of feedback on the baryonic content of haloes in the COLIBRE simulations`
  - **Question:** how do supernova and AGN feedback set halo gas fractions across halo mass?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: COLIBRE cosmological galaxy-formation runs.
    - Observable: halo gas mass fraction versus halo mass, `f_gas^200-M_200`.
    - Comparison data: Chandra, XMM-Newton, eROSITA stacks, and kSZ constraints.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure gas fractions as a function of halo mass and redshift.
    - Separate supernova-dominated and AGN-dominated feedback regimes.
    - Compare fiducial and hybrid AGN feedback models.
    - Benchmark against EAGLE and observational constraints.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - COLIBRE predicts a non-monotonic gas-fraction relation peaking near `M_200~10^11.5-12 M_sun`.
    - Supernova feedback expels halo gas below the peak.
    - AGN feedback depletes halo gas in groups and clusters.
    - Hybrid thermal plus jet-driven AGN feedback lowers gas fractions toward eROSITA and kSZ measurements.
    - Halo gas fractions remain sensitive feedback diagnostics even when galaxy populations look similar.
  - **Position:** high-value baryon-cycle entry because it connects feedback modeling to gas observables used in group and cluster cosmology.

- [2607.27977](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27977) Sophie L. Eden et al. `A search for HI absorption in distant star-forming galaxies with ASKAP-FLASH - II. Direct observations and stacking of 21 cm line`
  - **Question:** can ASKAP-FLASH detect associated or intervening H I 21-cm absorption in galaxies at intermediate redshift?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: ASKAP-FLASH H I 21-cm absorption survey.
    - Samples: star-forming galaxies and a radio-galaxy control sample.
    - Redshift range: `0.4<z<1.0`.
    - Detection: NVSS J214954-004657 at `z_HI=0.863`.
  - **Method:**
    - Search directly for H I absorption in target spectra.
    - Stack associated and intervening galaxy spectra.
    - Order associated stacks by radio-source brightness.
    - Compare stacking behavior as weaker sources are added.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - One new H I 21-cm absorption line is detected in a radio galaxy at `z_HI=0.863`.
    - Associated-galaxy stacks show tentative absorption when ordered by descending radio brightness.
    - The tentative signal becomes buried as weaker sources are added.
    - No stacked intervening-galaxy signal is detected.
  - **Position:** useful neutral-gas paper because it tests how far 21-cm absorption surveys can push galaxy gas measurements beyond the local Universe.

- [2607.28140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28140) J. Dietl et al. `A study of the large-scale formation in the environment of A3266: Infalling groups, filaments, and a premerger cold front`
  - **Question:** what large-scale structures feed the dynamically active galaxy cluster Abell 3266?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: Abell 3266 and neighboring galaxy groups.
    - X-ray survey: SRG/eROSITA all-sky data.
    - Environment data: NED member galaxies and SLOW simulation comparison.
    - Radial reach: out to `3R_100`.
  - **Method:**
    - Analyze X-ray images, surface brightness, and spectra in outskirts regions.
    - Search for filaments connecting A3266 to nearby groups.
    - Compare the observed group distribution with SLOW.
    - Interpret gas temperature, metallicity, and density in the filament.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - An X-ray filament connects A3266 to its northwestern group over `L_R200-R200=1.1(-0.1,+0.5) Mpc`.
    - The filament significance is `3.6 sigma`.
    - The filament temperature is `T=1.2(-0.2,+0.3) keV`.
    - The electron density is `n_e=8(-2,+1)x10^-5 cm^-3`.
    - The gas is hotter and denser than pristine WHIM, consistent with cluster-processed infall.
  - **Position:** important environment paper because it directly images cluster assembly along large-scale structure.

- [2607.28564](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28564) Ethan O. Nadler et al. `Dark Matter Constraints from Small-Scale Cosmic Structure`
  - **Question:** what do nonlinear small-scale structure probes currently say about dark-matter microphysics?
  - **Data:**
    - Probes: dwarf galaxies, strong lensing, Lyman-alpha forest, stellar streams, and high-redshift galaxies.
    - Scale frontier: structures below `~1 Mpc`.
    - Models: warm, fuzzy, self-interacting, and Standard-Model-interacting dark matter.
  - **Method:**
    - Review how each observable responds to altered matter clustering.
    - Map astrophysical observables to microphysical model limits.
    - Identify modeling uncertainties and observational systematics.
    - Emphasize probe combination and simulation-based inference.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Small-scale structure remains the key nonlinear testbed for non-CDM physics.
    - Different probes constrain different combinations of abundance, internal structure, and tidal response.
    - Future progress depends on joint modeling rather than isolated single-probe limits.
  - **Position:** useful reference review because high-redshift galaxies and the Lyman-alpha forest are part of the small-scale dark-matter constraint set.

- [2607.27316](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27316) Kailash Raman et al. `Semi-analytic Inference of Satellite Densities in the Cold Dark Matter Model Part I. Comparison to Ultra-faint Dwarf Kinematics`
  - **Question:** are Milky Way ultra-faint dwarf densities consistent with CDM satellite expectations?
  - **Data:**
    - Systems: Milky Way ultra-faint dwarfs.
    - Measurements: stellar velocity dispersions and stellar masses.
    - Model: semi-analytic cosmological satellite generator for CDM halo populations.
  - **Method:**
    - Infer dwarf density profiles from stellar kinematics.
    - Infer separate expectations from dwarf stellar masses and the stellar-to-halo mass relation.
    - Compare observed central-density diversity with CDM satellite predictions.
    - Test systematic variations in the semi-analytic modeling.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Compact ultra-faints appear overdense relative to the CDM population expectation.
    - Larger systems appear underdense.
    - For ultra-faints with at least `10` spectroscopic stars, the discrepancy persists at `~2.4 sigma`.
    - The framework sets up a repeatable test as future surveys find more dwarf satellites.
  - **Position:** useful dwarf-galaxy benchmark because satellite densities are a direct small-scale-structure observable.

## Survey Infrastructure, Synthetic Spectra, and Nearby Context

- [2607.27467](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27467) Aswin P. Vijayan et al. `Stellar photoionisation modelling in SYNTHESIZER`
  - **Question:** can SYNTHESIZER generate stellar plus nebular emission for synthetic galaxy observations?
  - **Data:**
    - Codebase: SYNTHESIZER.
    - Emission component: photoionized gas around young stellar populations.
    - Applications: toy models and large-volume cosmological simulations.
  - **Method:**
    - Integrate photoionized gas emission into the synthetic spectral pipeline.
    - Vary stellar population synthesis models, IMF, ionization parameter, gas density, geometry, abundance pattern, depletion, and dust.
    - Quantify how assumptions affect spectral diagnostics.
    - Demonstrate use across simplified and simulation-derived galaxy populations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The package can now model stellar and nebular emission self-consistently enough for survey mock work.
    - Spectral diagnostics vary strongly with assumptions about gas and stellar populations.
    - The tool links galaxy-formation models to next-generation spectroscopic observables.
  - **Position:** useful methods paper because high-redshift line interpretation needs consistent synthetic stellar and nebular spectra.

- [2607.27662](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27662) Yunning Zhao et al. `Galactic HII regions in LAMOST Medium-Resolution Spectroscopic Survey of Nebulae`
  - **Question:** how well can LAMOST MRS-N optical spectra confirm and characterize Outer-Galaxy H II regions?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: LAMOST Medium-Resolution Spectroscopic Survey of Nebulae.
    - Base catalog: WISE Galactic H II region catalog.
    - Sample: `280` H II regions and candidates in the Outer Galaxy.
    - Longitude range: `80<l<220 deg`.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure H-alpha, [NII] 6584, and [SII] 6717,6731.
    - Use line ratios to confirm H II regions.
    - Derive electron temperature, electron density, oxygen abundance, and distances.
    - Fit radial and azimuthal gradients.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The survey confirms `255` H II regions.
    - The confirmed set includes `90` previously known regions and `165` newly classified regions.
    - The sample spans `R_gal=8.16-15.36 kpc`.
    - The inner oxygen gradient is `-0.044+/-0.010 dex kpc^-1`.
    - The outer oxygen gradient is `-0.016+/-0.005 dex kpc^-1`.
  - **Position:** lower-redshift calibration paper because H II region abundance gradients anchor chemical-evolution baselines.

- [2607.28180](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28180) Karen O'Neil et al. `Searching in HI for Massive Low Surface Brightness Galaxies: II. Completing the UGC Sample`
  - **Question:** how many additional massive low-surface-brightness galaxies have measurable H I gas?
  - **Data:**
    - Telescope: 100-m Robert C. Byrd Green Bank Telescope.
    - Line: 21-cm H I.
    - Targets: `35` low-surface-brightness galaxies.
    - Ancillary detections: `5` additional galaxies found in blank-sky or alternate-velocity regions.
  - **Method:**
    - Observe LSB targets in the H I line.
    - Measure detections and nondetections.
    - Add accidentally detected gas-rich galaxies.
    - Combine with prior LSB H I work.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - `19/35` target galaxies are detected.
    - Only `2` targets had previously published data.
    - Five additional galaxies gain first published H I information.
    - The sample extends tests of extreme gas-rich, low-surface-brightness galaxy formation.
  - **Position:** useful nearby-galaxy context because LSB systems probe inefficient star formation and high gas fractions.

- [2607.28278](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28278) K. L. Luhman `JWST's Constraints on the Substellar IMF in NGC 2024`
  - **Question:** does JWST evidence require a substellar IMF turnover or minimum mass in NGC 2024?
  - **Data:**
    - Cluster: NGC 2024, age `<1 Myr`.
    - Imaging: JWST/NIRCam.
    - Spectroscopy: archival JWST/NIRSpec.
    - Spectroscopic sample: `87` sources.
  - **Method:**
    - Classify NIRSpec targets as cluster members or contaminants.
    - Identify brown dwarfs from spectral types.
    - Compare spectroscopic members with NIRCam photometric candidates.
    - Assess completeness, extinction bias, and saturation limits.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - `45` NIRSpec targets are classified as NGC 2024 members.
    - `17` have brown-dwarf spectral types.
    - Brown-dwarf mass estimates reach `4 M_Jup`.
    - The JWST data do not show evidence for a turnover below `12 M_Jup`.
    - The minimum mass of the IMF has not been detected.
  - **Position:** context paper because JWST low-mass IMF systematics affect stellar-population assumptions in unresolved galaxies.
