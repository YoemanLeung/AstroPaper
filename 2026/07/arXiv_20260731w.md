## Reionization, JWST Galaxies, and Compact AGN

- [2607.22834](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22834) Emma Giovinazzo et al. `Reionization driven by the few: the ionizing budget of galaxies at z=5-10 from JWST/NIRSpec`
  - **Question:** which galaxies dominate the ionizing photon budget during reionization?
  - **Data:**
    - Spectroscopy: `1428` JWST/NIRSpec spectra from the DAWN JWST Archive.
    - Redshift range: `5<z<10`.
    - Population link: UV luminosity functions extended to `z=15`.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit spectra with a picket-fence escape model.
    - Infer object-level escape fractions and escaping ionizing output.
    - Combine object posteriors with UV luminosity functions.
    - Derive hydrogen-ionizing luminosity functions and neutral-fraction evolution.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The integrated budget down to `M_UV=-13` is consistent with independent reionization probes under the adopted assumptions.
    - Reionization ends near `z~5.8`.
    - Bright sources around `M_UV~-20` dominate at `z=5-6`.
    - Fainter galaxies with `M_UV>-18` contribute roughly equally at `z>6`.
    - Sources with `f_esc>10%` are about `20%` of the population but produce about `87%` of the ionizing photons.
  - **Position:** weekly read-first because it converts JWST spectroscopy into an ionizing budget rather than only a galaxy-property census.

- [2607.25801](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25801) Cheng Cheng et al. `Lyalpha Escape in JWST/NIRCam F430M-Selected Halpha Emitters at z~5.5`
  - **Question:** how much Lya escapes from H-alpha-selected star-forming galaxies near the end of reionization?
  - **Data:**
    - Selection: JWST/NIRCam F430M-excess H-alpha emitters at `z~5.5`.
    - Spectroscopy: archival VLT/MUSE for Lya.
    - Sample: `12` galaxies with star-formation rates above `~0.1 M_sun yr^-1`.
  - **Method:**
    - Use H-alpha to anchor intrinsic Lya production.
    - Search for Lya emission in MUSE spectra.
    - Combine detections and upper limits.
    - Compare escape behavior with dust, SFR, and environment.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Lya is detected in `3/12` galaxies.
    - The population-averaged Lya escape fraction has a conservative upper bound of `<0.32`.
    - Detections preferentially occur in nearly dust-free systems.
    - Three of four Lya-detected galaxies lie in a known overdense structure.
  - **Position:** key reionization-era entry because H-alpha selection reduces the circularity of Lya-selected visibility studies.

- [2607.26269](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26269) Hiroto Yanagisawa et al. `ATLAS. II. Extremely High Incidence of Balmer Line Absorption with Predominant Blueshifts in LRDs: Statistical Insights through Comparison with Type 1 AGNs`
  - **Question:** how common is Balmer-line absorption in LRDs, and does it trace inflow or outflow?
  - **Data:**
    - Spectroscopy: JWST/NIRSpec spectra from the DAWN JWST Archive plus NIRSpec/IFU observations.
    - Sample: `40` LRDs with broad H-alpha and [O III] coverage.
    - Redshift range: `z~2.5-7.2`.
    - Comparison: SDSS low-redshift type 1 AGN plus `32` literature LRD Balmer absorbers.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify H-alpha and H-beta absorption.
    - Compare incidence rates and velocity distributions with low-redshift type 1 AGN.
    - Build a combined absorber census.
    - Interpret velocities with a radiation-pressure model.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - H-alpha absorption appears in `14/40` LRDs, or `~35%`.
    - The incidence is about `850` times the `~0.04%` SDSS type 1 AGN rate.
    - The combined LRD absorber census contains `46` systems.
    - Absorber velocities span `-430` to `+140 km s^-1`.
    - `38/46` absorbers, or `83%`, are blueshifted.
  - **Position:** central LRD paper because Balmer absorption directly probes dense gas near compact high-redshift accretors.

- [2607.26177](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26177) Sophie Koudmani et al. `How to raise a supermassive black hole: interpreting early JWST AGN with the AESOPICA simulations`
  - **Question:** can early JWST AGN samples be explained by ordinary seeds, efficient accretion, and selection effects?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: AESOPICA suite of `12` cosmological simulations.
    - Volume scale: `L=60 Mpc`.
    - Seed masses: `10^2-10^5 M_sun`.
    - Mock observations: BALMERSOPICA JWST broad-line selection.
  - **Method:**
    - Vary black-hole seed masses and accretion efficiency.
    - Forward-model JWST broad-line AGN detectability.
    - Compare intrinsic and selected scaling relations.
    - Identify observables that preserve seed-channel information.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Efficient accretion can assemble the bulk of the JWST AGN population from any seed mass in the explored range.
    - Light seeds need the most favorable accretion conditions.
    - Broad-line selection naturally recovers apparently overmassive AGN.
    - Low-mass black-hole mass functions and host gas-phase metallicities remain the best seed-channel discriminants.
  - **Position:** key early-AGN theory paper because it separates growth physics from selection bias.

- [2607.22966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22966) Zheng Ma et al. `A quasar hatching from a buried red phase at z = 3.7`
  - **Question:** what does a partially exposed red quasar reveal about the transition from buried to unobscured accretion?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: JADES-GS 209777 at `z=3.711`.
    - Facilities: JWST/NIRSpec, NIRCam, MIRI, HST, MUSE, Chandra, ALMA, and VLA.
    - Gas tracers: broad lines, X-rays, H-alpha and He I absorption, O I, Mg II, Na D, [O III], and Lya.
  - **Method:**
    - Separate visible nuclear activity from embedded gas and dust.
    - Use line diagnostics to identify dense gas near the nucleus.
    - Trace multiphase disturbed and extended emission.
    - Use ALMA dust emission to place the source in an infrared-luminous regime.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Broad lines and X-rays show that the nucleus is partly exposed.
    - H-alpha and He I absorption indicate dense gas close to the nucleus.
    - Disturbed low-ionization and ionized features indicate multiphase acceleration.
    - Extended Lya emission reaches more than `20 kpc`.
    - The infrared luminosity places the system in the ULIRG regime.
  - **Position:** high-priority AGN result because it gives a JWST-era view of feedback clearing a dusty high-redshift quasar.

## Quasar Surveys, Dual AGN, and Black-Hole Demographics

- [2607.27390](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27390) Ekaterine Dadiani et al. `Cosmic Pairs: A DESI Census of Dual and Offset AGN as Precursors to Massive Black Hole Binaries`
  - **Question:** how large is the DESI spectroscopic population of dual and offset AGN candidates?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: DESI DR1.
    - Sample: more than `7000` dual AGN and `27000` galaxy pairs containing one AGN.
    - Redshift range: `0<z<3.6`.
    - Simulation comparison: ASTRID.
  - **Method:**
    - Select kpc-scale dual and offset AGN candidates.
    - Correct observational systematics.
    - Compare star-formation response in primary and secondary hosts.
    - Predict black-hole merger and LISA-detectable fractions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The census expands known dual AGN by `~1-2` orders of magnitude at `0.2<z<0.4`.
    - It includes about `50` dwarf dual AGN candidates.
    - It triples the census at `z>2`.
    - Predicted black-hole merger fractions by `z~0` reach `~76%` for systems near `z~2`.
    - LISA-detectable fractions peak near `~37%` at `z~0.9`.
  - **Position:** weekly read-first for AGN demographics because it connects galaxy pairs to massive-black-hole-binary progenitors.

- [2607.27329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27329) Shir Aviram et al. `An Exploratory Analysis of New Large Gaia-informed Quasar Samples in SDSS-V`
  - **Question:** can Gaia-informed SDSS-V targeting build a larger and purer all-sky quasar sample?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: SDSS-V.
    - Targeting: GUA and Skewt-QSO methods using mainly WISE and Gaia.
    - Sample: more than `250000` confirmed quasars reaching `z~5`.
  - **Method:**
    - Select candidates with Gaia- and WISE-informed criteria.
    - Confirm spectra with SDSS-V.
    - Estimate luminosities, black-hole masses, and accretion rates.
    - Compare distributions with previous quasar catalogs.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Tens of thousands of new southern quasars are included.
    - Selection purity is well above `80%`.
    - Bolometric luminosities span `10^44-10^48 erg s^-1`.
    - Black-hole masses span `10^6-10^10 M_sun`.
    - Eddington ratios span `0.01-1`.
  - **Position:** important infrastructure because all-sky quasar selection underpins environment, demographics, and time-domain work.

- [2607.27887](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27887) W. Roster et al. `The eROSITA X-ray luminosity function of active galactic nuclei`
  - **Question:** what does eROSITA add to AGN X-ray luminosity function constraints?
  - **Data:**
    - Facility: SRG/eROSITA.
    - Redshift reach: `z~6`.
    - Luminosity coverage: about `8` decades.
  - **Method:**
    - Build an AGN sample with redshift information.
    - Fit a continuously evolving smoothly broken power-law XLF.
    - Infer SMBH accretion history.
    - Estimate optical/UV-missed AGN populations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - eROSITA supplies rare luminous AGN statistics across a wide area.
    - The XLF supports luminosity-dependent evolution.
    - The sample constrains both accretion history and missed AGN fractions.
  - **Position:** central AGN survey result because X-ray-selected demographics anchor obscuration-aware black-hole growth.

- [2607.25696](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25696) Angel Rodriguez Barbosa et al. `Tracing Obscured AGN Contribution and Number Fraction Across 0 < z < 6 with JWST`
  - **Question:** how does obscured AGN prevalence evolve when JWST mid-infrared coverage is included?
  - **Data:**
    - Fields: JWST SMILES and JADES in GOODS-S.
    - Wavelength baseline: `0.4-25 micron`.
    - Sample: `278` AGN across `0<z<6`.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit optical-to-mid-infrared SEDs with CIGALE.
    - Use MIRI to recover reprocessed AGN emission.
    - Measure AGN infrared contribution and number fraction with redshift.
    - Compare the larger SMILES footprint with earlier CEERS-based samples.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The sample is about `7` times larger than comparable CEERS analyses.
    - AGN fractions rise from `<=5%` at `z<2` to about `30%` at higher redshift.
    - Median AGN infrared contribution increases by up to `~0.15`.
    - AGN contribution and number fraction are nearly static over `log(L/L_sun)~8-12`.
  - **Position:** high-value JWST AGN-demographics paper because mid-infrared selection captures obscured accretion missed at shorter wavelengths.

## Gas, Feedback, and Large-Scale Structure

- [2607.21719](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21719) Lucas Tortora et al. `Outflows in super-Eddington quasars drive clumpy circumgalactic medium and extended H-alpha nebulae at z >= 6`
  - **Question:** how does rapid quasar growth reshape the ISM and CGM of massive systems at `z~6`?
  - **Data:**
    - Simulations: zoom-ins of a massive protocluster at `z~6`.
    - Physics variants: FABLE plus earlier black-hole seeding and mildly super-Eddington accretion.
    - Observable link: H-alpha nebulae, obscuration, and CGM neutral gas.
  - **Method:**
    - Compare fiducial and enhanced early-growth histories.
    - Track obscuration, ionizing escape channels, and metal-enriched outflows.
    - Follow cold neutral CGM clumps after feedback blow-out.
    - Predict extended H-alpha emission with radiative-transfer calculations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The central quasar remains Compton-thick for much of its early evolution.
    - Strong feedback opens escape channels.
    - Metal-enriched outflows generate cold, fast, neutral CGM clumps.
    - Neutral-hydrogen covering fractions increase after blow-out.
    - H-alpha nebulae become larger and brighter when feedback is stronger and obscuration is weaker.
  - **Position:** key CGM-feedback paper because it ties high-redshift quasar growth to JWST-observable extended gas.

- [2607.24911](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24911) Satoshi Yamada et al. `Vigorous turbulence driven by quasar-mode feedback in a cluster core`
  - **Question:** can a central quasar drive energetically significant turbulence beyond galactic scales?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: H1821+643 at `z=0.297`.
    - Instrument: XRISM.
    - Tracer: Fe XXV emission from the intracluster medium.
  - **Method:**
    - Measure high-resolution X-ray line broadening.
    - Localize line emission across cluster-core radii.
    - Interpret velocity dispersion as quasar-driven turbulence.
    - Estimate injected energy relative to radiative output.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Fe XXV line widths imply velocity dispersion of about `300 km s^-1`.
    - Broad emission originates mainly at `20-100 kpc`.
    - Energy injected beyond `20 kpc` is `>=1-10%` of the quasar radiative energy.
    - This is far above previous multiwavelength estimates of `<=0.01%`.
  - **Position:** important feedback entry because XRISM gives a direct turbulence measurement on cluster-core scales.

- [2607.24071](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24071) Xuanyi Wu et al. `Evidence for Azimuthally Anisotropic MgII Absorption around DESI Luminous Red Galaxies`
  - **Question:** is cool or warm CGM gas around massive quiescent galaxies azimuthally anisotropic?
  - **Data:**
    - Foregrounds: DESI DR1 luminous red galaxies.
    - Backgrounds: quasar spectra.
    - Redshift bins: `0.4<z_LRG<0.75` and `0.75<z_LRG<1.1`.
    - Radius range: `0.01-1.0` proper Mpc.
  - **Method:**
    - Assign an MgII doublet-window equivalent width to each LRG-quasar pair.
    - Subtract a redshift-matched random control.
    - Measure equivalent width by radius and azimuth.
    - Test major-axis enhancement with position-angle randomization.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Inner MgII absorption is stronger at `0.4<z_LRG<0.75` than at `0.75<z_LRG<1.1`.
    - Major-axis sightlines show enhanced absorption at `r_p~30-80 kpc`.
    - The major-minus-minor difference over `0.03-0.077 Mpc` is `0.184+/-0.075 Angstrom`.
    - The randomization probability is `p=0.013`.
  - **Position:** high-value CGM result because it extends azimuthal absorption mapping to massive quiescent halos with DESI statistics.

- [2607.28140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28140) J. Dietl et al. `A study of the large-scale formation in the environment of A3266: Infalling groups, filaments, and a premerger cold front`
  - **Question:** what large-scale structures feed the active cluster Abell 3266?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: Abell 3266 and nearby groups.
    - X-ray survey: SRG/eROSITA.
    - Radial reach: `3R_100`.
    - Environment comparison: NED member galaxies and SLOW simulations.
  - **Method:**
    - Analyze X-ray images, surface brightness, and spectra in cluster outskirts.
    - Search for group-connected filaments.
    - Infer gas temperature, metallicity, and density.
    - Compare with simulated local-web structure.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - An X-ray filament connects A3266 to its northwestern group over `1.1(-0.1,+0.5) Mpc`.
    - The filament is detected at `3.6 sigma`.
    - The temperature is `1.2(-0.2,+0.3) keV`.
    - The electron density is `8(-2,+1)x10^-5 cm^-3`.
    - The gas is consistent with cluster-processed infall rather than pristine WHIM.
  - **Position:** useful large-scale-structure paper because it directly images cluster assembly along filaments.

- [2607.26255](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26255) Pratik Dabhade et al. `Tracing the Cosmic web across Cosmic time through SKA observations of radio galaxies`
  - **Question:** how can SKA radio galaxies trace and diagnose the cosmic web across redshift?
  - **Data:**
    - Facility: Square Kilometre Array.
    - Frequency coverage: `50 MHz-15 GHz`.
    - Sensitivity regime: microJy to sub-microJy radio-source populations.
    - Ancillary data: optical, infrared, X-ray, SZ, redshift, and cosmic-web catalogs.
  - **Method:**
    - Use radio lobes, magnetic fields, and outflows as environment diagnostics.
    - Measure source size, morphology, spectral ageing, radio power, polarization, and Faraday rotation.
    - Place radio galaxies in 3D environments.
    - Connect populations to clusters, filaments, IGM gas, and protoclusters.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - SKA should detect large radio-source populations over broad redshift and environment ranges.
    - Radio lobes encode IGM pressure, ageing, and magnetization on megaparsec scales.
    - Radio galaxies can trace clusters, protoclusters, and lower-density cosmic-web structures.
  - **Position:** high-value survey-method paper because it ties radio AGN feedback to cosmic-web and protocluster mapping.

## Early Stellar Populations, Dark Matter, and Survey Methods

- [2607.24952](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24952) Raul Jimenez et al. `Reaching the Metallicity Floor at z~10: Lensed Star Clusters at Cosmic Dawn and Cosmic Noon`
  - **Question:** do lensed star clusters at cosmic dawn already occupy the metal-poor floor seen in old cluster systems?
  - **Data:**
    - Cosmic-dawn system: Cosmic Gems arc at `z=9.625`.
    - Cosmic-noon comparison: the Sparkler at `z=1.378`.
    - Photometry: `10` unique, doubly imaged cluster candidates in the Cosmic Gems arc.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit both cluster populations with homogeneous Bayesian stellar-population analysis.
    - Compare formation redshifts and metallicities.
    - Test models with and without binary evolution.
    - Interpret enrichment with closed-box and gas-regulator calculations.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Cosmic Gems clusters formed at `z_form~10-11`, with median `z_form~10.2`.
    - Individual metallicities are consistent with `[Z/H]<-1.2`.
    - `[Z/H]>=-0.5` is excluded for the Cosmic Gems clusters.
    - Binary-evolution models give `[Z/H]~-2.2` to `-2.7`.
    - Sparkler clusters formed later, at `z_form~2-3.5`, and are more enriched near `[Z/H]~-0.5`.
  - **Position:** high-priority cosmic-dawn entry because it links lensed cluster photometry to early assembly and enrichment.

- [2607.28564](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28564) Ethan O. Nadler et al. `Dark Matter Constraints from Small-Scale Cosmic Structure`
  - **Question:** what do nonlinear small-scale structure probes say about dark-matter microphysics?
  - **Data:**
    - Probes: dwarf galaxies, strong lensing, Lyman-alpha forest, stellar streams, and high-redshift galaxies.
    - Scale frontier: below `~1 Mpc`.
    - Models: warm, fuzzy, self-interacting, and Standard-Model-interacting dark matter.
  - **Method:**
    - Review how each observable responds to altered clustering.
    - Translate observables to microphysical model limits.
    - Identify astrophysical and observational systematics.
    - Emphasize probe combination and simulation-based inference.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Small-scale structure is the key nonlinear testbed for non-CDM physics.
    - Different probes constrain abundance, internal structure, and tidal response.
    - Joint modeling is needed to turn upcoming data into robust microphysical limits.
  - **Position:** useful reference because high-redshift galaxies and the Lyman-alpha forest are part of the dark-matter constraint frontier.

- [2607.27467](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27467) Aswin P. Vijayan et al. `Stellar photoionisation modelling in SYNTHESIZER`
  - **Question:** can SYNTHESIZER model stellar plus nebular emission for synthetic galaxy observations?
  - **Data:**
    - Codebase: SYNTHESIZER.
    - Component: photoionized gas around young stellar populations.
    - Use cases: toy models and cosmological simulations.
  - **Method:**
    - Add photoionized gas emission to the pipeline.
    - Vary SPS model, IMF, ionization parameter, density, geometry, abundance pattern, depletion, and dust.
    - Quantify effects on spectral diagnostics.
    - Demonstrate applications across simplified and simulation-derived galaxies.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - SYNTHESIZER now links galaxy-formation models to stellar and nebular spectra.
    - Spectral diagnostics depend strongly on gas and stellar-population assumptions.
    - The framework is useful for next-generation spectroscopic survey mocks.
  - **Position:** important methods paper because interpreting high-redshift emission-line surveys requires matched synthetic spectra.
