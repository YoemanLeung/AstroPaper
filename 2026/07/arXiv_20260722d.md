## Executive View

The read-first paper is [2607.18385](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18385) Huanian Zhang, which uses integral-field spectroscopy of `72` low-redshift galaxies to connect optical CGM line emission directly to central-galaxy star formation. The result is a useful low-redshift anchor for interpreting how escaped ionizing photons and feedback shape cool CGM diagnostics.

The high-redshift gas papers are especially strong. [2607.18974](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18974) Pasquier Noterdaeme reports an extreme `z_abs=4.1` molecular absorber toward a `z=4.7` quasar, with direct atomic-plus-molecular constraints implying `f_H2~60%`. [2607.18440](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18440) Prachi Prajapati revisits the CO-to-H2 conversion factor in `21` unlensed `z~1-4` DSFGs and argues that intermediate to near-Galactic `alpha_CO` values remain viable when gas geometry is handled realistically.

JWST-era early-Universe interpretation appears in two complementary entries. [2607.18868](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18868) Elie Cueto builds a semi-analytic model in which cloud-scale SFE, IMF, and stochasticity regulate bright galaxies at `z>10`. [2607.19057](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19057) Ignas Juodzbalis gives a JWST/NIRSpec census of Balmer absorption in Type 1 AGN, estimating that `44_-6^+21%` of LRDs show H-alpha absorption and connecting the absorption kinematics to radiatively driven outflows.

Two cross-listed papers matter for survey planning. [2607.18419](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18419) Dana R. Louie characterizes Roman WFI saturation and persistence from thermal-vacuum testing. [2607.18972](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18972) Seiji Toshikage shows that a source first selected as a `z~14` galaxy candidate is consistent with a Type Ia supernova at `z~4.3`, a direct warning for transient contamination in JWST high-redshift galaxy searches.

## CGM, Molecular Gas, and Quasar Absorbers

- [2607.18385](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18385) Huanian Zhang et al. `Star formation powers optical line emission from the CGM`
  - **Question:** what powers optical emission-line flux from the inner CGM of normal galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `72` normal low-redshift galaxies spanning `3` orders of magnitude in stellar mass and `4` orders of magnitude in SFR.
    - Observations: integral-field spectroscopy of the disk-halo interface.
    - Lines: H-alpha, [O II], and [O III] radial emission profiles.
  - **Method:**
    - Construct emission-line maps for individual galaxies.
    - Measure radial profiles from the main galaxy body to `5-10 R_e`, or roughly `0.1-0.25 r_vir`.
    - Compare extended line fluxes with galaxy SFR and stellar mass.
    - Use [O III]/[O II] behavior to infer ionizing-photon escape fractions.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - H-alpha, [O II], and [O III] profiles show a steep turnover at `(1-2) R_e`.
    - Outer profile slopes become shallower as central-galaxy SFR decreases.
    - Large-radius line flux correlates with SFR but not with stellar mass.
    - Escaping ionizing photons from star-forming regions can account for the inner-CGM emission.
    - Current theoretical models disagree on the dependence of cool CGM gas on central-galaxy SFR.
  - **Position:** read first because it turns low-redshift CGM emission into a direct test of star-formation feedback and ionizing-photon leakage.

- [2607.18974](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18974) Pasquier Noterdaeme et al. `Molecular gas hidden in plain sight in the early Universe`
  - **Question:** can strong H2 absorption reveal a hidden molecular-gas reservoir at high redshift?
  - **Data:**
    - Target: an intervening absorber at `z_abs=4.1` toward the `z=4.7` quasar SDSS J080023.02+305101.22.
    - Spectroscopy: archival data plus new VLT/X-shooter observations.
    - Absorber properties: metallicity about one-fifth solar and dust reddening `A_V~0.06 mag`.
  - **Method:**
    - Identify strong H2 absorption missed in earlier data.
    - Derive atomic and molecular hydrogen column densities directly.
    - Compare molecular fraction and metal-line kinematics with lower-redshift absorbers.
    - Evaluate selection and recognition biases in quasar-absorber searches.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The system has `f_H2=2N(H2)/(2N(H2)+N(HI))~60%`.
    - This is the highest directly measured molecular fraction at `z>0`.
    - The value is likely a conservative lower limit because H I includes unrelated atomic components.
    - Low-ionization metals span `Delta v~500 km s^-1`.
    - The detection implies that high-redshift molecular gas may be undercounted in current quasar samples.
  - **Position:** high-priority absorber paper because it links quasar spectroscopy, molecular gas, and early-Universe ISM/CGM census biases.

- [2607.18440](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18440) Prachi Prajapati et al. `Vz-GAL Dusty Star-Forming Galaxies: Revisiting the CO-H2 Conversion Factor Tension`
  - **Question:** do high-redshift DSFGs require a low ULIRG-like CO-to-H2 conversion factor?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `21` unlensed dusty star-forming galaxies at `z~1-4`.
    - Gas tracer: secure CO(1-0) luminosities from the VLA Vz-GAL survey.
    - Dust data: resolved ALMA `1 mm` continuum imaging at `~0.1 arcsec`.
    - Detailed subset: `12` galaxies with robust modeling constraints.
  - **Method:**
    - Estimate molecular gas masses from dust SED modeling.
    - Apply TUNER LVG modeling with a solar-metallicity gas-to-dust ratio of `100`.
    - Compare gas masses with isotropic virial and mixed rotating-plus-dispersion dynamical estimators.
    - Use GN20 at `z=4.055` as a resolved gas-geometry case study.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Dust and LVG methods give mutually consistent gas masses despite shared assumptions.
    - The inferred `alpha_CO` range is `~1.5-11.5`, with a median near Galactic `alpha_CO=4.3`.
    - Isotropic virial dynamical masses agree when realistic molecular gas sizes are used.
    - The mixed thick-disk estimator underestimates dynamical masses and produces artificially low `alpha_CO` limits.
    - Current data do not require `alpha_CO=0.8`.
  - **Position:** important DSFG calibration entry because gas masses set the timescale for dusty starbursts, compaction, and quenching at cosmic noon.

## Cosmic Dawn, LRDs, and High-Redshift Interlopers

- [2607.18868](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18868) Elie Cueto et al. `Bright Galaxies at Cosmic Dawn: A Cloud-Scale Star Formation Model Unifying Variable SFE, IMFs, and Stochasticity`
  - **Question:** how do cloud-scale star-formation physics change the abundance of UV-bright galaxies at `z>10`?
  - **Data:**
    - Model: a semi-analytic framework that follows discrete star-forming clouds in evolving dark-matter potentials.
    - Cloud properties: mass, density, metallicity, redshift, SFE, IMF, and short-timescale stellar feedback.
    - Output: burstiness regimes and UV luminosity functions.
  - **Method:**
    - Draw star-forming clouds sequentially from a cloud mass distribution.
    - Assign SFE and IMF using cloud mass, metallicity, and redshift.
    - Compare dynamic IMF, constant SFE, maximum-cloud-mass, and density-normalization variants.
    - Track how cloud-scale assumptions propagate into galaxy-scale UV luminosities.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Three regimes emerge: stochastic feedback-limited star formation in low-mass halos, bursty regulation by cloud masses, and smoother star formation in massive halos.
    - Top-heavy IMFs and enhanced SFEs in massive clouds amplify burstiness.
    - The IMF has the strongest impact on the UV luminosity function.
    - Constant SFE boosts the faint end, while reducing maximum cloud mass suppresses the bright end.
  - **Position:** high-priority theory entry because it connects JWST bright-galaxy counts to explicit sub-galactic star-formation assumptions instead of only halo abundance.

- [2607.19057](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19057) Ignas Juodzbalis et al. `An (in)complete NIRSpec census of Balmer absorption in Type 1 AGN -- radiation-driven outflows in little red dots, quasars and variable stars`
  - **Question:** how common is Balmer absorption in JWST Type 1 AGN and what drives it in LRDs?
  - **Data:**
    - Sample: `47` Type 1 AGN spanning `2<z<7`.
    - Instrument: JWST/NIRSpec, including R1000 and R2700 resolution regimes.
    - Populations: LRDs, Little Blue Dots, quasars, and stellar H-alpha absorbers.
  - **Method:**
    - Build a census of Balmer absorption while accounting for JWST spectroscopic incompleteness.
    - Compare LRD and Little Blue Dot incidence rates.
    - Test correlations between Balmer absorption velocities and spectral properties.
    - Compare LRD absorption with broad absorption-line quasars and stellar absorption analogs.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The inferred H-alpha absorption incidence in LRDs is `44_-6^+21%`.
    - The Little Blue Dot incidence is `<25%` at `2 sigma`.
    - R1000 data are strongly resolution-limited for separating Doppler broadening from optical-depth effects.
    - R2700 data reduce but do not remove the degeneracy.
    - Balmer absorption velocity correlates significantly with [O III] 5007 narrow-line luminosity.
    - LRD and broad-absorption-line quasar absorption are consistent with radiatively driven outflows.
  - **Position:** read first for LRD work because it ties a common JWST spectral peculiarity to outflow kinematics and NIRSpec resolution limits.

- [2607.18972](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18972) Seiji Toshikage et al. `A Type Ia Supernova Candidate at z~4.3: A Transient Interloper in the Search for z~14 Galaxies`
  - **Question:** can a high-redshift transient masquerade as a very high-redshift galaxy candidate?
  - **Data:**
    - Candidate: beacon_1420-5253_4770, initially identified as a `z~14` galaxy candidate.
    - Observational clue: non-detections before and after the detection epoch.
    - Comparison data: colors, magnitudes, light curves, and transient SED templates.
  - **Method:**
    - Test persistent-galaxy and transient interpretations.
    - Compare the observed colors and SED with Type Ia supernova templates.
    - Use the inferred redshift and event rate to discuss delay-time constraints.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The object is consistent with a Type Ia supernova at `z~4.3`.
    - Fe-group absorption in Type Ia spectra can mimic a Lyman break.
    - At `z~4.3`, the cosmic age is `~1.5 Gyr`.
    - The implied event rate requires a minimum delay time shorter than `1 Gyr`.
    - Transient contamination is a concrete risk for searches for `z~14` galaxies.
  - **Position:** practical high-redshift selection paper because it converts an extreme-galaxy candidate into a contamination case with direct survey-design consequences.

## Survey Infrastructure, Environment, and Morphology

- [2607.18419](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18419) Dana R. Louie et al. `Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope Wide Field Instrument: Bright Point Source Saturation Response and Persistence Properties from Thermal-Vacuum Testing`
  - **Question:** how does Roman WFI respond to bright saturated point sources and persistence in survey-like exposures?
  - **Data:**
    - Facility test: Roman WFI second Thermal Vacuum campaign.
    - Detector setup: two Sensor Chip Assemblies illuminated through the F146 filter.
    - Test sources: nine in-focus point sources with approximate magnitudes from `~4` to `~18`.
    - Exposure scale: `~170 s` survey-like illuminations.
  - **Method:**
    - Project sources through the Stimulus of Ray Cones telescope simulator.
    - Measure saturation-region growth as a function of source brightness.
    - Track adjacent-pixel non-linearity near the saturation front.
    - Measure post-illumination dark exposures to characterize persistence decay.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - A `~4 mag` source saturates a region about `~150` pixels in diameter after `~170 s`.
    - A `~12 mag` source saturates a region about `~15` pixels in diameter.
    - Pixels adjacent to the saturation front show non-linear behavior consistent with charge leakage.
    - Persistence after illumination is broadly similar across `~4-17 mag`.
    - Persistence decays to detector-background levels of `<=0.05 e- s^-1` within about `20 min`.
  - **Position:** useful Roman infrastructure entry because detector persistence and bright-source masks will affect wide-field time-domain, microlensing, and high-latitude survey products.

- [2607.19162](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19162) Niu Li et al. `Physical Properties of 6.7 Million Galaxies from the DESI Bright Galaxy Survey: Spectral Fitting and Systematic Tests with Mock Spectra`
  - **Question:** how reliable are full-spectral-fitting physical properties for DESI BGS galaxies?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: DESI DR1 Bright Galaxy Survey.
    - Sample size: `~6.7 million` galaxy spectra.
    - Derived quantities: stellar mass, stellar velocity dispersion, stellar population age, dust attenuation, and emission-line properties.
    - Validation set: mock spectra matched to DESI noise and spectral features.
  - **Method:**
    - Fit galaxy spectra with a customized full-spectral-fitting pipeline.
    - Recover known parameters from mock spectra as a function of continuum S/N and redshift.
    - Compare stellar masses with independent photometric SED-fitting estimates.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Stellar masses are recovered with negligible bias for spectra with `S/N>=5`.
    - Low-S/N spectra with `S/N<=5` show mild stellar-mass overestimation of `~0.1 dex`.
    - Emission-line fluxes are recovered with high accuracy and minimal bias.
    - The released value-added catalog supports large statistical studies of galaxy evolution.
  - **Position:** relevant low-redshift benchmark because DESI BGS provides a high-statistics calibration set for environment, stellar mass, and emission-line systematics.

- [2607.18414](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18414) Laura Ferrarese et al. `The Next Generation Virgo Cluster Survey (NGVS). II. A Catalog of Galaxies in the Virgo Cluster`
  - **Question:** what is the final deep optical membership catalog for the Virgo cluster across the NGVS footprint?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: NGVS with CFHT/MegaCam.
    - Footprint: contiguous `104 deg^2`, or `8.63 Mpc^2` at the Virgo distance.
    - Bands: `u*`, `g`, `i`, `z`, plus partial `r` coverage.
    - Catalog: `3680` bona fide Virgo galaxies.
  - **Method:**
    - Construct a cluster-member catalog from deep wide-field imaging.
    - Add photometric, structural, nuclear, morphological, stellar-mass, and radial-velocity information when available.
    - Quantify completeness relative to the Virgo Cluster Catalog.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - The catalog adds `2100` previously uncataloged galaxies.
    - Known Virgo membership increases by a factor of `2.3`.
    - The catalog spans `g=8.42-24.41 mag`, or `M_g=-22.67` to `-6.68 mag`.
    - Completeness reaches `g=18.6 mag`, corresponding to `M_g=-12.5 mag` and `M_*~1.6x10^7 M_sun`.
    - The `50%` completeness point is `g=22.0 mag`, corresponding to `M_g=-9.1 mag` and `M_*~6.2x10^5 M_sun`.
  - **Position:** useful environment baseline because Virgo remains the nearest high-density reference for dwarf demographics, morphology, and low-surface-brightness completeness.

- [2607.18654](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18654) Youngmin Baek et al. `Morphology-spin connection in the SAMI Galaxy Survey`
  - **Question:** which galaxy properties best explain stellar spin measured by `lambda_Re`?
  - **Data:**
    - Survey: SAMI Galaxy Survey.
    - Spin metric: `lambda_Re`, used as a stellar angular-momentum and kinematic-morphology proxy.
    - Predictors: bulge-to-total ratio within one effective radius, ellipticity, light-weighted age, and stellar mass.
  - **Method:**
    - Apply partial correlation analysis.
    - Apply partial least-squares regression.
    - Test whether non-IFS galaxy properties reproduce the observed `lambda_Re` distribution.
  - **Conclusion:**
    - Bulge-to-total ratio and ellipticity have the strongest correlations with `lambda_Re`.
    - These morphology indicators dominate the multivariate regression.
    - The result supports the fast-rotator sequence linking fast-rotating early-type galaxies and spirals.
    - Morphology indicators reproduce the broad observed `lambda_Re` distribution with scatter of about `0.12`.
    - The slow-rotator regime is not reproduced well from these simple properties.
  - **Position:** useful morphology-method paper because it clarifies when photometric structure can stand in for IFS-based spin in large imaging samples.

## Other Relevant Entries

- [2607.18707](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18707) Divyansh Srivastava estimates stellar metallicities from Gaia DR3 XP data using `1.20 million` LAMOST DR10 labels, reaching held-out mean absolute error `0.052 dex` and recovering a broken thin-disk radial metallicity gradient.
- [2607.19260](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19260) M.V. Legnardi uses JWST observations of an LMC outskirts field to measure the mass function down to `0.17 M_sun`, finding binary fraction `f_bin(q>0.6)=0.15+/-0.01` and MF slope `alpha=-1.49+/-0.16`.
- [2607.18761](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18761) Samuel McGuire develops a semi-analytic model for stellar binaries near SMBHs in the empty-loss-cone regime; GR precession reduces the merger fraction from `84%` to `3%`, and stellar tides reduce it to `0.4%`.
- [2607.18408](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18408) Asahi Idei combines Keck AO imaging with microlensing light-curve modeling for OGLE-2014-BLG-0676L, a Roman Galactic Plane Survey validation case but not a galaxy-evolution priority.
